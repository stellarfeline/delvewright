# Spike: eldritch visuals (vanilla 1.21.11)

**Question.** Can pinned vanilla Minecraft Java 1.21.11 show Lovecraftian, "unspeakable" horror visually, without leaning on text? Most of the answer is client-side rendering, which no server render or bot can judge, so the deliverable is a short walk the owner plays herself, plus a server rig that proves everything up to the packet the client receives.

Research tooling only: never shipped, not wired into CI, never read by the compiler. The pack is generated by `gen.py` (deterministic) and committed so it can be copied straight into a world.

## Play it

1. Close the world if it is open. The pack adds two biomes, and biomes load only when a world opens; `/reload` does not add them.
2. Open `datapack_test` (the pack is already in its `datapacks/` folder as `dw-eldritch-spike`). Cheats must be on for `/function`.
3. Run `/function dwe:start`. It loads the site and teleports you to its north end (x 4096, z 4088, well away from the island near 0,0).
4. Walk south. Each station has a glowing sign. Walk, do not fly fast: every station fires when you step into it.
5. At the end, `/function dwe:stop` removes the lab's entities and unloads the site. Blocks stay. Running `start` again rebuilds and resets everything.

Settings that weaken effects (Options > Accessibility, and Video): **Distortion Effects** below 100% turns nausea into a green overlay; **Darkness Pulsing** at 0% removes the pulsing (the fog stays); **Particles** below All can drop ambient ash; **Biome Blend** changes how gradually grass and water tint change. The owner's `options.txt` has all of these at full strength (distortion 1.0, darkness 1.0, particles All, clouds on, biome blend 5x5, every sound category 1.0).

## Readings

**`/fillbiome` and a connected client.** It reaches the client without a chunk reload. Measured: after the threshold fired, the bot received 2 `chunk_biomes` packets naming all 6 of the 6 changed chunks, and 0 `map_chunk` and 0 `unload_chunk` packets for those chunks. Cross-checked in the 1.21.11 client itself (`minecraft-1.21.11-client.jar` disassembled with `javap`, names resolved through Mojang's official 1.21.11 client mappings): `ClientPacketListener.handleChunksBiomes` calls `ClientChunkCache.replaceBiomes` for each chunk, then `ClientLevel.onChunkLoaded` (which clears the tint caches), then `LevelRenderer.setSectionDirty` for every section of the 3x3 chunks around each one. So fog and sky (read from the biome at the camera every frame) change at once, and grass, foliage and water tint are re-meshed without F3+A. Station 2 is the visual confirmation.

**What a biome cannot do in the overworld.** Environment attributes stack dimension < biome < timeline < weather. The overworld's `minecraft:day` timeline (read from the 1.21.11 jar's data) multiplies `sky_color`, `fog_color`, `cloud_color`, `sky_light_color` and `sky_light_factor` (a biome's value survives, darkened at night), takes the maximum of `star_brightness` (so a biome can force stars at noon, used here), and overrides `sun_angle`, `moon_angle`, `star_angle`, `sunrise_sunset_color` and (via `minecraft:moon`) `moon_phase`. A biome therefore cannot move the sun in the overworld; doing so needs a custom dimension or an edited timeline, both world-wide. Not measured on a client: read from the data. Attributes such as `ambient_light_color`, `block_light_tint` and `night_vision_color` documented online do not exist in 1.21.11 (absent from the jar).

## Expected strength and limits, per technique

- **Display-entity giant (station 1): the strongest technique.** Interpolation is done by the client every frame, so motion at 5-tick keyframes should look continuous; translation is interpolated linearly between keyframes and rotation spherically, so very fast curls would cut corners (these do not move fast). Limits: the body is visibly made of boxes where it bends sharply (overlap hides gaps, not the facets); light comes from the entity's own block, not from each segment, so the whole tentacle shares one light level (the submerged part is forced black with `brightness`); cost is negligible at 68 entities, and a few hundred would still be cheap on the server, with the client the likelier limit; anything translated far from the entity's own position still renders, but the entity stops rendering beyond its view range (default `view_range` 1.0, roughly 64 blocks, scaled by Entity Distance).
- **Environment attributes (station 2): strong, and the most controllable.** Fog distance, colour, sky light tint and silenced music change the whole mood of a place with no entities at all. Limits: the sun cannot be moved from a biome in the overworld; the timeline darkens everything at night; biome cells are 4x4x4, so the edge of a zone is blocky in grass tint and blended in fog.
- **Status effects (station 3): effective but short, and they read as "game effects".** Accessibility settings weaken them by design (Distortion Effects, Darkness Pulsing). The curse face is the most startling element and works once or twice; it is a particle, so a client with Particles below All may drop it (sent here in `force` mode; not tested at Minimal).
- **Impossible space (station 4): likely convincing.** The relative teleport keeps velocity and facing, so the body motion should not hitch; what can betray it is the visible far end (12 blocks nearer after each jump) and the daylight at the entrance if you turn round. Only a client can say whether the jump is invisible.
- **Living environment (station 5): mixed by expectation.** The enderman that only stares, in the dark, with its glowing eyes, is the likeliest to land; sculk sounds and the heartbeat are atmosphere, but players know them from the Deep Dark.
