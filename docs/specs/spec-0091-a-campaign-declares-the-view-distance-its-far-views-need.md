# spec-0091: A campaign declares the view distance its far views need — `world.view_distance` is the server's view distance, every far view is judged against the radius it serves, and the build states what it costs the host

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against engine `d67aec1e` (`origin/main`), read only — `DELVE_VIEW_DISTANCE`, `DELVE_SIMULATION_DISTANCE` and the `server.properties` emission in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit.rs`; `crates/delvec/tests/server_properties.rs`; `WorldContent` in `crates/dsl/src/stages.rs`; `Sightline` and `View` in `crates/dsl/src/siteplan.rs`; `sightlines` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/blockout.rs`; `Camera`, `prove_showcase` and `ray_meets_box` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/view/camera.rs`; `check_cutscenes`, `walk_cells` and `World::blocks_camera` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav.rs`; `ExpandedShot`, `AimTrack` and `Frame` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/camera.rs`; `SPAN_REACH`, `FOG_END_DEFAULT` and `Fog` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/loop.rs`; `[server].heap_max` in `versions.toml`, `tools/lib/server-heap.sh`, `validation/world-settings-entrypoint.sh`, `validation/Dockerfile.delve`, `validation/check-world-settings.sh`, `validation/packtest-run.sh`, `tools/creator/playtest-server.sh`, `tools/tests/test_server_heap.py`; `docs/reference/compiler.md`'s *World / build output* record; spec-0080 §2–§3, spec-0082 §8 row 8, spec-0086 §4.3, spec-0087 §criteria 19, spec-0026 §6. Two unmerged specs were read for §7 and are not depended on: spec-0090 on `feat/loop-far-end` and spec-0089 on `feat/post-beat-camera`. One rig was built and run for this spec, `tools/spike-view-distance/ at 80f1a72135e9`, against the pinned server image and the pinned client jar; its record is `tools/spike-view-distance/observations.json` (§2).
- **What it is for**: a landmark meant to be seen from across the map. Every delve is served a view distance of 10 chunks, a 160-block radius, pinned in the compiler; a hill-sized body seen as an outline from 450 blocks away is never sent to the client, so the view cannot render, and nothing in the tree refuses the design, the camera or the cutscene that relies on it — the defect surfaces in play (issue #888). This spec makes the view distance a declaration of the campaign whose far views need it, judges every far view against the radius the declaration serves, and has the build state what the declaration costs the host, which the hosting side meets (CLAUDE.md, *host hardware never caps a capability*; the owner's ruling that far views matter to design and are not cut for the host).
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record of what the tree does and what the rig measured. Each statement is marked **cited** (a line of the tree at `d67aec1e`, a reading in the rig's record, bytecode of the pinned client jar read through Mojang's official mappings, or a constitution rule) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0091`. One diagnostic, `DW0956` (§4), in five shapes. `world.view_distance` is a new stage-1 field, a format change: `dsl_version` moves to `0.36.0` through `tools/lib/version_sites.py`'s rows and every document in the repository moves with it.
- **Non-goals**: the simulation distance (§1: it never moves with the view distance, and this spec exposes no field for it); an entity seen from far (§7: the tracker's cutoff is the entity's, not the view's, and `entity-broadcast-range-percentage` stays an unpinned key nothing needs yet); fog (§7: an atmosphere closes a view inside the radius by design, and the check judges what is sent, not what is drawn); the player's own client setting (§6: nothing on the server raises it); a view distance below the engine's floor (§3); CPU as a stated resource (§5: measured and recorded, not computed per campaign — the server's tick cost at a declared distance is a reading, and the hosting side reads it in this spec's table).

## 1. The thing, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (`emit.rs`, `DELVE_VIEW_DISTANCE`): the shipped `view-distance` is a compiler constant, 10 chunks, with the reasoning that the largest delve built to date spans 114 × 165 blocks, so a 160-block radius reaches the far side of any scene in the corpus from any standpoint inside it, and that *it is the absence of any delve content past 160 blocks that stops it going up*. **Cited** (`DELVE_SIMULATION_DISTANCE`): the simulation distance is a separate constant, 10, whose job is to make the ticking rim a known radius for a whole-plane proof — the scene is force-loaded by `setup` and never released, so what ticks is the scene plus the rim, and the rim is backdrop by construction.

**Authored — the object.** How far a player must be able to see is a property of the world the party stands in, declared once beside the other statements about it (`time`, `weather`, `horizon`, `atmospheres`): `world.view_distance`, in chunks, the vanilla primitive the server's `view-distance` key already is (CLAUDE.md, *if vanilla provides a primitive content needs, the DSL exposes it first-class*). It is not a property of a camera, a sightline or a view: three objects can each need a far view, and a field on any one of them would be the second-bespoke-field shape; the one declaration serves all three and the server reads one number.

**Authored — the two distances move separately.** The simulation distance stays the pinned constant. Three reasons, the first measured: with the whole party online, moving the simulation distance with the view distance to 32 took the server from 4.2 ms to 53 ms a tick and from 54 % to 107 % of a core (§2.4), a cost that renders nothing — the scene ticks because it is force-loaded, and what the rim would tick is water or void. The second is the proof bound the constant exists for. The third is that no creator judgement lives in it: ticking is not a view.

## 2. What the tree does today, and what the rig measured

### 2.1 The tree

**Cited** (`server_properties.rs`): `view-distance` and `simulation-distance` are two of the 15 pinned keys, asserted at 10 and 10, and the build's `server/README.md` states both. **Cited** (`Dockerfile.delve`, `world-settings-entrypoint.sh`): the image's entrypoint derives `VIEW_DISTANCE` and `SIMULATION_DISTANCE` from the build's `server.properties`, and `check-world-settings.sh` asserts the derivation per key. **Cited** (`versions.toml` `[server]`): the heap ceiling every server gets is a constant, `4G`, measured once (vesperhold's 84 tiles plus 170 horizon templates: 44 OutOfMemoryErrors at 1G, 0 at 4G), with the note that *any formula from template count to heap would be invented*; the entrypoint's literal, `tools/lib/server-heap.sh` and `tools/tests/test_server_heap.py` hold every server entry point to it, and `playtest-server.sh --memory` / `-e MEMORY` override it.

**Cited** — what judges a far view today: nothing. `blockout::sightlines` walks a `vision` edge's segment for solid cells (`DW0821`) and never asks how long it is; `View` is counted in the site-plan binding line and consumed by nothing in the compiler; `prove_showcase` asks of a showcase camera that its eye cell is clear and that its central ray meets the loaded extent (`DW0724`), not how far along that ray the scene is; `check_cutscenes` ray-marches the dolly for solids (`DW0308`) and bounds the aim rate (`DW0347`), never the distance to the aim.

### 2.2 The set the server sends

**Instrument.** `tools/spike-view-distance/run.sh at 80f1a72135e9` boots the pinned image (`itzg/minecraft-server@sha256:3e7db256…`, `versions.toml` `[images.base]`, vanilla 1.21.11) on a flat world at one `view-distance` / `simulation-distance` pair per cell — the ocean superflat the compiler writes for `horizon: ocean`, or a void flat — and `measure.mjs` joins four mineflayer 4.37.1 bots (the player cap), each declaring 32 chunks to the server, stands them in spectator 2048 blocks apart at y 130, and reads every chunk column each client is sent once the count has held still (15 s at 10–24 chunks; 90 s at 32, where delivery under generation load takes over two minutes). Every rcon reply is read.

**Cited, the rig** (`observations.json`, `sent_chunks[].profile` of every cell): the set a client is sent is the chunks whose offset `(a, b)` from the player's chunk satisfies `(max(0, |a| − 2))² + (max(0, |b| − 2))² < n²`, `n` the view distance — one more ring than `n` along the axes (Chebyshev extent `n + 1`), cut at the corners. The counts: **473** at 10, **1057** at 16, **2181** at 24, **3725** at 32, each the formula's count exactly; `compiler::served::sent_chunks` is that formula and `served::tests::sent_chunks_is_the_rigs_count` holds it to every client reading in the record. A sent set that stops at Chebyshev 11 at view distance 10 guarantees 176 blocks on the axes from any standing position in the player's chunk (the rig's `guaranteed_radius_blocks`); this spec does not rest the served radius on that ring (§2.3).

**Cited, the rig** (the first matrix run, kept in the record as the client-side fact): with the bots at mineflayer's default client setting of 12 chunks, a server at 16 sent a Chebyshev-13 set of 637 chunks — the formula at `n = 12`. The client's own render-distance setting travels to the server in its client-information packet, and the server sends `min(server view-distance, client setting)`.

### 2.3 What the client draws

**Cited, the pinned client jar** (`1.21.11`, read through Mojang's official mappings: `net.minecraft.client.Options`): `getEffectiveRenderDistance()` returns `min(renderDistance option, serverRenderDistance)` when the server has sent its view distance, else the option; the `renderDistance` option is constructed with default **12**, range 2–32. **Cited** (`net.minecraft.client.renderer.ViewArea`): the section grid the renderer draws is `2 × effective render distance + 1` sections on each horizontal axis, centred on the camera's section.

**Authored — the served radius.** A player whose client is set to the declared number `n` draws a square of `n` sections around the camera's section; a camera at the far edge of its section sees `16 × n` blocks along an axis before the first undrawn section, and farther on every other bearing. So **the radius a declaration serves is `16 × n` blocks in every direction** (`dsl::viewdistance::served_radius_blocks`), what a player who set their client to it is sure of; the server's extra ring is margin nobody draws, because the effective render distance is capped at `n`.

### 2.5 The tracker

**Cited, the rig** (the `track` cell at view distance 32): a `block_display` and an `armor_stand` summoned east of a client were in its entity list at 128, 144 and 160 blocks and absent at 176, 192, 256, 320 and 368 — the same cutoff spec-0082 §8 row 8 measured at view distance 12 (seen at 160, nothing at 176). The server stops tracking those entities for a client between 10 and 11 chunks whatever the view distance: the cutoff is the entity type's tracking range at `entity-broadcast-range-percentage=100`, not the view distance.

## 3. The surface

**Authored.** Stage 1, `world.json`:

```json
"view_distance": 20
```

- An integer number of chunks, `10..=32`. The ceiling is the pinned server's (vanilla clamps `view-distance` to 32). The floor is the engine's own, 10: every proof in the engine is written against it (§7), and a smaller number only takes views away — nothing in a delve is designed for a tighter radius, so a declaration below it is refused rather than served (`DW0956`, shape A).
- **Absent = the floor.** Unlike `time`, this is not a design decision the engine would be supplying silently: the floor is the engine's statement of what every delve is served, printed on every build (§8) as *the engine's floor, undeclared*, and a design whose far views need more is the design that declares. Every campaign that declares nothing is served what it was served before this spec, byte for byte in `server.properties`.
- The one reading is `dsl::viewdistance::chunks(&campaign)`; the properties file, every refusal and the stated cost take it, and nothing reads the field directly.

## 4. The served radius, and what is refused

**Authored.** Let `R = 16 × view_distance` blocks (§2.3). A thing farther from a body than `R` is never sent to that body's client, so a far view aimed past `R` cannot render; one rule, `DW0956`, in five shapes:

- **A.** `view_distance` outside `10..=32` — validation tier (exit 1), on `/content/view_distance`, `dsl::viewdistance::checks`. The site plan's lines are then judged against the floor, so one bad number does not hide every far line behind it.
- **B.** A site-plan `sightlines[i]` whose `from`–`to` length exceeds `R` — validation tier, on `/content/sightlines/{i}`. Both ends are world coordinates the document already holds, so nothing is built to know it.
- **C.** A site-plan `views[i]` whose `eye`–`look_at` length exceeds `R` — validation tier, on `/content/views/{i}`. (`views[]` are the design's declared vantages, the rows the walk judges the silhouette from.)
- **D.** A showcase camera of `design/cameras.json` whose **subject** is farther than `R` from the lens — build tier (exit 3), the third shape of `prove_showcase` beside the clear eye and the met extent. The subject is the thing the camera is aimed at: the first occupied cell on its central ray inside the loaded scene (`nav::walk_cells` over the same occupancy the clear-eye shape reads), else the point where that ray enters the loaded extent (the entry `ray_meets_box` already computed and threw away). A camera standing inside the scene aimed at open sky has its subject at distance 0 and is not refused: what it frames in its lower half is a composition, not a far view.
- **E.** A cutscene keyframe farther than `R` from its aim — build tier, in `check_cutscenes` after the clip checks, over the expanded shot's frames: the aim at a keyframe's tick is `look_at` or a static subject (`AimTrack::Static`), or a moving subject's position at that tick (`AimTrack::Moving`); a shot facing along its travel (`AimTrack::Travel`) has no aim and is counted, not judged. Every keyframe is a body's eye for a tick, and the aim at that tick is what the picture is of.

**Authored — the message.** Every shape names what is aimed, how far, what `R` is and whether it was declared, and the prescription: `world.view_distance: ⌈d / 16⌉` — the fewest chunks that serve the distance, one edit to the one field, asserted to reach a different verdict by `crates/delvec/tests/view_distance.rs` and `view::camera`'s tests — or the two ends nearer; a distance past 512 blocks names the ceiling instead and prescribes only the latter.

## 5. The stated cost

**Authored, under CLAUDE.md** (*a declared capability's resource cost is computed and stated by the engine, and the hosting side meets it*). The cost of a view distance is the chunks the server keeps for its players, and the build states it in three places, computed from the declaration at the player cap of 4 (`compiler::served`):

- **The model.** Chunks sent per client: `served::sent_chunks(n)`, the pinned server's own set (§2.2). Live heap: `LIVE_BASE_MIB + LIVE_PER_CHUNK_KIB × chunks × players` (§2.4, the ocean backdrop, the heavier of the two a delve ships). The ceiling: the floor `versions.toml` `[server].heap_max` — the scene's cost, measured once, which covers the floor's view and which no view distance lowers — **plus twice** the fitted live increment of the declared distance over the floor, rounded up to whole GiB. Twice, because a JVM given exactly its live set collects continuously. At the floor the statement is the pin; at 16 it is 5G, at 24 6G, at 32 7G. `served::HEAP_FLOOR_GIB` is held equal to the pin by `tools/tests/test_server_heap.py`, and `served::tests` hold the fit to the record.
- **`server/resources.properties`**, emitted beside `server.properties`: `heap-max=<size>` and `players=4`, in the key=value form the image's entrypoint already reads. The entrypoint (both copies) exports `MAX_MEMORY` from it when the operator names none of `MEMORY`, `INIT_MEMORY`, `MAX_MEMORY`, else the pin's literal for a tree with no file; `check-world-settings.sh` asserts the derivation. `tools/lib/server-heap.sh`'s `dw_server_heap_max [build-dir]` and `dw_server_heap_env [override] [build-dir]` print the build's statement when a build is in hand; `playtest-server.sh up` hands its `--out` tree, and `packtest-run.sh` reads the build it boots for the ceiling it asserts in itzg's own words. An operator's own figure still wins everywhere, as it does today.
- **`server/README.md`** states the declaration (or the floor), the render-distance floor a player sets (§6) and the heap; the binding line (§8) states the heap on every build; the skill's hand-over states what the delve asks of its host.

CPU is recorded (§2.4) and not computed: the tick cost at sim 10 is flat in the view distance, and what the container spends is chunk delivery.

## 6. The player's client

**Cited** (§2.2–§2.3): the client sends its own render-distance setting to the server, the server sends the smaller of the two, and the client draws `min(option, server)`. **Authored — what the delve can and cannot do.** Nothing on the server raises a client's setting, and a player at the default 12 who joins a delve declaring 20 is served and drawn 12: the far view is simply not there for them, silently. So:

- the declaration is the **player's render-distance floor**, and it is stated where the player reads: the build's `server/README.md` says *a player who wants every far view this delve was designed with sets render distance to at least `n` chunks*, and the storybook of a delve declaring above the client default carries the render-distance line directly under its connect line (the skill's hand-over step, the number copied from the README);
- the creator is told on every build's binding line (§8), and the check is the engine's statement that the far views it refused nothing for are the ones a player at `n` sees;
- a client set above `n` gains nothing (capped at the server's), which is why the storybook line says *at least*.

## 7. Composition

- **spec-0080 (an atmosphere's fog).** `visual/fog_end_distance` closes a view inside `R` by design — a silhouette in mist is a legitimate far view — so `DW0956` judges what is sent, never what fog draws; an atmosphere's fog end past `R` is drawn to `R` and fogged by the client at its render edge as vanilla always does. **Authored**: no fog term enters the check.
- **spec-0086 / spec-0090 (the loop's view).** The loop kernel's span reach is `SPAN_REACH` = 128 blocks, inside the floor's 160, and its fog ends are read from the atmospheres; a declared distance only widens what is served, so a loop's near and far fields stay served under every declaration. **Authored**: the floor of 10 is what keeps the kernel's reach inside the served radius, and this is the reason a declaration below the floor is refused rather than honoured.
- **spec-0082 (assembly tracking).** §2.5: the tracker's cutoff is 10 chunks at every view distance, so a far view is of **blocks** — a sculpted body (spec-0087), a horizon ring, a tower — never of an assembly. `entity-broadcast-range-percentage` stays an unpinned key: raising it is a different capability no content needs yet, recorded here as the gap it is.
- **spec-0087 (a body the size of a hill).** The demo's criterion 19 asks for *a reachable vantage within the served view distance*; the campaign now declares the distance its vantage needs, and a vantage past it is `DW0956` at the plan.
- **spec-0026 (the valley horizon).** A surround ring beyond `R` is backdrop nobody is sent; a horizon whose silhouette the design needs declares the distance that serves it.
- **spec-0038 (standing fluid).** Its extent rule dilates by `max(view, simulation)` distance; the view half is now the declared number, and the simulation half is unchanged.

## 8. Binding lines and ledger

**Authored.** `delvec validate` prints `view distance binding: N chunk(s) (declared | the engine's floor, undeclared) serve R blocks in every direction; S sightline(s) and V view(s) judged against it, B beyond it.` — `dsl::viewdistance::Binding`, with every count computed from the objects. `delvec build` prints the same head followed by `C showcase camera(s) and K cutscene shot(s) judged against it, 0 beyond it; stated to the host: heap-max <size> for 4 players × <chunks> chunks each (server/resources.properties).` — `served::Binding`; the zero is a statement, because a refusal stopped the build before the line. The cost is in `server/resources.properties` and `server/README.md`; nothing else is ledgered, since a view distance is not a finding.

## 10. Decisions

1. The declaration is a world property, in chunks, the vanilla primitive; absent is the engine's floor, stated on every build.
2. The simulation distance never moves with it (§1, measured in §2.4).
3. The served radius is `16 × n` blocks in every direction — the client's square at the effective render distance — not the server's wider sent set.
4. The subject of a showcase camera is the first occupied cell on its central ray, else the ray's entry into the loaded scene.
5. The stated heap is the pin plus twice the fitted increment over the floor, in whole GiB; the pin stays the scene's floor and the fallback of a tree with no statement.
6. The client's setting is the player's to make; the delve states the floor they set, in the README and the storybook, and refuses nothing for it.
