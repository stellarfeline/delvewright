# spec-0083: The route proof takes a teleport as a link — a repeatable trigger that carries the party out of its volume is a leg every proof reads, gated on the stage the trigger requires

- **Status**: Approved
- **Ground**: written against engine `e3a6dd36` (`origin/main`), read only — `Plan::transport`, `Plan::critical_path_transport`, `Plan::transit_teleports`, `collect_transit_teleports`, `build_critical_path`, `path_triggers`, `trigger_may_be_performed`, `opens_a_way` and `collect_region_events` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/plan.rs`; `World::transit_teleports`, `World::is_teleport_source`, `positions_of`, `check_critical_path`, `check_branch_path`, `critical_path_routes`, `branch_path_routes` and `check_bodies_can_leave` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav.rs`; `population_roots` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/lethal.rs`; `STRIKE_REACH` and the party-cell population in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/strand.rs`; `check_reach_footprint` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/reach.rs`; the `teleport` proof and its ledger in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/teleport.rs`; `branch_transport_overlay`, the `critical-path.json` writer and the cutscene bracket (`cs_start`/`cs_tick`/`cs_end`) in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit.rs`; `Verb::Teleport` and `EnvTrigger` in `crates/dsl/src/stages.rs`; `TRANSPORT_TIMEOUT_MS`, `TRANSPORT_NEAR`, `TRANSPORT_SETTLE_MS`, `TRANSPORT_JUMP_BLOCKS`, `onForcedMove`, `stepSettled`, `atTransportDest` and `awaitTransport` in `harness/src/executor.ts`; the step parser in `harness/src/critical-path.ts`; `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py` with `tools/ci/gallery_units.py`; the gallery's `quests.json`, `layout-graph.json` and `probes/`; `crates/delvec/tests/fixtures/lift/` and `crates/delvec/tests/fixtures/teleport-volume/`; `docs/reference/compiler.md`'s effect `teleport` row, `DW0311`, `DW0872`, `DW0873`, `DW0881`, `DW0891`, `DW0921`, `DW0924`, `DW0816`, `DW0817` and the "Crossings, and what a leg is" record.
- **What it is for**: the route proof knows two ways the party moves — a walk, and the one crossing the compiler itself authors when consecutive objectives stand in different areas. An authored `teleport` is neither: the walk proof reads its source volume only to *stop judging* a leg that starts inside one, and no proof reads its destination as somewhere the party arrives. A correct campaign whose only way onward is a teleport between two fixed structures inside one area — a boat built once on each shore, a crossing played as a cutscene between them — is refused `DW0311`, and the refusal's remedy (a doorway, a gap, a fence ring) is about a walk nobody was going to take. This spec makes such a teleport a **link**: a leg the route proof, the branch proof, the waypoint artifacts, the bot, the leave proof, the danger population, the reach-footprint population and the layout graph all read, from one record, under one gate.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record of what the tree does today. Each rule below is marked **cited** (a line of the tree at `e3a6dd36`, a measurement recorded in the tree, or a constitution rule) or **authored** (this spec chooses). No fact here was taken from a running server: every runtime claim is either the emitter's own text or a measurement already recorded in `docs/reference/compiler.md`, and §2 says which.
- **Numbers**: spec `0083`; `DW0932` (a link's geometry), `DW0933` (a teleport under a cutscene), `DW0934` (a carry the graph and the quests disagree about); `DW0311`'s message widens and takes no new code. §7's layout-graph edge class is a stage-4 surface change inside the unpublished `dsl_version`, which unreleased format changes share.
- **Non-goals**: a teleport whose `from` and `to` stand in different areas (a crossing stays the compiler's: `DW0872`/`DW0873` and the per-objective `transport` map are untouched, §3.6); a `teleport` hosted anywhere but a trigger, which stays what it is today — a gather of whoever is inside the box, never leaned on (§3.1); the lift's two recorded gaps (an in-car affordance, a region at an offset — `crates/delvec/tests/v10_lift.rs`), which this spec does not reopen; a PackTest template for a `sequence`'s timeline, a standing debt spec-0031 records; any reset of fall distance (compiler.md, effect `teleport`: a teleport is not a rescue, and what resets the counter was explicitly not measured — this spec emits nothing toward it and the rule stands as written).

## 1. The defect, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (the tree at `e3a6dd36`). The route model has one notion of *being carried*: `Plan::transport`, objective id → absolute destination, derived in `build_critical_path` for every leg whose two ends stand in different areas, emitted as an unconditional `tp` in that objective's completion bundle, and mirrored per step into `critical_path_transport`, which `positions_of` reads to mark the next visited position `transport_before` so the walk proof, the route export and the waypoint artifacts all skip that leg as a ride. The harness reads the same marker as the step's `transport` field and waits for the landing (`awaitTransport`). That is one authority, and it is sound.

An authored `teleport` reaches that model nowhere. It reaches the route proofs through exactly one list, `Plan::transit_teleports`: every declared teleport's resolved **source volume**, from every effect root, forced or optional. `nav::World::is_teleport_source` consults it for one purpose — a walked leg whose start lies in a volume is judged with the world-load seals *lifted* — and `compiler.md` records the cost in so many words: *a genuinely-blocked leg with a teleport anywhere over its start is not judged*. `lethal::population_roots` reads the same list's destinations as cells the party is put at (the roots of `DW0891`'s population and, through it, of `DW0924`'s party cells). Nothing reads a destination as somewhere a leg **arrives**, so a leg that exists only through a teleport fails `DW0311` with a walk's prescription.

**The object the capability belongs to is the carry, not the verb.** A crossing and a teleport link are the same event from the proof's side: the party stands here, something fires, the party stands there, and no walk joins the two. The compiler authors the first and the campaign authors the second, and the model that reads them has to be one — a link only some proofs see is the second-authority defect the constitution names (**cited**: CLAUDE.md, *a capability belongs to the object class it acts on, not to the verb that first needed it*; *where two gates guard one artifact, read them together*). So this spec puts a link where the crossing already is — a `transport` marker on the step whose firing carries the party — and strikes the seal-lifting half-measure, which judged nothing.

## 2. What the tree does today

### 2.1 The verb, and what fires it

**Cited**, `docs/reference/compiler.md` effect `teleport` row and `Verb::Teleport`: `teleport{from, to{anchor, offset?}}` moves **everything inside a declared volume** to a mark; the selector is total over bodies (`tp @e[<box>,tag=!dw_fixture] <x> <y> <z>`, one line in a generated `teleport_<content-key>` function); `from` is the anchor-centred `StealthZone`; `to` resolves to a literal cell at build time; a compile-time affordance inside the volume is `DW0542`; a mark whose offset leaves its anchor's piece is `DW0897`. Fall distance carries across a teleport unchanged — measured Δ `0.0000` in 46/46 trials on the pinned 1.21.11 — and is charged in full at the destination.

**Cited**, `collect_transit_teleports`: a teleport is found by `for_each_gate_effect` in every root — an objective's `on_objective_complete`, a quest's `on_complete`, a trigger's `effects`, a trap's `payload`, a shop offer, a death or respawn bundle, a shortcut's `on_unlock` — and nested in a `sequence` step. **Cited**, `collect_region_events`: the region model draws a forced/optional line over the same roots — an objective or quest bundle fires at a step the party is forced through; a trigger's openings fire at the `trigger` step the path performs it in and a trigger the path never performs opens nothing; a trap payload, a death bundle, an offer and a shortcut's far side are **optional**, and *an unguaranteed firing may seal a region and may never open one* (spec-0051: optional is a claim about the proof).

### 2.2 What fires once, and what fires again

**Cited**, compiler.md emission row `interact`: completion despawns the objective's interaction entity and its marker (`kill @e[tag=dw_i_<obj>]`) *so a finished objective is not clickable*; the completion line is gated on the quest's arming score and a finished objective answers a stray click with silence. Every objective completes once; its bundle fires once. **Cited**, `EnvTrigger::once`: *fire at most once (default `true`, mirroring objective completion)*. **Cited**, compiler.md `environment triggers[]` emission: a `once` trigger guards on `#trig_<id> dw.sys`; a non-`once` trigger fires on every click, the click record being consumed in the same `tick` pass so a held click fires once per press; the trigger's body is summoned at `setup_finish` and is never despawned by firing. So the one authoring surface in the tree that lets a thing be used again is **`triggers[]` with `once: false`**, and it exists already: the gallery's `trigger/read-the-label` and the lift fixture's `trigger/call-lower` / `trigger/call-upper` (each a `use` trigger, `once: false`, whose `sequence` teleports at tick 2) are written that way.

### 2.3 How the path performs a trigger

**Cited**, `path_triggers` / `trigger_may_be_performed`: the path performs a trigger for two reasons only — its bundle **opens a way** (`open-gate` / `open-way`) or **pays a flag debt** a later step reads (`Flow::trigger_debts`). It is placed at the first path step where its `requires_flags` hold and none of its `forbids_flags` is set (`flags_at[si]`) and the party is in the area its target stands in, else directly in front of the reader. The `trigger` step is in `plan.critical_path` with a `pos` (the body's cell), so the leg into it is a leg `DW0311` proves. Numeric gates (`requires_state`) are **not** evaluated for performing: a press whose state gate is shut broadcasts no marker and the step fails where it stands. `statepath` (`DW0879`) judges a numeric gate against the writes the path performs, over `Flow`'s replay — the one ordered model the campaign has.

### 2.4 How the bot rides a transport

**Cited**, `harness/src/executor.ts`: `awaitTransport(dest)` waits up to `TRANSPORT_TIMEOUT_MS` (15 000) for `atTransportDest` — within `TRANSPORT_NEAR` (4) blocks of `dest` horizontally and 4 vertically — then waits for footing; the forced-move handler resets the pathfinder on a horizontal jump of at least `TRANSPORT_JUMP_BLOCKS` (64), a threshold chosen because areas sit 256 apart; `stepSettled` accepts a landed transport as the step's completion on a walk's failure path. **Cited**, `harness/src/critical-path.ts`: the optional `transport` key is accepted on the `talk-to`, `reach`, `kill`, `collect` and `interact` step shapes and **not** on `trigger`, whose accepted keys are `action`, `trigger`, `on`, `anchor`, `npc`, `pos`, `range`. A `trigger` step that carried the party would be a parse error today.

### 2.5 What a cutscene does to a position

**Cited**, `emit.rs` cutscene bracket: `cs_start` summons one `dw_csmark_<bare>` marker (`execute at @p run summon minecraft:marker`), tags `@a`, sets spectator; `cs_end` runs `gamemode adventure @a`, **`tp @a @e[tag=dw_csmark_<bare>,limit=1]`**, kills the cameras and the marker, and untags. So a cutscene is itself a gather: at its end every player online stands on the one marker cell saved at its start. A `teleport` that fires **while** the bracket is open — in the same flat bundle as the `cutscene`, or in a `sequence` step whose tick falls before `cs_end`'s — is undone for everyone at `cs_end`, and nothing in the tree refuses or warns about it. This is the emitter's own text, not a vanilla fact, and it decides §3.5.

### 2.6 Who reads the party's position

**Cited**, one line each. `nav::positions_of(start, steps, transports)` is the one enumeration of visited positions for the exported path and for every branch path; `check_critical_path`, `check_branch_path`, `critical_path_routes` and `branch_path_routes` all read it, so a leg marked `transport_before` is skipped by the proof and by the waypoint export together. `lethal::population_roots` = entry + every checkpoint seat + every teleport destination; it roots `DW0891` (more population → more refusals) and `DW0924`'s party cells (more population → **fewer** strand findings). `reach::check_reach_footprint` (`DW0881`) roots its population at *the walk from the campaign's own start* alone, so a body put down by any carry — a crossing included — is outside it. `check_bodies_can_leave` (`DW0921`) names two ways out of a pocket: the route itself, and every reached cell the boundary clock returns a body from. The layout graph's closure (`DW0816`) and critical-path claim (`DW0817`) run over five edge classes — `walk`, `stair`, `drop`, `barred`, `vision` — none of which is a carry. `nav.rs` prints a `binding:` line for four proofs and none for `DW0311`.

## 3. Decisions

### 3.1 A link is a repeatable trigger that carries the party out of its own volume

**Authored.** A **link** is a `teleport` effect hosted in a `triggers[]` entry declared `once: false`, of any trigger kind, whose `from` volume the performing body stands inside (§3.2). Nothing else is a link. A teleport in an objective's or quest's bundle, in a `once` trigger, in a trap payload, a shop offer, a death or respawn bundle or a shortcut's far side is a **gather**: whoever is in the box travels, once, and the route proof never leans on it.

The reason is the party. A delve is played by one to four bodies and the selector is a box: the one who clicks the tiller while the other three are still on the jetty leaves alone, and a bundle that fired on an objective's completion does not fire again. The means onward has to be one a straggler can use, and the tree already says what that is (§2.2). This is also why the rule is at the **root** and not a flag on the verb: `once: false` is the property that makes the carry re-usable, and it belongs to the trigger, which is the object that fires.

`DW0311`'s message widens and takes no new code: when a leg that fails has a gather whose volume contains the leg's start, the message names the teleport and says it fired from a root the party cannot fire again, and prescribes a `use`/`strike`/`approach` trigger with `once: false` hosting the same effect; the walk prescription (a doorway, a gap, a fence ring) is dropped from that message, because a walk was never the way.

### 3.2 Where the party stands when it is carried

**Authored.** A link's **stand cell** is a standable cell inside its `from` volume from which the trigger's act is performed: for `use`, `strike` and `strike-npc`, a cell whose body's eye (`metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` over its feet) is within `strand::STRIKE_REACH` (3.0) of the trigger body's box — the same in-reach rule `DW0924` already uses for a strike, read from the one site; for `approach`, a cell within the trigger's `range` of its `pos`. Where several qualify, the stand cell is the one nearest the leg's start by proven route length, ties broken by cell order (ADR-0006). The leg into the link ends at the stand cell, not at the body's `pos`, and the leg out of it begins at the link's `to` cell. A link with no stand cell is refused `DW0932` (§5): the lever is outside its own boat, and the body that pulls it is not carried.

The stand cell exists so the proof and the bot stand in the same place (§4): a human who presses from outside the box goes nowhere and presses again from inside, which is visible and harmless; a proof that let the bot press from wherever the pathfinder stopped would be green on a run the box never saw.

### 3.3 The link is live under the trigger's gate and the effect's own

**Authored**, over the cited model of §2.3. A link is **live** at a path step when the trigger's `requires_flags` hold and none of its `forbids_flags` is set at that step (`flags_at`), the teleport effect's own `when` guard holds by the same reading, and every `requires_state` on either compares true against the writes the path has performed by that step — the `statepath` replay `DW0879` already runs, widened from *a gate the path cleared* to *a gate the path has open here*. A link that is not live at a step does not exist for that step's leg; the proof walks on and reports it under §6 as considered and shut.

### 3.4 When the path takes a link

**Authored.** Legs are proven in path order, and a link is taken only where a walk fails. For a leg whose walk from its start to its end finds no route under that leg's seals, the proof retries it through links: for each live link, in `triggers[]` declaration order, whose stand cell the start can walk to, the leg becomes *walk to the stand cell · carry · walk from `to` to the end*, with the last segment itself retried through the links not yet used on this leg. The first decomposition that routes is the leg's route; a `trigger` step for each link taken is spliced into the path directly in front of the leg's end, in the order taken, each carrying `transport = to` and `stand`; none routing is `DW0311`, whose message names every link considered on that leg and why each failed (shut at this step, stand cell unreachable, `to` cannot reach the end). A link whose trigger the path already performs for one of §2.3's two reasons is performed from its stand cell and marks `transport` all the same — a performed trigger that carries, carries.

This is the flag-debt pattern (`Flow::trigger_debts`) applied to a route debt: a trigger is performed directly in front of the thing that needs it. Taking a link only where a walk fails is what keeps every campaign without one byte-identical and keeps a teleport shortcut, like a shortcut gate, out of the proof that the delve is finishable the long way.

### 3.5 A teleport fires after the cutscene it travels with

**Authored**, from §2.5. A `teleport` — link or gather — in a root that also plays a `cutscene` must fire **after** that cutscene's `cs_end`: in a `sequence`, its step's `at_ticks` must exceed the tick `cs_end` runs at, which the emitter computes from the shots; a `teleport` and a `cutscene` in one flat bundle, or a `sequence` whose teleport tick is at or before `cs_end`'s, is refused `DW0933`, naming the root, the teleport, the cutscene and the first tick that would hold. The issue's own shape — a crossing played as a cutscene — is therefore written as *cutscene, then teleport*, and the party watches the shore recede before it stands on the far boat. The refusal applies to gathers too, because `cs_end` undoes those as well, and a rule that is about what a cutscene does to a position is about every teleport under one.

### 3.6 What a link is not

**Authored.** A link's `from` anchor and `to` anchor stand in the same area: a teleport between areas stays a gather, and a leg between areas stays the compiler's crossing (`DW0872`, `DW0873`, `Plan::transport`, `DW0494`), which has its own refusals and its own population and would otherwise be two mechanisms for one leg. A link's `to` cell lies outside its `from` volume: a link leaves its place, and a teleport onto a cell inside its own box is a regroup, which is a gather. A link's `to` cell is standable (`World::is_standable`) in the world as the link's own root leaves it at the teleport's tick — the root's region writes with an earlier tick applied, forced, since they fire with it — because the leg out of the link begins there, and a route position is a cell a body stands on; a link onto air is a drop, and a drop is not where a leg begins. Each of these three is a fault under `DW0932` (§5).

### 3.7 The seal-lifting rule is struck

**Authored.** `Plan::transit_teleports`, `World::transit_teleports` and `World::is_teleport_source` are removed. A walked leg that starts inside a teleport's volume is judged like every other leg; where the teleport is a link, the leg is the link (§3.4), and where it is a gather, the party walks. The sentence recording the cost in `compiler.md` goes with it. `population_roots` is re-pointed at the two lists §3.8 names.

### 3.8 Two populations, named by their quantifier

**Authored**, from §2.6's finding that one root list feeds two proofs of opposite monotonicity. The plan carries two root sets with their quantifiers in their names. **Put-at** roots — cells the party *may* be put at: entry, every checkpoint seat, every crossing's entry point, every teleport destination, link or gather. They root `DW0891`, where a wider population can only refuse more. **Stands-at** roots — cells the party *certainly* stands at on the forced route: entry, every checkpoint seat, every crossing's entry point, every link's `to`. They root `DW0924`'s party cells and `DW0881`'s population. For `DW0924` this is a **tightening**: a gather's destination from an optional root no longer counts as a place the party can be in reach from, and the strand it hid is reported. For `DW0881` it closes two silences at once: a raised anchor on the far shore, reachable only by a link, and one in any area reached only by a crossing, are now judged — that direction is conservative (more offenders found).

### 3.9 A link is a way out of a pocket

**Authored.** `check_bodies_can_leave` (`DW0921`) adds a third way out beside the route and the boundary return: a reached cell that is a stand cell (§3.2) of a link live in the configuration being judged, since from it a body performs the trigger and arrives at `to`, a route cell. The link is repeatable by construction (§3.1), so this is not leaning on a one-shot. The binding line gains the count of link stand cells that served as ways out.

## 4. The bot

**Authored**, over §2.4. A `trigger` step that carries the party carries two more keys in `critical-path.json` and every `branch-path-<branch>.json`: `transport: [x, y, z]` (the link's `to`, the field every other carried step already has) and `stand: [x, y, z]` (§3.2). The parser's accepted-key list for `trigger` gains both; the shape of every other step is unchanged. The bot walks to `stand` as a block goal, performs the act exactly as a `trigger` step does today — a real right-click, strike or walk into range, never a command — passes on the trigger's fired marker, then runs `awaitTransport(transport)` with its existing three phases. `TRANSPORT_JUMP_BLOCKS` is not consulted for a link (the walk to `stand` has already completed, so no stale path survives) and is not lowered: it separates a crossing from knockback and stays a crossing's constant.

Observability is the harness's own obligation: a hop whose `to` lies within `2 × TRANSPORT_NEAR` blocks horizontally **and** within 4 vertically of `stand` is one `atTransportDest` would already accept before the press, so the parser refuses it as a `CriticalPathParseError` naming both cells and the constant. The compiler is not told the harness's tolerance; a link too short to observe is the harness's finding, where the constant lives.

## 7. The layout graph draws the carry

**Authored**, from `DW0816`/`DW0817` (**cited**: the graph's critical path is *authored rather than derived, so that it is a claim the machine verifies*). `edges[]` gains a class `carry {id, a, b, one_way?, gating}`: a connection a body is carried over, with no seam, no sill, no door check and no body; the derivation writes nothing for it; `one_way` as `walk` carries it (absent = both ways, each direction then owed a link); `gating` required, the same projection `barred` carries, because a carry that is live from the start is a hole in the graph's own claim. The closure crosses it like any gated edge. At stage 5, each link is matched to the `carry` edge joining the node of its `from` station to the node of its `to` mark in its direction, and each `carry` direction to at least one link; a mismatch either way is `DW0934` (§5). The graph's binding line counts `carry` edges beside its other classes.

## 8. The gallery's obligation

**Authored**, under spec-0039. The gallery binds one **link** that is the only way onward on some leg — a `use` trigger, `once: false`, hosting a `cutscene` then a `teleport` in one `sequence` as §3.5 requires, between two places of one area joined by a `carry` edge and by nothing else; its existing gather (the `teleport` in `obj/clear-the-muster`'s bundle) stays a gather, so both kinds are in one artifact and the ledger's partition is non-zero in both columns. ## 9. Decisions for the owner

None blocks this spec. The ruling worth her eye, reported with its reason rather than asked: **a teleport fired by an objective's completion is never the way onward** (§3.1). The problem it answers is a party of four boarding one at a time: the first click carries one body and the bundle never fires again. The cost is that the issue's example is authored as an objective (*board the boat*) that sets the stage plus a trigger (*the tiller*) gated on it that carries, rather than as one objective whose completion carries.
