## 1. A datum counts bodies in a volume, and a trigger fires when one enters

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `state[]`, `requires_state[]`): a datum is a declared, integer-valued runtime value every one of the twelve gate sites reads through `requires_state`; it is written by `set-state`, `add-state`, `clear-state`. **[cited]** (spec-0114 §4; `compiler.md` `triggers[]`): every objective completes on a player's act and every trigger kind is a player's act. **[cited]** (the vanilla record §7): a box selector `@e[x,y,z,dx,dy,dz]` counts a body whose hitbox intersects the box, so a body on the box's rim is counted by its hitbox, not its centre, and `execute store result score … if entity` writes the count; a body teleported into the box is counted on the next read.

**[authored] The census is a datum with a source.** `state[]` gains an optional `counts`:

```json
{"id": "state/goats-home", "scope": "party", "initial": 0,
 "counts": {"region": {"anchor": "anchor/pen", "extent": [3, 2, 2]}, "of": {"wave": "wave/goats"}}}
```

`region` is the anchor-centred box every region in the engine is (`StealthZone`, through the one `Plan::zone_box`); `of` names the bodies — `{"wave": id}`, `{"actor": id}`, `{"npc": id}` or `{"kind": "minecraft:goat"}` — by the tag the engine already puts on them (`dw_wave_<id>`, `dw_actor_<id>`, `dw_npc_<n>`) or by type. A counted datum is **derived**: the engine writes it, `execute store result score #party dw.s_<local> if entity @e[<tag or type>,<box>]`, on a self-scheduling `census_tick` every 10 ticks that runs while any census is live (started in `setup`, replace mode, no per-tick cost when none is declared), and the author never does — `set-state`, `add-state` or `clear-state` naming a counted datum is `DW1088`. `scope` must be `party` (a count of bodies is not per player; `player` is `DW1088`). Every gate reads it as it reads any datum: the pen's gate shuts (`interact` … `requires_state goats-home at-least 3`), the dusk line gated on the goats being home, the cake's fate.

**The trigger.** `triggers[].on` gains **`body-enters {state}`**: a trigger that fires when a counted datum rises from zero — the first body of its set inside its box. Named by the datum, not by a second region, so the one count has one home; the datum must carry `counts` (`DW1088`). It is polled by the same `census_tick`, edge-triggered (fires when the count is at least 1 and the trigger's own `once`/flag guards hold; with `once: false` it re-arms when the count returns to 0), and runs its effects with no acting player (`Audience::Party`; `actor` inside is `DW0503`). The cake: `{"id": "trigger/cake-taken", "on": {"body-enters": {"state": "state/raiders-on-stage"}}, "effects": [{"type": "set-block", …air}, {"type": "set-flag", "flag": "flag/cake-taken"}]}`.

**The static proof, ruled.** A mechanic that rests on a mob's own AI is judged like combat: the process is skipped and the result judged. Static: for a census `of` a wave or an actor, the path from each body's seat — the wave's spawn anchor (every mob of it), the actor's mark — to the counted region is connected **for that body's footprint** (`nav::entity_dims` from the pinned registry table, the same walk `DW0386` proves a lane and `DW0325` an actor's move), else `DW1089`. For `of: kind` and `of: npc` nothing is proven statically (a kind names no seat; an NPC walks only by `move-npc`, which `DW0307` proves) and the binding line says so. Runtime, in the harness: a step gated on a counted datum (an objective or trigger whose `requires_state` names it) carries `census: [{state, tag, region, count}]`; once the step's act has walked to where it acts (its stance) and acts next, the executor **teleports** the bodies of every set into its region in one turn on its opped command channel (the `mock` channel, reply bracketed by `tools/lib/rcon.mjs`'s rule), holds each body that has its AI still (`NoAI`) until the act ends, reads the datum's score back after one census period, and refuses the step by name when the game's count disagrees with the bodies it placed. The pen is proven as a pen, and the herding is the playtest's.

**Story worlds.** A `body-enters` trigger is an elective ambient act (spec-0108's class): the raiders reach the stage in one world and not in another. Its entry deed in a world that performs it is the trigger's own step, `{"action": "trigger", "on": "body-enters", …}`, carrying the census of its datum with one body — the harness teleports one body of the set into the box, as for any census, and passes on the trigger's own fired marker; the step names no place the party walks to; the compiler's story model credits its producers as it credits any ambient act's.

## 2. A target block is shot

**[cited]** (the vanilla record §1): `minecraft:target_hit` is granted to the **shooting player** when a projectile the player launched lands on a target block; it fires for an arrow from a bow and for a thrown snowball; it does not fire for an arrow summoned with no shooter; the criterion's `projectile` predicate can bound the projectile's position to the block's cell, and `signal_strength` reads how near the centre it struck. **[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `triggers[]`; spec-0082 §8): an arrow passes through a `minecraft:interaction` without a record, which is why no existing trigger kind sees a shot.

**[authored]** `triggers[].on` gains **`shot`**: a trigger at a place whose `prop` is `minecraft:target` (the trigger's existing `prop` row; a `shot` without a prop, or with any other block, is `DW1090`). Emission: one advancement per trigger with the `target_hit` criterion, `projectile.location` bounded to the target's cell, rewarding the trigger's fire function as a `use` trigger's press does (the presser is the shooter, `Audience::Solo`). Three hits are the present state pattern: `once: false`, `add-state` a datum, a gate at 3. A `shot` trigger owes a bow or a crossbow and arrows obtainable before it on every path that performs it — read by `nav::movement_items`, the walk `DW1043` already makes over every item the campaign puts in a hand — else `DW1090`.

**The static proof, ruled.** A reachable standing cell with a **clear line of sight** to the target block, no arc, no piercing: the compiler finds, in the walk region, a standable cell between 4 and 40 blocks of the target whose eye (`metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` over its feet) sees the block's centre through air (the sight rule `sightlines[]` and `DW0824` read, `sight-through-blocks.md`), nearest the trigger's place first; none is `DW1091`. The cell is exported on the step as `stand`. Runtime: the bot lands **one real hit** — a new act in the harness's `trigger` executor for `on: shot`: equip the bow, aim at the block's centre raised by an elevation taken from the distance, draw for the full charge, release, and pass on the trigger's fired marker; a miss re-aims from a short table of elevations (the record §1 gives the elevation that hit at 27 blocks) and the step fails by name after the table is spent. Arrows are consumed; the step says how many it spent.

## 3. An item is raised

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `compass`; `emit/guidance.rs` `use_advancement`): the engine already drives `minecraft:using_item` for the compass, re-armed by its reward every tick. **[cited]** (the vanilla record §9): `using_item` is granted every tick a spyglass is held in use.

**[authored]** `triggers[].on` gains **`use-item {item}`** with the ordinary `at` place and `range` (the `approach` selector's volume — the trigger fires for a player **inside** the place's box holding the item in use). Emission: one advancement per trigger, `using_item` with the item and `player.location` in the box, rewarding the fire function (presser audience). `item` is any id of the pinned item registry (`DW0143`); an item the pinned game never reports in use — one with no use duration — is `DW1092`, read from a data file `crates/dsl/data/item-use-duration-1.21.11.json` the maintenance regenerator writes from the pinned jar (`Item#getUseDuration` over the registry; a bow, a crossbow, a spyglass, a shield, a trident, a goat horn, a brush, every food). The bot: equip the item, `activateItem` inside the box for one second, pass on the marker.

## 4. A wave scales with the party

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `waves[]`): `mobs[].count` is a constant; `name` marks one body (`DW0983`); `DW0312` proves the spawn cells and `DW0387` a ring's. **[authored]** `count` takes an integer or `{"base": b, "per_player": p}`: the bodies summoned are `b + p × (n − 1)` for `n` players present at the spawn (`execute store result score #n dw.sys if entity @a[tag=!dw_cutscene]`), so one player meets `base`. Emission: each body past `base` is its own summon line on its own proven cell, behind `execute if score #party_n dw.sys matches <k>..` with a paired countdown increment (as built: a recursive `spawn_<wave>_more` loop would summon every further body on one cell, and the seats `DW0312` proves are one per body). Proofs: `DW0312` and `DW0387` are judged at the four-player maximum `b + 3p`; `DW0983` refuses `name` on any count whose maximum exceeds one; the muster the harness reads at a `kill` step expects `base` (one bot). No new code: a count whose shape the schema bounds (`base ≥ 1`, `per_player ≥ 0`) is `DW0100`.

## 5. A rocket carries its stars

**[cited]** (the vanilla record §2): a firework rocket's burst deals damage to a body it bursts on only when it carries explosions, and the damage rises with the star count; the record states the measured figures per star, and that a crossbow the bot loads from its off hand fires the rocket. **[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `firework`): the `firework` effect already types `explosions` (1–7 of `{shape, colors, fade_colors?, trail?, twinkle?}`) and `flight`.

**[authored]** The one stack shape every item site uses — `give-item`, `loot[].items`, `equipment` slots, `drops`, class kits, shop offers — gains optional **`fireworks {flight?, explosions}`**, the `firework` effect's own type, emitted as the `minecraft:fireworks` component. It is admitted on `minecraft:firework_rocket` alone; on any other item it is `DW1095`. The rocket in the storeroom and the rockets the crossbow rack holds are written once, with the stars the story gives them.

## 6. A fill and an edit wait for their beat

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `loot[]`): a loot fill is emitted in `setup_finish` at world init with no gate. **[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `batches[]`): a world-edits batch is applied at build. **[authored]**

- `loot[]` gains **`filled: "at-load" | "by-effect"`** (default `at-load`); a `by-effect` fill is emitted as its own function `fill_<loot>` that the verb **`fill-loot {loot}`** calls from any bundle (the dusk beat, gated on `flag/cargo-saved`). The holder census counts a `by-effect` fill as loot (it is declared); a `by-effect` fill no bundle calls, and a `fill-loot` naming an `at-load` fill, are `DW1093` (one rule, two messages: a fill nothing fires is the unemitted shape; a second fill of a filled holder is a double write).
- `world-edits.json` batches gain **`applied: "at-build" | "by-effect"`** (default `at-build`); a `by-effect` batch is computed at build like any batch — its cell writes resolved against the assembled world — and emitted as `edit_<batch>`, one `fill`/`setblock` run per contiguous write (vanilla's `fill` cap of 32768 cells split by the emitter), called by the verb **`apply-edits {batch}`**. The lamps of the town come on by a `replace` batch over the town's box; the bunting is a batch of its cells. The region model takes the batch's writes as dated runtime writes through `plan::effect_writes`, cell by cell with the block's collision class, as a `set-block` is taken. The light proof (`DW0210`) judges every reachable cell under both states of every `by-effect` batch — applied and not — because a batch may darken as well as light, and nothing in the record credits a runtime write with light. A `by-effect` batch nothing applies, and an `apply-edits` naming an `at-build` batch, are `DW1094`.

## 7. A quorum of objectives

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `after[]`; spec-0051 non-goals): `after` is the AND of its objectives; objective-level optionality inside one quest was a declared non-goal. **[cited]** (spec-0133 §1, §5): an objective settles done or expired; a settlement is an outcome world.

**[authored]** `after` admits a **quorum** beside its ids: `"after": ["obj/a", {"any": ["obj/b", "obj/c", "obj/d"], "at_least": 2}]`. A quorum holds when `at_least` of its members are **done**. When a quorum first holds, each member not yet done is **lapsed** — the third settlement of spec-0133's class: settled, its completion bundle never fires, its act refused after by the guard, its announcement closed with the chrome line `Objective lapsed: <title>` (`limit.lapsed`, a third chrome string), and it counts toward its quest's completion. Lapsing is an outcome the story worlds enumerate: a witness per subset of members of size `at_least`, by id order, under spec-0133's enumeration and cap. The all world settles each quorum by its first `at_least` members by id order; a witness is the all world with one other subset, and a lapsed member is no step of a world's path — nothing is done to lapse it. Every reader of `after` answers through one definition, `Objective::after_holds` (a plain id settled, a quorum's members done), and reads edges (`after_edges`, every member) or what certainly comes first (`after_required`, never a member) by name. Emission: each member's completion sums the members' `dw.o_` scores into `#q_<obj>_<i> dw.sys` (quorum `i` of the objective waiting on it) and, at `<at_least>..`, runs `lapse_q_<obj>_<i>`, which runs `settle_lapsed_<m>` for every member not settled; every guard of the waiting objective reads `#q_<obj>_<i> dw.sys matches <at_least>..`; a member carries a settled score `dw.os_<m>` as a limited objective does, and its guards refuse it once settled. `settle_lapsed_<m>` mirrors `settle_expired_<obj>` without a marker or a bundle: it settles, prints the chrome line for an announced member, stops a limited member's clock, runs its cleanup and asks its quest. The schema bounds `at_least` to `1..=|any| − 1` and refuses a repeated id (`DW0100`): a quorum of every member is the plain list, and a quorum of none is no requirement. `DW0140` reads quorum members as `after` edges. The crates: six `collect` objectives of one crate each, every one with spec-0133's limit from `quest-start`, the delivery `after` all six (settled either way), its completion bundle setting `flag/cargo-saved` under `requires_state cargo at-least 4` where each crate's completion adds one — the timed form needs no quorum, and the quorum is the untimed one ("bring any four").

## 8. A thing taken off an actor

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `actors[].answers`; spec-0119; `click-attribution.md` §3): a declaring actor carries an answer box, a `minecraft:interaction` enclosing the body, whose blow and touch are attributed to the presser by `player_hurt_entity` and `player_interacted_with_entity`. **[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `actors[]`, `drops`; `DW0490`): an actor's `equipment` takes the wave mob's shape, and a slot a declaration names must be one the body's equipment fills.

**[authored]** `triggers[].on` gains **`strike-actor {actor}`** and **`use-actor {actor}`**, the actor's twins of `strike-npc` (no `at`; mismatch `DW0194`; an actor `actors[]` does not declare, `DW0112`). Either rides the actor's answer box, which the actor gains when a trigger names it as it gains one when it declares an answer; `use-actor` on a `vulnerable` actor is refused as `struck` is (`DW1056`). The taking is `give-item` gaining **`from: {"actor": id, "slot": <slot>}`**: the stack given is the one the actor's `equipment` declares in that slot (no second spelling of the item), and the actor's slot is emptied in the same bundle (`item replace entity @e[tag=dw_actor_<id>,limit=1] <slot> with minecraft:air`); a slot the actor's equipment does not fill is `DW0490`, the same question the `drops` check asks, from the same function. The fox: `move-actor` to the den, `on_arrive` setting the flag the `use-actor` trigger requires, the trigger's `give-item from: {actor: fox, slot: main_hand}`.

## 9. An effect aimed at a body

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, the envelope rows): `audience` is who the effect addresses — `party` or `actor` — and reaches every verb `Verb::addresses_players` names; `in` narrows it to a box. **[cited]** (the vanilla record §8): `effect give` and `damage` aimed at a mob by tag act on it, and `damage … by <player>` knocks it back from the player.

**[authored]** `audience` gains two arms, **`{"wave": id}`** and **`{"actor": id}`**: the effect addresses the bodies of that wave or that actor, through the tag the engine puts on them; `in` composes (the bodies inside the box). The verbs split by what they can address: `give-effect`, `clear-effect` and `damage` address a body; `narrate`, `give-item`, `play-sound`, `stop-sound` and `particle` address a player, and a body audience on one of them is refused under `DW0942`'s rule — an audience the verb cannot address — with its own message. `damage-players` is renamed **`damage`**: it damages whom its audience names, players by default; a body audience takes `by: "actor"` as an option for the knockback from the acting player (`damage … by @s`), refused where emission has no acting player (`DW0503`). The bell: `give-effect glowing 10` with `audience: {wave: raiders}`; the pen's charge: `damage 4 by: actor` on the wave inside the street box.

## 10. Water in the route proof

**[cited]** (`nav.md`, the reach graph; `nav/world/reach.rs`): the reach graph already rests a body `Afloat` and `Submerged`, and the stranding proof (`DW0322`, `nav/horizon.rs`) already flies a swimmer's launches and refuses a body of water with no climb-out, at the climb-out band of a cell at the water's level or one over it. **[cited]** (spec-0038 §2.4): routes never credit water, so no route may require it. **[cited]** (`body-movement.md` §6): a body in water moves on the fluid arm of `travel` and leaves it over a one-block rim by the fluid jump.

**[authored] Ruled**: a connected body of water — its surface and its inside — counts as reachable; the climb-out and stranding rules are reused as they stand; breath and underwater light are out of scope. The route leg's walk relation (`nav::route`, `neighbors_fp`) gains the water moves: from a standable cell into an adjacent water cell at or under its level; between adjacent water cells of one connected body, in every direction including down and up (a swimmer rises); from a water cell onto a standable cell within the climb-out band `horizon.rs` already defines. A water cell is priced at the simulator's swim rate — `dsl::metrics::body` under `Rules::Game` over a flat water body, measured once by the implementation into the metrics table as `pacing.ticks-per-block-swimming` (provisional, `DW0813` names it) — beside the 4 t/block sprint. `DW0314` accepts an exported waypoint whose cell is water as afloat. The route's `critical-path-waypoints.json` carries each water cell on a leg with `medium: "water"`, and the harness's pathfinder admits liquid on such a leg (`Movements.liquidCost` finite, `allowFreeMotion` off) and never otherwise. Lava stays impassable. A tide (spec-0038) is per-state as every route proof is. **Debt, recorded**: a submerged stretch longer than a body's breath is not judged; the ledger row names it against this spec, and the binding line prints the longest submerged run of every leg so it can be read.

## 11. What this spec leaves out, by name **[authored]**

A census of **players** (the box selector of `in` already addresses them, and a player count is not a story datum). A `body-enters` with a **second region** (the datum's is the one). A shot at anything but a **target block** (the game records no other). A **crossbow** leg for the bot (the shot leg draws a bow; a crossbow's charge-then-fire is a second act, recorded in the vanilla record for the day it is wanted). A per-player count scaled by **difficulty** or **class**. A rocket's **damage** modelled in the engine (the record states it; the fight is the designer's). A `by-effect` edit the **route proof** credits as opened (its writes are dated; a clear into water keeps `clear-region`'s stated limit). A quorum of **quests**. A thing taken off an **NPC** (an NPC carries no equipment; a stage-2 field is another spec). An effect aimed at an **NPC** (never harmed, never a fight). **Breath**, **underwater light**, a **current** that carries a body, and a **boat** (spec-0135).
