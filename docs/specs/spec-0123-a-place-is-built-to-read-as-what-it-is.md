# spec-0123: A place is built big enough, and built to read as what it is — one box per building, twice the room, a trade legible from the threshold, life outside the door, a side that may stand no wall, and one style per building

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against engine `5ac141996` (`origin/main`, Batch C), read only — `docs/reference/compiler.md` (*The map pipeline — `site-plan`*, *The blockout*, the detail-plan section, `render-plan.json` *player-POV shots*), `docs/reference/delvec/compiler/blockout.md` (`DW0838` and the commons), `docs/reference/delvec/compiler/detail.md` (`DW0843`–`DW0845`, `DW0987`, `delvec allocation`), `docs/reference/grammar.md` §2d, §2f and §4c (`contract-closure`), `docs/reference/clear-width.md`, spec-0098 §2, §5b and §14, spec-0120 §3–§5, spec-0121; and spec-0122 at the branch `feat/impl-0122` — its handout (`scripts/handout.py`), `scripts/starter.py`, `references/detail.md` and `references/visual-review.md` — which this spec builds on and does not restate. Evidence: one hillside town built twice through `/new-delve` on release 1.14.0, under two cuts, and played; the cut's site plans and generation records in the content repository.
- **What it is for**: the two builds were played side by side, and the better one still fell short in seven ways. Each is a **ruling** of that playtest, taken here as given and marked **[ruling]**; this spec turns each into guide text the skill hands an agent, or an engine capability, with criteria a machine checks. An eighth ruling came from the playtest of Part A's open-sides demo (§3.5): the open-sides mechanism passed, and a front and a landmark read badly for a reason that is systemic and not per building. Every other statement is **[cited]** (a reading of the tree, a constitution rule, a research page) or **[authored]** (this spec chooses). The research is `docs/reference/place-craft.md`; §3.5 stands on its §7.
- **Numbers**: spec `0123`. Three diagnostics, `DW1063` (§3.1), `DW1064` (§5.4) and `DW1065` (§5.4); the narrowed open side is `DW1057`'s, with its population widened (§5.4); §3.5 adds no diagnostic — its census refuses nothing. `dsl_version` moves to `0.65.0`: `boxes[].sides` (§5.1) and `nodes[].references[]` (§3.3) each change what a document says; the door the walk opens (§3.1) and the piece census (§3.5) change no document and take no number.
- **Non-goals**: a size class the engine infers from a place's label (placement.md: *a place carries no size class*); a storefront, a stall or a pavilion as a kind of box; a door, a gate or a counter named per building type, anywhere (§3.5: the construction names the leaf); a kind of landmark the engine recognises (a fountain is read, never inferred); a door moved by redstone or by a quest effect (an iron door is a gate and stays one, §3.1); an approach depth the engine judges beyond spec-0120's one cell (§11); the trench check (§11, an open question for the implementation); a volume detailed like a place (spec-0098 §10's falsifier, not triggered here).

## 1. One box per building or connector **[ruling; cited: spec-0098 §5b, ADR-0032]**

The cut that won is the one the engine already describes: a scene is a place and a piece, a road is a place, a flight is a place, a connector that is a structure is a place. The ruling makes it the only cut the page teaches: `references/placement.md` says **cut the site with one box per building or connector**, and names what else is a place — a yard, a garden strip, a square's centrepiece, each an open-sided place (§5). The two-terrace cut is not described as an alternative. Guide text; no engine change.

## 2. Twice the room **[ruling; authored where stated]**

A building's play space is about twice what the first cut gave it, so its author has room for rooms (`place-craft.md` §2). `references/placement.md` states the numbers the research page authored — a trade building at least 12 × 12 of play space, 14 × 14 where a workshop and a shop front both stand, a one-room house free to be smaller — and says how they bind: the geometry brief states a floor fact per trade and the plan binds each trade building's box to it with `identities[]` (`{"of": "box-extent", "node", "axis"}`, `ge`), so the size is a declaration the engine holds the plan to (`DW0833`) and never a class it infers. Guide text over an existing surface; no engine change.

## 3. Detail, as the trade

### 3.1 Several rooms, joined by doors — an engine capability **[ruling; cited: `grammar.md` §2d, `docs/reference/doors-by-hand.md`; authored where stated]**

A building may have several rooms joined by **doors** — door blocks, not open doorways. The grammar carries the rooms: a space is one floor, several rooms on one floor are one space or several spaces joined by `walk` edges through their doorways, a storey above is a second space and a `climb`. What the engine lacked was the door: the nav model held every door block as a barrier, so a door between rooms was a wall and every door was built as an open doorway.

**What the pinned game does** is established once in `docs/reference/doors-by-hand.md`, read from the pinned server jar's bytecode with its read site named: an adventure-mode body opens by hand every door, trapdoor and fence gate whose set type `canOpenByHand` is true — every wood, copper — and does not open an iron door or an iron trapdoor; the server's use-on-block path reads the game mode once, for spectator, and adventure takes survival's path. Cited; treated as constant.

**The engine, authored on it**:

1. **The walk admits a door a hand opens.** In the nav model's one predicate (`can_be`, `World::neighbors`) a door, trapdoor or fence-gate block whose set type opens by hand is a cell a body passes in either state the block stands in — the body opens it and walks through; an iron door or iron trapdoor stays what it is today, a barrier closed and a passage open. The set is **derived** from the pinned block-state registry (`*_door`, `*_trapdoor`, `*_fence_gate` by id, minus `iron_door` and `iron_trapdoor`) and printed in a binding line, `doors by hand: N block(s) passable, 2 held`, never written by hand. Every proof that reads the predicate follows — the route proofs, `DW0921`, `DW1011`, the clear-width columns (a wooden door in a corridor is a clear column) — with no change of their own.
2. **A barred edge keeps iron.** A `barred` contract edge's `bar.block`, resolved through the palette, must be a block a hand does not open: `DW1063`, *`<piece>` edge `<a>`→`<b>`: the bar is `<block>`, which a player opens by hand; a bar is iron bars, an iron door, an iron trapdoor or a solid*. Validation tier, over the contract's declared block; the derivation's own bars (`SEAM_BAR`, `SEAM_HATCH`, `SEAM_COVER`) are iron already and are counted, not judged.
3. **The bot opens doors.** The harness's walker opens a wooden door on its path as a player does (its pathfinder's door capability), so the critical-path run crosses a closed door between two rooms; the harness asserts the crossing and never the door's mechanics.

`references/detail.md`'s *The program's contract* says: rooms are spaces joined by `walk` edges through their doorways, and a doorway between rooms holds a wooden door block, which the walk opens; a door the story shuts is iron and a `barred` edge. Guide text over the capability.

### 3.2 Reads as its trade **[ruling; authored from the research]**

A plain house with a loom and wool in it does not read as a weaver's. The rule is `place-craft.md` §3's four parts — the working object in its working position; rooms that follow the work, at least two; a front that says the trade before the door opens; every room with a use a player could name — and its test: stand at the threshold and name the trade with no sign read. The handout carries the rule (§3.3) and the review applies the test (§3.4). The four parts say **what** a front shows; §3.5 says **how** it is built, and the two are read together: a front that shows the trade through a counter and a door built as two unrelated parts has met the third part and failed §3.5, and the reviewer's two lines (§3.4) record each on its own. Neither rule names a door per trade; the third part's examples — a sign, goods at a counter, the workroom open to the street — are what is shown, and the leaf that shows it is the construction's (§3.5 part 2).

### 3.3 References, per place **[ruling; authored]**

Each building type needs human-built references to work from, and the handout carries them. The home is the place's own design row:

- **`layout-graph.json` `nodes[].references[]`** — optional, `{url, shows, terms}`: a URL (`https://` only, the schema's `format`), one sentence saying what the reference shows and what to take from it, and the terms the source states (`"CC BY-SA 4.0"`, `"none stated"`). Three strings, none player-facing, none inventoried. A node declaring none is legitimate; validation prints `references binding: R reference(s) over N of M node(s)` and refuses nothing. A reference is **looked at, never copied**: an unlicensed or non-commercial source is ideas only (ADR-0013), and the handout says so in the row's own line.
- **Gathered at step 9, before the first handout**, by the orchestrating session: for every place with a `clearance` ceiling (a building), at least two references of human builds of that trade — a smithy, a general store, a weaver's — each read for what §3.2 asks (where the working object stands, how the rooms divide, what the front shows), recorded as rows. `references/detail.md` gains the step; it is the orchestrator's because the rows are a design document's and a place author holds nothing but its handout (spec-0121 §1).
- **`scripts/references.py <campaign> <place>|--all`** — standard-library Python, named from `SKILL.md`: fetches each row's URL into `<campaign>/.out/references/<place stem>/<n>.<ext>` when the response is an image, records a page that is not one as a page, writes `index.json` beside them, and prints `references: R row(s); F image(s) fetched, P page(s), E failed`. The author reads a fetched image with its file tool and a page by its URL. Nothing fetched is committed: `.out/` is build output, and a reference image is not the campaign's to redistribute.
- **The handout** (`scripts/handout.py`) gains one section after *The campaign, in one breath*: **What this place is, and what it is built from** — the node's `intent` and `note`; each reference row with its `shows`, its terms, the ideas-only line and the fetched file where one exists; and the fixed paragraph of §3.2's four parts with the threshold test, written for every place with a `clearance` ceiling. For a sky-open or way place the paragraph is §4's instead. The binding counts gain `references`.

### 3.4 The review judges the trade from the threshold **[ruling; authored]**

No house interior stood on a POV frame, because no house was on the critical path. The build draws one more review shot kind beside `pov`: **`threshold`** (`compiler::render_plan::threshold_shots`) — for every box, one shot per portal seam on a vertical face, eye at the opening's middle column on the sill at `1.62`, looking along the face's inward normal at the play space's centre at eye height; and one per open side (§5) no seam covers, eye in the outside approach cell at the side's middle. Drawn in the configuration of the critical path's first arrival at the place, else the base world; the eye is judged by `DW0724` like every camera; `expect` reads *the place reads as its intent: <intent>*; `shows` lists the declared targets in frame. The index states `threshold shots: T over P place(s), D door(s), O open side(s)`; a plan with no portal and no open side writes `0`.

`references/visual-review.md` gains the method, after the POV sequence: **read every threshold frame blind** — write the trade the picture shows before opening its index entry, then read `expect`; a frame whose trade cannot be named, or is named wrong, is a finding at the place's program (step 9, a fresh author with the finding in its handout). The frame carries no label unless `--labels` is passed, and the review does not pass it for this read.

**The same frame is read a second time, still blind, for style** (§3.5) **[ruling; authored]**. Before the index entry is opened the reviewer writes two more lines under the trade. **The front**: do the parts in frame — counter, door, window, posts, beam, wall, eave — belong to one construction, in one word, and if not, which part jars and against what (*the door stands in air over the counter*; *the beam stops short of the door*; *the third block kind has no job*). **The thing**: for a frame whose eye stands in an open side looking into a place with no `clearance` ceiling — a square, a yard, a court — the name of what stands in it, in one word (*fountain*, *well*, *statue*, *tree*, *nothing*), then the index's `expect` is read against it. The threshold shot is the frame that reads both: for a front it stands in the outside approach cell at the open side's middle, or on the sill of the door, at eye height, looking in — the body's own view of a front; for a square it stands at the side's middle looking at the centre, where a silhouette is read before detail. A front answered with a jarring part, or a thing named wrong or not nameable, is a finding at the place's program exactly as a missed trade is: step 9, a fresh author, with the finding and `place-craft.md` §7's four parts in its handout, and the census entry (§3.5) the build wrote for that piece quoted beside the finding so the author sees the doors and their framing, the heads, the kinds and the water the reviewer saw. The reviewer writes *the front* with the entry's `doors` and `openings` open beside the frame — a door entered with its head absent is read against the picture, never refused by it — and *the thing* with its `water`. The read is blind so that the label cannot supply what the picture does not; the reviewer writes the three lines for every threshold frame and the review index states `threshold read: T frame(s), trade misses M, front misses F, thing misses N`.

### 3.5 Built in one style — coherent parts, openings that follow construction, a landmark that reads as itself **[ruling; cited: `place-craft.md` §7; authored where stated]**

The parts of one building and of one frontage are visually coherent with each other — proportion, height rhythm, the consistency of material and element; the choice of door, opening or counter follows from how the building is constructed, and a different construction makes a different door right; a landmark reads as what it is. The case: the open-sides demo's smithy stood a one-course counter and a two-course door one cell in from a five-course open face, air over both, and the door read as abrupt; its fountain, a flat ring of stone round one cell of water at the rim's height, read as a well. The ruling refuses the per-building answer — no *a smithy takes a fence gate* — because the problem is systemic: it is the question of how a better-styled building is built. **[ruling]**

**The rule** is `place-craft.md` §7's four parts, authored there from the sources and held here as the guide text: **one module** — every part measured in the body's unit (a course, a body of two, a storey) and meeting at its lines; **openings follow construction** — an opening is a hole in a wall with jambs and a head, what fills it follows how the wall is built, and the leaf is chosen last, so a walled front, a framed front, a timber-framed front, a masonry front and a front with no wall each take a different counter and door, and a front with no wall builds the frame the wall would have been, one cell inside the open ring, and stands its counter and door in it; **lines run through** — one head course over a front's openings or a hierarchy the author means, a horizontal that terminates the front over every opening, solid upon solid, the trim that frames one opening framing all, one structural material and one infill; **a landmark reads by its silhouette from the approach** — what the thing does is visible in its shape at eye height from the cell where a body enters, and its parts obey the first three. **[cited]**

**The guide text**: `references/detail.md`'s contract gains the four parts as four lines and the sentence that no door is named per trade; the handout's §3.3 section — *What this place is, and what it is built from* — carries them after the trade paragraph for every place, and for a place with an open side (§5.3) adds one sentence to *The frame*: *your open side stands no wall; build the frame the wall would have been one cell inside the ring, and stand what you open there in it*. The research page is named from the contract so an author who wants the sources can read them. **[authored]**

**What the engine measures** — one census, stated and never a refusal, because every question here is one of intent the author did not declare (CLAUDE.md: a check never infers an undeclared intent in order to refuse) **[authored]**. `validation/piece-census.json` is written by the build beside `clear-width.json`, one entry per bound piece:

1. **Doors and gates, and their framing.** For every `*_door` lower half in the piece: whether each of its two jambs — the cells beside it perpendicular to its `facing`, at both of its courses — and the cell over its upper half are cells a body cannot be in by the nav model's one predicate (`can_be`, §3.1), recorded per cell as present or absent; for every `*_fence_gate`, the same for the two cells beside it along its axis at its course. Another door block of the same kind beside a door is read as the other leaf of a double door, and that leaf's far jamb is the one recorded. Trapdoors are not entered: a hatch lies in a floor or a wall that is its own frame. Iron doors are entered like wooden ones. The derivation's own bars (`SEAM_BAR`, `SEAM_HATCH`, `SEAM_COVER`) stand in the ring the whole writes and are not the piece's. Binding line: `doors framed: D of N`, N the doors and gates entered, D those with every framing cell present. The ruling's smithy writes its door as unframed — the cell over its upper half is air — and the review reads that line; a door standing free in a ruin, or a gate at the end of a hedge line, writes the same line and is the author's to mean.
2. **Openings per face.** Per shown face (`shown_faces`, `DW0885`'s surface) and per open side's first inward layer: each 4-connected run of cells a body can be in, within the face's cells from the floor course to the top of the play space, an open side's own cells excluded, with its head course, sill course and width, so a reviewer sees whether the heads share a line.
3. **Kinds per face.** The distinct block ids of the face's cells (states stripped) with each one's cell count and the longest unbroken straight run of one kind, so a reviewer sees whether three kinds each have a job and whether a run exceeds a storey (`roof-and-facade-craft.md` §6).
4. **Water.** The piece's water cells counted, with the highest and lowest course relative to the walk plane, so a reviewer sees whether the water stands above the ground or lies below a rim.

The index prints `piece census: P piece(s), F face(s), O opening(s), W water cell(s); doors framed: D of N`; a piece with no shown face, no open side and no door writes an entry with zeros, stated. The census is a measurement the review reads beside the threshold frame: whether a door was meant to stand in a wall, whether a shared head or a hierarchy was meant, whether a kind has a job, and whether a thing reads as a fountain are the author's intent and the reviewer's eye, and no number here decides them.

**What stays a review judgement**, said plainly: the module (whether the counter, door and beam meet at the body's lines), the construction (whether the leaf chosen follows the wall it stands in, a framed door included), the material (whether each kind has a job), and the landmark (whether the shape says what the thing does). The engine measures what a body cannot be in and what the face is made of; the reviewer reads the frame with the census beside it. Nothing here is refused because a hierarchy of heads, a free-standing door, a third kind and a flat ring are each a legitimate intent nobody declared; a declared head, and a declared framing the engine confirms, are §11's next shapes if the blind read keeps finding the same miss.

**Rejected [authored]**: *a door, gate or counter per trade or per building type* (the ruling); *a refusal of a door whose jambs or head a body can be in* (a door standing free in a ruin or a gate where a hedge line ends is a design, and a door block does not declare a wall — the census records the framing and the review reads it); *a `front` or `landmark` kind on the box or the node* (a design decision the engine has no business making; a fountain inferred from water above the ground would refuse an intent nobody declared); *a refusal on the count of block kinds in a face* (three kinds with three jobs is a building, three without is a review finding, and the count cannot tell them apart); *judging the frontage against a house style* (a borrowed vocabulary is wrong on the wrong building, Brief 11's finding, and whether a vocabulary is borrowed is read, never measured).

## 4. Life outside the door **[ruling; authored]**

Bushes, potted flowers and props that make the outside feel lived in were too few. Where they live is settled by what owns the ground (`place-craft.md` §4): a street's **verge** (the columns outside its `clear`, spec-0120 §4.1), a yard or garden strip cut as its own **open-sided place** (§5), and the **commons** dressed by the stage-7 edit script (`scatter`, `plant`, `fragment`). `references/placement.md` says a town's cut includes its yards; `references/detail.md`'s contract names the verge as the street's to dress; the handout's §3.3 paragraph for a way place says what stands opposite it — the neighbour summary already names each facing building's intent — so a bench stands by the inn and a trough by the smithy. Guide text; no engine change.

## 5. A side that may stand no wall — an engine capability **[ruling; authored]**

A place may be fully open: each side independently walled or open, 0 to 4 walls; a park fountain and its patch of ground is one region; nothing requires a place to be enclosed. An open shop front is one case of this — a street face declared open, its reach proven — and was a quality win. It is designed as **one per-side capability on the place**, not a storefront special case.

### 5.1 The surface — stage 4, `boxes[].sides`

```json
{ "node": "node/smithy", "extent": [14, 12], "floor": { "datum": "datum/street" }, "ceiling": { "clearance": 5 },
  "roof": { "courses": 4, "eaves": 1 },
  "sides": { "south": "open" } }
```

`sides` is optional, an object keyed by the four horizontal side names (`north`, `south`, `east`, `west`), each `"wall"` or `"open"`; a key absent is `wall`, so a box with no `sides` is today's box. `up` and `down` are not keys: a lid is `ceiling`, a floor is `floor`. The schema admits nothing else (`DW0100`). `fmt` writes the object with its keys in the engine's side order.

**What `open` says**: this place stands no wall on that side. Its ring cells along that face, from the floor course to the top of the play space, hold nothing a body cannot be in; the place's ground continues across them at the walk plane; what lies beyond is the neighbour's or the commons'. It says nothing about traversal: whether a body crosses there is the connection's (a `contact` seam, spec-0053) and the whole's (`DW0838`).

### 5.2 The derivation **[cited: `compiler.md` *The blockout*; authored]**

A stand-in on an open side raises no ring wall above the floor course; its floor course runs across the ring on that side, so a body steps on and off level, and the fixed ground under it is the whole's as ever. A roofed stand-in's lid and roof zone are unchanged — a pavilion is a lid on posts, and the posts are the piece's. Nothing else the derivation writes moves, and a box with no open side derives byte for byte as before (the perturbation of criterion 11 proves the one byte that moves).

### 5.3 The allocation and the handout **[cited: `detail.md` *`delvec allocation`*; authored]**

`delvec allocation` hands `sides`: per open side, the face, its boundary cells (piece-local, the ring run along the face excluding the two corner cells), the walk plane there, and whether a `contact` seam covers any of its cells. The handout's *The frame* section states each open side as it states a seam — *your **south** side is open: cells …, nothing stands in them, your ground runs across them* — and the neighbour summary says *its side toward you is open* where a neighbour declares one, so the facing author dresses the foot of an open front and not a wall. The starter answers an open side as it answers a seam: the ring cells open, the floor course across them.

### 5.4 The checks **[cited: spec-0120 §3, `grammar.md` §4c, `detail.md` `DW0844`; authored]**

1. **A door in an open side**: a seam with `opening` on a face declared `open` is refused at validation — `DW1064`, *`<edge>` is a portal in the `<face>` face of `<place>`, which is declared open: there is no wall to cut it in — declare the side `wall`, or make the crossing a `contact`*. A `contact` on an open side is admitted: it is that side's crossing, stated from the connection.
2. **An open side is a way**, judged by `compiler::nav::width` as spec-0120 judges every way, one authority: its corridor is its boundary columns at the walk plane plus one approach cell outward where that cell is no seam's; cells a `contact` on that side covers are judged under the contact and counted once; a column is clear by the nav model's one predicate in every state its cells can take. **Narrowed is `DW1057`** — a fence post, a planter, a hedge end standing in one column of an open side — and the ledger row's kind is `open side`; the binding line gains `O open side(s)`. **Shut is this spec's finding**: every column taken is `DW1065`, *the `<face>` side of `<place>` is declared open and built shut: <n> of <n> columns taken, first at <cell> by <block>* — a side open in the plan and walled in the piece, a hedge along the whole ring, or an open side at the region's edge with nothing beyond it. Build tier, naming every taking cell with its block and state.
3. **The piece's gates read a handed open side as allocated**: `contract-closure` takes an open side's boundary cells as a declared opening of the space behind them, and `DW0844` admits a face opening whose cells are an open side's as answering it — a face answering neither a seam nor an open side stays the discovered seam. A frontage a body crosses is answered with an `exterior` edge whose `via` is the side's cells; a counter front a body does not cross stands its counter at the play space's edge, inside, so the ring before it is the place's open ground and the counter is the space's boundary.
4. **Nothing else is new**: `DW0838` judges whether an open side joins two places outside an allocated crossing — a frontage onto a street box with no `contact` between them reds there, and the remedy the diagnostic already names is the seam; the commons rule of spec-0098 §14 admits an open side onto the commons with no declaration; `DW0885` reads a wall side's solid boundary cells and finds none on an open side; the exposure of the floor course's edge is the ground's as today.

### 5.5 Rejected **[authored]**

- *A `storefront`, `stall` or `pavilion` box kind.* Each is 0 to 3 walls plus what the piece builds inside; the kind would carry a design decision the engine has no business making.
- *`open` as a traversal declaration.* The connection already says where a body crosses (`contact`), and a second authority over one crossing would be the pair's defect; an open side that is not a crossing — a counter front — exists, so `open` cannot mean *crossed*.
- *Inferring an open side from the piece's bytes.* The stand-in needs it before any piece exists, and a check that infers an intent to refuse is not a check.

## 6. Wide ways keep wide approaches **[ruling; cited: spec-0120 §3, `clear-width.md` §3]**

A three-wide grand stair with a one-wide approach in one room — a newel and a fireplace left one cell — is a defect. **What the engine checks** (cited, spec-0120): every way of the plan and every piece way a bound piece's contract declares (`K`), the body cell over every tread at every course, **the foot's approach row at the host floor and the head's beyond the plane**, one approach cell deep; a structure in any column is `DW1057`, nothing excuses it. The case seen — a stair declared three wide by its `via`, one approach column free — is exactly the shape spec-0120's demo level stands beside, and the gate is confirmed there, not here. **What is guide text** (`place-craft.md` §5): a wide way's approach is as wide as the way, at least three; its landing as deep as the way is wide; a hearth, a newel, a pillar three to five cells from a stair; the newel in the next column over. `references/detail.md`'s contract carries the three lines. An approach depth beyond one cell is §11.

## 7. Seams make no trench **[ruling; authored]**

At a seam between places the ground must not leave a trench a body walking the main road falls into and must detour to get out of. **What the guide asks** (`place-craft.md` §6, into `references/detail.md`'s contract): a seam at grade is walked at the sill — both pieces draw their ground to the sill at the crossing; the ring row across it is paved by the side the plan hands it to; no cell of a street's or a flight's corridor lies below the walk plane with higher cells before and after it. **What a check would have to measure** is §11; the reach graph's `DW0921` asks whether a body is soft-locked, a trench a body jumps out of is not a pocket, and its zero on the case seen is therefore not evidence against the ruling and not a defect this spec proves.

## 11. Open questions and limits

- **The trench check.** What it would measure: along every way's corridor (spec-0120 §3's columns, the ring row across a contact at grade included), the standable floor height at consecutive stations; a station lower than both its neighbours along the travel, by one course or more, is a trench, and the ring row across a seam at grade is held to the sill. What stays open: whether the measurement is a way's (a new verdict of `nav::width`) or a seam's (a widening of the fixed-ground rule, with the side that paves the ring row — spec-0122 §11 row 2); and how a designed step down inside a way is declared so it is not refused. Until it is built, §7's guide text is what holds, and the ruling's case stays a ledger row.
- **An approach deeper than one cell.** The ruling asks for a landing as deep as the way is wide; the engine judges one cell (spec-0120 §3, `clear-width.md` §3). A declared depth on the way (`approach: n`) is the next shape; a depth inferred from the width would refuse an intent nobody declared.
- **The counter's reach.** An open side with a counter proves the body reaches the counter's near face (the side is whole) and the talk-to and interaction proofs reach what stands behind it; whether a counter *reads* as a counter is the demo level's question.
- **An open side on a `solid` site** faces rock one cell out and is refused as built shut; a carved commons on a solid site is spec-0098 §14's open question, not this spec's.
- **The threshold eye in a shut seam**: a gated portal holds a bar in its cells; the shot stands in the configuration of first arrival, where the bar may stand, and sees the bar. Whether a second shot after the opening beat is owed is decided on The Four Trades.
- **A declared head line, a declared framing.** §3.5 states a front's heads and each door's framing in the census and refuses nothing, because a hierarchy of openings and a free-standing door are each a legitimate intent nobody declared. If the blind read keeps finding the same miss, the next shape is an opt-in declaration on the piece that the engine confirms — the head course of a named face held over every opening of that face, with an opening the author exempts named by its cells; or `doors: framed` on the piece, under which every door and gate it holds must have every framing cell present and a door the author means free is named by its cell. A head inferred from the other openings, or a wall inferred from a door block, would refuse an intent nobody declared and is not that shape.
- **A front's beam in the open ring.** §5.1 says an open side's ring cells hold nothing a body cannot be in from the floor course to the top of the play space, and §3.5 part 2 therefore stands a front's frame one cell inside the ring. The clear-width check judges the ring's columns at the body's height, so a beam above the body's reach in the ring passes the check while the stated semantics exclude it: the pair disagrees above two courses. Whether the ring may carry a beam above the body — a pavilion's lintel on posts in the corners — or the frame must stand inside is decided on The Front and the Fountain, and the losing text is changed, never both kept.
