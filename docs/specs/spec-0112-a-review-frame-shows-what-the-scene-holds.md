- **What it is for**: the visual review of a built delve. Every demo level built against the newest checks met one wall: the review frames are rendered from the world as a server saved it at load, so a frame cannot show what the step it stands on has done — an objective's prop, lever, bell or sign placed when the objective activated, a body spawned by a beat, a region a beat filled or cleared — and the renderer draws no body the engine places, so the questions the newest checks ask (does the keeper face the party, does the lever read as the one to pull, does the figure show while it rises) cannot be put to a frame at all. One POV frame rendered pure black: its camera stood inside the bar of a door that is shut at load and open on the leg the camera stands on. A multi-shot cutscene has no review frame of any shot. This spec gives every review frame one answer to "what state is this a picture of", renders it from a world the build writes in that state, makes the frame say per object what it shows and what the renderer cannot draw, proves the camera clear in that very world, and gives every cutscene shot a frame at its key ticks.

## 1. The defect, from the tree

**Cited** (the tree at the ground revision).

1. **A review frame is a picture of the server's save at load.** `run_scene` emits every scene of `render-plan.json` over one `world_path`, the directory `resolve_world` demands `level.dat` and a region file of, which only `validation/world-save.sh` produces: a boot of the shipped image stopped and copied the moment `#placed dw.sys` reads 1 — the last line of `setup_finish`. So the save holds what setup placed (the relight fixtures, the trigger props, the NPC bodies and their hitboxes, the trap hardware, the containers' loot, the shortcut and shop affordances) and nothing an objective's activation places (an `interact` objective's `prop`, hitbox and marker, a `collect` chest, a `reach` marker), nothing a beat places (`spawn-npc`, `spawn-assembly`, an actor), and no region write. `delvec scene` already prints the half of this spec-0089 §8 stated: `review frames render the world at load; <k> of <m> POV shots stand in a configuration other than load`.
2. **The renderer draws none of the bodies the save holds.** The pinned core has no entity class for any kind the engine summons (§2.2), so the NPCs, markers, hitboxes and assembly parts the save carries are empty air in every frame, and nothing in the frame or its index says so.
3. **A camera is proven clear in a world no frame is rendered from.** `render_plan` takes the nav `World` the emit built the proofs on and `verify_camera_eyes` asks `World::is_clear` of each eye there. That world is the assembled model with every gate threshold cleared; the world-load seals enter the route proofs as step-0 fills of the region state (`World::world_load_seals`, `region_state_at`), never the base `World`. A POV eye standing in a gate region that is shut at load and open on its leg is therefore clear to the proof and inside the bars in the frame — the pure black frame. `check_cutscenes` asks `blocks_camera` of the same base world, so a dolly through a region shut at the cutscene's step passes `DW0308` the same way.
4. **A cutscene shot has no frame.** `render_plan` emits seven kinds (spawn, interior, seam, npc, interact, gate, pov) and no `cutscene`; `check_cutscenes` proves the dolly and the pan and writes no camera anywhere a renderer reads. The gallery declares five cutscenes of thirteen shots (`gallery/quests.json`: shots of 6, 4, 3 + 2 × 8, 1 and 10 seconds) and its committed view set holds 172 views of which 0 are of a cutscene.
5. **The written world exists and stops short.** spec-0089 gave a showcase camera a world the engine writes per configuration (`view::beat::stands`, `view::world::write`) and named the review frames as the writer's next caller. That world is `Configuration::blocks` over `picture_base` — the assembly, the gate seals, the relight fixtures and the trigger props, less every gated trap's trigger shut at load — and carries no block entity (a sign the template holds is blank), no entity, and no placement an activation or a beat makes.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 What a step places, and where the emitter says so

**Cited** — `compiler/emit`. Every block or body the datapack puts in the world outside the structure templates comes from one of these sites, in this order of firing:

| When | What | Site |
|---|---|---|
| `setup_finish`, in this order | the relight fixtures (`setblock`), the NPC bodies and hitboxes (`summon minecraft:mannequin …` with `Rotation:[yaw,0]` for a skinned NPC, `summon <base_entity> …` otherwise, then `summon minecraft:interaction`), the environment triggers' hitboxes and props (`env_trigger_setup`: `setblock <prop>` for a hand-pressed prop, `summon minecraft:interaction` otherwise), the trap hardware and disarm affordances, the containers' loot (`item replace block`), the shortcut and shop affordances and markers | `emit/functions.rs`, `emit/npc.rs`, `emit/trigger.rs`, `emit/trap.rs`, `emit/loot.rs`, `emit/shortcut.rs`, `emit/economy.rs` |
| `activate_<obj>`, the tick an objective activates, once | a `collect` chest (`setblock` + `item replace`); an `interact` objective's hand-pressed `prop` (`setblock`, the block is the affordance), else its hitbox (`summon minecraft:interaction`) with its `prop` block or, when the objective is marked, a glowing lantern `item_display`; a marked `reach` objective's end-rod `item_display` | `emit/objective.rs` `activation_commands` |
| an objective's completion | the objective's tagged entities removed (`dw_i_<obj>`, `dw_r_<obj>`) | `emit/objective.rs` |
| a beat the path fires | `spawn-npc` (the same `npc_summon_commands`), `despawn-npc`, `spawn-assembly` (`asm_summon_<s>`: an `item_display` root and `block_display` parts), `despawn-assembly`, an actor's spawn (`summon <entity> …`), the region writes (`fill`), a bonfire's arming (`summon minecraft:interaction`), a cutscene's stand-ins (`summon minecraft:mannequin`) | `emit/npc.rs`, `compiler/assembly.rs`, `emit/actor.rs`, `emit/effect.rs`, `compiler/standin.rs` |

The region writes are the one class the compiler already models per step (`RegionEvent`: region, write, `fire_step`, forced, the block laid). Every other row is emitted as a command string at its site and modelled nowhere: `picture_base` re-derives two of them (the relight fixtures from `light::Placement`, the trigger props from `pressable::trigger_props`) and `affordance.rs` audits the summons by reading the emitted lines back (`summoned_class_subject`), which is the shape an audit of this class takes in this tree.

### 2.2 What the pinned core draws

**Cited** — the installed pin, read as a class list and string tables.

1. **Entities it has a class for**: `se/llbit/chunky/entity/` holds `ArmorStand`, `PlayerEntity`, `PaintingEntity`, `SheepEntity`, `PigEntity`, `CowEntity`, `ChickenEntity`, `MooshroomEntity`, `SquidEntity`, `CopperGolemEntity`, and the block-entity pictures `SignEntity`, `WallSignEntity`, `HangingSignEntity`, `WallHangingSignEntity`, `StandingBanner`, `WallBanner`, `BannerDesign`, `HeadEntity`, `SkullEntity`, `Lectern`, `Book`, `Campfire`, `BeaconBeam`, `CopperGolemStatueEntity`, `LilyPadEntity`, `CoralFanEntity`, `WallCoralFanEntity`, `SporeBlossom`, `CalibratedSculkSensorAmethyst`, `FlameParticles`. `SceneEntities` maps exactly eight entity ids to a class: `minecraft:armor_stand`, `minecraft:painting`, `minecraft:sheep`, `minecraft:pig`, `minecraft:cow`, `minecraft:chicken`, `minecraft:mooshroom`, `minecraft:squid`; players are loaded from the save's player data (`loadPlayers`). **There is no class for** `mannequin`, `villager`, `zombie`, `interaction`, `item_display`, `block_display`, `text_display`, `item` or `item_frame` (the one `minecraft:item_frame` string in the core is a block-id entry of `MinecraftBlockProvider`). Every kind the engine summons today — a skinned NPC and a stand-in (`mannequin`), a plain NPC (its `base_entity`), a hitbox (`interaction`), a marker and an assembly root (`item_display`), an assembly part (`block_display`) — is one the core draws from no world. An actor or a wave mob whose declared `entity` is one of the eight is drawn.
2. **Where it reads them**: `Chunk` reads a chunk's `block_entities` (`BLOCK_ENTITIES_POST_21W43A`) and the entities of the `entities/` region directory (`ENTITIES_POST_20W45A`; `MCRegion` names `entities`), beside the pre-flattening `Level.Entities` and `Level.TileEntities`. Which classes are loaded is `EntityLoadingPreferences` — `loadPlayers`, `loadArmorStands`, `loadBooks`, `loadPaintings`, `loadBeaconBeams`, the six mobs, `loadOtherEntities` — read from the Chunky home's `PersistentSettings` and from a scene's own `entityLoadingPreferences` object (`SceneEntities`, which `Scene` loads it through). A scene that states none draws what the creator's Chunky home happens to say.
3. **What a picture needs**: a sign's `front_text`/`back_text` `messages`, `color` and `has_glowing_text` (`SignEntity`, legacy `Text1`–`Text4` too); an armour stand's `Pose` (`Head`, `Body`, `LeftArm`, `RightArm`, `LeftLeg`, `RightLeg`), `ArmorItems`/`HandItems` or `equipment` (`head`, `chest`, `legs`, `feet`), `Small`, `ShowArms`, `NoBasePlate`, `Invisible`, `Rotation`; a painting's `variant` and `facing`. A block map carries none of these (spec-0089 §2.2 item 4, **cited**), and the written world of spec-0089 writes none (`view::world`: "no entities, block entities or ticks"); a server save carries the template's.

### 2.3 What the configuration model holds, and does not

**Cited** — `nav/route/mod.rs`, `nav/world/mod.rs`, `view/beat.rs`. A configuration is a region state: every forced `RegionEvent` whose `fire_step` is a causal ancestor of the arrival (`Plan::gate_fired_before`), latest write per region, with the world-load seals entered as step-0 fills. `Configuration::blocks(base)` lays it over a block map; `path_configurations` keys the critical path's distinct states by first arrival and maps every step to one (`per_step`); the render plan already writes `after: {step, cells_moved}` on every POV shot from it (spec-0089 §8). The model holds no block entity, no entity, no activation placement and no clock: `reachable_clocks` collects every `set-time`/`set-weather` the campaign can reach as one unordered set (the quantifier `DW0890` needs), and nothing says which clock a given step has set. The nav `World` a configuration is laid over has every gate threshold cleared (`nav/world/mod.rs`, *gate thresholds cleared*), and `render_plan` proves its cameras against that base and not against any configuration (§1 item 3).

### 2.4 How a frame is judged today

**Cited** — `snapshot.rs`, `render_plan.rs`, `nav/cutscene.rs`. `delvec snapshot` is the one place the tree reads a frame: `collect_targets` lists every anchor, gate region, NPC post, actor post, interact marker, stealth zone and trigger of the plan; `resolve_targets` projects each through the camera (`Camera::project`), keeps those inside the frustum with a `screen_bbox` and a `distance`, and asks `VoxelGrid::occluded_target` nine sight lines (the box centre and the corners of a box inset by 0.3) against the grid with the target's own cells excluded; the rest are `out_of_frame`. spec-0110 lifts the nine-line rule into `compiler::framing::sight` and holds the snapshot's `occluded`/`out_of_frame` to it, one function for the cutscene proof and the snapshot. `render_plan` judges one thing of every camera, that its eye cell is clear (`DW0724`), at the base world; `check_cutscenes` judges a dolly's clearance (`DW0308`) and pan (`DW0347`) at the base world. Nothing joins a frame's camera to the snapshot's targets: a review frame's index entry is its `expect` prose and nothing measured.

### 2.5 What a frame costs

**Cited** — `references/visual-review.md` (*What the set costs*), `tools.md` §4a, `showcase-shots.md` §3: one POV scene at the review tier (`sppTarget` 500, `DEFAULT_SIZE` 1024 × 1024) took **299 s** at `-threads 10` on a ten-core machine, and a thirteen-frame POV set **82 minutes**, about 380 s a frame once a set is going; a 128-spp quarter-size draft is about 20 s. The world-writer's cost is not measured on this tree (spec-0089 recorded the gallery's load world as 6 chunks and 32,768 bytes, and the snapshot's assembly plus grid flattening at about 30 ms); the placement boot `world-save.sh` runs is **92 s** on The Stranding (`tools.md`, `playtest-server.sh`'s measurement), before any beat is driven.

## 3. The state a review frame claims

**Authored**, on §1–§2. A review frame is a picture of one **configuration**: a block map, the objects the datapack has placed by then, and a clock.

1. **A POV frame stands in its leg's configuration** — the one `path_configurations` already names on the shot (`after`): the state the leg is walked under, every forced write credited and every object a step before it placed and no later step removed.
2. **A cutscene frame stands in the configuration of the step that fires the cutscene** — the arrival at the step after the firing step, as a showcase camera `after` that step does (spec-0089 §4.1), because a cutscene a completion schedules plays after the completion's own writes.
3. **Every other kind (spawn, interior, seam, npc, interact, gate) stands at load**, as every one does today: an overview of the assembly is a picture of the assembly.
4. **The bytes are the engine's, never a server's.** The configuration's world is written by `view::world::write` from the model the proofs read, as spec-0089 settled for the showcase cameras and for the same reason: a frame that disagrees with a proof shows a defect of the model instead of hiding it under the server's physics, and two writes of one build are byte-identical. A world saved from a server that ran setup and was driven to the step is the cross-check (§7.2), never the source: it costs a placement boot and a bot walk per configuration (§2.5) and no two of its saves are the same bytes.
5. **The clock is the configuration's.** The latest forced `set-time` and `set-weather` whose firing is a causal ancestor of the arrival, else the world's own (§6.6). A review frame has no picture row to take a sky from, so spec-0089 §6.4's rule for a showcase camera — the picture's sky — does not reach it; what a player on that leg sees is the clock the story has set.

## 4. The placements ledger

**Authored**, on §2.1. `compiler::placed` holds **every placement the datapack makes outside the templates, and every removal**, as one record each, and the emitter writes its command from the record:

```
Placed { fired: Firing, object: PlacedObject }
Firing      = Setup | Step(usize) | Unforced(blame)            // the region model's three stances
PlacedObject = Block { cell, state }
             | BlockEntity { cell, state, nbt }                // a sign with its text, a chest with its loot
             | Entity { kind, pos, yaw, tags, picture }        // a body, a hitbox, a marker, an assembly part
             | Removed { tags }                                // the despawn an objective's completion runs
```

1. **One producer.** `Placed::command(&self) -> String` is the only way a `setblock`, `fill`, `summon`, `item replace block` or `kill @e[tag=…]` line enters `setup_finish`, `activate_<obj>`, `spawn_npc_<id>`, `spawn_actor_<id>`, `asm_summon_<s>` and the completion despawns. The region writes stay `RegionEvent` and are not duplicated here: a `Placed::Block` is a single cell the datapack sets, a `RegionEvent` is a box a beat fills or clears, and the writer lays both (§5). `light::Placement` and `pressable::trigger_props` become readers of the ledger's `Setup` rows, so `picture_base` stops re-deriving them.
2. **The firing is the region model's.** `Firing::Step(i)` is the `critical_path` index of the step whose completion, activation or performed trigger runs the command — an activation is credited to the step that activates the objective (the step before the objective's own, or `Setup` for a first objective active from load), a completion's despawn to the objective's step, a beat's spawn to the step whose bundle fires it; `Unforced(blame)` for a beat nobody has to play (a sprung trap, a bought offer, a flag-gated bundle), which no configuration lays, as `RegionState::blocks_over` lays no unforced write. The ordering is the one function spec-0110 §5.6 lifts out of `dsl::npc` (`DW0195`, `DW0198`): same effect list at an earlier index, an earlier `sequence` step, a quest-graph ancestor, a prerequisite's bundle; this spec reads it and does not re-implement it.
3. **What a configuration holds of the ledger**: every row whose `Firing` is `Setup` or a causal ancestor of the arrival, applied in firing order, a `Removed` deleting every earlier `Entity` carrying one of its tags; cells laid later win. `Configuration::placed(&ledger) -> Vec<&Placed>` is that function, beside `Configuration::blocks`, and the writer, the frame's `shows` list (§6.5) and the cross-check (§7.2) all read it.
4. **The audit is structural and stated.** `crates/delvec/tests/placed_ledger.rs` reads every emitted function of a fixture build the way `affordance.rs` reads summons, and holds every `setblock`, `fill`, `summon`, `item replace block` and `kill @e[tag=` line of the functions named in item 1 to a ledger row and every ledger row to a line, in both directions, printing both counts; a line with no row is a producer the ledger does not know.

## 5. The worlds the build writes

**Authored**, on spec-0089 §5 (**cited**) and §2.2.

1. **The build writes them.** `delvec build` writes one world per distinct configuration its frames stand in under `<out>/validation/worlds/<key>/` — `at-load`, or `after-<step>` with `/` as `-`, `view::beat::after_key`'s scheme — through `view::world::write`, the one writer, and records each file in `manifest.json` as every output is. The delve image copies `datapack`, `server`, `manifest.json` and `critical-path.json` and nothing under `validation/` (`validation/Dockerfile.delve`), so no written world ships. Two builds of one tree write byte-identical worlds (ADR-0006, the writer's own invariant). `delvec written-world` keeps writing the load world alone for a campaign the staging gate checks; its bytes equal `validation/worlds/at-load`'s byte for byte, asserted.
2. **The blocks** are `Configuration::blocks` over `picture_base`, then the configuration's `Placed::Block` and `Placed::BlockEntity` cells laid in firing order — so an `interact` prop stands on its leg's frame and not before it — and every laid cell marked in `Reached`, so a prop a beat sets over the generator's ground does not let the ground through.
3. **The block entities.** Every chunk carries `block_entities`: the templates' own (`nbtread::Block::nbt`, the compound the structure holds for the block at that cell, with `x`, `y`, `z` and `id` written as the game writes them) wherever the cell still holds the template's block, and the ledger's `BlockEntity` compounds. A sign the template lettered reads in the frame; a sign a `setblock` placed with no text is blank, as the game's is. `block_entities_omitted` therefore changes meaning: it counts the block-entity-pictured cells whose compound the world **does not** hold — a measured zero on a campaign whose signs all come lettered — and the binding line keeps the count under its name.
4. **The entities.** `entities/r.<x>.<z>.mca` holds every `Placed::Entity` the configuration holds, one compound per body — `id`, `Pos` (the summon's position), `Rotation` (`[yaw, 0]`), `Tags`, a `UUID` derived from the tag list and position so two writes agree — and, for the kinds the core draws (§2.2 item 1), the picture keys the summon carries (an actor's armour-stand pose or equipment, a painting's variant). A kind the core has no class for is written all the same: the world is the one the server would hold, the cross-check (§7.2) reads it, and the frame says the body is not drawn (§6.5).
5. **The scene says what to load.** Every scene `delvec scene` writes states `entityLoadingPreferences` with every class the core can load set on, so a frame never depends on the creator's Chunky home, and names its world by absolute path as every scene does.
6. **The biomes** stay the build's biome map at load, as spec-0089 §6.3 left them.

## 6. The frames

### 6.1 POV frames

**Authored.** Every `pov` shot keeps its camera and its `after`, gains `world: <key>` — the key of the world it is rendered from, `at-load` where `after.cells_moved` is 0 and the configuration holds no placement, else `after-<step>` — and `sky` (§6.6). `delvec scene` writes each scene's `world.path` from the shot's key, and `review_frame_limit`'s line is replaced by the binding line of §8: there is no longer a limit to state.

### 6.2 Cutscene frames

**Authored**, on §2.4 and spec-0110 §2 (**cited**: the lens is the camera entity's position; the client's default vertical field of view is 70° on an 854 × 480 window; the proof reads the keyframes `ExpandedShot::frames` emits and the client's linear tween between them).

1. **Which cutscenes.** Every cutscene a critical-path step fires — the bundles `hold::completion_bundles` names for the step, at any depth of their `sequence` steps, and a performed trigger's effects — in path order, then list order. A cutscene no critical step fires (a branch's, a dialogue option's, a trap's) gets no frame and is counted by name on the binding line (§8), as a cutscene a reviewer judges in game or in the rehearsal flow.
2. **Which ticks.** Per shot, the pose at tick 0 and, for a shot whose camera or aim moves, the pose at its last tick (`shot_ticks`): the two ends of what `DW0308` proves between. A static shot has one frame.
3. **The camera** is the keyframe pose — position, yaw and pitch as the client holds them at that tick (`Frame`, three decimals) — at `fov` 70, the client's default. It is never moved: like a POV eye it *is* the viewer's, so an eye inside a block is a build error against the shot (§6.4).
4. **The id**: `cutscene/<step>/<n>/shot<i>/t<tick>` — the firing step's id slug (`obj-clear-the-muster`, `trigger-strike-the-sentinel`), `n` the cutscene's index among those the step fires, `i` the shot's index, `tick` `0` or the last tick. The kind is `cutscene`; the shot carries `step` (the firing step's id), `after` (its configuration, as a POV shot's), `world`, `sky`, the shot's `seconds` and `subject` where spec-0110 is in the tree, and an `expect` list composed as a POV shot's is: the cutscene's place in the player's words, the shot's subject where declared, and `camera at the shot's own lens — a spectated view, not overhead or orbit`.
5. **The world** is the configuration arriving at the step after the firing step (§3.2), with the ledger rows that step's own completion places credited — a `spawn-assembly` in the same list ahead of the cutscene stands in the frame, as spec-0110 §5.6 says it stands in the shot.
6. **The snapshot's bridge** (`delvec snapshot --shot cutscene/…`) frames the same pose from the campaign alone, as it frames a `pov/…` id from the recomputed routes, so the gallery's render gate (`check-gallery-render.py`) can draft every declared cutscene view.

### 6.3 The other kinds

**Authored.** `spawn`, `interior`, `seam`, `npc`, `interact` and `gate` shots stand at load (`world: at-load`) and gain the same `world` and `sky` keys, so every shot of the plan states its world and nothing is left to a default.

### 6.4 A camera is clear in the frame's own world

**Authored**, on §1 item 3. `render_plan` asks `verify_camera_eyes` of every shot **against the configuration's world** — `Configuration::world(base)` with the ledger's blocks of that configuration laid, through one `World` per distinct key — and `Shots::push` stands a movable kind's eye up (`stand_in_open_air`) against that same world. `check_cutscenes` ray-marches each shot's polyline and chords through the firing step's configuration. A `pov` or `cutscene` eye inside a block in its own world is `DW0724` naming the shot, the cell, the world key and the block there; the message for a `cutscene` eye names the shot's path or `bearing`/`dist` as the repair, as `DW0308` does. `camera_eye_proof` states how many eyes were proven in how many worlds.

### 6.5 What a frame shows, and what the renderer cannot draw — one sight rule

**Authored**, on §2.4. Every shot's index entry (`shot-index.json`) gains:

- **`shows`**: every snapshot target (`collect_targets`'s kinds) and every `Placed::Entity` of the frame's configuration inside the frame's frustum, each with `kind`, `id`, `cell`, `distance`, `screen_bbox`, `occluded`, and for a body `facing` (its yaw, with the compass word the POV `expect` already uses) — computed by `snapshot::resolve_targets` over a `VoxelGrid` built from the frame's own world map, the same nine-line rule the snapshot's manifest states and spec-0110 moves into `framing::sight`. There is one definition: when spec-0110 lands, `resolve_targets` calls `framing::sight`, and this spec's test (`crates/delvec/tests/review_frame_shows.rs`) holds the index's `occluded` equal to a `delvec snapshot --shot <id>` manifest's, target for target, on the gallery.
- **`undrawn`**: the subset of `shows` whose object the pinned core has no class for (§2.2 item 1; the eight drawn kinds are a table beside `scene::CHUNKY_CORE`, so a pin bump re-reads it), each with `judge_by`: `snapshot --shot <id> --labels` (the post is stamped on the draft) or `in game`. This is `DW1039` (§7.1), a report per frame.
- **`out_of_frame`** for the targets the snapshot would list so, as the manifest does.

A frame whose `shows` holds a body the renderer cannot draw says so in words a reviewer reads before the picture; a frame that shows a lever says the lever is in frame at its distance, and whether anything stands between. Nothing here judges a composition (spec-0110 does, for a cutscene shot's declared subject); `shows` is a measurement of the frame, never a refusal.

### 6.6 The clock

**Authored**, on §2.3. `collect_region_events`'s walk also collects every `set-time` and `set-weather` as a `ClockEvent { fire_step, forced, time | weather }`, at the one site that already decides a beat's firing step and stance; a configuration's clock is the latest forced `ClockEvent` of each kind whose `fire_step` is a causal ancestor of the arrival, else the world's declared `time` and `weather`. Every shot states it as `sky: {time, daytime_ticks, weather, set_by}` — `set_by` the step id or `world` — and `delvec scene` builds the scene's sun, sky and fog from the shot's `sky` through `scene::sky_of`, the one function (spec-0079 §5), while the plan's top-level `sky` stays the declared initial hour for the panorama. The clocks a configuration can hold are a subset of `reachable_clocks`' set, asserted, so `DW0890`'s quantifier is untouched and the two readings cannot disagree about which hours exist.

## 7. The codes

### 7.1 `DW1039` — a frame posts a body the renderer cannot draw (report tier, `compiler::view::index`)

A review frame whose `shows` holds an entity of a kind the pinned core has no class for. Printed once per frame by `delvec scene` — the shot, the body's id, kind, cell, facing and distance, and `judge_by` — and written into the shot index as `undrawn`; the set's binding line counts them. Never a refusal (`DW0893`'s precedent: a report where a check cannot draw the line a person can): the frame is right, and the body is real; what the report protects is a reviewer reading empty floor as an absent keeper.

### 7.2 `DW1040` — the objects the engine writes are not the objects the pinned server places (build tier, `compiler::view::world`)

`tools/ci/check-written-world.py` gains, beside its cell comparison, a comparison of the **placed objects** of the written load world against the `world-save.sh` save of the same build, read through `tools/lib/anvil.py` (which gains the `block_entities` and `entities/` readers the core's own loader has, §2.2 item 2): every block entity by cell and compound, every entity by kind, cell and tag set. A difference in no named class reds `DW1040` naming the object; the classes measured and counted separately, never allowlisted: the server's own bookkeeping keys (`UUID`, `Motion`, `Fire`, `Air`, `OnGround`, `PortalCooldown`, `Health`, `Attributes`, `Brain`, `Rotation`'s second component), a container's loot the server re-rolled from a `LootTable`, and a stand-in or a wave body present only once a player has played. `--record` writes both verdicts under the build's manifest sha256, and `staging-gate.py --written-world` admits on neither alone. The cells stay `DW0955`: two object classes, two codes, one tool and one reader.

### 7.3 Widenings

- **`DW0724`** judges every camera in the configuration's world instead of the base world (§6.4), and `cutscene` eyes beside the seven kinds. A direction: it can only turn a plan red that was green.
- **`DW0308`** ray-marches a dolly through the firing step's configuration instead of the base world (§6.4). The same direction.
- **`DW0955`** compares the cells of a written load world that now carries the ledger's setup blocks laid from the ledger rather than re-derived; its classes and remedy are unchanged, and on the gallery its model count stays 0 (criterion 12).

## 8. The record: the plan, the index, the ledger, the binding lines

**Authored.**

- **`render-plan.json`**: every shot carries `world` and `sky`; `pov` shots keep `after`; `cutscene` shots are appended after `pov` in the order of §6.2 with the keys named there; `camera_eye_proof` gains `worlds` (the keys proven in). The committed `gallery/render-plan.json` view set gains the gallery's cutscene views, so a cutscene frame that vanishes is a red naming it (`check-gallery-render.py`).
- **`shot-index.json`**: every entry gains `world`, `sky`, `shows`, `undrawn` and `out_of_frame` (§6.5); a `cutscene` entry carries `step`, `shot`, `tick` and `seconds`.
- **`validation/review-frames.json`**, written by the build: per world key its chunk count, cell count, block-entity count, entity count and sha256; per shot its key and the counts of `shows` and `undrawn`; the cutscenes framed and the cutscenes no critical step fires, by name; the canonical hash of the shot list (`shots_digest`); `unchecked` naming §9's items.
- **Binding lines**, printed by every `delvec build` with a render plan: `review frames: <m> shot(s) in <k> world(s) (<l> at load, <a> after a step); <c> cutscene frame(s) of <s> shot(s) in <n> cutscene(s), <u> cutscene(s) no critical step fires: <names>; cameras proven clear: <m> of <m> in their own worlds` and per world `world: <key> <chunks> chunk(s), <cells> cell(s), <g> ground cell(s), <b> block entit(ies), <e> entit(ies) (<d> drawn by the pinned core), sha256 <hash> -> <dir>`; by every `delvec scene`: `frames: <m> scene(s) over <k> world(s); shows: <t> object(s) in frame over <m> frame(s), <u> not drawn by the pinned core (DW1039) in <f> frame(s); skies: <j> at the world's clock, <q> set by a step`. A campaign with no cutscene prints `0 cutscene frame(s)` and a campaign whose bodies are all out of frame prints `0 not drawn`: measured zeroes.
- **`validation/render-shots.sh`** drops its world gate — the build holds every world a scene names — and refuses instead a plan whose shot names a world key the build directory does not hold (a build from an engine before this spec), naming the key and the remedy, a rebuild; its `shot set ready` line counts the cutscene frames separately, because that is the number the reviewer multiplies (§10). `--world` is removed from `delvec scene` and `delvec panorama`: a review frame has one world source, and a flag that could point it at a server save is a second authority for what the frame shows (spec-0089 §5.3's argument, **cited**). `world-save.sh` keeps its one remaining caller, the cross-check (§7.2), and `check-whole-map-render.py` stops writing a stub world.

## 9. What a frame does not show, by name

**Authored**, on §2.2 and spec-0089 §6 (**cited**). Each is in `review-frames.json`'s `unchecked` and on the index entry where it applies.

1. **A body the core has no class for** — every NPC, hitbox, marker, stand-in and assembly part today. Said per frame (`undrawn`, `DW1039`) with where and which way it faces; never drawn by a substitute.
2. **A body's pose at a tick** — a `move-npc` in flight, an assembly mid-clip. The frame holds the post the ledger holds; a cutscene frame of a moving subject shows the subject's post, and spec-0110's proof holds the subject in frame along its track.
3. **The party and its stand-ins** (spec-0095): no build-time pose exists.
4. **Particles, weather in the air, the moon** — the core draws none (spec-0079 §2.1).
5. **A collapse's debris and `then_floor`**, a `set-atmosphere` repaint, a flood's spread past its laid cells, an unforced write — as spec-0089 §6 and §11 name them.
6. **A cutscene no critical step fires** — counted by name; judged in game.
7. **The rendered pixels of an entity the core does draw** are confirmed by eye on the demo level (§11), as spec-0110 confirms a client's drawing: nothing in the tree reads a frame's pixels for a body.

## 10. The cost on the creator's machine

**Authored**, on §2.5 (**cited** figures).

- **The frames.** The review tier is unchanged: about 300–380 s a frame at 1024 × 1024 and 500 spp on ten threads. A cutscene adds one frame per static shot and two per moving one: a 15-shot finale adds up to 30 frames, about 3 hours at that tier on that machine, which the `shot set ready` line states before the first render and the reviewer's reading order decides (the POV sequence first, as today; the cutscene frames of the shots spec-0110 refuses nothing on next; a 64-spp draft to judge framing). Nothing here raises a budget or renders a frame nobody asked for: the scenes are emitted, the renders are the creator's choice, and a set not rendered is reported as not rendered.
- **The worlds.** Writing a world is in-process and runs once per distinct configuration at build time, not per frame; the writer's cost on the gallery is measured by criterion 3 and written on the binding line as `written in <ms>`. The alternative — a server boot per configuration — starts at 92 s before a single beat is driven.
- **The proofs.** `DW0724` and `shows` build one `World` and one `VoxelGrid` per distinct world key (the snapshot's grid flattening is about 30 ms) and ask a cell or nine lines per frame: milliseconds per frame.

## 13. Decisions

- **A review frame is rendered from the engine-written world of its own configuration**, never from a server save. The alternative, a save driven to the step, costs a boot and a bot walk per configuration, yields bytes no two runs share, and leaves the frame unable to say why it disagrees with a proof.
- **What the datapack places enters the picture through one ledger the emitter writes its commands from.** The alternative — re-deriving each placement class in the writer, as `picture_base` does for two of them today — is a third reading of what setup does, and the audit of §4.4 is what keeps the one reading whole.
- **The build writes the worlds, under `validation/`.** The alternative, writing them at `delvec scene` time, hands `scene` the campaign and the prefabs and leaves the world outside the manifest; a world the build writes is hashed with every other output and never ships.
- **A body the core cannot draw is reported per frame, with where to judge it, and never substituted.** A figure composited from a skin or an armour stand written in a mannequin's place is a world that is not the proofs' world.
- **A review frame takes its configuration's clock; a showcase camera keeps its picture's.** A review frame has no picture row to answer; a player on that leg sees the clock the story has set.
- **A cutscene gets two frames per moving shot and one per static shot**, at the ends `DW0308` proves between, and no frame for a cutscene the critical path never fires, counted by name. A frame per tick is a film, not a review set.
- **No new code for a rule that exists**: the camera proof and the dolly proof widen their quantifier under their own codes; the new codes are the report (`DW1039`) and the second object class of the cross-check (`DW1040`).
- No DSL change; no ADR.

## 14. What stayed out, and why

- A substitute body in a frame (an armour stand for a mannequin, a composited figure): a world that is not the one the proofs read, and a picture a reviewer would believe.
- A frame per cutscene tick, or along the dolly: the ends are proven clear and framed; the in-betweens are the client's linear tween, and a film is the rehearsal flow's medium.
- The server save as a frame's source: §13's first decision.
- A review of the frame's composition (fill, thirds, headroom): spec-0110's non-goal, for the same reason.
- Branch paths' frames and cutscenes: the critical path is the only path the review frames walk today; a branch's frames are the writer's next caller.
- Reading a rendered frame's pixels for a body: no instrument in the tree reads a frame, and the demo level's eye is where a drawn body is confirmed.
