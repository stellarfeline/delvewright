## 1. The defect, from the tree

**Cited** (the tree at the ground revisions, and the content records).

1. **A review frame is a picture of the server's save at load.** `run_scene` emits every scene of `render-plan.json` over one `world_path` only `validation/world-save.sh` produces: a boot of the shipped image stopped and copied the moment `#placed dw.sys` reads 1, the last line of `setup_finish`. The save holds what setup placed and nothing an objective's activation, a beat or a region write places.
2. **The renderer draws none of the bodies the save holds.** The pinned Chunky core has an entity class for eight vanilla kinds and for none the engine summons — `mannequin`, `villager`, `interaction`, `item_display`, `block_display`, `text_display` — so every NPC, marker, hitbox and assembly part is empty air in every frame, and nothing says so.
3. **A camera is proven clear in a world no frame is rendered from.** `render_plan` asks `verify_camera_eyes` of the nav `World` with every gate threshold cleared; a POV eye in a gate region shut at load and open on its leg is clear to the proof and inside the bars in the frame — the pure black frame. `check_cutscenes` asks `blocks_camera` of the same base world.
4. **A cutscene shot has no frame.** `render_plan` emits seven kinds and no `cutscene`; the gallery declares five cutscenes of thirteen shots and its committed view set holds 172 views of which 0 are of a cutscene.
5. **The written world exists and stops short.** `view::world::write` writes `Configuration::blocks` over `picture_base` and carries no block entity, no entity, no light and no placement an activation or a beat makes.
6. **The renderer is the bottleneck of demo-level development.** One review frame at the tier the scene carries (1024 × 1024, 500 samples) took **299 s** at `-threads 10` on a ten-core machine and a thirteen-frame set **82 minutes** (`references/visual-review.md`, *What the set costs*). The Armoury's set is 25 scenes: one was rendered (the panorama, at 300 samples), five POV frames were started and stopped unfinished between 22% and 62% of their samples, nineteen were never rendered (`campaigns/the-armoury/GENERATION.md`, *Visual review — deferred*). The Muster Loft's review stopped before its set was drawn; the Reckoning rendered one frame of 37 and stopped two in progress (their `GENERATION.md`). Each JVM `validation/chunky.sh` starts takes the HotSpot default heap: on this 16 GiB machine `java -XX:+PrintFlagsFinal` answers `MaxRAMPercentage 25`, `MaxHeapSize 4294967296` — 4 GiB a process — and every render is followed by a second JVM for the denoiser's passes.
7. **The draft renderer draws a layout, not a scene.** `delvec snapshot` paints each cell one flat colour from the vendored palette, draws no texture, no light, no entity and no block entity, and draws a coastal place's sea floor without its water; the room the Armoury's record judged on it says so: *those drafts draw no entity either, so how the rack, the frame and the items on them read is the owner's walk-through*.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 What a step places, and where the emitter says so

**Cited** — `compiler/emit`. Every block or body the datapack puts in the world outside the structure templates comes from one of these sites, in this order of firing:

| When | What | Site |
|---|---|---|
| `setup_finish`, in this order | the relight fixtures (`setblock`), the NPC bodies and hitboxes (`summon minecraft:mannequin …` with `Rotation:[yaw,0]` for a skinned NPC, `summon <base_entity> …` otherwise, then `summon minecraft:interaction`), the environment triggers' hitboxes and props, the trap hardware and disarm affordances, the containers' loot (`item replace block`), the shortcut and shop affordances and markers | `emit/functions.rs`, `emit/npc.rs`, `emit/trigger.rs`, `emit/trap.rs`, `emit/loot.rs`, `emit/shortcut.rs`, `emit/economy.rs` |
| `activate_<obj>`, the tick an objective activates, once | a `collect` chest; an `interact` objective's hand-pressed `prop`, else its hitbox with its `prop` block or, when marked, a glowing lantern `item_display`; a marked `reach` objective's end-rod `item_display` | `emit/objective.rs` `activation_commands` |
| an objective's completion | the objective's tagged entities removed | `emit/objective.rs` |
| a beat the path fires | `spawn-npc`, `despawn-npc`, `spawn-assembly` (an `item_display` root and `block_display` parts), `despawn-assembly`, an actor's spawn, the region writes (`fill`), a bonfire's arming, a cutscene's stand-ins | `emit/npc.rs`, `compiler/assembly.rs`, `emit/actor.rs`, `emit/effect.rs`, `compiler/standin.rs` |

The region writes are the one class the compiler models per step (`RegionEvent`). Every other row is emitted as a command string at its site and modelled nowhere: `picture_base` re-derives two of them and `affordance.rs` audits the summons by reading the emitted lines back.

### 2.2 What Lumenwright is, and what it draws

**Cited** — the Lumenwright tree at `2b4a0b35`.

1. **What it is.** An offline, headless renderer for a Java Edition world save: a Rust workspace of two members, `lumenwright` (the library and its CLI) and `lumenwright-kernel` (a `#![no_std]` path-tracing kernel), with `lumenwright-ptx` excluded from the workspace because it builds only for `nvptx64-nvidia-cuda`. GPL-3.0-or-later (`LICENSE`, `[workspace.package].license`), in the ADR-0013 allowlist and the engine's own licence. Version `0.1.0`; **not on crates.io**; no CI workflow in the repository; no `rust-toolchain.toml` (edition 2024, builds under the engine's pinned 1.97.1).
2. **Two modes, one scene.** `quick`: a triangle rasterizer with a z-buffer that looks like the vanilla client — per-face shading, smooth lighting and ambient occlusion from the save's stored sky and block light, biome tints, cutout and translucent layers, distance fog, a sky gradient and the sun sprite. `hq`: a unidirectional path tracer over a sparse brick grid — sun and sky light with next-event estimation for the sun, emissive blocks, diffuse bounces, Fresnel water, ACES or gamma or filmic tonemapping, progressive sample accumulation, an optional linear HDR `.pfm`. Both modes bake the world once into one quad list (`SCOPE.md` §4.1).
3. **Backends.** One `Backend` trait (`render/mod.rs`): `cpu` (rayon), and `metal`, `vulkan`, `dx12` through `wgpu` 30.0.1 with WGSL kernels naga translates, selected at run time (`BackendKind::Auto` takes the first GPU adapter wgpu finds and falls back to the CPU with a warning; a software adapter is refused). `cuda` runs the kernel crate compiled to PTX through `cudarc` with `dynamic-loading` (no toolkit at build, `libcuda` at run time) and is a cargo feature, `cuda`, whose `build.rs` panics unless `scripts/build-ptx.sh` has written `target/ptx/lumenwright.ptx` with the `nvptx64-nvidia-cuda` target installed; `gpu` is a second cargo feature, on by default. CUDA has never executed (no NVIDIA GPU on the development machine); Vulkan and DX12 build the same code as Metal and have never executed; an open Lumenwright issue holds that run.
4. **The library surface.** `lumenwright::job::run(&RenderArgs) -> RenderReport` is the whole pipeline behind the CLI, and it resolves the client jar itself (`--minecraft-jar`, then a launcher's jar for the world's version, then `~/.chunky/resources/minecraft.jar`, then a SHA-1-verified download from Mojang unless `--offline`), loads a square of chunks of radius `--render-distance` around the camera, and writes the PNG. Its pieces are public: `World::open(dir, dimension)` needs `level.dat` with a `Data` compound; `World::load_area(min_chunk, max_chunk, with_entities)` loads an inclusive chunk rectangle with its entities and block entities; `assets::load_resources(jar, packs)` layers resource packs over the jar; `Scene::build(store, registry, volume, entities, block_entities, opts)`; `mesh::build_mesh` takes `Lighting { sky_factor, gamma, smooth }` and `QuickSky::new(rays, w, h, sky_colour, fog_colour, sun_dir, fog_range, sun_rect)` from the caller; `voxel::build_hq` and `hq_params` fill a `Params` whose `sun_dir`, `sun_color`, `sky_zenith`, `sky_horizon` are plain fields; `render::create_backend(kind)`; `post::tonemap`, `post::downsample`, `post::write_png`. `RenderReport` carries `EntityStats { rendered, fallback_boxes, skipped }` and `BlockEntityStats { rendered, skipped, text_lines, glyphs }` by kind, and the timings.
5. **Entities it draws**: 57 mobs from `assets/entity_models.json`, posed by `Pos` and `Rotation`, variant textures from a NBT field; `mannequin` and `player` with the skin `profile.texture` names when a resource pack carries it, else the default skin; `block_display` through its block model and `transformation`; paintings; item frames (the frame, not its content). **Invisible by rule** (`is_invisible_kind`): `marker`, `interaction`, `area_effect_cloud`, `lightning_bolt` — the hitbox the engine summons draws nothing, as in the game. **Not drawn**: `item`, `item_display`, held items and equipment (an open Lumenwright issue: items drawn); `text_display` (an open issue: text displays drawn); any other kind, as a box the size of its hitbox under `--unknown-entities box`, or nothing under `skip`.
6. **Block entities it draws** from the chunk's `block_entities`: signs and hanging signs with their text in the vanilla bitmap font (front and back, dye colour, glowing outline, styled spans, `translate` keys), bells, chests (single, double, trapped, ender, copper), banners with their pattern layers, decorated pots, skulls and player heads from the profile, the lectern's and enchanting table's books. Not drawn: beds, shulker boxes, the conduit, open lids, italic or obfuscated text.
7. **Light.** Quick mode reads the save's `SkyLight` and `BlockLight` nibbles (`BlockVolume::light`): a section with no sky-light array inherits the bottom of the next stored layer above it, and full light above the world — so a save with no light arrays is lit at sky 15 and block 0 everywhere, inside and out. HQ mode lights the scene itself.
8. **Determinism and parity, measured there**: identical inputs give bit-identical images on CPU and Metal in both modes; quick mode CPU against Metal differs in 0.075% of pixels by more than 8/255, hq at 32 samples in 0.03%, because both backends draw the same RNG streams and sample textures nearest-neighbour from a buffer (`SCOPE.md` §4.2–§4.3, §6).
9. **Performance, measured there** (Apple M2 Pro, 10 CPU and 16 GPU cores, 1.21.11 assets): a 33 × 33-chunk save at 1920 × 1080 in quick mode 0.33 s end to end on Metal and 0.29 s on the CPU; hq at 1280 × 720 and 256 samples 21 s on Metal for that save and 11.8 s for the showcase world; the CPU about 6× slower in hq. Candle-lit rooms need about 4,096 samples because emitters have no next-event estimation (an open issue).
10. **Dependencies that reach an embedding crate**: `wgpu 30.0.1` and `naga 30.0.1` — the same versions `delvec`'s `Cargo.lock` already holds through Nucleation; `image 0.25` with `png` only — already in `delvec`'s lock; `glam 0.34` beside the engine's `0.27` (two versions, no conflict); `fastnbt`, `flate2`, `rayon`, `serde_json` shared; `zip 9.0.3` with default features, which pulls `aes`, `bzip2` (`libbz2-rs-sys`, Rust), `deflate64`, `lzma`, `zstd` (`zstd-sys`, a C build) — the class `crates/delvec/Cargo.toml` narrowed its own `zip` away from for the cross-check shelf gate; `ureq 3.4` with its TLS stack, used only by the downloader.
11. **The cost of a CLI frame on the gallery, measured for this spec.** Instrument: the `lumenwright` release binary built from the local checkout at `f337b180b1d3a2b9d8d0e5216365bf19bc34a01f` (one commit behind `2b4a0b35`, which adds the block-entity models and moves nothing in the quick path), `delvec 1.13.0` built from this branch at `8eaf4df3` (dev profile), the gallery built from `prefabs/gallery-generator`'s output and its load world written by `delvec written-world` (15 chunks, 12,464 cells, sha256 `4b0a29b5…`, no light arrays, no entities, one blank sign), every one of the plan's **172 shots** (148 pov, 10 gate, 5 interior, 4 npc, 3 seam, 1 spawn, 1 interact) rendered one process each at 1024 × 1024 from the shot's `pos` and `look_at` with `--render-distance 20` and the pinned client jar, timed by `/usr/bin/time -l` over the whole loop. **Quick, Metal: 172 frames in 27.75 s wall, 56.9 MB peak resident, per-frame total 0.107 / 0.143 / 0.305 s (min / median / max)**, of which about 0.12 s a frame is loading the jar's assets, paid once per run inside one process. **Quick, CPU: 23.49 s wall, 45.7 MB peak, 0.089 / 0.118 / 0.247 s.** **HQ, one interior camera (`interior/hall/0`), 1024 × 1024, 256 samples: Metal 1.60 s total (1.39 s tracing), 94.1 MB; CPU 20.0 s (103 s of CPU time), 49.8 MB.** Every frame opened as a picture of the hall (its lanterns, doorways and bench), lit at the uniform sky light a world with no light arrays carries.

### 2.3 What the configuration model holds, and does not

**Cited** — `nav/route/mod.rs`, `nav/world/mod.rs`, `view/beat.rs` at `a845fa11`. A configuration is a region state: every forced `RegionEvent` whose `fire_step` is a causal ancestor of the arrival, latest write per region, with the world-load seals entered as step-0 fills. `Configuration::blocks(base)` lays it over a block map; `path_configurations` keys the critical path's distinct states by first arrival and maps every step to one; the render plan writes `after: {step, cells_moved}` on every POV shot from it (spec-0089 §8). The model holds no block entity, no entity, no activation placement and no clock: `reachable_clocks` collects every `set-time`/`set-weather` the campaign can reach as one unordered set, and nothing says which clock a given step has set. The nav `World` a configuration is laid over has every gate threshold cleared, and `render_plan` proves its cameras against that base and not against any configuration.

### 2.4 How a frame is judged today

**Cited** — `snapshot.rs`, `render_plan.rs`, `nav/cutscene.rs`. `delvec snapshot` is the one place the tree reads a frame: `collect_targets` lists every anchor, gate region, NPC post, actor post, interact marker, stealth zone and trigger of the plan; `resolve_targets` projects each through the camera, keeps those inside the frustum with a `screen_bbox` and a `distance`, and asks `VoxelGrid::occluded_target` nine sight lines against the grid with the target's own cells excluded; the rest are `out_of_frame`. spec-0110 lifts the nine-line rule into `compiler::framing::sight`. `render_plan` judges one thing of every camera, that its eye cell is clear (`DW0724`), at the base world; `check_cutscenes` judges a dolly's clearance (`DW0308`) and pan (`DW0347`) at the base world. Nothing joins a frame's camera to the snapshot's targets. `tools/ci/check-gallery-render.py` drafts every committed view through `snapshot` and reads its manifest for a non-zero binding, and states that **no pixel is committed or compared**, because renderer output is not covered by ADR-0006 across drivers; `render/gpu.rs` records the same for the GPU arms (*pixel-equal within tolerance, not byte-identical — GPU float rasterization; renders are validation artifacts, excluded from ADR-0006 byte-identity*).

### 2.5 The engine's light model

**Cited** — `compiler/light.rs`. `LightModel` builds a light-voxel field over the assembled world — per-cell opacity (`passes_light`) and a block-light emitter table at 1.21.11 values (`emission`) — and `flood(effective_sky)` seeds block light at every emitter and sky light at every sky-open light-passing cell, propagates both −1 per step through light-passing cells in a fixed frontier order, and yields a `BTreeMap<[i32;3], u8>` of the max of the two; `DW0210` reads it for the reachable walkable cells, `Relight` places declared fixtures against it, `delvec prefab lighting` measures a piece with it. The vanilla rule it follows (**cited**: minecraft.wiki, [*Light*](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Light)): block light drops by one per block from its source; sky light is 15 at a cell exposed to the sky and drops by one per block in every direction other than straight down from a 15, where it keeps its level. The field holds sky and block light as one maximum; the two arrays a chunk stores are the two terms before the maximum (`LightField::compute_sky` and the block flood are already computed separately).

### 2.6 What the two gates that package the engine require

**Cited** — `tools/ci/check-publishable.sh`, `tools/ci/build-release-binaries.sh`, ADR-0017, ADR-0026. `check-publishable.sh` packages `delvewright-dsl` and `delvec` and rebuilds `delvec` from the tarballs alone outside the workspace, so every dependency of `delvec` must resolve from a registry; `cargo package` refuses a `git` dependency that names no version and strips the `git` source from one that does, so the verify build then asks crates.io for that version. It is a required check (`.github/required-status-checks.txt`). `build-release-binaries.sh --check-only` runs `cargo check --target` for the five shelf targets from one host, so a dependency whose build script compiles C for the target reds it; that is why `delvec` declares `blake3`'s `pure` feature and `zip` without defaults. The release publishes to crates.io first and undrafts the GitHub release second, under one approval (ADR-0026 §3); a crates.io version is permanent (ADR-0017).

### 2.7 What the architecture record allows

**Cited** — ADR-0023 §3: the engine is one binary and no cargo feature gates any subcommand, so the surface is unconditional code and the shelf gate proves per target that `--help` lists the clap surface parsed from source; a subcommand is named after the object it acts on. §5: a renderer comes from the registry by exact version, with the feature that turns its C build paths off declared in `delvec`'s own manifest; its revisit trigger admits a git pin inside the workspace *as an ordinary reviewed edit* when the registry's cadence stops carrying the API the engine needs. §7: a tool that cannot be bundled is acquired at the step that needs it, Chunky named as the type specimen. §9: the client jar is a creator prerequisite the toolchain never downloads without saying so. ADR-0025: two crates are published and a feature never adds a crate to the workspace; a third-party dependency is not a workspace crate. ADR-0006: same input, byte-identical datapack and world; `check-gallery-render.py` and `render/gpu.rs` record that rendered pixels across drivers were never inside that invariant (§2.4). ADR-0021 §5 names the client-jar pin and fetch-once cache *in the exact shape of `validation/server-bootstrap-cache.sh`*, and ADR-0023's consequences list it as unimplemented; the sha256 already exists as `versions.toml [render] textures_sha256` and the `client-jar` pin. **ADR-0023 holds Lumenwright as it is**: a registry dependency at release, a git pin by revision in development, the surface unconditional in `delvec`, the jar handed in by the engine's own resolution. No ADR is superseded; §7's example of a lazy-loaded external changes from Chunky to `delve_skin`'s Python environment, which the same section already names.

## 3. The state a review frame claims

**Authored**, on §1–§2. A review frame is a picture of one **configuration**: a block map, the objects the datapack has placed by then, the light they make, and a clock.

1. **A POV frame stands in its leg's configuration** — the one `path_configurations` names on the shot (`after`): the state the leg is walked under, every forced write credited and every object a step before it placed and no later step removed.
2. **A cutscene frame stands in the configuration of the step that fires the cutscene** — the arrival at the step after the firing step, as a showcase camera `after` that step does (spec-0089 §4.1).
3. **Every other kind (spawn, interior, seam, npc, interact, gate) stands at load.**
4. **The bytes are the engine's, never a server's.** The configuration's world is written by `view::world::write` from the model the proofs read, for spec-0089's reason: a frame that disagrees with a proof shows a defect of the model instead of hiding it under the server's physics, and two writes of one build are byte-identical. A world saved from a server driven to the step is the cross-check (§8.2), never the source.
5. **The clock is the configuration's.** The latest forced `set-time` and `set-weather` whose firing is a causal ancestor of the arrival, else the world's own (§7.6).

## 4. The placements ledger

**Authored**, on §2.1. `compiler::placed` holds **every placement the datapack makes outside the templates, and every removal**, as one record each, and the emitter writes its command from the record:

```
Placed { fired: Firing, object: PlacedObject }
Firing      = Setup | Step(usize) | Unforced(blame)
PlacedObject = Block { cell, state }
             | BlockEntity { cell, state, nbt }        // a sign with its text, a chest with its loot
             | Entity { kind, pos, yaw, tags, nbt }    // a body, a hitbox, a marker, an assembly part
             | Removed { tags }
```

1. **One producer.** `Placed::command(&self) -> String` is the only way a `setblock`, `fill`, `summon`, `item replace block` or `kill @e[tag=…]` line enters `setup_finish`, `activate_<obj>`, `spawn_npc_<id>`, `spawn_actor_<id>`, `asm_summon_<s>` and the completion despawns. The region writes stay `RegionEvent`: a `Placed::Block` is a single cell, a `RegionEvent` is a box, and the writer lays both. `light::Placement` and `pressable::trigger_props` become readers of the ledger's `Setup` rows.
2. **The firing is the region model's.** `Firing::Step(i)` is the `critical_path` index of the step whose completion, activation or performed trigger runs the command; `Unforced(blame)` for a beat nobody has to play, which no configuration lays. The ordering is the one function spec-0110 §5.6 lifts out of `dsl::npc`; this spec reads it.
3. **What a configuration holds of the ledger**: every row whose `Firing` is `Setup` or a causal ancestor of the arrival, applied in firing order, a `Removed` deleting every earlier `Entity` carrying one of its tags. `Configuration::placed(&ledger) -> Vec<&Placed>` is that function, beside `Configuration::blocks`.
4. **`nbt` is the summon's whole compound**, the one the command carries — a mannequin's `profile`, a `block_display`'s `block_state` and `transformation`, an `item_display`'s `item`, a `text_display`'s `text`, a villager's `VillagerData` — so the written body is the body the server would hold and the renderer draws what it can of it (§2.2 items 5–6).
5. **The audit is structural and stated.** `crates/delvec/tests/placed_ledger.rs` reads every emitted function of a fixture build the way `affordance.rs` reads summons, and holds every placing line to a ledger row and every row to a line, both counts printed.

## 5. The worlds the build writes

**Authored**, on spec-0089 §5 (**cited**), §2.2 and §2.5.

1. **The build writes them.** `delvec build` writes one world per distinct configuration its frames stand in under `<out>/validation/worlds/<key>/` — `at-load`, or `after-<step>` with `/` as `-` (`view::beat::after_key`) — through `view::world::write`, the one writer, and records each file in `manifest.json`. `validation/Dockerfile.delve` copies nothing under `validation/`, so no written world ships. Two builds of one tree write byte-identical worlds (ADR-0006). `delvec written-world` keeps writing the load world alone for the staging gate; its bytes equal `validation/worlds/at-load`'s, asserted.
2. **The blocks** are `Configuration::blocks` over `picture_base`, then the configuration's `Placed::Block` and `Placed::BlockEntity` cells laid in firing order, every laid cell marked in `Reached`.
3. **The ground the generator would make.** Every chunk the world writes carries, under and around the layout, the layers the shipped level generator pins for the horizon (spec-0013: the superflat bedrock, stone and, on an `ocean` horizon, water to `SEA_LEVEL`), so a coastal place stands in its sea and a frame needs no renderer-side water plane (the Chunky `water_world` and the snapshot's `SEA_PLANE_NOTE` both go). The loaded extent is `scene::loaded_extent`'s — the layout, the valley's landform and the plan volumes — and the writer fills it whole, so a frame loads every chunk the build writes and no block a `world-edits` write puts outside a placed box's chunk is left out of the picture, which closes the open engine issue of a render omitting blocks world edits write outside the placed boxes' chunks.
4. **The block entities.** Every chunk carries `block_entities`: the templates' own (`nbtread::Block::nbt`, with `x`, `y`, `z` and `id` written as the game writes them) wherever the cell still holds the template's block, and the ledger's `BlockEntity` compounds. `block_entities_omitted` counts the block-entity-pictured cells whose compound the world **does not** hold.
5. **The entities.** `entities/r.<x>.<z>.mca` holds every `Placed::Entity` the configuration holds, one compound per body — the ledger's `nbt` with `id`, `Pos`, `Rotation` (`[yaw, 0]`), `Tags` and a `UUID` derived from the tag list and position so two writes agree.
6. **The light.** Every section carries `BlockLight` and `SkyLight`, the two terms of `LightModel::flood` (§2.5) over the configuration's blocks — block light from the emitters the configuration holds (a lit lantern a beat places lights the frame on its leg), sky light seeded at 15 — packed as the game packs them (4 bits a cell, `y`-`z`-`x` order). Quick mode then draws the lightmap the client draws from the same two numbers, which is what the player sees; a world with no light arrays is lit at full sky everywhere (§2.2 item 7) and is not a picture of the room. The server computes light on load; §8.3 holds the engine's arrays to the server's.
7. **The biomes** stay the build's biome map at load, as spec-0089 §6.3 left them.
8. **`level.dat`** stays spec-0089's minimal compound: `Data{DataVersion, LevelName, SpawnX, SpawnY, SpawnZ, version, DayTime}`, with `DayTime` the configuration's clock (§7.6) so a world opened by any reader reports the hour the frame is of.

## 6. The renderer

### 6.1 The integration shape

**Authored**, on §2.2 and §2.7.

1. **Lumenwright is a library dependency of `delvec`, and every frame is drawn in the `delvec` process.** No scene file is emitted, no second program is launched, no renderer home is resolved. `crates/delvec/Cargo.toml` declares `lumenwright` with `default-features = false, features = ["gpu"]`; the surface it backs is unconditional code in `delvec`, so ADR-0023 §3 holds: every build of `delvec` carries the same renderer with the same backends, and the shelf gate's `--help` comparison sees one surface.
2. **The subcommand is named after the object: `delvec frames`.** `delvec frames <build-dir> [-o <dir>] [--shot <id>]… [--backend auto|cpu|metal|vulkan|dx12] [--labels] [--size <px>]` renders every shot of the build's `render-plan.json` (or the named ones) from the build's written worlds into `<out>/<shot-stem>.png`, writes `shot-index.json` beside them (`delvec index` folds in: one producer of the index, the renderer), and prints the binding lines of §9. `delvec frames --campaign <dir> (--camera x,y,z,yaw,pitch[,fov] | --at <anchor> [--orbit <deg>] [--dist <n>]) [-o <png>]` is the authoring loop's viewport (spec-0015: placement and assembly, no emission): it writes the load world of the assembled campaign in-process under the output's directory and renders one quick frame of it, with the frame manifest beside it. `delvec scene`, `delvec index`, `delvec snapshot` and `validation/render-shots.sh` go (§10). `delvec panorama` and `delvec cameras` keep their names and render their frames the same way; `delvec cameras --preview` is a quick frame and `--draft` an hq frame at `DRAFT_SPP` over `DRAFT_DIVISOR`; `delvec edit` renders its per-batch frame through the same function. Every camera is handed to Lumenwright as `pos`, `yaw`, `pitch` in Minecraft's own convention and the vertical `fov`; the plan's `yaw = atan2(-dz, dx)` convention goes with the Chunky emitter, and `render-plan.json` writes Minecraft yaw and pitch, so the plan, the frame, the snapshot's `--camera` and `/tp` speak one convention.
3. **The backends.** `--backend auto` (the default) takes the GPU wgpu finds — Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Linux, DX12 or Vulkan on Windows — and falls back to the CPU backend, **printing the fallback and the backend taken on the first line of every run**. A named backend that is not available is refused by name (`DW0723`). `cuda` is not mounted: it has never executed on any hardware, its PTX is a build-time artifact of a toolchain the shelf does not carry, and Vulkan serves an NVIDIA GPU in both modes; it enters when Lumenwright's backends have executed on real hardware and the PTX ships in the crate (§13), as an edit to the backend list. **On a machine with no GPU the whole surface runs on the CPU**: quick frames at the CPU cost of §2.2 item 11 (0.12 s a frame on the gallery, 0.29 s on a 33 × 33-chunk save at 1920 × 1080), hq frames at about 6× the GPU's time (20 s against 1.6 s for the gallery hall at 1024 × 1024 and 256 samples). CI runs the CPU backend.
4. **The mode is the engine's decision, per frame, from what the frame asks; no flag chooses it.** Every shot of `render-plan.json` carries `mode: quick | hq`, derived: `quick` for every review kind — `spawn`, `interior`, `seam`, `npc`, `interact`, `gate`, `pov`, `cutscene` — because a review frame's question is what the player sees, and quick mode draws the lightmap the client draws from the light the world carries (§5.6); `hq` for the showcase cameras and the panorama, whose question is the picture. A review frame whose question is **light or atmosphere** — its area declares `lighting` or carries a `dark` lighting stamp, or its `sky.set_by` is a step, or its weather is not clear — is marked `hq` by the same derivation **from phase 2** (§16), when Lumenwright's emitter next-event estimation and hq exposure (§13) make a lamp-lit hq frame readable inside the budget; until then those shots carry `mode: quick` and the binding line counts them as `light frames rendered quick: <n>`. The hq review budget is 256 samples at the review size, the showcase budget the camera's own `spp`.
5. **What the engine hands Lumenwright, and what it never asks of it.** The world directory and the exact chunk rectangle of `scene::loaded_extent` (never a radius around the camera); the client jar's path, resolved by `delvec`'s one ladder (`--textures`, `$DELVEWRIGHT_CLIENT_JAR`, the creator's jar at the location Init I5 writes it) and refused by name when absent (`DW0723`) — **Lumenwright's own jar resolution and downloader are never reached from `delvec`** (ADR-0023 §9); the build's `resourcepack.zip` when the build has one (spec-0084, spec-0009), read from the build directory and never from a flag; the camera; the mode, backend, size and budget; `unknown_entities: skip`, so no body is drawn as a box; the sky (item 6). It reads back the pixels, the per-kind entity and block-entity statistics (item 7) and the timings.
6. **One authority for the game's sky.** The engine computes the sky from `scene::sky_cell(daytime_ticks, weather)` — the client's lightmap sky colour, fog colour, day factor and weather darkening read off the pinned client (`compiler.md` §4, spec-0079) — and `scene::sun_at` the sun direction from the pinned day timeline, and hands them to Lumenwright as the caller-stated `QuickSky` colours and `Lighting { sky_factor, gamma }` of quick mode and the `sun_dir`, `sun_color`, `sky_zenith`, `sky_horizon` of hq mode; Lumenwright's own derivation of the vanilla sky from the tick (`sky::celestial_angle`, `biome::sky_color`) is its CLI's default when a caller states none and is never what a `delvec` frame shows. Rain and thunder reach quick mode as the client's darkened sky and fog colours and reach hq mode as an overcast sky block with the sun's colour at zero; precipitation, clouds and fog in hq mode are not drawn until Lumenwright's atmosphere work (§13), and every rain frame's index entry says so under `unchecked`.
7. **What is drawn is what the renderer reports, never a table.** `undrawn` (§7.5) is computed from Lumenwright's `EntityStats` and `BlockEntityStats` of the frame's own run: a kind in `skipped` or `fallback_boxes` is undrawn for every body of that kind in frame, a kind in `rendered` is drawn. From phase 2 the object-id buffer and per-frame manifest (§13) replace the kind-level reading with a per-body one and give each body its visible pixel count.

### 6.2 The dependency, in development and at release

**Authored**, on §2.6–§2.7.

1. **In development `delvec` depends on Lumenwright by git revision**: `lumenwright = { git = "https://github.com/stellarfeline/lumenwright", rev = "<40 hex>", default-features = false, features = ["gpu"] }`, the revision recorded once in `versions.toml [render] lumenwright_revision` and `.github/pins.toml` (`lumenwright-revision`, policy *moved by a reviewed edit*), and `validation/check-versions.sh` holds the manifest's `rev` equal to the pin. The alternative, a `path` dependency through the `lumenwright` symlink at the repository root, builds whatever that checkout holds — a dirty tree, a branch, a revision nobody named — and errors on nothing; a revision that does not exist refuses to resolve, and code written against another revision's API refuses to compile. The symlink stays a developer's convenience for reading and is named by no manifest.
2. **At release `delvec` depends on Lumenwright by exact registry version**, `lumenwright = "=0.x.y"`, as ADR-0023 §5 has Nucleation, with `versions.toml [render] lumenwright_version` and `lumenwright_license = "GPL-3.0-or-later"` beside it. **A crates.io release of `delvec` needs `lumenwright` and `lumenwright-kernel` on crates.io first** (the kernel is a path dependency of the library and `cargo package` refuses it unpublished). This is a release-time obligation: `tools/ci/check-publishable.sh` reds on the git dependency, by construction and correctly — `cargo install delvec` cannot work until the registry holds the renderer — so **the implementing branch cannot enter `main` under a git dependency while that gate is required**, and the implementing branch's first merge is therefore after the last two items of §13: Lumenwright's documentation brought to release quality, then its first publish. The irreversible effect, named: a crates.io version of `lumenwright` and of `lumenwright-kernel` is permanent, the names are claimed for good, and every later change the engine needs is a new published version (ADR-0017). Nothing here waits on the engine release: the gate's premise is `cargo install`, so the alternative of landing the feature first is not a merge order the planner can choose but a loosening of a required check, which this spec does not make.
3. **The shelf gate must stay green on the five targets**, so Lumenwright narrows its dependency features for an embedding crate (the narrowed dependency set, §13) before the pin that `delvec` takes: `zip` without defaults, the downloader and its TLS stack behind Lumenwright's `download` feature that `delvec` does not enable. `cargo tree -e normal -p delvec | grep -- -sys` names only the platform bindings (`windows-sys`, `wayland-sys`, `ndk-sys`, `js-sys`, `web-sys`) after the pin.

### 6.3 Determinism, and what a frame's canonical hash covers

**Authored**, on §2.2 item 8, §2.4 and ADR-0006 (**cited**).

1. **Byte-identical where the invariant reaches.** The written worlds, `render-plan.json`, `shot-index.json`, `validation/review-frames.json` and the frame manifests are byte-identical across two builds of one tree, as every output is. The CPU backend's pixels are byte-identical across two runs on one machine and across thread counts (a per-pixel sample stream keyed by pixel and sample index), asserted by criterion 9 with `--threads 1` against `--threads 10`.
2. **Across backends and GPUs the pixels are not promised byte-identical, and this spec says so rather than measuring it once and hoping.** Lumenwright measures bit-identity between CPU and Metal on one machine and parity bounds beside it (§2.2 item 8); a second GPU vendor's shader compiler may contract or reorder floating-point work the WGSL leaves open, which is the reason `check-gallery-render.py` and `render/gpu.rs` already keep rendered pixels outside ADR-0006. The rule stays: **no pixel is committed or compared by a gate**; a frame is for eyes.
3. **What the canonical hash covers.** `review-frames.json`'s `shots_digest` is the hash of the shot list with each shot's `world` key, world sha256, camera, mode, sky and `shows`/`undrawn` — everything the engine derives — and never the PNG bytes. Per frame the manifest carries the backend and Lumenwright's revision that drew it, so a frame's provenance is readable without the frame. From phase 2 the object-id buffer (§13) is integer data the kernel computes identically on every backend and enters the per-frame hash; the colour buffer never does.

## 7. The frames

### 7.1 POV frames

**Authored.** Every `pov` shot keeps its camera and its `after`, gains `world: <key>` — `at-load` where `after.cells_moved` is 0 and the configuration holds no placement, else `after-<step>` — `sky` (§7.6) and `mode` (§6.1 item 4). `review_frame_limit`'s line is replaced by the binding line of §9: there is no longer a limit to state.

### 7.2 Cutscene frames

**Authored**, on §2.4 and spec-0110 §2 (**cited**: the lens is the camera entity's position; the client's default vertical field of view is 70°; the proof reads the keyframes `ExpandedShot::frames` emits and the client's linear tween between them).

1. **Which cutscenes.** Every cutscene a critical-path step fires — the bundles `hold::completion_bundles` names for the step, at any depth of their `sequence` steps, and a performed trigger's effects — in path order, then list order. A cutscene no critical step fires gets no frame and is counted by name on the binding line.
2. **Which ticks.** Per shot, the pose at tick 0 and, for a shot whose camera or aim moves, the pose at its last tick: the two ends of what `DW0308` proves between.
3. **The camera** is the keyframe pose at that tick (`Frame`, three decimals) at `fov` 70, never moved: an eye inside a block is a build error against the shot (§7.4).
4. **The id**: `cutscene/<step>/<n>/shot<i>/t<tick>`; the kind is `cutscene`; the shot carries `step`, `after`, `world`, `sky`, `mode`, the shot's `seconds`, `subject` where spec-0110 is in the tree, and an `expect` list composed as a POV shot's is.
5. **The world** is the configuration arriving at the step after the firing step, with the ledger rows that step's own completion places credited.
6. **`delvec frames --shot cutscene/…`** renders the shot alone, as it renders a `pov/…` id, so the gallery's render gate drafts every declared cutscene view.

### 7.3 The other kinds

**Authored.** `spawn`, `interior`, `seam`, `npc`, `interact` and `gate` shots stand at load (`world: at-load`) and carry `world`, `sky` and `mode`, so every shot of the plan states its world, its clock and its mode and nothing is left to a default.

### 7.4 A camera is clear in the frame's own world

**Authored**, on §1 item 3. `render_plan` asks `verify_camera_eyes` of every shot **against the configuration's world** — `Configuration::world(base)` with the ledger's blocks of that configuration laid, one `World` per distinct key — and `Shots::push` stands a movable kind's eye up (`stand_in_open_air`) against that same world. `check_cutscenes` ray-marches each shot's polyline and chords through the firing step's configuration. A `pov` or `cutscene` eye inside a block in its own world is `DW0724` naming the shot, the cell, the world key and the block there. `camera_eye_proof` states how many eyes were proven in how many worlds.

### 7.5 What a frame shows, and what the renderer did not draw — one sight rule

**Authored**, on §2.4 and §6.1 item 7. Every shot's index entry (`shot-index.json`) gains:

- **`shows`**: every target of `collect_targets`'s kinds and every `Placed::Entity` of the frame's configuration inside the frustum, each with `kind`, `id`, `cell`, `distance`, `screen_bbox`, `occluded`, and for a body `facing` — computed by `resolve_targets` over a `VoxelGrid` built from the frame's own world map, the nine-line rule spec-0110 moves into `framing::sight`. The grid, the camera projection, the targets and the manifest move from `compiler::snapshot` to `compiler::framing` with the snapshot's raster left behind (§10); one definition, and `crates/delvec/tests/review_frame_shows.rs` holds the index's `occluded` equal to the frame manifest's, target for target, on the gallery.
- **`undrawn`**: the subset of `shows` whose body the renderer's own statistics for that frame put in `skipped` or `fallback_boxes` (§6.1 item 7), each with `reason` — what the renderer does not draw yet, in words (`items`, `text displays`; §13) — and `judge_by`: `frames --labels` (the post is stamped on the frame) or `in game`. This is `DW1039` (§8.1), a report per frame.
- **`out_of_frame`** for the targets the manifest lists so.
- **`unchecked`**: the frame's own list from §11 — the weather under rain, a night-vision room's lightmap until the night-vision lightmap lands (§13), a body's pose at a tick.

A frame whose `shows` holds a body the renderer did not draw says so before the picture; `shows` is a measurement of the frame, never a refusal.

### 7.6 The clock

**Authored**, on §2.3. `collect_region_events`'s walk also collects every `set-time` and `set-weather` as a `ClockEvent { fire_step, forced, time | weather }`; a configuration's clock is the latest forced `ClockEvent` of each kind whose `fire_step` is a causal ancestor of the arrival, else the world's declared `time` and `weather`. Every shot states it as `sky: {time, daytime_ticks, weather, set_by}`, and `delvec frames` builds the frame's sky from the shot's `sky` through `sky_cell` and `sun_at` (§6.1 item 6), the one function path; the plan's top-level `sky` stays the declared initial hour for the panorama. The clocks a configuration can hold are a subset of `reachable_clocks`' set, asserted. A night frame is dark because the sun is below the horizon and the lightmap's day factor is the night's (the open engine issue of a clear night rendering as a bright dusk closes on criterion 6's midnight frame, never by a renderer-side setting).

## 8. The codes

### 8.1 `DW1039` — a frame posts a body the renderer did not draw (report tier, `compiler::view::index`)

A review frame whose `shows` holds a body the renderer's own statistics for that frame put in `skipped` or `fallback_boxes`. Printed once per frame by `delvec frames` — the shot, the body's id, kind, cell, facing, distance, the reason and `judge_by` — and written into the shot index as `undrawn`; the set's binding line counts them. Never a refusal: the frame is right and the body is real; what the report protects is a reviewer reading empty floor as an absent keeper, or a bare wall as a missing sign.

### 8.2 `DW1040` — the objects the engine writes are not the objects the pinned server places (build tier, `compiler::view::world`)

`tools/ci/check-written-world.py` gains, beside its cell comparison, a comparison of the **placed objects** of the written load world against the `world-save.sh` save of the same build, read through `tools/lib/anvil.py` (which gains the `block_entities` and `entities/` readers): every block entity by cell and compound, every entity by kind, cell and tag set. A difference in no named class reds `DW1040` naming the object; the classes measured and counted separately, never allowlisted: the server's own bookkeeping keys (`UUID`, `Motion`, `Fire`, `Air`, `OnGround`, `PortalCooldown`, `Health`, `Attributes`, `Brain`, `Rotation`'s second component), a container's loot the server re-rolled from a `LootTable`, and a stand-in or a wave body present only once a player has played. `--record` writes the verdicts under the build's manifest sha256, and `staging-gate.py --written-world` admits on none alone. The cells stay `DW0955`.

### 8.3 `DW-next` — the light the engine writes is not the light the pinned server computes (build tier, `compiler::view::world`)

The same tool's third comparison: per cell inside the loaded extent, the written `SkyLight` and `BlockLight` against the server save's, printed as a distribution of the absolute difference per array (`|Δ| = 0, 1, 2, ≥3` with the denominator) — a light diagnostic reports a distribution, never one worst cell. A cell differing by more than 1 in either array reds `DW-next` naming the cell, the two values and the nearest emitter or sky opening, except in the classes it names and counts: a cell under a block the server flowed or settled (the `DW0955` classes, whose light moves with them), and a cell the server's light engine has not yet propagated to at the moment of the save (counted as `unlit-by-server` and bounded by criterion 11). A difference of exactly 1 is reported in the distribution and reds nothing: the vanilla rule has a −1 the field's seeding can place one cell off under an overhang (§2.5), and a lightmap level one apart is one step of sixteen. Allocated by the planner; phase 2.

### 8.4 Widenings

- **`DW0724`** judges every camera in the configuration's world instead of the base world (§7.4), and `cutscene` eyes beside the seven kinds. It can only turn a plan red that was green.
- **`DW0308`** ray-marches a dolly through the firing step's configuration instead of the base world. The same direction.
- **`DW0955`** compares the cells of a written load world that carries the ledger's setup blocks laid from the ledger and the horizon's ground layers (§5.3); its classes and remedy are unchanged, and the ground layers are compared where the save holds them.

## 9. The record: the plan, the index, the review set, the binding lines

**Authored.**

- **`render-plan.json`**: every shot carries `world`, `sky` and `mode`, and its camera in Minecraft yaw and pitch; `pov` shots keep `after`; `cutscene` shots are appended after `pov`; `camera_eye_proof` gains `worlds`. The committed `gallery/render-plan.json` view set gains the gallery's cutscene views.
- **`shot-index.json`**, written by `delvec frames` beside the frames: every entry carries `world`, `sky`, `mode`, `backend`, `renderer` (Lumenwright's revision or version), `shows`, `undrawn`, `out_of_frame` and `unchecked`; a `cutscene` entry carries `step`, `shot`, `tick` and `seconds`.
- **`validation/review-frames.json`**, written by the build: per world key its chunk count, cell count, block-entity count, entity count, the light arrays' cell count and sha256; per shot its key, mode and the counts of `shows` and `undrawn`; the cutscenes framed and the cutscenes no critical step fires, by name; `shots_digest` (§6.3); `unchecked` naming §11's items.
- **Binding lines.** `delvec build` with a render plan: `review frames: <m> shot(s) in <k> world(s) (<l> at load, <a> after a step); <q> quick, <h> hq, <n> light frame(s) rendered quick; <c> cutscene frame(s) of <s> shot(s) in <n> cutscene(s), <u> cutscene(s) no critical step fires: <names>; cameras proven clear: <m> of <m> in their own worlds` and per world `world: <key> <chunks> chunk(s), <cells> cell(s), <g> ground cell(s), <b> block entit(ies), <e> entit(ies), light arrays over <chunks> chunk(s), sha256 <hash> -> <dir>, written in <ms>`. `delvec frames`: `renderer: lumenwright <rev|version>, backend <name> (<auto: fell back to cpu | named>)` on its first line, then `frames: <m> of <m> shot(s) rendered over <k> world(s) in <s> s, peak <MiB> MiB; shows: <t> object(s) in frame over <m> frame(s), <u> not drawn by the renderer (DW1039) in <f> frame(s); skies: <j> at the world's clock, <q> set by a step`. A campaign with no cutscene prints `0 cutscene frame(s)`; a campaign whose bodies are all out of frame prints `0 not drawn`: measured zeroes.
- **`validation/render-shots.sh` goes**; `delvec frames` is the ladder step, and it refuses a plan whose shot names a world key the build directory does not hold, naming the key and the remedy, a rebuild. `--world` goes from `delvec panorama` and `delvec cameras`: a frame has one world source. `world-save.sh` keeps its one remaining caller, the cross-check (§8.2–§8.3); `check-whole-map-render.py` stops writing a stub world and reads the panorama's framing from the frame manifest it already reads the camera from.

## 11. What a frame does not show, by name

**Authored**, on §2.2 and spec-0089 §6 (**cited**). Each is in `review-frames.json`'s `unchecked` and on the index entry where it applies, with the Lumenwright issue that closes it where one exists.

1. **An item, a held item, an item display, an item frame's content, an armour stand's or mannequin's equipment** — Lumenwright's items work (§13). Said per body (`undrawn`), never drawn as a box.
2. **A text display** — Lumenwright's text-display work (§13).
3. **A bed, a shulker box, the conduit, an open lid, a ringing bell, italic or obfuscated sign text** — the remaining block entities (§13).
4. **The weather in the air, clouds, hq-mode fog, the moon** — Lumenwright's atmosphere work (§13); the sky's colours under rain are drawn (§6.1 item 6), the rain is not.
5. **A night-vision room's lightmap** — the night-vision lightmap (§13): until it lands a `dark` + `night-vision` frame is drawn at the lightmap without the effect and its entry says `judge in game under night vision`.
6. **A body's pose at a tick**, the party and its stand-ins (spec-0095), a collapse's debris and `then_floor`, a `set-atmosphere` repaint, a flood's spread past its laid cells, an unforced write — as spec-0089 §6 and §11 name them.
7. **A cutscene no critical step fires** — counted by name; judged in game.
8. **The rendered pixels of a body the renderer does draw** are confirmed by eye on the demo level (§15) until the object-id buffer's per-body pixel count (§13) enters the index.

## 12. The cost on the creator's machine, and the ceiling

**Authored**, on §1 item 6 and §2.2 items 9 and 11 (**cited** figures).

- **The record with Chunky.** 299 s a frame at the review tier on ten threads, about 380 s a frame once a set is going, a 4 GiB default heap a JVM with a second JVM per render for the denoiser's passes; the Armoury's 24 views and panorama at that rate are **2.0–2.5 hours of the whole machine**, and parallel processes buy wall time, never core-hours (`visual-review.md`, *Parallelism buys wall time*). Three demo levels stopped their review short at that cost (§1 item 6).
- **The measured rate with Lumenwright**, one process a frame, assets reloaded each time: **0.14 s a quick frame on Metal and 0.12 s on the CPU** at 1024 × 1024 on the gallery's 15-chunk world, 172 frames in under 28 s either way at under 60 MB peak resident; one hq frame at 1024 × 1024 and 256 samples 1.6 s on Metal and 20 s on the CPU (§2.2 item 11). A campaign-sized world costs more per frame to load and mesh (0.33 s at 1920 × 1080 for a 33 × 33-chunk save, §2.2 item 9) and the library integration pays the asset load once per run.
- **The ceiling phase 1 must meet, measured on the implementing tree and recorded in `tools.md` §4a with the instrument's revisions**: the whole review set of The Armoury (`demo/the-armoury`, 24 views and the panorama) through `delvec frames` on this machine in **at most 60 s wall and 1 GiB peak resident on the CPU backend, and at most 60 s on the GPU backend**, each quick frame at the review size in at most 2 s; the gallery's 172 shots in at most 120 s on either backend; the panorama and one showcase camera at 1600 × 900 and 256 samples in at most 60 s each on the GPU and 10 minutes on the CPU. A set that misses the ceiling is a red, never a lowered ceiling.
- **The worlds.** Writing a world is in-process and runs once per distinct configuration at build time, not per frame; the writer's cost, with the light flood, is measured by criterion 3 and written on the binding line as `written in <ms>`. The alternative, a server boot per configuration, starts at 92 s before a beat is driven.
- **The proofs.** `DW0724` and `shows` build one `World` and one `VoxelGrid` per distinct world key and ask a cell or nine lines per frame: milliseconds a frame.

## 13. The Lumenwright work, in the order the engine needs it

**Authored.** Each is an issue in `stellarfeline/lumenwright`, named here by what it says — an open one, or one this spec opens — with its acceptance. An issue is closed by a Lumenwright revision the engine then pins; nothing here is implemented in the engine's tree.

**Before the engine's phase 1 can pin a revision**

1. **The library entry point for an embedding engine** (an issue this spec opens). `lumenwright::render(&Request) -> Result<Rendered>`: the request names an opened `World` or a directory, an inclusive chunk rectangle, an explicit jar path and resource packs, the camera, mode, backend, size, budget, the entity policy, and optionally the caller's sky and lighting (`QuickSky` colours, `Lighting`, the hq `sun_dir`/`sun_color`/`sky_zenith`/`sky_horizon`); it performs no network access, resolves no jar, writes no file and prints nothing; it returns the pixels, `EntityStats`, `BlockEntityStats` and the timings. `job::run` becomes a wrapper over it. **Acceptance**: the CLI's PNG and the library's pixels for one fixture are byte-equal; a request with a jar path that does not exist errors by name without touching the network; documented at the crate root.
2. **The narrowed dependency set for an embedding crate's cross-check** (an issue this spec opens). `zip` without default features (`deflate` only); the downloader and its TLS stack behind a `download` feature on by default for the CLI; no C build script reachable with `default-features = false, features = ["gpu"]`. **Acceptance**: `cargo check --target` for `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, `aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu`, `x86_64-apple-darwin`, `aarch64-apple-darwin` and `x86_64-pc-windows-msvc` from a macOS host with no cross C compiler; `cargo tree -e normal` under that feature set names no `*-sys` crate but platform bindings.

**Phase 2 — holders and signs visible, light frames, the per-body manifest**

3. **Items drawn** (open): dropped items, item frame contents, `item_display`, held items, armour-stand and mannequin equipment, through the 1.21.4+ item model pipeline with block items baked as blocks and flat items extruded. **Acceptance** (added): the Armoury's holders — its rack, its frame and the items on them — appear in a quick frame of its written world, and `EntityStats` moves them from `skipped` to `rendered`.
4. **Text displays drawn** (open): text, `line_width`, `background`, `alignment`, `billboard`, `see_through`, `shadow` and the transformation, in both modes, with a fixture entry.
5. **The night-vision lightmap in quick mode** (an issue this spec opens). `Lighting` gains `night_vision: bool`; the lightmap is the client's under the effect (`LightTexture.updateLightTexture`'s night-vision term, read off the pinned client and cited in the issue). **Acceptance**: a fixture room at block light 0 renders legible with the flag and near black without; the flag moves no pixel of a lit cell at light 15.
6. **Hq exposure keeps sunlit surfaces in range** (open): sunlit sand and grass inside the display range at the defaults, measured against quick mode's albedo of the same frame.
7. **Emitter next-event estimation** (open): a candle-lit room at 256 samples within a set rms of a 4,096-sample reference, parity and determinism tests green. With the exposure item this is what lets §6.1 item 4's light frames render hq inside the budget.
8. **Hq geometry outside its own voxel** (open): bells and big dripleaf render the same in quick and hq. A bell is a delve device.
9. **The object-id buffer and the per-frame manifest** (open): per pixel the id of the block cell or entity the primary ray hit, losslessly; per frame every entity inside the frustum with drawn, visible pixel count, screen box and, for anything not drawn, the reason; the id buffer identical across backends for one input. Replaces §6.1 item 7's kind-level reading with a per-body one and enters the per-frame hash (§6.3).
10. **Atmosphere** (open): the client's weather look in quick mode (darker sky and fog colour, rain fog distance), fog and sky presets in hq mode; the caller-stated sky of the library entry point is the way in.

**Phase 3 — showcase quality and the release**

11. **A denoiser** (open) for showcase frames. Under ADR-0023 §3 no cargo feature may gate it in `delvec`: it is either unconditional code or an external library loaded at run time and acquired at the step that needs it (§7), stated in the issue before it is built.
12. **Kernel profiling** (open) (≤ 30 s a 1600 × 900 showcase frame on an M2 Pro); **CUDA, Vulkan and DX12 executed on real hardware** (open), after which `cuda` may enter `delvec`'s backend list together with **the PTX shipped as a committed, hash-bound artifact in the crate** (an issue this spec opens) so the backend builds without the `nvptx64` toolchain; **the stress test on a dense natural world**, **entity layers with a second texture**, **the vanilla fidelity gaps** and **the remaining block entities** (all open).
13. **Documentation at release quality** (an issue this spec opens), each page for one reader, in the present tense of the current version: `README.md` (a user), `docs/SCOPE.md` (a contributor: what is in, what is out, how each decision is held), crate-level and public-API documentation on every `pub` item `cargo doc` renders without warnings, a CLI reference generated from clap, and a CI workflow running `cargo test --workspace`, `clippy -D warnings` and `fmt --check` on every push. **Acceptance**: `cargo doc --no-deps` clean of missing-docs warnings under `#![warn(missing_docs)]`; `cargo publish --dry-run` green for `lumenwright-kernel` and `lumenwright`; the CLI reference lists every flag `--help` lists, checked by a test.
14. **The crates.io publish of `lumenwright-kernel` and `lumenwright`** (an issue this spec opens), after the documentation, by Lumenwright's own CI. The irreversible effect: both names are claimed and every published version is permanent. This is the precondition of §6.2 item 2 and of the first merge of the engine's implementing branch.

## 16. Acceptance criteria

`delvec` is this tree's binary, built from the crate manifests this tree carries. Each criterion is red on the ground revision by the shape named and green on the implementing tree; a criterion the implementation cannot yet satisfy is named as debt in the pull request body, never a pass. **Phase 1 alone removes the bottleneck for demo levels**; phase 2 makes the holders, the signs' bodies and the light frames judgeable; phase 3 is showcase quality.

**Phase 1 — Lumenwright draws every frame, from the configuration's world**

1. **The dependency.** `crates/delvec/Cargo.toml` names `lumenwright` by `git` and `rev` with `default-features = false, features = ["gpu"]`; `versions.toml [render] lumenwright_revision` equals the manifest's `rev` and `validation/check-versions.sh` reds when they differ (a perturbation of one hex digit); `grep -rn chunky` over the tree outside `docs/specs/`, `docs/adr/` and `docs/notes/` finds nothing; `cargo tree -e normal -p delvec | grep -- -sys` names only platform bindings; `tools/ci/build-release-binaries.sh --check-only` is green on the five targets. *Red on the ground: no `lumenwright` in the lock.*
2. **One producer.** `crates/delvec/tests/placed_ledger.rs` builds a fixture with a relight fixture, a trigger prop, an NPC, an `interact` prop, a marked `reach`, a `collect` chest, a `spawn-assembly` and a trapped chest, and holds every placing line to one `Placed` row and every row to one line, both counts printed; the trapped chest's payload is `Unforced`. *Red on the ground: no `compiler::placed`.*
3. **The build writes the worlds, with their light.** `delvec build` on the fixture writes `validation/worlds/<key>/` for every distinct key its shots state, each in `manifest.json`; two builds are byte-identical; `validation/worlds/at-load` equals `delvec written-world`'s output byte for byte; every written section carries `BlockLight` and `SkyLight`, and `tools/lib/anvil.py` reads back the lantern's cell at 15 and a cell under a roof with no emitter at sky 0; the ocean fixture's chunks carry water to `SEA_LEVEL` where the layout does not; the binding line states each world's counts, the light arrays, its hash and `written in <ms>`; `validation/Dockerfile.delve` copies nothing under `validation/`. `crates/delvec/tests/review_frame_worlds.rs`.
4. **Block entities and entities are written and drawn.** The written world of a template with a lettered sign carries the sign's compound; every ledger entity stands in `entities/r.*.mca` with its whole summon compound, `Pos`, `Rotation`, `Tags` and `UUID`; `delvec frames` of a fixture with a mannequin NPC in frame reports the mannequin in `shows` and not in `undrawn`, and the sign's glyphs in the frame's `BlockEntityStats`; the ocean fixture's shore frame shows water (`shows` of the sea cells' palette through the manifest). `review_frame_worlds.rs`, `tests/frames.rs`, `tools/tests/test_anvil_objects.py`.

## 17. Decisions, and what stayed out

- **Lumenwright is the one renderer, linked as a library, for every review frame and showcase render.** The alternatives — Chunky kept for the light frames, or the draft raycaster kept for the authoring loop — each keep a second renderer with its own convention, install, cost and blind spots, and the record shows what the second one cost: three demo levels unreviewed.
- **Quick mode is every review frame's mode; hq mode is the picture's, and the light frames' from phase 2.** The review's question is what the player sees, and the lightmap drawn from the light the world carries is that; a path-traced frame answers how a picture looks. No flag chooses: the plan carries `mode`, derived.
- **The world carries its light, written by the engine's own light model.** The alternative, letting the renderer light an unlit save, draws every room at full sky; the alternative of a server boot per configuration costs 92 s before a beat is driven and yields bytes no two runs share. The server's light is the cross-check (`DW-next`), never the source.
- **The engine states the sky; the renderer draws it.** One reading of the client's sky lives in the engine and is cited; a renderer's own derivation is a second authority for one vanilla fact.
- **A git pin in development, the registry at release, and the publish gate's red is the truth it tells.** The alternatives — a path dependency that errors on nothing, or a gate relaxed to let a git dependency through — are each a loosening; the honest order is Lumenwright's documentation, its publish, then the engine's merge.
- **A body the renderer did not draw is reported per frame, with the issue that will draw it, and never substituted.** A box the size of a hitbox where an item should be is a picture a reviewer would believe.
- **No pixel enters a hash or a gate.** The canonical hash covers what the engine derives and, from phase 2, the renderer's integer id buffer.
- **What stayed out**: `delvec render piece|batch|fidelity-gate` and `delvec viewer`, the prefab-admission pictures, keep Nucleation and deepslate — a prefab piece is not a review frame or a showcase render, and the fidelity gate's magenta scan is renderer-independent; with Lumenwright in the binary, two renderers mesh a block, which is a follow-up spec's question and is named here so it is not discovered later. `cuda` until it has executed somewhere. A frame per cutscene tick. A review of a frame's composition (spec-0110). A branch path's frames and cutscenes. Reading a frame's pixels for a body before the object-id buffer.
