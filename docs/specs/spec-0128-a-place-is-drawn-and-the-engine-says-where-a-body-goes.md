# spec-0128: A place is drawn, and the engine says where a body goes — the starter's model, the grammar it asks, the blocks a place may write, and a space as tall as its blocks

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against `feat/town-merge` @ `00af8b112` (the town branch, spec-0127 on top), read whole — `.claude/skills/delvewright/skills/new-delve/scripts/starter.py` (`Model`, `Plot`, `run`, the block constructors), `scripts/handout.py` (*Step 1*, *The blocks you may write*, *What this plot holds*), `crates/delvec/src/grammar/survey.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/grammar/cli.rs` (`survey`, `blocks`), `crates/delvec/src/grammar/contract.rs` (`contract-well-formed`, `contract-reachability`, `fall_in_pockets`, `walk_joined`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/plot.rs` (`size_band`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/detail.rs` (`bands`); `docs/reference/tools.md` (`grammar survey`, `grammar blocks`, `starter.py`, `handout.py`), `docs/reference/grammar.md` §2d, §4d; spec-0121, spec-0122 §6.3, spec-0125 §5, spec-0127. Evidence: the town (content `demo/town-starter-3`), whose streets and plots were drawn by place agents through this starter; the commit record of `feat/town-starter`, `-2` and `-3` names what each round of agents could not do without.
- **What it is for**: the town's fourteenth requirement, in the owner's words — *each place agent starts fast from a starter template, not by reading the engine*. A place agent is a fresh session with one handout; what it needs to build a place it either finds in the handout and the starter or goes looking for in the engine's source, and the second is the defect. This spec records the surface the town's agents were given so that `/new-delve` on `main` hands the next town's agents the same. Every statement is **[ruling]** (a judgement made at the town), **[cited]** (a reading of the tree) or **[authored]** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0128`. One new diagnostic, `DW1075` (§8: a sign text the sign at its anchor cannot show); the starter otherwise refuses in its own words before anything is written, and what it refuses is what `DW1002`, `DW0730`, `blocks-exist` and `states-complete` would refuse later. The stage-5 `signs` section (§8) is carried by the `dsl_version` the town branch already declares (0.69.0). The grammar document version is unchanged: `part` (1.14.0) is spec-0125's, and a space's levels are a reading of the blocks, not a key.
- **Non-goals**: a second place generator or a copy of the starter in a campaign (§2: the generator imports it); a drawing tool that decides where a body goes (§3: the engine says); a block the starter admits that the palette refuses (§5); a biome, theme or style the starter chooses (the design's, through the handout); a house, barn or shop the starter draws (a shape is the agent's; the starter draws only the empty place).

## 1. The rulings **[ruling]**

1. **An agent starts from a program that already passes.** The first town round's agents began from the handout's reading list and the engine's grammar reference, and most spent their session learning the contract before building anything; the starter (spec-0122 §6.3) gave every place an empty-but-correct program first. The second and third rounds started from it and built.
2. **The building is drawn, not edited in.** Editing the starter's rule tree by hand — splits, calls and claims — was the second round's bottleneck and its defect class (a wall where the claim said air). An agent draws cells and names parts; the program is derived from the drawing.
3. **The engine says where a body goes.** Each agent's private answer to *which floor is walked, which is sealed, where a walk joins two rooms* disagreed with the gates that judged the piece afterwards. The questions the gates answer are asked of the engine at drawing time, through one verb, and the drawing is claimed from the answer.
4. **A wrong block is named at the generator, not at the check.** A lantern hung in air, a potted flower the palette refuses, a misspelt stair state were found by `detail --check` after a full run; the starter refuses them before it writes, naming the block and the cell.
5. **Two street rounds carried the same private kit.** Full-state block constructors, a gable roof, a declared top — each written twice in two generators; they belong to the starter once.
6. **A space is as tall as its blocks give it.** The one-floor rule of `contract-well-formed` refused the castle as one plot (a whole site is one space with its halls, ditches and towers); what the rule guaranteed — a body can get back from wherever it falls — is the reach graph's to judge over the space's real cells.

## 2. The starter as the one implementation **[authored; cited: spec-0122 §6.3, `starter.py`]**

`scripts/starter.py` ships in the plugin; `handout.py` copies it beside every handout with the place's `allocation.json` and `blocks.json`. Run bare (`--allocation <file> --out <program>`) it reads every handed value from the allocation and writes the empty place: the ground to the claim's bottom (one floor course on an aloft place), a wall on every ring cell from the walk plane up except an open side's run, a lid and the roof zone on a roofed place, every seam answered as the region its contract edge goes `via` or the bar it owns, every owed name a `mark`, a plot's parts laid as regions of their declared cells, a sconce every four cells. Every handed value is a `handed/…` parameter with the allocation's value as its default; a number that is not handed is the starter's choice. A side with no ring cell of its own (every walk-plane cell a seam's opening, or outside the frame) and a side on a raised opening's face are not declared.

A place's generator at `<campaign>/design/generator/<stem>.py` **imports** `Model` and `run` from `$DELVEWRIGHT_SKILL/scripts/starter.py` — never a copy — and hands `run` a `build(place) -> Model`. `run` accepts `--delvec` (default: the `delvec` on `PATH`); a drawn model needs a binary on the machine that runs the generator, the empty place needs none.

## 3. The model, and what the engine says of it **[authored; cited: `starter.py` `Model`, `Plot`; `survey.rs`]**

### 3.1 `Model`
Cells in piece-local coordinates (+x east, +z south, the walk plane at the place's datum). A cell holds a palette role or a literal block state with every property written. `set`, `get`, `box`, `clear`, `hollow`; `part(x0, x1, z0, z1, stem)` names columns as `part/<stem>`'s; `mark(stem, x, z, facing)` stands an owed name; `show(*faces)` declares sides of the piece finished exterior beyond the four walls (`"up"` for a roof, a chimney or a lamp on the top course; a side that is not one of the six is refused in words); `door`, `bed`, `tall` stand both halves; `roof(x0, x1, z0, z1, head, along, mat, fill?, overhang?, ridge?, hip?)` lays a gable or hipped roof whose eave course sits level with the wall heads; `finish` writes connecting blocks' states (stair shapes, the arms of fences, walls, panes and bars) from the neighbours' names. A write into a seam's opening is skipped and counted on stderr. The block constructors (`stairs`, `slab`, `pillar`, `door`, `trapdoor`, `fence`, `fence_gate`, `wall`, `pane`, `lantern`, `leaves`, `bed`, `candle`, `torch`, `wall_torch`, `campfire`, `ladder`, `chain`, `barrel`, `button`, `grass_block`, `tall_plant`) each return one full state of the pin.

### 3.2 `delvec grammar survey --file <request.json>`
The request is `{size, blocks: [[x, y, z, state], …], keep?, from?, spaces?, palette?}`; the answer, one JSON object on stdout, is what the piece's own gates would say of the drawing, from the gates' own functions and no second copy: `standable` (`nav::standable_cells`), `reached` (the reach graph's flood from `from`, kept inside `keep`), `sealed` (`contract::passable_component`, the out-of-walk gate's `sealed` kind), `joined` (`contract::walk_joined`, the edge proof's `walk` arm — every pair of `spaces` a walk joins both ways), `unsupported` (every block the pinned `canSurvive` table drops, with what would hold it — `DW1002`'s rule), `refused` (every distinct block `Allowlist::default_building` refuses — `DW0730` — with its route where one exists), `containers` (every block `container_kind` counts) and `malformed` (every written state the pinned registry refuses or that leaves a property out — what `blocks-exist` and `states-complete` refuse in the finished piece). It writes nothing; an unparseable block or a cell outside `size` is exit 2.

### 3.3 `delvec grammar blocks`
Prints `{admitted, containers, refused}` over the pinned registry — air aside, every block admitted or refused, the containers among them — in the repository's canonical JSON form, so a saved copy is what `delvec fmt` writes and what the binary prints at once.

### 3.4 How `run` claims the drawing
`run(build)` asks the survey of the drawing with the place's ways in as `from` and the parts' columns as `spaces`, and refuses before writing — naming block and cell — on any `malformed`, `unsupported` or `refused` block. Then it claims every cell from the answer: a reached cell goes into the part whose column it stands in (the room where no part is drawn); floor no body reaches, and the air sealed over it, is claimed `unwalked` (an out-of-walk region with the stated reason); a `walk` edge joins two parts only where `joined` says a body crosses, carrying `rise` where their floors differ; a walked part none of whose floor is reached is declared seen and never entered and the survey is asked again. A ladder the starter stands to a raised opening is bound in the model's palette (`ladder-<facing>`) and its pillar stays a full block. It prints each part's claimed columns against the band the allocation hands (`part sizes, claimed columns of the band`). Nothing in the starter decides where a body goes.

## 4. The handout **[authored; cited: `handout.py`]**

*Step 1: start from the starter* tells the agent to run the starter, check it green, then write the generator importing the starter and drawing into its `Model`, and that it refuses a dropped, refused or malformed block before writing. *The blocks you may write (A admitted, R refused)* lists every refused block with its route where one exists and every container the census counts, from `blocks.json` saved beside the handout; a `delvec` with no `grammar blocks` is exit 2. *What this plot holds* (spec-0125 §6.1) states per part its band from the allocation's `bands` (`plot::size_band`: three quarters to five quarters of the declared cells, in whole columns). The handout closes with: *nothing outside this handout's reading list is needed, and nothing outside it is read*.

## 5. A space spans as many levels as its blocks give it **[authored; cited: `contract.rs`]**

`contract-well-formed` no longer refuses a space whose standable span exceeds two levels. What the one-floor rule guaranteed is judged by the reach graph over the space's real cells in `contract-reachability`: every standable cell is reached from the way in (unchanged), and **a fall-in pocket** — a reached standable cell of a space spanning more than two levels with no movement sequence back to where a body came into that space (the entry's mouths, or a cell first reached from outside it) — is refused, naming the space, the cells and the remedy (a step, a stair, a lower wall, or close the gap). The binding line reads `fall-in pockets (reach graph): N space(s) spanning more than two levels judged (…), C standable cell(s) reached in them, P pocket(s) a body cannot leave`; a contract whose spaces all span two levels or fewer judges none and says so.

## 7. Rejected **[authored]**

- *A generator that keeps its own nav.* Two generators kept a private "walkable" and disagreed with the gates; the question has one answer and the survey is its one entry point.
- *Copying the starter into the campaign.* A copy fixes the starter at the day it was copied and leaves every later repair with the engine; the generator imports from the plugin.
- *A `--no-survey` mode for a drawn model.* A drawing nobody surveyed is claimed by a guess; the empty place needs no survey and the drawn one needs it.
- *Keeping the one-floor rule with a `levels` escape.* A declaration that a space may be tall says nothing a body can be judged by; the reach graph judges the fall.

## 8. A sign says what the campaign wrote **[ruling; cited; authored]**

**[ruling]** The town's shops hang signs over the street, and a sign a place author can stand but cannot letter is a blank board; where vanilla provides the primitive (a sign block entity's front and back text), the DSL exposes it first-class (CLAUDE.md, *No hacks at any layer*).

**[authored]** The text is bound the way every block payload is (`prefab-procedure.md` §6, spec-0021): a stage-5 `signs[]` entry `{id, anchor, front, back?}` names the anchor the piece declares at the sign's own cell. A capability belongs to the object class it acts on: the sign is furniture the piece places, its words are campaign content that is translated, so the piece declares the cell and the campaign writes the words — no second path beside `loot`. A grammar program names the cell with a `mark`; the starter's `Model.mark(stem, x, z, y=…)` names the block at that cell rather than a floor a body stands on. `front` holds one to four lines and `back` up to four (`DW0100`); every non-empty line is player-facing (`sign.<id>.<side>.<i>`, kind `sign`).

**[cited]** Read from the pinned 1.21.11 client jar: `SignText.LINES` is 4; `SignBlockEntity.getMaxTextLineWidth()` is 90 and `HangingSignBlockEntity`'s is 60; `AbstractSignRenderer` draws each message as `font.split(message, maxWidth).get(0)`, so a wider line is cut. Which board a block draws on is its block entity type (`minecraft:sign`, `minecraft:hanging_sign`).

**[authored]** Emission is one `data merge block` per entry in `setup_finish`, each declared side written whole (vanilla's colour and glow defaults beside its four messages, a missing line an empty component), through the placements ledger so the review worlds hold the text. `DW1075` refuses at build a cell holding no sign, two entries on one sign, and a line wider than its board in the English or any declared translation; a text in a place still standing in its stand-in is deferred to its detail, named on the `sign binding:` line.

## 9. A town's mechanisms are the starter's, and a program states its cut order **[ruling; authored]**

**[ruling]** What one town's agents learned is reproducible in the next town's creation: the know-how lives in the skill and `docs/reference/`, never in a campaign's private helpers, a planner's notes or a worker's report.

**[authored]** `scripts/town.py` beside the starter holds every settlement mechanism the town's generators used — ways, street furniture, a lot's edges, small buildings, boats and quays, a front's seam and sign station, the seen region — each one implementation, frame-relative, parameterised by what its caller states (seed, clear width, axis, edge, roles), nothing read from a node name; `starter.py` re-exports it on first use (`from starter import stretch`). `Model.shift(dx, dz)` stands a drawing moved; `run` reads the cells unshifted. The design — palettes, which trade gets which front, a coast — stays in the campaign. The checklist the town was judged by is `place-craft.md` §10 and the skill's `references/town.md`, named from step 2.

**[authored]** A program is cut where a cell's kind can change, and its split sizes are differences of neighbouring cuts that mix handed bounds with literals. The start rule's one alternative is guarded by the order the cuts were emitted in: in every split the program emits, each handed cut stays strictly between its neighbours, and a handed bound that coincided with another stays level with it. A plan edit that moves a handed value past a literal is refused at rule `place`, naming the bound and the literal; inside the guard no split sizes a box negative. The remedy is to re-run the generator. Rejected: re-sorting the cuts at expansion — the cells between two cuts were read at the emitted order, so a re-sorted split would build boxes the drawing never held.

## 10. What a fresh author met **[ruling; authored]**

**[ruling]** Everything learned building the town is reproducible in future creation: a fresh agent authors a town place from `/new-delve` alone. A fresh agent rebuilt a plot and a street stretch that way; every point at which the skill was not enough is closed here, in the strongest form available.

**[authored]** *The starter does the right thing, or refuses before writing.* The ground course is the ground the whole hands each column (`ground.columns`, which the allocation hands as full states), each block a `ground-<n>` palette role, a ring column from its nearest column inside; the `floor` role only where nothing is handed. A drawn plot whose seam opening has a cell touching no cell of the room its edge enters — nor a cell of another opening that does, `contract-well-formed`'s rule asked of the claims about to be written — is refused before writing, each cell named with what stands behind it (a part's columns, a full block, floor claimed `unwalked`). The allocation hands a way place's `way` (axis, clear width, clear columns piece-local); `stretch`, `lane` and `ramp` take it from `place.way` and refuse a stated value that differs. No default thing a town helper stands is a container the census counts; `verge_life(…, at=[(kind, row, side)])` places what the caller states and returns every placement. `lamp`, `bracket` and the market's awning declare `up` shown when they draw on the frame's top course, and `DW0885`'s remedy names the program surfaces (`Model.show`, a program's `shown_faces`) beside a library piece's metadata. Unreached floor the contract declares out of walk is taken as declared by the gates' reachability finding.

**[authored]** *`detail --check` sees what the commit sees.* The check writes the commit into a scratch copy of the campaign and the library and runs the commit's own battery over the whole it leaves: every refusal the commit can give, the check gives at the commit's exit, by construction — one function, not a list of observers kept in step. This lifts spec-0127 §5's restriction to three proofs. A program not in canonical form is refused by both where it enters (`DW0773`).

**[authored]** *The handout and the CLI say one true thing.* The wave bound counts a place whose row binds the piece `detail` writes from a program that is gone. The walk plane is handed once (`datum_y`; `ground.floor_y` is gone), and the handout states the ground course as one under it. `grammar expand --file` names the piece for the filename up to its first `.`. `render piece --view …,cutaway=<y>` takes every course from piece-local `y` up off; `cutaway=true` takes off the top course that holds a block. `frames --campaign --program <place>` draws a place from its program as it stands, committed or not, its gates passing or not; `--at node/<place>` frames the place's box from outside, never pulled into it.

**[authored]** *Reference research.* Where the community has no build of the trade, the trade's real building from a record of it and community builds of the nearest trade sharing its arrangement; a page that cannot be read is not read some other way, and a row never read is no reference (`references/detail.md` step 0).
