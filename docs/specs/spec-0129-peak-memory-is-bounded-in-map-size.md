- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against `origin/feat/memory-bounded` @ `f4f82bb34`, read whole, no code changed; the branch head `806a17b7c` adds only a merge of `main` that moves none of the files named here. Files read: ADR-0037 (the decision and its measurement tables); `crates/delvec/src/compiler/{cost,sections,cellset,burial,light}.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/par.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/world/reach.rs` and `reach/{agreement,budget,spill,bank,pages,chunks,states,persist,tests}.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/view/world.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit/mod.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/detail.rs`; `crates/delvec/tests/{cli,thread_count,reach_cache}.rs`; `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (the `rust-windows` and `docs` jobs), `.github/required-status-checks.txt`, `.github/ci-reach.toml`, `tools/ci/{check-required-contexts,check-delvec-built-once,tree-digest}.py`, `tools/maintenance/rss-sample.sh`; `docs/reference/compiler.md` (the store, the budget, the end-of-run lines, the cost ledger) and `docs/reference/tools.md`; spec-0113 §7; CLAUDE.md *Host hardware never caps a capability*, *Debug doctrine*, *Measurement doctrine*, *Vacuity*. Every measurement cited below is one ADR-0037 records, named by the engine revision it was taken at.
- **What it is for**: a build held the whole map in memory several times over, and the largest holdings grew with the site region's volume or with the cells a body can reach — the town (95 places, an 86 M-cell region) peaked at 14.06 GiB, and growing its region by 57 M cells cost 1.86 GiB more. The ruling is that peak memory is about constant in map size: a fixed working set, every whole-map stage reading and writing the map in bounded chunks, and the engine stating what a build costs before it starts; the hosting side meets the stated cost and the engine never refuses a map for its size. ADR-0037 records the decision, the established practice it rests on and the measurement of each step. This spec states what holds for the engine developer, what judges each claim, and what the branch still owes — so the feature can merge under CLAUDE.md's *no feature merges into `main` without an approved spec*.
- **Research**: ADR-0037 *Established practice* is the record (the game's own sectioned chunk format, per-chunk light flooding with border spill, HPA* over a portal graph, external-memory BFS, streaming connected components, paged sparse-volume leaves). Every rule below is marked **[cited]** with the ADR-0037 section or constitution line it rests on, or **[authored]** where this spec decides.
- **Numbers**: spec `0129`. No DW code: nothing here changes what the compiler refuses or warns. `dsl_version` does not move: nothing here changes what a document says. One new file in the output tree, `validation/resource-cost.json`, indexed by `manifest.json`; one changed file, `validation/reach-graph.json`, whose counts move as §1.4 declares.
- **Non-goals**: a faster build (CPU time is recorded beside every row and not judged here); a budget a host sets (§1.2 refuses it); a map refused or truncated for its size; a weaker proof anywhere (§1.4: every exact answer stays exact); a second budget, spill policy or scratch location for a structure that pages next (§1.2, §1.3: it registers with the one).

## 1. The store and the budget

### 1.1 One section store **[cited: ADR-0037 §1]**

The assembled world is one section store (`compiler::sections::SectionMap<BlockState>`, as `blockstate::BlockMap`): 16 × 16 × 16-cell sections by section coordinate, each a palette of the states it holds and one index per cell — a byte while the palette holds at most 255 states, two after, the palette compacted before the index widens. A section nothing wrote is absent and reads as what the level generator lays there. The store answers what a `BTreeMap<[i32; 3], _>` answers and iterates in the same cell order, so no consumer observes the sectioning. The judged copy (`Assembled::judged_within`), the write judge's map, the review world's block map and every whole-world `CellMap<V>` (the light model's blocks, the nav world's `partial` and `material`) are stores too. A template's cells are written grouped by section and the judged copy is taken section by section, so the working set while the world is assembled and cut is one section of each store. Every build prints `world store: N placed cell(s) in S section(s) of 16 x 16 x 16, B byte(s)[; the judged copy …] (ADR-0037 §1)`.

**Declared, not built** **[cited: ADR-0037 §1]**: the judged world as a column mask over the store, in place of a second store of the judged columns and the wall. On the town the copy is 16,360,704 bytes of records against a masked view that every reader of the judged world would take; on that balance it stays owed and is named in §6.

### 1.2 One resident budget, from needs the structures measure **[cited: ADR-0037 §1]**

One budget module (`nav::world::reach::budget`) is the only place a working set is summed or a holding given up. Every structure that pages — the reach graph's edge lists, its snapshot pages, the section stores' index arrays — states a `Need` and registers; it adds no budget of its own. A need is the working set the structure's walk has had to hold, never a number a host sets: the lists' is the 3 × 3 ring of reach chunks at the largest any list has decoded, with the ring's buffered pushes; the snapshot's is four 3 × 3 rings of the tallest window's pages; the stores' starts at a 3 × 3 × 3 ring of sections at the wide index (221,184 bytes) and is raised as the build runs — a read-back within one turnover of its store's arrays is a re-reference, and a sample of 64 read-backs of which half or more are re-references doubles the need, until the walk's working set stays. An array given up long before is read back cold however few cells of it the walk touches, so a sliver written into a section placed long before, a second sequential pass, and a copy's first read of a shared record raise nothing. The budget is the sum of the needs stated. Past it, the structure that just grew gives up its least recently used holdings first, down to three quarters of its own stated need and no further, then every other is asked, the stores never below three quarters of theirs, until the total stands at three quarters of the budget: no structure is emptied for another's growth. What any structure answers never depends on what is resident. A run that took a closure prints `reach budget: R byte(s) resident at the end, under one budget of N byte(s), the sum of the working sets stated: … (ADR-0037 §1)`; a run whose stores held a section prints `world store pages: … the need raised T time(s) for a walk found thrashing, to N byte(s); … (ADR-0037 §1)`.

### 1.3 Scratch files: one policy, one place **[cited: ADR-0037 §1, *Consequences*]**

A holding given up is written to a scratch file in the system's temporary directory, never under `-o` — the output tree is the deliverable the determinism gates hash — and read back on demand, positionally, while writers append. One policy for every spill (`reach/spill.rs`): a scratch file that cannot be opened is no file and the structure holds its records in memory; one that was opened and then refuses a write or a read back stops the run with a message naming the file, the operation, the error and the bytes written. A build's disk is the map's size there. The bank file a run keeps with a campaign (`DWBANK03`, read and written chunk by chunk by `delvec detail` and by a run that sets `DELVEC_REACH_CACHE`) is a persisted artifact under the campaign, not a scratch file, and is outside this rule.

### 1.4 Neither the budget nor the chunking is an input to any emitted byte **[cited: ADR-0037 §1, §4; ADR-0006]**

The section side (16, the game's), the reach chunk side (`chunks::SIDE`, 32 columns), the budget in force and what is resident at any moment decide residency and disk traffic and nothing else: every exact answer — the reach closure, the leak floods, the light per region, the write judge — is the answer the unchunked form gives, and the output tree is byte-identical to the engine before the store, file by file, with the two exceptions declared here and nowhere else. **Declared**: every launch is judged against the closure at the start of its round rather than against what earlier batches of the same round had grown, so the reached states are the same set and the edges, launches and arcs the binding counts move — on the town 1,366,129 states either way; 9,024,330 edges against 8,882,406; 130,843,256 launches simulated against 121,972,219; 86,855 arcs credited against 86,799 — and `validation/reach-graph.json` with the `manifest.json` line that hashes it are the only output bytes that differ from `main` for that reason. The counts that depend on the chunk side (`reach portals:`, every end-of-run line of §1.2) are written to stderr only. A build at any thread count writes the same tree (`tests/thread_count.rs::the_output_is_the_same_at_every_thread_count`, which strips the machine's own lines — `reach snapshot:`'s served-by-recent-pages count and `world store pages:` — and asserts the count of lines stripped equal).

## 3. The engine states a build's cost before it starts **[cited: ADR-0037 §3; CLAUDE.md *Host hardware never caps a capability*]**

Before the whole-map stages start, `delvec build` and `delvec detail` print one line, `resource cost: …` (`compiler::cost::ResourceCost`), computed from the documents and the store alone — the site plan's region, the horizon, the judged columns, the reach window those give through the snapshot's own rule (`nav::window_box`), the store's sections — and `delvec build` writes the same numbers as `validation/resource-cost.json` with `"computed_never_measured": true`. The line states: the reach window at most and its reached bitmap; the chunks across it at the chunk side and their face cells; the portal edges at most (face cells × the most edges one node holds); the working set before any walk (the stores' ring + the snapshot's pages + the lists' buffered pushes) and the lists' largest chunk at most (every cell of a chunk a node with every edge, by the window's height); the summaries at most (face labels: judged sections × 6 × 128; configurations: the region writes + 1; plain-sea summaries: one per configuration under an ocean, none under a void); and the scratch bytes (the stores' arrays of the whole and the judged copy). Every `at most` figure is an upper bound a build cannot exceed; the working set before any walk is a floor the budget starts at, not a bound. The ledger holds nothing measured, so two builds of one campaign write one file; what a build reached is the end-of-run lines of §1.2 and `reach lists:`, `reach portals:`, `reach snapshot:`, `reach closures:`, written nowhere in the output tree. The hosting side meets the stated cost and reads the measured lines for what a build took; the engine does not refuse a map for either.

**[authored]** The record names no numeric factor between a computed bound and the measured figure it bounds: the relation is upper bound, factor 1, and the slack is stated per figure where it has been measured (the lists' largest chunk is three orders over the town's measured 5,295,392-byte chunk). A side-by-side of every `at most` figure against its end-of-run line on the town is owed (§6) and is where a tighter plan-derived bound would be argued.

## 5. How a memory claim is measured **[cited: CLAUDE.md *Measurement doctrine*; ADR-0037 *The closures, measured* and after]**

A peak is measured with two instruments that share no configuration: GNU `/usr/bin/time -v` (the kernel's maximum resident set from `wait4`, and CPU time) wrapping `tools/maintenance/rss-sample.sh` (VmRSS and `/proc/<pid>/io` once a second beside the timestamped stderr, so a stage line stands beside the resident set at that second); the two peaks are compared and the one that disagrees is the instrument failing; a spike shorter than the sampler's second is the kernel's figure and the sampler's is the plateau. The second method for the output is the file-by-file comparison of the trees two engines write (`tools/ci/tree-digest.py --compare`, or per-file `sha256` with the count of files stated), which shares nothing with either memory instrument. Each engine is force-rebuilt from its revision with cargo's exit status asserted and its binary named by revision; two runs of each map on each engine; the town and the widened town (the town's region grown by 100 cells on every horizontal side, same 95 places, derived from the town by the measurement's own script) are the two maps, and the difference between them is the figure that says whether memory grows with the region. CPU time is the timing claimed; wall time on a shared machine is recorded and not claimed. A measured figure goes in the commit body and the record, never in the output tree.

## 7. Open questions

- **Does spec-0113 §7 gain the memory column and the chunk-invariance criterion** ADR-0037's *Consequences* names, or does this spec stand as the one authority for both? A second document stating one rule is two authorities; the recommendation is that this spec is the authority and ADR-0037's line is finalised to point here when the record is accepted.
- **Is the section side a variable the proofs should vary?** It is the game's constant (16) and no test varies it; §4 proves the store unobservable at that side, which is what §1.4 asserts. A criterion over several sides would need a knob the engine does not have.
- **Does the judged mask stay a debt?** §1.1 declares the balance; if the copy's cost under the budget is accepted, the row leaves §6 and ADR-0037 §1 is finalised to say so.
- **The widened town's peak increment.** At `e55089315` the widened town peaks 0.09–0.18 GiB over the town against a run-to-run spread of 0.03–0.06 GiB on each map; the record attributes the excess to the stores' need doubling once more on the widened town (the first debt of §6) and to spread. Criterion 5 reads the need, which is the exact figure; whether the peak increment is also held to the spread once the walks are sectional is decided when they are.
