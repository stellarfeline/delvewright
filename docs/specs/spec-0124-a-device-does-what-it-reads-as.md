- **Ground**: written against engine `7d4a1a80dcfd50b1df97152379b90685447bb388` (`origin/main`), read only — `Objective::Interact` (`requires_item`, `missing_item_hint`) in `crates/dsl/src/quest/objective.rs`; `BONFIRE_SAVE_LABEL_EN`, `BONFIRE_REST_LABEL_EN` and the bonfire label fields in `crates/dsl/src/quest/effect.rs`; `is_player_openable` in `crates/dsl/src/blockshape.rs` (read by `compiler/lethal.rs` and `compiler/sculk.rs` only); `EdgeGating` and `Edge::shuts` in `crates/dsl/src/layout.rs`; the shut-gate model in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/shutmodel.rs`; the seated-structure population of `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/width.rs` (`trigger_props`); the act lexicon `crates/delvec/data/acts-en.json` and `crates/delvec/tests/act_check.rs` (spec-0114); `docs/reference/compiler.md` rows `edges[].gating` (spec-0107), `open-gate`, `happening`, `bonfire`; `docs/reference/game-writing.md` F4, L1, L3, L4. - **What it is for**: a device whose logic is wrong is found in the owner's one QA hour, not by the machine. In one demo, a wooden door in the hall's wall is the object of a step titled *Unbar the store door*, the keeper's key is used on it, and the step's happening says the door *opens*; after the key the door still does not open and the only thing the key changed is that the chest beside it will now lift its lid. A rest point's second button is captioned *Save and quit*; it saves and quits nothing. In a second demo, a two-course door stands free on a floor beside a one-course counter, with nothing on either side of it: a door that closes nothing. Both demos are retired as test vehicles for this question [ruling]; what this spec is for is that no later level gets a device whose logic is wrong, by refusing each shape where it is declared or built.
- **Research**: `docs/reference/device-logic.md` is the record — lock and key, affordance and signifier, cause and effect at a distance, captions — each rule marked cited or authored, with the licence of every source. §3 names the rules this spec rests on; nothing is invented here.
- **Numbers**: spec `0124`. Four diagnostics: `DW-new-a-door-that-is-no-way` (§7.1), `DW-new-an-opening-that-opens-nothing` (§7.2), `DW-new-an-effect-out-of-sight` (§7.3), `DW-new-a-door-that-closes-nothing` (§7.4). `DW1045` (spec-0114) widens what it reads under its own code (§6.4). The surface change is stage 2 and takes the unreleased `dsl_version` minor other unreleased surface changes share; this spec states no version literal.
- **Non-goals**: whether a door looks like a door, or follows the wall it stands in (spec-0123's question, on its own branch); the words of a caption beyond the acts the lexicon knows (guide text, §10); the owner's own reading of a device in play (the playtest judges it); a shut way's `learned` word (spec-0118); the width of the way (spec-0120); trapdoors and fence gates under §6.5 (the ruling names door blocks; §6.5 says why each stays out and what would widen it).

## 1. The defect, from the documents

**What was declared.** `site-plan.json`: seam `edge/store-door`, *the latched store door in the hall's wall*, `opening: door`, on the hall's east face at `4`, meeting the store at `4`. `layout-graph.json`: `edge/store-door` is a `walk` from `node/hall` to `node/store` with **no `gating`** — in the engine's model the way is open from world load. `quests.json`: `obj/unbar-the-store`, an `interact` at `anchor/store-door`, `prop` a shut `minecraft:spruce_door`, `requires_item` the key, `release: true`, title *Unbar the store door*, `happening {verb: opens, subject: anchor/store-door}`; `obj/lift-the-lid` on the chest is `after` it. The README's steps 6 and 10 say the key unbars the door, that from then on it is an ordinary door opened by hand, and that the player goes through it into the store.

**What was built.** The hall prefab's contract puts the store doorway at the via column `[13, 1..2, 5]` — an open void in the east wall, the way the layout graph declares. The door prop stands at `anchor/store-door` = `[12, 1, 5]`, `facing: west`: the cell inside the room directly in front of the void, its leaf flush against the wall plane, so from the hall it reads as the door *in* the doorway. The generation record states the departure: a prop in the doorway cell was refused (`DW0845`: the anchor resolves into the piece's `via`, not play space), so the door was stood in front of it. No required anchor resolves into `node/store`: every objective anchor is in the hall and the finish is the shed, so the store is on no proof's route.

**The two models never met.** To the layout graph and every proof that reads it, hall and store were joined by an open doorway from the first tick; to the quest layer, a key was used on a pressable that happened to be a door; to the player, a barred door was unbarred and did not open. The key's one observable effect — the chest's `after` — is Boris the Brave's event flag (`device-logic.md` §1): a cause with no visible lock.

**Why no gate caught it.**
- `DW0845` refused the one placement that would have made the door the way's closure, and prescribed nothing; the author moved the door one cell into the room, where the engine saw a pressable and no longer a way. A gate that refuses a remedy's only honest shape and names no other is half of a pair defect (CLAUDE.md, *Pairs*): spec-0117 prescribes *the door the party unbarred is a door, opened and shut by hand*; the seam model refuses a door prop in the plane (`DW0845`) and spec-0120 refuses one in the approach column (`DW1057`, criterion 10, *an `interact` prop … in an approach column*). There is no cell in which a door prop may be a seam's closure.
- spec-0118's `DW1053` judges gated connections; the edge had no gating. spec-0120's `DW1057` judges the way's width; the way was whole (the void is air). The separation proof (`DW1011`) judges shut gates; none was declared. The route proofs reach required anchors; none was in the store.
- `happening.verb: opens` is a chronicle statement nobody reads against the effects: the step opened nothing and the chronicle says it opened the door.
- The README is prose; nothing reads a hand-over step against what the ladder walked (§6.6).
- Whether the released door should have opened by hand is settled by spec-0117 (*release*: the box is killed and the block is vanilla's thereafter); the documents record a door that did not. Either the engine revision that built the demo did not honour its own release, or the door the owner pressed was never the way — in both readings nothing judged the door as a way, and the second is proven above. The first is not established here, and the demo is retired [ruling]; it is confirmed, if at all, on the demo level of §9, never on this content.

**The caption.** `fx.the-house.oc.hear-the-keeper.2.save_label` is *Save and quit* (zh *保存并退出*); the engine default is *Save only*, and the option *moves the checkpoint and does nothing else* (`compiler.md`, `bonfire`). The engine knows the option's one act; the label names two.

**The free-standing door** (*The Front and the Fountain*, content `e053e77`). The old stall's program (`programs/stall.json`) stands a two-course `minecraft:spruce_door[facing=south]` (roles `door`, `door-top`) on the stall's front row beside a one-course counter, under a five-course open face: the cells beside the leaf hold a counter one course high on one side and air on the other, and air over both courses above the counter — a body walks round it on either side. The piece's one contract edge is the whole open front (`stall-front`, nine columns); the door is in no via. The census of spec-0123 §3.5 entered it as the one unframed door of the row (`doors framed: 1 of 2`, the build's own line) and, by that spec's design, refused nothing: its *Rejected* list names *a refusal of a door whose jambs or head a body can be in* as a design the author may mean (*a door standing free in a ruin*). The ruling that follows the playtest reverses that: a door must do a door's job; shut, it stops a body passing, open, it lets one pass; a door that does not close a way is not placed or gets wall joined to both sides of it, for every door block in the built world whether or not a story addresses it [ruling]. The stall's door is what §6.5 now refuses.

## 3. What practice does — the rules this spec rests on

From `docs/reference/device-logic.md` §5, each cited or authored there: **R1** a lock's object is the key's object; **R2** a key's effect is in the presser's sight from where it is used, or the beat shows it; **R3** a door the story addresses is a way — it stands in the opening between two places and opens by hand onto the far place once its step has passed; **R4** [ruling] every door in the built world closes an opening — shut, its leaf stops a body passing between the two cells it separates and the cells beside it at both courses stop a body; open, it lets one pass; a door that closes nothing is not placed or gets wall joined to both sides; **R5** a caption names the acts the button performs and none it does not; **R6** a remote effect neither in sight nor shown is refused, and a deception of a learned rule is a declared exception.

**Reconciled with what is ruled, and not re-opened.** A hidden mechanism may be far from its effect and the close-up shot shows the effect (`content.md`, spec-0118): R2's second clause is that ruling, and §6.3 reads the shot as the declared reveal. spec-0114 reads the title's act; §6.4 reads a label's acts through the same lexicon and nothing else. spec-0116, spec-0118 and spec-0120 read nothing this spec changes; §6.1 reads spec-0120's corridor as the place a door's shutter may stand. spec-0117's *release* is the open state of §5.1's door; this spec gives it the cell it had no legal way to stand in.

**Declared change against spec-0123 §3.5.** That spec's census *refuses nothing* for doors, and its *Rejected* list names a refusal of an unframed door as a design the author may mean. The ruling supersedes that for doors: §6.5 refuses every door that closes nothing, and the free-standing door in a ruin is no longer a design the engine admits — a ruin's door gets a stub of wall on both sides or is not placed. The census stays as it is: the measurement it writes (`doors framed`, the head included) is the one §6.5 reads its jambs from, so there is one computation of a door's framing and two readers of it; nothing of spec-0123's other lines (heads, kinds, water) moves. The specimen that spec's demo built to be read — the old stall's door of *The Front and the Fountain* — is what the refusal now rejects, and spec-0123's row for that demo is amended by the round that lands this one to say so.

## 4. The decision

**Every door closes an opening** [ruling]. Every door block in the assembled world — a piece's, the stand-in's, a prop, a seam's shutter — stands between two cells a body could otherwise pass between, with cells that stop a body beside its leaf at both courses; shut, its leaf stops the crossing; open, it does not. A door that closes nothing is refused where it is built (§6.5, §7.4), with the two remedies the ruling names: remove it, or join wall to both sides. There is no scenery door and no story-addressing condition: the rule is the block's.

**A door the story addresses is a seam's shutter.** The engine already has the object that bars a way until the story opens it — the gated edge and its seam (spec-0107) — and the object that answers a press and opens by hand once released (spec-0117). This spec joins them: a gated edge may declare that its shutter is a **door** rather than bars, the derivation stands the door leaf in the plane and the gate region over it, the shut model reads it as shut, the box answers presses on it while shut, and the verb that opens the gate **releases** the door instead of filling air — the kind of opening is decided by the object (CLAUDE.md, *Vacuity*: where the choice is among kinds, the object determines the kind). The key is an `interact` at the seam's own anchor; `prop` is not written there because the seam knows its block.

With that cell in existence, a door the story addresses anywhere else is refused: there is now a place for it to be what it reads as, and the refusal names it (§7.1). A door nobody addresses is still held to R4 by §6.5: it closes an opening of its own piece — a room's door in a room's wall — or it is refused.

**A step that says it opens something opens it.** `happening.verb: opens` on an objective, an option or an effect is held to the effects of the node: something it carries opens a gate, a way or a door-shut seam, and the `subject`, when it names an anchor, is the thing opened (§6.2). `seals` likewise, to `close-gate`. The chronicle stops being able to say a thing opened when nothing did.

**A key's effect is in sight or shown.** For every `interact` with `requires_item`, each world write its completion performs is within the presser's sight from the cell the key is used at, or a shot in the same completion frames it (§6.3).

**A caption names the acts of its button.** The two bonfire labels are read through the act lexicon: every lexicon verb in a label is an act the option performs (§6.4). Every other caption is guide text (§10).

**Each check confirms a declared intent and never restricts.** The intent is the declaration: a door block placed (the block's whole behaviour is the intent it declares, and the ruling makes that reading the engine's), a door the story addresses (anchored, keyed or said to open), a `happening` that says *opens*, a `requires_item` that makes a key, a label the author wrote. A campaign that declares none of them passes with every count `0` stated.

## 5. The surface

### 5.1 The layout graph — stage 2

```json
{ "id": "edge/store-door", "class": "walk", "a": "node/hall", "b": "node/store",
  "gating": { "flags": ["flag/store-unbarred"], "opens_from": "a", "shut": "door" } }
```

**`gating.shut`** is an optional field of `EdgeGating`, a closed set: `bars` (the default, unwritten — the derivation of spec-0107 exactly as it is) and `door`. `door` is admitted on a `walk` whose seam's `opening` is `door` (one cell wide, two high; the site plan's own word); anywhere else — a `stair`, `climb` or `drop`, a wider opening, a `carry` — it is refused (§7.1, shape *c*). With `door`:

- **The derivation** stands a door leaf in the plane's two cells: `half=lower` and `half=upper`, `open=false`, `facing` toward the side `opens_from` names (toward `a` when `either`), `hinge=left`. The block is the host piece's palette `door` role where the piece declares one, else the derivation's own `minecraft:oak_door` (the stand-in's palette, recorded in `compiler.md` with the row). Where a bound piece owns the cells, the piece ships the door against the gate region it owes, as it ships bars today.
- **The gate region** `anchor/seam-<edge>` is the door's two cells. The world-load seal measures it shut by `open=false`, as it measures bars by presence.
- **The shut model** (`shutmodel.rs`) and every proof that asks it — `DW1011`, `DW1053`/`DW1054`, the route proofs — read a shut door's column as a cell a body cannot be in, exactly as a bar's; the open configuration reads it as clear. The corridor judgement (spec-0120) reads the door's cells as the way's own gate region (its §3, *Excluded*), so the shutter is never a narrowing of its own way.
- **The press** (spec-0117): while shut, the box stands over the door and answers `not_yet`, `missing_item` and the wrong-side line as a sealed thing's does; `opens_from` decides which face carries `missing_item` and which the wrong-side answer.
- **The opening.** `open-gate` on `anchor/seam-<edge>`, a `shortcuts[]` entry's `gate` and a `timed_gates[]` entry's `gate` naming a door-shut seam **release** the door: the box is killed and nothing is filled; the leaf stays and is vanilla's, opened and shut by hand. `close-gate` on a door-shut seam shuts the leaf (`open=false`) and re-stands the box. A timed gate's `crush` on a door is refused (§7.1, shape *c*): a leaf is not a portcullis.

### 5.2 The key — stage 5

```json
{ "id": "obj/unbar-the-store", "type": "interact", "anchor": "anchor/seam-store-door",
  "requires_item": "minecraft:tripwire_hook", "sets_flag": "flag/store-unbarred",
  "answers": { "missing_item": "Locked. The keeper's key opens it, held in your hand.",
               "not_yet": "The store door is barred from within. Ring the bell first." },
  "happening": { "verb": "opens", "subject": "anchor/seam-store-door", "text": "…" },
  "on_complete": [ { "type": "open-gate", "anchor": "anchor/seam-store-door" } ] }
```

An `interact` anchored on a door-shut seam's `anchor/seam-<edge>` writes no `prop` and no `release`: the seam's door is the pressable and its release is the opening. A `prop` or `release` written there is refused (§7.1, shape *d*): a second statement of what the seam already declares. The objective's completion carries the verb that opens the gate, as every gated edge's opener does today; the gating term it raises is the one the edge names, and `DW1011` judges the pair as it judges bars.

### 5.3 Labels

No surface change. `rest_label` and `save_label` are read as §6.4 says.

## 6. The analysis

### 6.1 A story-addressed door is a seam's shutter — `compiler::nav::device`

**The population.** Every **way-block** the story addresses: a pressable whose block `is_player_openable` and which is the `prop` of an `interact` objective, the `prop` of a `use` trigger, a shortcut's `unlock`, or a `disarm`'s `via`; and every door-shut seam (§5.1). `W` way-blocks over `P` pressables; `D` door-shut seams over `G` gated connections.

**The verdict.** A way-block passes when its cells are a door-shut seam's gate region (it *is* the shutter, reached through §5.2's anchor). Every other way-block is refused `DW-new-a-door-that-is-no-way`, shape *a*: the message names the pressable, its block, its cell, the nearest seam's plane and the two remedies — declare the edge gated with `shut: door` and anchor the step on `anchor/seam-<edge>`, or make the pressable a block that is not a way (a lever, a button, a bell). A `release: true` on a pressable that is not a way-block is spec-0117's own business and is not read here.

**Shape *b* — a shutter onto nothing.** A door-shut seam whose far side has no body cell within one approach cell of the plane in the open configuration (the site plan's floor proofs `DW0836`/`DW0837` should have caught the plane; this names the door) is refused with the far cell and its block.

### 6.2 A happening that says *opens* — `compiler::happening`

**The population.** Every `happening` with `verb` `opens` or `seals`, on an objective, a story-weight option or a story-node effect. `H` of `T` happenings.

**The verdict.** `opens` passes when the node's own effects — an objective's `on_complete`, an option's effects, the effect itself — carry an `open-gate`, an `open-way`, a shortcut opening or a timed gate's open, and, when `subject` names an anchor, that anchor is the gate region or the way opened. `seals` passes on `close-gate`, `close-way` or a timed gate's close the same way. A `happening` whose node opens nothing, or opens a thing its `subject` does not name, is refused `DW-new-an-opening-that-opens-nothing`, naming the node, the verb, the subject and what the node does carry.

### 6.3 A key's effect is in sight or shown — `compiler::nav::device`

**The population.** Every `interact` with `requires_item`, and every world write its completion performs: `open-gate`, `close-gate`, `open-way`, `set-block`, `fill-region`, `clear-region`, and the release of a door-shut seam. `K` key objectives over `O` objectives; `E` writes over them.

**The verdict.** A write passes when some cell of the region written has a clear line within 64 blocks from the presser's eye at the key's anchor cell, through `DW0863`'s own sight predicate and no copy (CLAUDE.md, *One question, one implementation*), judged in the configuration the key is used in with the write applied; or when the same completion carries a `cutscene` one of whose shots has the region as its `look_at` or framed subject (spec-0110's predicate). A write that is neither is refused `DW-new-an-effect-out-of-sight`, naming the objective, the write, the distance, the nearest blocking cell, and the two remedies: stand the device where its effect is in view, or add the shot. A key whose only write is its own seam's release passes by construction (the door is the anchor).

### 6.4 A label's acts — `DW1045` widened

The act lexicon (`acts-en.json`) gains three acts: `rest` (*rest, sit, sleep, warm, camp, stay*), `save` (*save, mark, remember, record, keep*) and `quit` (*quit, exit, leave, log out, log off, disconnect, return to menu*). The `bonfire` effect's `rest_label` and `save_label` are read as spec-0114 reads a title: every lexicon verb in the label (head word and every conjoined verb, split on *and*, *&*, *,*) is an act the option performs — the rest option performs `rest` and `save`; the save option performs `save`. A verb the lexicon does not know is not judged. A label naming an act its option does not perform is refused `DW1045` with the label, the act and the option's acts. *Save and quit* on the save option is refused on `quit`; *Save only*, *Rest and save*, *Rest* and *Save* pass. Binding: `L` labels judged over `B` bonfires, with the count of lexicon verbs found.

### 6.5 Every door closes an opening — `compiler::nav::device`, over `census::doors`

**The population.** Every `*_door` lower half in the **assembled world** — bound pieces, the stand-in's walls, a prop, a door-shut seam's shutter (§5.1), of every door kind: wooden, iron, copper. `N` doors. It is enumerated by `census::doors` run over the whole's extent (spec-0123's one authority for a door's framing cells, reused and never copied); the census's own ledger stays per bound piece, so the two counts are stated with their denominators and `N ≥` the census's.

**The verdict.** A door **closes an opening** when all three hold, each through the authority that already answers it:
1. **The jambs stop a body.** Its two jamb cells at both courses — the `Framing` rows of role `jamb` the census records for it, a double door's far jamb for its pair — are each a cell a body cannot be in (`can_be`, the nav model's one predicate), in the as-built state.
2. **The sides are two sides.** The two cells the leaf separates — the cell in front of its `facing` and the cell behind, at the lower course — are each a cell a body can be in with the door open, and the body column over each is clear.
3. **Shut, the leaf stops the crossing.** `World::leaf_stopping` (`Leaf::stops_crossing`, the one rule `DW1066` and `DW1068` read) answers *stopped* for the crossing between the two side cells with the door `open=false`, and *not stopped* with it in its open state.

The head over the upper half is recorded by the census and not required here: a body cannot pass a two-course leaf from the floor, and a door in a wall two courses high under open sky does its job. A door failing any clause is refused `DW-new-a-door-that-closes-nothing`, naming the door, its cell and block, the clause, the cell that fails it with what stands there, and the two remedies: remove the door, or join wall to both sides (and, for clause 2, open the side it is walled against). A door-shut seam's shutter passes by construction (the plane's jambs are the wall, its sides the two approach cells) and is counted.

**Trapdoors and fence gates, and why they stay out.** The ruling names door blocks. A **trapdoor** is a hatch: its leaf lies in a floor or a wall that is its own frame, the census does not enter it for that reason, and its own rule is `DW1066` (a trapdoor that shuts a body in); its decorative uses — a shutter, a table top — close nothing and are the game's building vocabulary, so a closing rule on trapdoors would refuse what nobody rules against. A **fence gate** does a door's job in a fence and the census already enters it with its two neighbours; the rule would carry over unchanged (neighbours stop a body; the leaf separates two sides). It is not widened here: the ruling was given for doors, and widening it to gates is proposed in one line (§12) and waits.

**The ledger and the line.** `validation/devices.json` gains a `doors` array with one row per door — cell, block, the three clauses with the failing cell — and `DW-new-a-door-that-closes-nothing binding: N door(s) in the assembled world (C in bound pieces, the census's N); n refused` is printed. A campaign with no door prints `0 door(s)`.

### 6.6 What stays a review judgement, and the blind review line

- **Whether a device reads as itself** — a door that looks like a door, a bar that looks barred, a key that looks like it fits — is spec-0123's and the playtest's.
- **Whether a caption beyond the lexicon's verbs says what the button does** is the writer's, under F4 and R5.
- **Whether a hand-over step promises what the ladder walked.** The README's *what to look for* steps are prose. **Blind review line**, a hand-over step in `references/hand-over.md`: a fresh agent is handed the campaign's *what to look for* section and the build's `validation/critical-path.json`, `validation/ways.json` and `validation/shut-ways.json`, and nothing else — not the DSL, not the author's notes — and returns, per numbered step, the proof entry that performs the act the step names, or *no proof walks this*. The table is appended to the hand-over under the steps. A miss — a step naming an act no proof walks — is a promise to the owner that no machine kept, and the step is rewritten or struck before the hand-over is sent; it is never left for the owner's hour. The reader is a fresh agent because the author knows what was meant and will read it in.

## 10. Guide text

For `references/placement.md`, the `layout-graph.json` item, beside the gating sentence:

> **A door the story opens is the shutter of its way.** A door the player unbars, unlocks or is told to go through stands in the opening between two places — the edge carries `gating` with `shut: door` — and the step that opens it is an `interact` on `anchor/seam-<edge>`, holding the key. It opens by hand onto the far place from then on. A door a step presses that is not a way is refused; where a thing is pressed but is not a way, it is a lever, a button or a bell.

For `references/detail.md`, where a program's openings are taught:

> **Every door closes an opening.** Shut, a door stops a body passing; open, it lets one pass. A door stands in a wall with wall joined to both sides of it at both courses, and a floor a body can stand on in front and behind. A door that closes nothing — standing free on a floor, beside a counter, against a wall with nothing behind — is not placed, or wall is joined to both sides of it; the engine refuses the rest. A hatch is a trapdoor, and a gate in a fence is a fence gate; a door block is never scenery.

For `references/content.md`, step 5, beside *A line names a thing as the player sees it*:

> **A key is used on the thing it opens, and its effect is in view or shown.** The object the key is held to is the object that was barred; what the press changes is visible from where the player stands, or the beat carries a shot of it. A step whose `happening` says *opens* carries the effect that opens the thing it names. **A button says what it does and nothing it does not**: a rest point's second button saves; it does not quit, leave or end anything, and its caption may not say so.

For `docs/reference/game-writing.md` §8, as **L5**, beside L3:

> **L5 — A device does what it reads as.** Every door closes an opening — shut it stops a body, open it lets one pass, and wall is joined to both sides of it; a door the story addresses opens onto the place behind it; a key's effect lands where the key is used, or the beat shows it; a caption names the acts the button performs and none it does not. **Cited** (`device-logic.md` §1–4: Dormans, Norman, Gaver, England). **Machine half** (spec-0124 §6).

## 12. What stays open

- **A double door.** `shut: door` is one leaf on a one-wide opening; a two-wide opening with two leaves is a second shape the derivation does not stand, and a seam two wide stays barred by bars. Measured on the first campaign that wants one.
- **A trapdoor as a shutter.** A floor way (`drop`, `climb`) shut by a wooden trapdoor the party opens by hand is the same idea through a floor; `shut` stays `bars` there until a level wants it.
- **Fence gates under §6.5.** Proposed in one line: a fence gate closes an opening in a fence by the same three clauses, read from the census's gate entry; widening the ruling from doors to gates waits on the owner.
- **A door that is told, not keyed.** A door-shut seam opened by `gating.quest` with no `interact` at its anchor has no `missing_item` line to carry; `DW1053`'s `told` reads the seam's answers as today.
- **Captions beyond the lexicon.** A label whose verbs the lexicon does not know is not judged; the lexicon grows from playtests, through spec-0114's own procedure.
- **The hand-over table is a step, not a gate.** §6.6's blind review produces a table nothing refuses on; whether its *no proof walks this* rows can become declarations the engine reads is the next question, asked when a round has three such tables to read.
