- **What it is for**: a player who finishes one thing and can tell what to do next, in a delve whose quest plan is a web (`web-narrative.md`): several next objectives may be open at once and the party may split. In the first full playtest of a quiet delve the party could not: 18 of 30 objectives carried no `hint` and steps went unseen; a title said *go* where talking completes the step; nothing said that the world's blocks are right-clicked; interact props did not read as the thing their text names; a fight's bodies were seated out of sight. The first draft of this spec answered with four refusals and an opening teaching block. This revision replaces that design with the decisions that stand: a hint says where to go and what to do, as a writing rule in the skill and not a machine check (§3); the title-verb rule is a compile-time warning the creator adjudicates (§5); guidance is **moderate**, not marker-free — a **compass** each player aims at one of the open objectives (§6) and a **proximity highlight** the author declares on an act whose block or body does not read as the thing, lit only while a player is within a declared range (§7); instructions live in a **journal** item read any time — a book each player carries that holds the author's manual pages, written only where the setting contradicts vanilla or an item needs them, the engine's compass page, every open objective with its title and hint, and every objective already done, rewritten as objectives activate and complete and surviving a disconnect, because a chat line is gone after a reconnect and the chat history shows nothing older (§8); a row of identical levers is a legitimate puzzle, so the twin rule goes (§9).
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record. An unsupported claim is named as one. Every outside source is used for its ideas only (ADR-0013). Every mechanism is a vanilla primitive of the pinned client, named with the page that documents it (CLAUDE.md, *No hacks at any layer*).

The surface change is stage 5 and takes the unreleased `dsl_version` minor the first draft already moved to, stated once by `crates/dsl/Cargo.toml`; this spec states no version literal.

## 1. The defect, and the object class

**Cited** (`promise.rs`, module doc): *what the game tells the party and what the machine actually requires are not the same thing* is one defect class, judged in one module over one object class — the player-facing promise attached to an objective. **Cited** (`acts.rs`, module doc): an **act** is a thing the party must do at a place to advance a walked path — every objective step of every kind, and every `use`, `strike`, `strike-npc` or `strike-assembly` trigger the path performs; an `approach` or `step` trigger is a place on the route, not a thing to press.

**Authored — guidance is three jobs, one mechanism each.** Finding the act from where the party stands is the compass (far), the sight rule and the hint (§4, §6). Picking the thing out of its scene on arrival is the highlight (§7). Holding what the player was told — how this world is operated, what is open and what is done — is the journal (§8), one book that can be opened again after the chat has scrolled or the player has reconnected. A fourth mechanism for any of the three is the defect: the always-on lantern of spec-0093 did the highlight's job without a range and without the author's say, and goes (§7.6); the opening teaching block did the journal's job as chat that cannot be re-read, and goes (§8.9); a second book for the manual beside the journal would be two items for one job, so the manual is pages of the journal. Each mechanism is keyed to the object it acts on — the objective, the trigger, the campaign — never to a verb.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 Where guidance comes from

- **Cited** (`game-writing.md` §8 L1): the level carries the information and the text restates it; a destination is recognised by its building. **Cited** (`web-narrative.md` §5): Lynch's landmarks, paths, edges, nodes and districts (via Totten); a landmark that draws from afar; light and props lead; in a hub each spoke's mouth is visible and distinct, so the open threads are seen, not listed.
- **Cited** (Nielsen Norman Group, *10 Usability Heuristics*, #6 *Recognition rather than recall*): "information required to use the design … should be visible or easily retrievable when needed." **Authored**, from it: an act the party can see needs no line; an act it cannot see owes one; and what the party was told once must be retrievable later — the manual, not a chat line that scrolls away.
- **Cited** (the Skywind project's quest design principles, rule 1): "The quest must be fully completable without quest markers. This means descriptive objectives and detailed journal entries"; "markers are a subset of objectives, not the objective itself." **Authored**, from it and from the playtest: the compass is an aid over text and level that already work; a hint still says where to go and what to do (§3), and `DW0863` still asks that an act be in sight or told (§4) whether or not the compass points at it.

### 2.2 Text that names the act, and what a machine reads in prose

- **Cited** (`game-writing.md` §1 F1): functional text starts with the verb and names the thing and the place. **Cited** (Nielsen Norman Group, heuristic #4 *Consistency and standards*). **Authored**: the verb a title opens with is a claim about what the hands will do, and the objective's `type` is the engine's record of it; two declarations that disagree are judged by membership in a closed set.
- **Cited** (`game-writing.md` §5): *Functional text without action or place* measured **8 of 26 (31 %)** precision — "not usable as a refusal"; *Hint-for-statement* **6 of 16 (38 %)** — "human review only". **Cited** (the measurement commit on this branch, taken with `delvec` built from it): the lexicon on the motivating campaign read 26 of 60 strings — 9 of 18 English titles exactly as the hand count, and 2 of 12 English hints and 5 Chinese hints that were **false positives** (`head` the noun read as the verb; `说` of reported speech read as *talk*). **Authored**, from both: a closed set cannot tell a noun from a verb, so the rule that reads a title is advice to the author with its evidence printed, never a refusal (§5); the rule about what a hint says is a writing rule with no machine half (§3).

### 2.3 A compass, and a chosen objective

- **Cited** (UESP, *Quest Targets Tab*, the Construction Set for Oblivion and Skyrim): "all targets (for the player's active quest) which pass their conditions will be displayed on the player's compass and map" — the compass follows the quest the player has made active, and several targets of it may show at once. **Cited** (previews of Fable II, GamesRadar and GameBanshee): the breadcrumb trail is a line on the ground that "dynamically adapts to guide you to whatever character or quest item is relevant at the time", can be turned off in the pause menu, and does not punish leaving it. **Authored**, from both: the established form is one tracked objective per player, chosen by the player among the open ones, with the aid switchable by the design.
- **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Compass*): a compass points "in the horizontal direction of the Overworld's spawn point" and does so "when viewed in most ways; including in the inventory, when held by any player or entity"; a lodestone compass "points toward that lodestone's horizontal position"; with its lodestone broken or in another dimension "the needle spins in random directions". **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Data component format*, `lodestone_tracker`): `target` holds `pos` ("the integer coordinates of the lodestone") and `dimension`; `target` is "Optional. If not set, this compass spins randomly"; `tracked` — "If `true`, the component is removed when the lodestone is broken. If `false`, the component is kept." **Authored**, from it: a compass carrying `lodestone_tracker={target:{pos,dimension},tracked:false}` points at any block position with no lodestone there, and one carrying no `target` spins — the two states this spec needs, both vanilla item data the engine writes.
- **Authored — the choice is a menu, not a gesture**: the player right-clicks while holding the compass, a vanilla dialog lists the open tracked objectives, and the one clicked is followed. Crouching to cycle is the last resort, behind even a `trigger` command whose tab completion lists the open objectives, because a menu shows every open thread by name and reaches any of them in one click. **Cited** (the LodestoneTP data pack, Modrinth): a compass has no use action of its own, which is why vanilla-only packs bind sneaking to it; vanilla does give it one, below.
- **Read from the pinned jar** (server and client 1.21.11, with Mojang's mappings; the read sites are in `docs/reference/compiler.md`'s `compass` row): **(a)** `dialog show <targets> <dialog>` takes an inline dialog as well as a registry id (`ResourceOrIdArgument$DialogArgument` over `Dialog.CODEC`); a macro line substitutes a compound or a list as its SNBT text (`MacroFunction`'s stringify through `Tag#toString`), so `$dialog show @s $(dialog)` shows a dialog built in storage with any number of buttons; `multi_action`'s `actions` is a non-empty list, so a menu with nothing in it is never shown. **(b)** `CompassItem` does not override `use`; `Item#use` starts use on a `minecraft:consumable` component, and the `minecraft:using_item` advancement trigger fires every tick the item is held in use, so a right-click is detected with an edge latch; the needle and the `lodestone_tracker` are not read by use (`CompassItem#inventoryTick`, the client's `compass_angle`), and with a `consume_seconds` no hold reaches the compass is never consumed (`Consumable#onConsume` runs only when the time is done) and its sound never starts; in adventure mode `ItemStack#useOn` refuses a lodestone, so the tracker cannot be rewritten by the player. **(c)** `trigger` registers no permission requirement (level 0), and a dialog's `run_command` of a level-0 command with no signable argument is sent without a confirmation screen (`ClientPacketListener#sendUnattendedCommand`, `verifyCommand`), so a non-op adventure player's click runs `/trigger dw.cpick set <ordinal>`, once the objective is enabled for them. Button labels and tooltips are components, so they translate. **Cited** (`emit/dialogue.rs`): the NPC dialogues carry their state as `2^n` variants because their buttons are fixed at build time; this menu is built at the moment it opens, from the pending guards, so it costs one dialog however many threads are open.

### 2.4 A highlight the party is near

- **Cited** (Game Accessibility Guidelines, *Give a clear indication that interactive elements are interactive*): players "can have difficulty distinguishing which UI elements or in-game items are intended to be interactive". **Cited** (the Witcher 3 REDkit wiki, *Work with clues, containers (all that glows)*, by its title and the search summary of its body): the senses mode highlights "gameplay entities such as containers, posters, clues, lights, doors" and a player source names its range as "very limited". **Cited** (the Portal post-mortem as reported live at GDC 2008): the force field that playtesters did not understand was changed to glass — the object was changed until it read. **Authored**, from the three: the first remedy for a prop that does not read is the author's (a better block, a better scene); where the scene must keep the block, the author declares a highlight, and it lights only near, so it is a cue on arrival and never a beacon from afar.
- **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Glowing*): "The Glowing effect causes entities to glow with an outline that can be seen through blocks and entities"; the outline "is white by default, but can be set to display other colors if the entity is part of a team". **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Display* and its display-entity data template): "Display entities have no hitbox"; `block_display` "can display any block model using a block state object"; `glow_color_override` "Overrides the glow border color. If -1, uses the color of the team that the display entity is in"; `transformation.scale` scales "the model centered on the origin" and `translation` moves it; `view_range` defaults to 1.0 and the entity is drawn within a distance scaled from 64 blocks. **Cited** (a Planet Minecraft comment on a block-display pack): a display scaled to about 1.001 sits just outside the real block and does not fight its texture — a community observation, not an official statement. **Authored**: the glow outline is the highlight; a block is given one through a `block_display` of its own state, fitted a hair larger than the block so the two do not fight; a body is given one through the glowing effect and a team colour; the outline is drawn per entity, so it is seen by every player once any player is near (§7.4, a stated limitation).

### 2.5 A journal read any time

- **Cited** (the Skywind principles, §2.1): "descriptive objectives and detailed journal entries" are what makes a quest completable without markers, and the journal must keep old information available. **Cited** (`game-writing.md` §8 L1): "the objective journal restates what the story has already told the player … so a player who skimmed or forgot can look it up" — a sentence the engine had no surface for until now. **Authored**, from play: a chat line is gone once the player disconnects, and the chat history shows nothing older than the session, so the record of the open and done objectives is an item in the hand — an item is saved with the player and comes back with them.
- **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Data component format*, `written_book_content`): `pages` is "a list of the pages in the book", each page's text "a text component representing the text content of the page"; `title` "overrides the base item name" and is plain text; `author` "is shown in the item tooltip". **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Item modifier*, `minecraft:set_written_book_pages`): "Manipulates the pages of the `minecraft:written_book_content` component"; `pages` is "a list of pages as filterable text components", each "a text component"; `mode` is one of `append`, `insert`, `replace_all`, `replace_section`; `/item modify entity <targets> <slot> <modifier>` "invokes a modifier alone upon the target slot"; a modifier's `conditions` are predicates that must all pass. **Authored**, from it: a book whose pages depend on state is rewritten in its slot by a chain of static item modifiers — one that replaces all pages with the fixed prefix, then one per objective that appends that objective's page, each applied under the objective's own guard — so the book is dynamic while every page the engine can write is a committed file; no block, no macro over text and no NBT edit of a player's inventory, which vanilla does not allow.
- **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Text component format*, `keybind`): "Displays the name of the button that is currently bound to a certain configurable control … This uses the client's own control scheme." **Cited** (a map-making guide, minecraftmaps.com): written books are the form adventure maps give players for rules and welcome text. **Cited** (`chrome.rs`): a compiler-authored line is a keyed chrome string translated with the engine. **Authored**: the manual pages are the author's, in the campaign's voice, carrying a key through the key span and never a typed key name; the compass page is the engine's, because the compass is the engine's mechanism.
- **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Dialog*): a data pack may add dialogs to the pause screen through the `#minecraft:pause_screen_additions` tag. **Authored**: considered and not taken — a pause-screen page is a second surface for the journal's job, cannot list what is open (§2.3), and a book in the hand is the one the player already knows.

### 2.6 Sight, and how far it reaches

- **Cited** (`body.rs`, `blocks_camera`; `walk_cells`): a line of sight is stopped by a full-cube solid, a 1.5-tall fence or wall, or a fence gate; the traversal is the one a declared sightline (`DW0821`) and the cutscene clip are proven with. **Cited** (spec-0091 §2.5; Mojang bug tracker MC-157896): a body past 176 blocks is never sent; the client draws a body of a block or less to 64 blocks at default settings. **Cited** (`promise.rs`, `SIGHT_BLOCKS`): the one reach sight has is 64 blocks (unsupported claim, as the first draft stated: no reading of a player at a screen was taken; the demo level is where it is looked at).

### 2.7 A pressable among its own kind, reconsidered

- **Cited** (the motivating campaign): three identical `red_mushroom_block` valves stood together and nothing said which to turn. **Cited** (the measurement commit): the gallery's second and third valves were refused among four twin cells and were changed to buttons to pass. **Authored — the finding re-read**: the valves were a puzzle with no clue, which is a design fault of that scene; a row of identical levers with a clue elsewhere is a legitimate puzzle, and a rule that forbids the row forbids the puzzle. The rule infers an intent the author did not declare — that twins are a mistake — against CLAUDE.md's *a check confirms intent; it never restricts*. It is removed (§9). The half of the finding that is a mechanism gap — a prop that does not read as the thing — is the highlight's (§7).

### 2.8 The motivating campaign, measured

**Cited** (`campaigns/the-stranding/quests.json` at `campaign/stranding`): `guidance` is `{announcements: shown, markers: hidden}`; 30 objectives — 16 `interact`, 8 `talk-to`, 6 `kill`; 18 carry a `title`, 12 a `hint`; none declares `marker`; the first objective is an untitled, unhinted `interact` on a `spruce_sign`; 5 of the 6 kills are untitled; 44 triggers, of which 13 are `use` and 8 `strike-assembly`. **Cited** (the measurement commit on this branch): `DW0863` over 105 acts on 3 walks found 36 announced with a hint, 45 in sight and 24 refused (8 distinct acts); `DW1015` read 26 of 60 strings (§2.2). Under this revision the campaign's `markers: hidden` line is deleted with the field (§7.6), its titles are warned about and not refused, and its redo declares `highlight` where a prop does not read and writes a manual only if its setting contradicts vanilla.

## 3. A hint says where to go and what to do — a writing rule

**Authored — the rule.** A `hint` names the act as the player performs it and a place the player can find from where they stand — a landmark they can see and a position relative to it or to themselves (`game-writing.md` §1 F1's landmark rule). *Past the bell, left of the arch: pull the lever by the gate.* It never names a compass point, a solver term or a thing the player has not been told (§10).

**Where it lands.** The `/new-delve` skill's `references/writing-craft.md` §F (functional text) carries the rule as a hard rule beside the verb rule, and `references/quest-capabilities.md` §Objectives replaces its *Hint wording* bullet with it. No machine reads a hint for whether it says where to go (§2.2); the machine's half of a findable act is §4 and §10, and the creator agent is the reader of this rule. A hint judged wanting in a playtest is a content finding against that campaign, never a new check.

## 4. An act the party can find — `DW0863`, widened to every act

**Authored — the rule, unchanged from the first draft and implemented on this branch.** On every walked path — the exported critical path and each realised branch's own path, the walks `Flow::journal` already takes for `DW0982`, each fixed with the links its route proof took (`acts::fix_walks`) — every act is **found** when either holds:

1. **it is announced with a `hint`** (`Objective::announced` and a non-empty `hint`; a trigger has no lines and cannot take this arm); or
2. **it is in sight from where the party stands**: a clear line, by `walk_cells` over `World::blocks_camera`, from the eye of a body standing on the **previous act's cell** — the `pos()` of the preceding positioned `Step`, or where that step's completion carries the party, or the entry cell for the first act — to the act's **target**, in the configuration that walk holds there, within `SIGHT_BLOCKS`; and, for a `kill`, the wave's anchor is also within the wave's reach of that cell (`wave_aggro_radius`).

The **target** per kind: an `interact`'s anchor cell; a `reach-anchor`'s anchor cell; a `talk-to`'s NPC station on that path; a `collect`'s container cell, else the compiler's chest at its anchor, else the dropping wave's anchor; a `kill`'s wave anchor; a `use`/`strike` trigger's `at` cell, or the struck NPC's or assembly's cell. The eye is `cell_center(prev) + (0, 1.62, 0)`; the target point is chest height for a body or a wave, the block's centre for a block. A kill fired by several acts is judged from each firing act's cell.

An act that is neither is refused with `DW0863`, naming the path, the previous act and its cell, the target and its distance, the first blocking cell, and both remedies — give it a `title` and a `hint`, or move the act or its target into sight. **This is a tightening against the engine before this branch, in those words**: a `kill` found by reach alone is no longer found; an objective of another kind out of sight with no hint, which nothing refused, is refused. The compass (§6) is not a third arm: it points, and the hint or the sight says what the thing is.

**Pairs.** `DW0862` (a hint on an unannounced objective) is unchanged and is this rule's other half. A hint written to satisfy arm 1 is read by §5 and §10 as well. Binding line (`ActBinding`): `DW0863 binding: N act(s) over W walk(s) — A announced with a hint, S in sight (K kill(s) also in reach), T trigger(s) judged by sight alone; R refused`. Zero acts is stated.

## 5. A title names the act its type completes on — `DW1015`, a warning

**Authored — the lexicon, kept.** `crates/dsl/data/act-verbs.json` holds, per objective kind and per language, the verbs that name the act vanilla completes the kind on; one verb belongs to one kind in a language (`actverbs.rs`'s unit test holds the five sets disjoint); English matches whole words and phrases case-insensitively, Chinese matches substrings, longest entry first (`Table::verbs_in`). The seed is the first draft's; a verb a campaign needs that is not here is an engine edit to the data file.

**Authored — the rule, demoted.** For every objective, in the English document and in every sidecar whose language has a table: a **title** with no verb of its own kind's set, and a **hint** with a verb of another kind's set and none of its own, are reported as `DW1015` **warnings** — `Diagnostic::warning`, `Severity::Warning`, `Group::AboutTheCampaign` — and the run **exits 0** when nothing else refuses. The message states the string, the verb it found and the kind that verb belongs to, lists the kind's own verbs, and says what the creator decides: whether the title names what the hands do. **This is a loosening, in those words**: the first draft refused these strings; §2.2's measured false positives (a noun read as a verb, reported speech read as a talk) are why the verdict is the creator's. Nothing else moves: the check runs at `delvec validate` beside `DW0862`, and the binding line stays: `DW1015 binding: N title(s) and H hint(s) over L language(s) judged (U string(s) in languages with no table); R warned`. A gallery probe cannot carry a warning (`check-gallery-coverage.py` demands a refusal), so the rule's coverage is its tests (acceptance 3) and the gallery's own titles, which all carry their kind's verb.

## 6. The compass — each player aims it at an open objective

### 6.1 The surface

Stage 5, in the `guidance` block spec-0093 owns:

```json
{
  "guidance": { "announcements": "shown", "compass": "shown" },
  "quests": [{
    "objectives": [
      { "type": "interact", "id": "obj/the-slate", "anchor": "anchor/slate", "title": "Read the slate", "compass": "hidden" }
    ]
  }]
}
```

`guidance.compass` is a `Visibility`, `shown` when absent — the campaign's default. `objectives[].compass`, on every kind, is the objective's own value, absent means the default. An objective is **tracked** when it is **announced** (spec-0093: a `title` and a resolved `announcement` of `shown`) and its resolved `compass` is `shown`. An objective nobody is told about is not on the compass: `compass: shown` on an unannounced objective is `DW1017` (§6.6). A campaign with `guidance.compass: hidden` and no objective opting in gives no compass and no compass page in the journal (§8.2); the engine states `compass binding: 0 tracked objective(s) of N`.

### 6.2 The item

**Authored.** Every player receives, with the kit at class selection (`class_apply_<c>`), one `minecraft:compass` carrying `custom_name` (the chrome string `compass.name`, `italic: false`), `custom_data` `{dw_compass: 1b}` so the engine never touches another compass, and a `lodestone_tracker` the engine writes (§6.3). It is re-given on respawn and whenever a refresh (§6.3) finds none of the player's 41 slots — `container.0`–`container.35`, `armor.head`, `armor.chest`, `armor.legs`, `armor.feet` and `weapon.offhand`, the list `emit/checkpoint.rs` already walks for the rest's mend, holding it (`execute unless items entity @s <slot> minecraft:compass[custom_data~{dw_compass:1b}]` over every one of them), so a dropped compass comes back. The bot's path never touches it. **Read from the pinned jar** (`SlotRanges`, `Player#getSlot`): `container.36`–`container.53` resolve to nothing on a player, so the armour and off-hand slots are named, never numbered. **Read from the pinned jar** (`CompassItem#getName`): a compass carrying `lodestone_tracker` is named `item.minecraft.lodestone_compass` and never reads `item_name`, so the name is a `custom_name`, which `ItemStack#getHoverName` reads first; a `custom_name` is drawn italic unless it says otherwise.

### 6.3 Where it points

**Authored — a derivation, never typed.** Each player holds a per-player score `dw.track`: the ordinal of the tracked objective they follow (the engine's own numbering, document order: quest order in `quests.json`, then objective order), `0` for none. A player's **followed** objective is the tracked objective their `dw.track` names when that objective is **pending** — its `pending_guard` holds: quest active, every `after` complete, its gate, not complete — else the **first pending tracked objective in document order**, else none. The engine resolves this in `compass_refresh`, run `as` a player, and aims the compass:

- the compass in whichever of the player's 41 slots holds it (`execute if items entity @s <slot> …`, one line per slot over `container.0`–`container.35`, `armor.head`, `armor.chest`, `armor.legs`, `armor.feet` and `weapon.offhand`, the list `emit/checkpoint.rs` already walks for the rest's mend) is **replaced** with a compass whose `lodestone_tracker` is `{target: {pos: <target>, dimension: "minecraft:overworld"}, tracked: false}` — `tracked: false` because no lodestone stands there, `minecraft:overworld` because it is the one dimension a delve builds (`emit/server.rs` generates no other) — through a macro function the target is handed to;
- with nothing to follow, the compass is replaced with one whose `lodestone_tracker` has **no `target`**, which vanilla draws as a spinning needle: *nothing is open*. A plain compass pointing at world spawn was rejected because the spawn is a place with no meaning in a delve.

The **target** per kind is §4's, with two that move: an `interact`'s or `reach-anchor`'s anchor cell; a `collect`'s container cell, else the compiler's chest, else the dropping wave's anchor; a `talk-to`'s NPC body — its live position (`@e[tag=dw_npc_<n>,limit=1]`, read into the macro's storage), because an NPC may walk (spec-0101); a `kill`'s **nearest living body of its wave**, per player — the body carrying the wave's tag (`dw_wave_<id>`) nearest the player holding the compass (`execute at @s`, `sort=nearest,limit=1`; `@e` selects only living entities, so a body in its death animation is never picked), its live position read into the macro's storage — and, while no body of the wave is alive (it has not spawned, or every body has died before the objective completes), the wave's anchor. §4 still judges a `kill` at its wave anchor: sight is proved before anything spawns.

**When it refreshes.** `compass_refresh` runs as every player at every objective activation and completion and every quest activation and completion (the events that move the pending set), as the one player at a pick (§6.4), a join and a respawn, and every 20 ticks for a player whose followed objective is a `talk-to` or a `kill` (`compass_aim_followed` on the clock `#gclock`, for exactly the players whose `dw.tbody` the last aim set). When the `kill` completes, its completion refresh resolves the followed objective again by the rule above. Nothing runs per tick for a static target, so a player arranging their inventory is not fought.

### 6.4 The choice

**Authored.** The engine's compass carries `minecraft:consumable` (`consume_seconds` longer than any hold, `animation: none`, no particles), so a right-click with it in either hand starts using it, and the advancement `compass_use` (`minecraft:using_item` matching the compass by its `custom_data`) runs as that player every tick it is held in use; a per-player latch (`dw.cuse`, kept while the use continues and run down by the tick after it stops) makes one press open the menu once. The menu is a `multi_action` dialog built in storage at the moment it opens: its title the chrome string `compass.choose`, and one button per **pending** tracked objective in document order — the label its title (with the chrome mark `journal.following` when it is the one followed), the tooltip its hint — whose action is `run_command` `trigger dw.cpick set <ordinal>`; it is shown by `$dialog show @s $(dialog)` and closes on a click (`after_action: close`), and it does not pause the game. With nothing open the menu is not shown and the player reads `compass.nothing`. The tick enables `dw.cpick` for every classed player, and a player whose `dw.cpick` moves is **picked**: `dw.track` takes its value, the compass refreshes, the line of §6.5 is told, and the journal is rewritten so the mark moves. A pick that names an objective no longer pending resolves as §6.3 does, to the first pending one. Any of k open objectives is one press and one click away.

### 6.5 What the player is told

**Authored.** Whenever a player's followed objective **changes** — by a pick, by completion, by a new objective opening when none was followed — the player reads one chat line: the chrome string `compass.following` with the objective's title (`Following: Read the slate`), and, when more than one tracked objective is pending, a second chrome string `compass.others` with the count and the input (`Other open tasks: 2 — use (Right Button) the compass to choose.`, the key as a `keybind` argument). When nothing is open, `compass.nothing` (`Nothing to follow yet.`). The chrome strings are rows of `dsl::chrome::ALL`, translated with the engine, and the counts stated in `docs/reference/compiler.md`'s chrome section move with them. The line is the moment; the journal (§8) is the record, and the same change rewrites the player's journal so the followed objective wears its mark there.

### 6.6 `DW1017` — a compass on an objective nobody is told about

Validation tier (`promise`, beside `DW0961`): `compass: shown` on an objective that is not announced — no `title`, or a resolved `announcement` of `hidden` — is refused with `DW1017`: the compass would point at a thing the player has no name for and the line of §6.5 would have nothing to say. The remedy is the objective's: announce it, or drop the field. An inert declaration is refused, never ignored (spec-0093 §7's rule). Binding line: `compass binding: T tracked objective(s) of N — F by their own field, D by the campaign default; R refused`.

## 7. The proximity highlight — the thing is lit while a player is near

### 7.1 The surface

Stage 5, on the acts that have a block or a body to light:

```json
{ "type": "interact", "id": "obj/the-valve", "anchor": "anchor/valve", "prop": { "block": "minecraft:lever[face=wall,facing=north]" }, "title": "Turn the valve", "highlight": { "within": 6 } }
```

`highlight: { within }` is admitted on `interact`, `collect`, `talk-to` and `kill` objectives and on every `triggers[]` entry; `within` is the range in blocks, an integer from 1 to 64 (the schema's bound; `SIGHT_BLOCKS` is the farthest an outline is drawn, §2.6). **The author declares it per act**: a check never infers that a block does not read (CLAUDE.md, *a check confirms intent*). Absent means no highlight, and a campaign that declares none states `highlight binding: 0 highlight(s)`.

### 7.2 What is lit

**Authored — the body per target**, the same object `compiler::pressable` and §4 already resolve:

| act | what glows |
|---|---|
| `interact` with a `prop`, or standing on an authored block; `use`/`strike` trigger with a `prop` or on an authored block; `collect` with a container | a `minecraft:block_display` summoned at the cell with the block's exact state, `transformation.scale` `1.002` and `translation` `-0.001` so it stands a hair outside the real block, `glow_color_override` the engine's highlight colour; it has no hitbox, so the click still reaches the block or the hitbox beneath |
| `talk-to`; `strike-npc` trigger | the NPC's body (`dw_npc_<n>`), through the glowing effect and the engine's `dw_hl` team for the colour |
| `kill` | the wave's bodies (`dw_wave_<id>`), the same way, from the moment they exist |

A block the client draws through a block-entity renderer rather than its block model — the bell among the hand-pressed blocks (`blockshape::is_hand_pressed`), the chest among containers — shows in a `block_display` as its model alone; for those the engine summons an `item_display` of the block's item at the cell instead, oriented by the state's `facing`. The list of such blocks is engine data with its provenance recorded; it is **authored** here and confirmed on the demo level (§12), which is where a bell's outline is looked at.

### 7.3 When it is lit

**Authored.** While the act is **armed** — an objective pending, a trigger armed — the tick judges `execute at <the target cell or body> if entity @a[distance=..<within>]`, with the observer guard the engine's polls already carry, and toggles the glow on the **edge** through a per-act holder score: on when a player first comes within range, off when the last leaves. The display is summoned with the act's prop at activation (or at `setup_finish` for a trigger) and removed at completion with the objective's cleanup; a body's glow is cleared at completion. A highlight is never on before the act is armed: a lit lever behind a shut gate is the beacon §2.4 rejects.

### 7.4 What vanilla cannot do here

**Cited** (§2.4): the glow is a flag on the entity, drawn by every client that sees it. **Authored — the limitation, stated**: a highlight one player's nearness turns on is seen by every player within entity range; a split party sees a glow across the map through walls while a teammate stands at the thing. The outline is drawn only within the entity's render range (64 blocks for a one-block body), which bounds the effect. Per-viewer glow needs per-viewer entities, which vanilla has no primitive for; the feature is bounded here rather than built on a hack.

### 7.5 `DW1016` — a highlight with nothing to light

Validation tier (`promise`): `highlight` on a `collect` with no `container` (the items fall from a wave and nothing stands to light), or on a trigger that is an `approach`, a `step` or a `strike-assembly` (a place, a plate's cell, or an assembly the engine lights as no one body), is refused with `DW1016`, naming the act and the remedy (drop the field, or give the act a thing to light). An `interact` or a `use`/`strike` trigger with no prop on open air is `DW0963` already (§7.6). Binding line: `highlight binding: H highlight(s) over A act(s) — B on a block, Y on a body; R refused`.

### 7.6 What this supersedes in spec-0093

**Authored.** The always-on lantern over a prop-less `interact` and the end rod over a `reach-anchor` are **superseded**: `objectives[].marker`, `guidance.markers`, `Objective::marker_shown`, the two summons in `emit/objective.rs` and `DW0962` (a marker beside a prop) are removed. One mechanism per job: the highlight is the one marker kind, it binds to the thing the player acts on, and it asks the author. Spec-0093 §6.5 proposed this in its *what stays open*: "the hitbox is the wrong carrier for any player-found object; moving the shown path to a required prop touches every existing interact without one and the lantern's whole purpose, so it is proposed, not done." It is done here. **`DW0963` tightens, in those words**: an `interact` with no `prop` whose cells hold air in the assembled world is refused with no marker hatch — the act sits on a visible block (a `prop` or an authored block) or it is refused; a `reach-anchor` has nothing to show and is found by sight, hint and compass. On this tree the cost is 4 prop-less `interact` objectives across the gallery and the fixtures and 4 `marker` declarations; the motivating campaign deletes one line.

## 8. The journal — the manual, the open objectives and the done ones, read any time

### 8.1 The surface

Stage 5, in the `guidance` block:

```json
{
  "guidance": {
    "manual": {
      "title": "The Ferryman's Notes",
      "pages": [
        "Things on this shore answer to a hand. Stand close and [[key|use]] on a lever, a bell, a sign.",
        "The ferry comes when the bell is rung twice. Once is for the dead."
      ]
    }
  }
}
```

`manual` is the author's part of the journal and is optional; **absent is the default and the right choice for most delves**. An author writes it only for a setting that contradicts vanilla behaviour or for an item that needs instructions (the rule lands in the skill, §13). `title` names the journal in the hand (the chrome title `journal.title`, `Journal`, when absent); `pages[]` is one string per book page, each a line of the span grammar (spec-0096) with the key span `[[key|…]]` for any key the text names; both are inventoried (`guidance.manual.title`, `guidance.manual.pages.<i>`, `TextKind::Narration`) and transcreated like any line. `pages` may not be empty when `manual` is declared (the schema's bound). Nothing else about the journal is authored: what it lists is derived from the objectives.

### 8.2 The book, and what it holds

**Authored — one item, four sections, in this order.** Every player receives, with the kit at class selection, one `minecraft:written_book` with `custom_data` `{dw_journal: 1b}`, `item_name` the journal's title translated (so the hand and the tooltip read the player's language), and `written_book_content` with `title` empty, `author` the campaign's English title, and `pages`. **Read from the pinned jar** (`ItemStack#getCustomName`, `WrittenBookContent`): a non-blank `written_book_content.title` names the item ahead of `item_name`, as plain text of at most 32 characters, so the title is written empty — the codec admits it — and the translated `item_name` names the book. The pages are:

1. **The compass page** (only when the campaign has a compass, §6.1): the chrome strings `journal.compass.*` — what the compass follows, that using it (the use key as a `keybind` argument) opens the list of open tasks to follow, that a spinning needle means nothing is open, and that the open tasks are the pages that follow.
2. **The manual** (only when `guidance.manual` is declared): the author's pages, each lowered through `emit::text::tr` so it is a translate component with its English fallback and its key spans as `keybind` components.
3. **Open** (chrome heading `journal.open`): one page per **announced** objective that is currently **pending** (its `pending_guard` holds), in document order: its title in bold, then its hint when it has one, and the mark `journal.following` (`◆ You are following this.`) on the one this player's compass follows (§6.3). An objective nobody is told about never appears, as it never appears on the compass.
4. **Done** (chrome heading `journal.done`): one page per announced objective that is **complete**, in document order, its title struck through — one per page because the primitive of §8.3 writes whole pages and never a line inside one.

A campaign with no announced objective, no compass and no `manual` gives no book and emits nothing for it, byte for byte. A campaign with any of the three gives the book; the sections it has no content for are absent, never empty headings.

### 8.3 How the pages are rewritten

**Authored — a chain of committed item modifiers, applied in the slot.** The engine emits, per campaign, the item modifier `journal_prefix` (`minecraft:set_written_book_pages`, `mode: replace_all`, the compass page and the manual pages) and, per announced objective, `journal_open_<obj>` (`mode: append`, the objective's open page), `journal_open_following_<obj>` (the same page with the mark) and `journal_done_<obj>` (`mode: append`, the objective's done page). `journal_rewrite`, run `as` a player, finds the slot holding the player's journal (`execute if items entity @s <slot> minecraft:written_book[custom_data~{dw_journal:1b}]`, one line per slot over the 41 slots of §6.3, as §6.3 finds the compass) and runs a macro `journal_write` with that slot, which applies `journal_prefix`, then for each objective in document order `execute if <pending guard> if score @s dw.track matches <ordinal> run item modify … journal_open_following_<obj>`, `execute if <pending guard> unless score @s dw.track matches <ordinal> run item modify … journal_open_<obj>`, and after all open pages `execute if score <party> <obj_score> matches 1 run item modify … journal_done_<obj>`. Every page the engine can write is a committed file in the datapack; `item modify` edits the book where it lies, so the player's hotbar does not reorder. Two builds are byte-identical.

**When.** `journal_rewrite` runs as every player at every objective activation and completion and every quest activation and completion — the events `compass_refresh` runs at (§6.3) — and as the one player at a compass choice (the mark moves), a join and a respawn.

### 8.4 Each player holds their own, and gets it back

**Authored.** The book is per player, because an item is, and because the mark of §8.2 is per player; its open and done sections are the party's state and read the same in every copy at the same moment. **A late joiner or a reconnecting player** holds a journal written from the current state: a join runs `journal_rewrite` (the `dw_joined` path for a first join, the per-tick join detection for a return) and gives the book first when the player has none. **A dropped or lost journal**: a `minecraft:item` entity whose `Item` carries the engine's `custom_data` (`dw_journal` or `dw_compass`) is removed by the tick, so a stale copy never lies on the floor, and the next `journal_rewrite` or `compass_refresh` gives the player a fresh one. A journal that survives a death in the inventory is rewritten at respawn; one that does not is re-given there.

### 8.5 A web shows as several open pages, and one mark

**Authored.** In a web (`web-narrative.md` §2–§4) several announced objectives are pending at once; each is a page of the open section, so a player reads every open thread by title and hint, and the mark says which the compass follows. A thread not yet unlocked has no page: the journal never names what the player has not been told (`game-writing.md` §8 L1, §10). An objective a branch has closed leaves the open section the moment its guard stops holding (its quest ended, or a flag now forbids it) and enters the done section only if the party completed it; the journal reads the party's scores and infers nothing. The compass page says how to move the mark.

### 8.6 The chat line stays

**Authored.** Spec-0093's announcement — `New objective: <title>`, the hint, the sound, `Objective complete: <title>` — is **kept beside the journal**: the chat line is the moment, the journal is the record, as a quest-updated toast and a journal are two surfaces of one event in the games §2.3 cites. `compass.following` (§6.5) stays for the same reason. Nothing moves to the journal alone.

### 8.7 Opening text

**Authored — none by default.** The engine writes no opening line and asks for none. A campaign that wants one writes a `narrate` in its first quest's bundle, as today. **The `teaching` block is removed** (§8.9): the journal is where instructions live, because a line in chat at the first lever is gone by the second.

### 8.8 A page fits — `DW0330`, widened

**Cited** (`textfit.rs`): `DW0330` measures an on-screen line against its box with the pinned font and warns, in the source and every declared translation. **Cited** (Minecraft Wiki, *Book and Quill*): "No page may be longer than 14 lines, and each line can have a width of 114 pixels", and "the width of a line of text is determined by the number of pixels, not characters". **Authored**: a journal page is on-screen text in that box, which no GUI scale changes, so `DW0330`'s population gains the pages the engine writes: each `guidance.manual.pages.<i>`, the compass page, and each announced objective's open page (its title and hint together, the mark's line counted), a key span measured at the longest default key name as the first draft fixed (`textstyle::measured`); a page that overflows is a `DW0330` warning naming the page and language. **Read from the pinned jar**: the 100-page limit is the book and quill's (`WritableBookContent.MAX_PAGES`); a written book's `pages` codec, its network codec, `set_written_book_pages` (`ListOperation.UNLIMITED_CODEC`) and `BookViewScreen` bound no page count, and `BookViewScreen` wraps a page at 114 pixels and draws at most 14 lines, which is the box `DW0330` measures. The journal therefore has no page bound. **This is a loosening, in those words**: the first text of this revision refused a campaign whose announced objectives plus prefix exceed 100 pages, on the wiki's book-and-quill limit; the written book has no such limit, so nothing is refused.

### 8.9 What this removes

`guidance.teaching`, `Teaching`, `TeachLine`, `InputClass`, the input classes derived per act (`acts::class_of_objective`, `class_of_trigger`, `Act::class`, `required`, `teach_sites`, `TeachSites`), the emission (`emit/teaching.rs`, `emit/packtest/teaching.rs`, the activation and tick and setup lines), and the first draft's `DW1016` (teaching and need disagree) are removed. **This is a loosening, in those words**: no check asks that an input be taught before it is needed. What stands behind it: the highlight picks out the thing to act on where the block does not read, the journal carries instructions a player can return to, and a vanilla player right-clicks a lever without being told. The key span stays — the manual's pages carry it, the engine's compass page carries `[[key|sneak]]` through its argument, and the translator's fact check keeps a key span whole (`tools/creator/i18n-translate.py`, unchanged from the first draft).

## 9. A pressable among its own kind — the rule is removed

The first draft's `DW1017` (no block of the same id within `INTERACTION_REACH` of a pressable), `TwinBinding` and `check_pressables_alone` are removed. **This is a loosening, in those words**: a lever in a row of levers builds. §2.7 is the reason: the rule infers a defect from a shape that is also a legitimate puzzle. The ledger row `str-08` is re-triaged `content`: its note records that only the instance — three identical valves with no clue — was a design fault of that scene, that the half of the finding about a prop not reading as the thing is the author's `highlight` (§7), and that no general check exists or is owed. `DW0861` is unchanged (an adopted container still owes its `title` and `item_name`).

## 10. A hint's names are told — `DW0982`, widened

**Authored, unchanged from the first draft and implemented on this branch.** `TextKind::Objective` strings — an objective's `title` and `hint` — are **uses** as well as tellers: a declared name occurring in one is judged against when the play order told it, at the state the objective is announced; a title that introduces a name by `telling.rs`'s own `introduces` rule tells and is not refused. The message is `DW0982`'s, naming the objective.

## 16. What stays open

- **The journal's granularity.** The primitive writes whole pages, so a done objective costs a page and the done section is not a packed list (§8.2); a packed list needs a page per reachable combination, which is the dialog's cost (§2.3) in another place.
- **Per-viewer highlight.** Vanilla draws a glow for every client (§7.4).
- **A hint's landmark.** Whether a hint names a place the player can find is prose (§2.2, §3): the skill's rule and QC read it.
- **A sign as a prop.** In adventure mode the click reaches the engine's hitbox over a sign and nothing else; whether a sign reads as pressable is the author's, and `highlight` is the author's answer where it does not.
- **The skill's own ordering defects** are fixed on their own pull requests; this spec touches neither.
