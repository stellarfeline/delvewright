- **What it is for**: a player who presses a thing and is told what happened, on a thing that is really there. In a playtest, a prop that an objective would later open was clicked before its step and said nothing; a puzzle solved by several presses went on accepting presses after it was solved and punished one of them; and in a second playtest a shortcut's far-side unlock — a glowing lever icon the compiler summons beside an invisible hitbox — was seen glowing through the shut door from the wrong side and pressed from there, giving the shortcut away before the party had earned it. The general form of all three: every click target is a block the world has, drawn and occluded like the world, and it answers every press — *not yet* before its step, and no change to settled state after. The engine today summons an invisible `minecraft:interaction` for every interaction point and, for several of them, a permanently glowing `minecraft:item_display` icon; it spends a click that arrives outside the window in silence, and never stands a repeatable trigger down because an objective it feeds has completed. The engine already has the whole mechanism for a press on a block (`default_block_use` at the block's cell, spec-0093 §6.5), for a press that must not reach the block under it (`compiler::clickbox::ClickBox`, a `minecraft:interaction` box strictly nearer than any block it stands in — `DW1013`), for a press answer (a `use` trigger addressed to its presser, a `narrate` on the action bar, an advancement that observes without consuming) and for a thing lit only when a player is near (spec-0104 §7). This spec makes a real block the body of every interaction point — pressed on its own where the pinned game reports the press and the press changes nothing the world keeps, and through an invisible click box fitted to the block's own outline everywhere else, so that a door does not open, a lever does not flip and a chest does not open its screen while the press is not its turn — and extends the answer to every pressable.

The pinned game's own report of a press on a block is read in §2a, with read sites. Each statement below is marked **cited** (a line of the tree at the ground revision, a reading of the pinned jar or pinned data, a published source on a record page, or a constitution rule) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0117`. ## 1. What the tree does today, re-verified

Each claim the finding's record made is re-read at the ground revision; none has changed. One fact the record did not name is added last, because it decides the shape of this spec.

- **Cited** (`emit/functions.rs`, the activation driver; `emit/objective.rs`, `activation_commands`): an `interact`'s `minecraft:interaction` body — or, for a prop vanilla reports the use of, the prop block alone — is placed by `activate_<obj>` the tick the objective's `pending_guard` first holds, once, behind the `#act_<obj>` sentinel. **Cited** (`completion_cleanup`): `complete_<obj>` runs `kill @e[tag=dw_i_<obj>]`. So the body exists exactly while the objective is pending; a prop block placed at activation stays as scenery. **Cited** (`plan/path/region.rs`): the region model registers that prop as a write sealed from load and forced from the completion step, because "activation has no step of its own".
- **Cited** (`emit/functions.rs`, the `interact` tick lines): the completion line runs under `pending_guard` and the hand guard; the `missing_item_hint` line runs under `pending_guard` and the negated hand guard; the reset `scoreboard players reset @s dw.i_<obj>` runs unconditionally. A click that arrives while the objective is not pending is spent and nothing is said. For a hand-pressed prop the `i_<obj>` advancement fires on `default_block_use` at the cell in every state, so a lever the piece authored is flipped before its step and spent in the same silence.
- **Cited** (`quest/objective.rs`, `l10n.rs`): `missing_item_hint` is the one authored answer an `interact` carries, inventoried as `obj.<quest>.<obj>.missing_item_hint`, kind `Refusal`; no field answers a press before the step or after it.
- **Cited** (`emit/trigger.rs`, `trigger_poll_guards`): a trigger's dispatch gate is its `once` sentinel, its `forbids_flags`, its `requires_flags` and its `requires_state`, and nothing else; no term reads an objective. A `once: false` trigger whose writes feed an objective's gate fires after that objective completes exactly as before.
- **Cited** (the campaign, counted): 30 objectives, 16 `interact` (3 with `requires_item`, all 3 with a `missing_item_hint`); 41 triggers, 14 `use`, every one `once: false`, 4 gated by flags, 10 addressed to the presser, 6 of them answers to sealed things. The repeated-press shapes are seven repeatable strike triggers each counting into a datum and standing down on a flag they set themselves, and three repeatable, ungated `use` triggers each setting a datum. The puzzle's own stand-down read a flag set later than the solve; the engine had no way to say *this press belongs to that objective*.
- **Cited — what the record did not say** (`compiler.md`, the press-answer rows; `trigger.rs`, `DW0429`; `plan/trigger.rs`, `collect_press_answers`): a press answer is already "not a mechanism — it is an ordinary trigger": `EnvTrigger { on: use, audience: presser, once: false, effects: [narrate { style: actionbar, text }] }`, authored, or synthesized by the compiler from a `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint`; dispatched by a `player_interacted_with_entity` advancement whose reward revokes itself so the object answers every press; gated through `trigger_poll_guards`; emitted, inventoried, proven and claimed (`DW0811`) by the same code every authored click uses. ## 2. What practice does

**Cited** (`press-answers.md` §1–§3): feedback within a reasonable time is how a user learns the outcome of a prior interaction and decides the next step (Nielsen Norman #1); an error says in plain language what is wrong and what to do (#9; `game-writing.md` F2); essential information is conveyed neither by sound alone nor by text alone (Game Accessibility Guidelines); the pinned game's own refused use — a locked container pressed without its key — plays a sound and writes one line on the action bar, and `minecraft:block.chest.locked` is that sound in the pinned registry.

**Cited** (spec-0104 §2.4): a thing that does not read as usable is first repaired by the author — a better block, a better scene; where the scene keeps the block, a highlight is declared and lights only near, "a cue on arrival and never a beacon from afar"; the glow outline "can be seen through blocks", so a permanent one is a beacon.

**Authored — the synthesis.** A refused press is answered on the action bar, to the presser, on every press, with a line the author wrote and a sound the engine plays; the engine never writes the words; a pressable that would be silent in a state a player can press it in is refused at build, as a sealed body is. The thing pressed is a real block the world draws and occludes; nothing visible stands in for it, and where its own press would act or would go unreported an invisible box fitted to it takes the press instead; it glows only near, only while the press can act, and only from the side the press is made from.

## 2a. What the pinned game reports of a press on a block

Every statement is **cited** from the pinned server jar named in the ground line unless marked otherwise.

- **The one place a press on a block is reported.** `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn(ServerPlayer, Level, ItemStack, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)` reads the block at the hit position; in spectator mode it opens a menu or passes; otherwise it computes `flag` (either hand holds an item) and `flag1` (the player is sneaking **and** `flag`), copies the stack, and, when `flag1` is false, calls the block's `useItemOn`; if that result `consumesAction()` it fires `CriteriaTriggers.ITEM_USED_ON_BLOCK` and returns; if the result is `TryEmptyHandInteraction` and the hand is `MAIN_HAND` it calls the block's `useWithoutItem`, and if **that** `consumesAction()` it fires `CriteriaTriggers.DEFAULT_BLOCK_USE.trigger(player, pos)` and returns; otherwise it falls through to the item's own `useOn` (firing `ITEM_USED_ON_BLOCK` on a consumed result) or returns `PASS` (bytecode offsets 0–320). `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.handleUseItemOn` fires `CriteriaTriggers.ANY_BLOCK_USE` **only when** `useItemOn`'s result `consumesAction()` (offsets 272–296). So: a right-click on a block whose use nothing consumes fires **no advancement criterion at all**; `minecraft:default_block_use` is exactly "the block's own empty-hand behaviour ran", held by `location` predicates to a block id and a position (`block_use_criterion`); `minecraft:any_block_use` adds nothing a pressable needs. A sneaking press with an item in either hand bypasses the block entirely and is never reported; so is an off-hand press.
- **The defaults.** `BlockBehaviour.useWithoutItem` returns `PASS`; `BlockBehaviour.useItemOn` returns `TRY_WITH_EMPTY_HAND`. A block that overrides neither is **unobservable**: stone, iron bars, a barrier, a wall, a sign that is waxed (its body was not read; stated from the class list and the override alone).
- **The bell.** `BellBlock.useWithoutItem` → `onHit(level, state, hit, player, true)` → `SUCCESS` when it rang, else `PASS`. `onHit` rings only for a **proper hit** (`isProperHit`): never on a `Y` face, never above `0.8124` of the block's height, and by attachment — floor: the face lies on the bell's `facing` axis; single or double wall: the face lies off that axis; ceiling: any side. `attemptToRing` calls the block entity's `onHit(direction)`, plays `SoundEvents.BELL_BLOCK` (volume 2, pitch 1), emits `GameEvent.BLOCK_CHANGE`, and **writes no block state**. A ring is an animation and a sound that end by themselves.
- **The button** (14 ids in the pinned registry, every `*_button`: acacia, bamboo, birch, cherry, crimson, dark oak, jungle, mangrove, oak, pale oak, polished blackstone, spruce, stone, warped). `ButtonBlock.useWithoutItem`: when `powered` is already true it returns `CONSUME` — the press is **reported and changes nothing**; otherwise `press` sets `powered=true`, updates neighbours, schedules its own tick after `ticksToStayPressed` (**20** for the stone and polished blackstone buttons, **30** for every wooden one — the constructor constants in `Blocks`), plays the click and emits `BLOCK_ACTIVATE`; `SUCCESS`. A press is a pulse that reverts by itself; during the pulse a second press is reported and does nothing.
- **The lever.** `LeverBlock.useWithoutItem` → `pull`: `powered` toggled and written, neighbours updated, the click played, `BLOCK_ACTIVATE`/`BLOCK_DEACTIVATE`; `SUCCESS`. A press is a **persistent** state change the world shows until the next press.
- **The door.** `DoorBlock.useWithoutItem`: when the door's `BlockSetType.canOpenByHand()` is false it returns `PASS` — the iron door (`iron`: hand `false`) is **unobservable**; otherwise `open` is toggled and written, the sound played, `BLOCK_OPEN`/`BLOCK_CLOSE` emitted; `SUCCESS` — a wooden or copper door (`oak`, `stone`, `copper`: hand `true`) **opens**. `TrapDoorBlock.useWithoutItem` reads the same `BlockSetType.canOpenByHand()`, returns `PASS` when it is false, else `toggle` and `SUCCESS` — the iron trapdoor is unobservable, every other trapdoor opens. `FenceGateBlock.useWithoutItem` reads no `BlockSetType`: when `open` it writes `open=false`; else it writes `facing` toward the player and `open=true`; then the sound, `BLOCK_OPEN`/`BLOCK_CLOSE`, and a consumed result — every fence gate opens by hand.
- **The campfire.** `CampfireBlock` overrides only `useItemOn`, which consumes the action only for an item the `CAMPFIRE_INPUT` recipe set accepts (`placeFood`); an empty hand falls to the default `useWithoutItem` → `PASS`. A campfire is **unobservable** as a press target.
- **The chest.** `ChestBlock.useWithoutItem` → `openMenu` on the server and returns `SUCCESS` **whatever happened in the menu** — a container carrying a `lock` the hand does not match is still reported (the pinned game's own refused use, press-answers §3, is this path). A chest's press is reported, and its effect is a screen.
- **Overriding one or both methods** (headers read, bodies not): the barrel, lectern, note block, decorated pot, chiseled bookshelf, cauldron, candle, flower pot, jukebox, bed, cake, crafting table, shelf, copper golem statue, repeater, comparator, daylight detector, enchanting table, anvil. Each one's empty-hand use either opens a screen or writes a state the world keeps (**unsupported** per block beyond the override; each is a click-boxed body, §3a).

## 2b. What the pinned game gives a block for an outline, and what a click box is

Every statement is **cited** from the pinned server jar and mappings named in the ground line unless marked otherwise. Sixteenths throughout; `[a,b]` is a closed range on an axis within the block's own cell.

- **The shape helpers** (`Block`): `cube(s)` is the cube of side `s` centred in the cell; `cube(x,y,z)` the centred box of those three extents; `column(xz, y0, y1)` the square column of side `xz` centred, from `y0` to `y1`; `column(x, z, y0, y1)` the same with two footprint extents; `boxZ(xy, z0, z1)` the box of `xy` across and `xy` tall centred, from `z0` to `z1` on z; `boxZ(x, y, z0, z1)` with two cross extents; `boxZ(x, y0, y1, z0, z1)` with every extent named. `Shapes.or` is the union, `Shapes.join(a, b, ONLY_FIRST)` is `a` minus `b`; `rotateHorizontal`, `rotateHorizontalAxis`, `rotateAll` and `rotateAttachFace` each build a map from one canonical shape to every orientation of its property.
- **The lever** (`LeverBlock.makeShapes`): canonical `boxZ(6, 8, 10, 16)` — the wall lever facing north, `[5,11]` across, `[4,12]` tall, `[10,16]` deep against the wall it hangs on — mapped by `rotateAttachFace` to `face` and `facing`: a floor lever stands `[0,6]` tall on a 6 × 8 base whose 8 lies along `facing`'s axis; a ceiling lever hangs `[10,16]`; a wall lever's 8 is vertical. Its outline does not change with `powered`.
- **The button** (`ButtonBlock.makeShapes` and its shape lambda): canonical `boxZ(6, 4, 8, 16)` mapped by `rotateAttachFace`, then clipped by `join(shape, cube(12), ONLY_FIRST)` while unpowered — `[5,11]` across, `[6,10]` tall, `[14,16]` deep for the wall button facing north; a floor button is `[0,2]` tall on a 6 × 4 base — and by `cube(14)` while powered, leaving one sixteenth (`[15,16]`). The pressed button is thinner than the unpressed one.
- **The bell** (`BellBlock.<clinit>`): `BELL_SHAPE` = `column(6, 6, 13)` ∪ `column(8, 4, 6)` — the bell `[5,11]²` from `[6,13]` over its lip `[4,12]²` from `[4,6]`. Ceiling: ∪ `column(2, 13, 16)` (the hanger). Floor: `cube(16, 16, 8)` under `rotateHorizontalAxis` — the whole frame, `[0,16]` across and tall, `[4,12]` deep, the bell inside it. Double wall: ∪ `column(2, 16, 13, 15)` (a bar across the cell) under `rotateHorizontalAxis`. Single wall: ∪ `boxZ(2, 13, 15, 0, 13)` (a bar from the wall to the bell) under `rotateHorizontal`.
- **The door** (`DoorBlock.<clinit>`): `boxZ(16, 13, 16)` under `rotateHorizontal` — a leaf `[0,16]` across and tall, three sixteenths deep against one face of its cell, the face chosen by `facing`, `open` and `hinge`; the same leaf in each of the two halves. A door is one block in two cells.
- **The trapdoor** (`TrapDoorBlock.<clinit>`): the same leaf under `rotateAll` — closed bottom `[0,3]` tall over the whole cell, closed top `[13,16]`, open: the leaf against the face opposite `facing`.
- **The fence gate** (`FenceGateBlock.<clinit>`): `cube(16, 16, 4)` under `rotateHorizontalAxis` — the bar `[0,16]` across and tall, four sixteenths deep on the gate's axis; the `in_wall` variant is derived from it (**unsupported**: the derivation and the open-state selection were not read).
- **The campfire** (`CampfireBlock.<clinit>`): `column(16, 0, 7)` — the whole footprint, `[0,7]` tall.
- **The chest** (`ChestBlock.<clinit>`): `column(14, 0, 14)` — `[1,15]²` from `[0,14]`; a double chest's halves are `boxZ(14, 0, 14, 0, 15)` under `rotateHorizontal`, reaching the shared face.
- **No vendored file carries these.** `collision-tops-1.21.11.tsv` carries each state's collision bottom and top, measured by `getCollisionShape`; the lever and every button carry `-` there (no collision). An outline is `getShape`, a different method with a different answer (**cited** from the tree's `PROVENANCE.md` row and the dumper).
- **The click box's own shape** (`Interaction`, `EntityDimensions`): the entity's dimensions are `EntityDimensions.scalable(width, height)` from its synced `width` and `height` (each `1.0` by default); `makeBoundingBox(x, y, z)` is the box from `x − width/2` to `x + width/2`, `y` to `y + height`, `z − width/2` to `z + width/2`. **A click box is a square in plan**: one `width` serves both horizontal axes, centred on the entity's position; its floor is the position's `y`. `isPickable()` returns `true`.
- **A press on the box is consumed and recorded.** `Interaction.interact(Player, Hand)` on the server writes `interaction = {player: <uuid>, timestamp: <game time>}` and returns `CONSUME` (on the client, `SUCCESS` when `response` is set, else `CONSUME`); the block under the box is never asked. `skipAttackInteraction(Entity)` for a player attacker writes `attack = {player, timestamp}`, fires `CriteriaTriggers.PLAYER_HURT_ENTITY` for that player with zero damage on a server level, and returns `!response`. The client picks the entity over the block when the entity's box is strictly nearer along the crosshair's ray (**cited** from `compiler::clickbox`'s module docs, which name `GameRenderer.pick`; the client jar was not read here).

## 3. The decision: one pressable class, one press-answer mechanism, three states

**Authored — the object class.** The thing a player right-clicks is a **pressable** — `compiler::pressable` is already the one authority for its body, for an `interact` objective and for a `use` trigger alike, and §3a makes it the authority for every interaction point the engine arms. A pressable is in one of three states at any tick:

| State | An `interact` | A trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **open** — the press acts | its `pending_guard` holds (quest active, every `after` complete, its gate, not complete) | its own gate holds and, if `once`, it has not fired; with `serves` (§4), also the served objective is pending or the served gate is sealed |
| **settled** — the act is done | `dw.o_<obj>` is 1, or its quest is complete | `once` and fired; or `serves` and the served objective is settled or the served gate is open |
| **shut** — not open and not settled | any other tick | any other tick |

An `interact` with `requires_item` has a fourth answer inside **open**: the hand lacks the item (today's `missing_item_hint`). The other pressables of §3a have the same three states: a shortcut unlock and a disarm are open while armed and settled once spent; a bonfire and a shop are shut before their arming beat (the bonfire) and open ever after; neither settles.

**Authored — the answer is the existing press-answer trigger, synthesized from a declared wording.** Exactly as `sealed_hint` was lowered by `collect_press_answers` into a presser `use` trigger riding the seal's body, each answer declared here is lowered into a presser `use` trigger on the pressable's own block, gated to the one state it answers, carrying `narrate { style: actionbar, text }` under `Audience::Solo`. Nothing is added to the emitter but the gate derivation; the dispatch, the l10n path, the `env_trigger_<id>` PackTest, the `DW0811` claim and the `dw-` id namespace (`DW0428`) are the ones the seal answer already has. Two consequences follow and are taken: the block of a pressable must exist in every state a player can press it in, so it stands from `setup_finish` to the end of the delve (§5.1); and the states a press can be in are what decide which answers are owed (§6).

**Authored — no baked default, so silence is refused.** `DW0429`'s reasoning is adopted whole: a default wording or a default-only cue would be the compiler making a design statement about this door on the author's behalf. A pressable that would be silent in a state a player can press it in is `DW1051`, the sibling of `DW0429` over the pressables it did not cover. The engine's half — the block, the dispatch, the state, the sound — is supplied; the words are the campaign's. This is a tightening against the engine before this spec, in those words: an `interact` that answers nothing before or after its step, which nothing refused, is refused.

**Rejected, authored.**

- *A default line from compiler chrome, hidden by a switch.* The quest chrome (`New objective:`) names a thing the engine owns; the words a shut door says are the campaign's tone, which `DW0429` already refuses to invent.
- *An answer that is an effect bundle.* It would let a not-yet answer write a flag or a done answer move the world — the very writes the finding forbids after a step settles — and would need a refusal for writing verbs that the string form makes unnecessary. A reply is one `Refusal` line; a beat is a trigger the author writes.
- *A party-addressed answer.* A reply to a press tells the one who pressed (`trigger.rs`: "broadcasting it tells four players about a door three of them are nowhere near").
- *Refusing a `once: false` trigger that feeds an objective and is not stood down.* A bell that can be rung forever and tallies its rings for an ending is a legitimate design; which of two readers of a datum is "the" objective a press serves is a candidate, not a match (CLAUDE.md, a resolve-by-name over a non-unique scope). The author states it (§4 `serves`), and the skill says when to.
- *Objective state as a term of the one gate* (`requires_flags` / `forbids_flags` / `requires_state` gaining an objective term). The general form, and the right home in the long run; it touches every gate consumer and every flow proof, and this finding needs one binding, not a fourth gate term. Recorded open (§11).

## 3a. The body is a real block the world has, pressed on its own or through a click box fitted to it

**Authored — the rule.** An interaction point — a thing the delve asks a player to click — is a **real block at a cell** the player sees, drawn and occluded exactly as the world is; no `item_display` marks it. It is pressed in one of two forms, and the block decides which (§2a, §2b):

- **on its own** — where the pinned game reports the empty-hand press (`default_block_use` at the cell) **and** the press changes nothing the world keeps: the bell (a ring that ends by itself), the fourteen buttons (a pulse that reverts by itself), and the lever **where every press acts** (an ungated, repeatable, unserved `use` trigger, a bonfire, a shop — the flip *is* the act, and no state exists in which a flip would show what the story does not have). Nothing is summoned; the block is the body and the game's own report is the detector;
- **through a click box** — everywhere else: an invisible `minecraft:interaction` fitted to the block's own outline (§2b, the fitted rule below) stands over the real block, so the crosshair's pick lands on the box and never on the block inside (`DW1013`). The box takes the press, the block does not act, and the press is reported to the engine with its presser (`player_interacted_with_entity` on the box's tag, the detector every seal answer already used). Three reasons a block gets the box, each read in §2a: its press **acts on its own** (a door opens, a trapdoor and a fence gate open, a lever flips where a shut or settled state exists, a chest, a barrel, a lectern or a note block opens a screen or keeps a state); its press is **unobservable** (a campfire, an iron door, an iron trapdoor, bars, a wall, any block with the default `useWithoutItem`); or the press is a **left-click** (`strike` at a place: the pinned game reports no left-click on any block). Where the block should act by vanilla once its turn comes — a door that may now be opened by hand — the box is **removed** for that window and the block's own report is the detector (the state table below).

A body a player strikes or speaks to — an NPC's dialogue hitbox, the assembly a player hits — is a **creature or a thing that is hit**, not an interaction point; it keeps its own fitted `interaction` entity, which is the body's shape and not a click box of this class. The recovery stake's drop is the one interaction point that stays an entity (§11): its cell is chosen at runtime, so no block can stand for it; it loses its permanent glow and gets no glow that shows through a wall.

**Authored — what becomes of `compiler::pressable::Body`.** `Block { cell, block }` is the body of every interaction point; a `Block` body carries, derived and never typed, whether it is pressed on its own or through a click box, and the box's fitted geometry. `Rides { tag, owner }` remains for exactly two owners — an NPC's hitbox (a `strike` at its stand anchor, `strike-npc`) and an assembly's body (`strike-assembly`) — and for nothing else. `Point` and `Region` are **removed**: a click box stands over one real block at its cell, never in the air and never over a region (`DW1059`, §6). `Nothing` stays (`DW0426`: the anchor resolves to nowhere).

**Authored — the fitted rule.** `ClickBox::fitted_to(cells, outline)` is the one builder of a click box over a block, beside `ClickBox::sized` (an assembly's declared hitbox); `ClickBox::column` goes. Given the block's outline — every box of its `getShape` for the exact blockstate placed, in sixteenths, read from the measured table `crates/dsl/data/outline-shapes-1.21.11.tsv` (below) — and its envelope `E = [x0,x1] × [y0,y1] × [z0,z1]` over the body's cells (one cell; two for a door): with `m = CLICK_MARGIN` (`0.01`), the box's `width` is `max(x1 − x0, z1 − z0) + 2m`, its `height` is `(y1 − y0) + 2m`, its floor is `y0 − m`; on the longer horizontal axis its centre is `E`'s centre; on the other axis its centre is `E`'s centre moved by the least amount that keeps the box inside the body's cells expanded by `m` on every face (a leaf 16 across and 3 deep gets the cell-wide square centred on the cell; a wall lever's 6 × 6 square sits against its wall, `m` into it). Every number is exact decimal (`Dec`); sixteenths are exact in it. Two facts hold by construction and `DW1013` proves them over the shipped bytes (§6): the outline lies inside the box by at least `MIN_PROTRUSION` on every face — the lemma of `compiler::clickbox` restated over the outline instead of the unit cube, so every ray from an eye outside the box that reaches the block has crossed the box first — and the box lies inside the body's cells expanded by `m`, so it reaches no neighbouring cell's own affordance. **Cited** (§2b): the entity is a square in plan, so a leaf gets a square that is the cell on its thin axis; that is the shape the pinned entity has, and no second entity is summoned to narrow it.

**Authored — the shape table is measured.** `crates/dsl/data/outline-shapes-1.21.11.tsv` is produced by `tools/maintenance/dump-outline-shapes.py`, the sibling of `dump-collision-tops.py`: every blockstate's `getShape(EmptyBlockGetter.INSTANCE, BlockPos.ZERO, CollisionContext.empty())` as its list of boxes in sixteenths, with the jar's and the mappings' hashes in its header (the frozen measurement names its instrument by exact revision). `delvewright_dsl::blockshape::outline` reads it — the one authority for "what is this blockstate's outline", beside `measured_collision`. §2b is the second method: the constants read from the bytecode, sharing no configuration with the runtime dump; the implementation lands the comparison (`blockshape::tests`: each §2b shape equals the table's row) committed. A blockstate the table gives no box for (air, a fluid, `structure_void`, `light`) has no outline and is not a body (`DW1059`).

**Cited, then authored — which form each block gets.** From §2a: a block is pressed on its own when its empty-hand use is **reported** (its `useWithoutItem` consumes the action) **and** that use **changes nothing the world keeps**; every other real block is pressed through a click box. The kinds, and the body in each of §3's states:

**Authored — the acknowledgement is not an effect.** A button's pulse and a bell's ring are the pinned game saying *the press landed* — the same role press-answers §2 gives the engine's cue — and both are over within a second and a half, leaving the world as it was. A lever's flip is a statement about the world that stands until someone changes it; a door's opening is the act the story was keeping. That is the line between the two forms, and it is drawn by what the block keeps, not by what it shows for a moment.

**Authored — release.** A boxed block that acts on its own has, at settlement, one of two futures, and the author states which: the box **returns** (the default) and the block answers `done` and does nothing — the lever that lowered the bridge stays down; or the block is **released** (`release: true`) and is vanilla's thereafter — the door the party unbarred is a door, opened and shut by hand, and answers nothing because opening is the answer. A released pressable owes no `done` and admits none (`DW1052`). `release` on an own-form block, which was never boxed, is inert (`DW1052`); `release` on an unobservable block would leave a silent scenery block where a pressable stood (`DW1051`).

**Authored — the box and the window.** For an `interact`, the window opens and closes at two events the engine already has — `activate_<obj>` kills the box, `complete_<obj>` returns it unless released. For a `use` trigger whose gate is a predicate over flags, state and `serves`, the box is **reconciled against the gate** each tick: while the gate holds no box stands, while it does not hold one does (§5.1). For an unobservable block and for every `strike` the box stands from `setup_finish` to the end of the delve, and the dispatch gate alone decides what a press does. A box over a block the engine removes — a sealed gate filled to air by `shortcut_open_<id>` or an `open-gate` — is killed in the same function.

**Authored — the sealed door answers for itself; the knocker is the second form.** A sealed gate's own block — the leaf of a barred door, the bars of a grate — is a real block the player sees and pushes on, and with a click box over it the push is intercepted and answered: a `use` trigger with `serves: {gate: <gate anchor>}` anchored at a cell of the gate region takes the gate's block there as its body, with no `prop` (the gate already names its blocks; a second declaration of one cell's block is `DW1052`'s inert shape), is open exactly while the gate is sealed, and has **no settled body**: `shortcut_open_<id>` and an `open-gate` fill the region to air and kill the box in the same function, so it owes and admits no `done` (`DW1052`). **Recommended** over the knocker wherever the gate has a block that reads as the way through, because the thing the player pushes on is the thing that answers. The **knocker** — a bell or a button face-adjacent to the region on the side that owes the line, a `use` trigger with the same `serves` — remains admitted for a gate with no such block (a tunnel filled with rubble, a wall of a dozen cells), and, being a block that stays, it does owe `done`. `DW0429` accepts either form (§6). For a shortcut, whose door is pressed from both sides, the gate-answer trigger speaks to a presser on the **sealed side** — the compiler derives that gate term from `wrongside`'s approach cells, the author types nothing — and the shortcut's own `answers.not_yet` speaks to a presser on the **far side** (§4), so one leaf has two lines and the engine knows which side the hand is on.

**One cell, one block.** Two pressables whose cells coincide declare the same block or are refused (`DW0896`, re-stated over blocks, §6); one press on the shared block fires every advancement whose criterion names that cell, each as its own listener, so two `interact` objectives on one bell in sequence build. Two boxed pressables on one cell share **one** click box (a second co-located box is the ray-pick tie `DW0422` names) carrying both tags; a door's two cells are one body and one box.

**A door is named by its lower half.** A `prop` naming a door names the `half=lower` state; the compiler places both halves (`setblock` places exactly the state it is given, so the upper half is derived — same `facing`, `hinge` and `open`, `half=upper`) and fits one box over the two cells. A `prop` naming `half=upper` is refused (`DW1059`). This is the one body that spans two cells, and it spans them because the pinned game's door does.

**Declared as a loosening, in those words:** this spec's class is block-only **except the recovery stake marker**, which keeps its entity body until its block form is specified (§11); the ruling it executes admits no exception, and this spec records one. The block-only amendment's removal of `strike` at a place, and its exclusion of the lever with a shut or settled state, of doors, of containers and of the campfire, are **withdrawn** by this amendment: each is a real block with a click box fitted to it; criteria 2, 5 and 14 assert less than they did, and say so.

## 3b. Visibility — drawn like the world, lit only near and only from the right side

**Authored.** A block body is drawn and occluded by the client exactly as every block is; nothing the compiler summons marks it — a click box is a `minecraft:interaction`, which renders nothing, so a boxed block looks exactly like the same block unboxed. Every `Glowing:1b` the compiler emits for an interaction point is gone with its icon (`affordance_hardware` and every caller; the `dw_marker` and `dw_hw_<tag>` tags; the stake marker's flag). The one glow mechanism is spec-0104 §7's `highlight { within }`, admitted on every pressable of §3a (the `interact`, the `use` trigger, the shortcut unlock, the bonfire, the shop, each disarm) and on `stakes[]`, so a creator who wants a thing lit declares it per act, and a campaign that declares none states its zero. This folds in the defect recorded as the issue titled *guidance: compiler-summoned hardware glows through walls and gives a shut way away from the wrong side*: the far-side lever icon that glowed through the door is gone, and its block is a button or a bell the wall hides.

**Authored — the lit set.** A highlight is lit while the pressable is **open** (§3) and a player stands in its **lit set**: the walkable cells within `within` of the block from which the block's pressed face is in sight — line of sight by `body.rs` `blocks_camera`, the traversal spec-0104 §2.6 cites — computed at build and emitted as the union of boxes the tick tests, instead of §7.3's sphere. A shortcut unlock's lit set lies on the far side; a knocker's on the side it answers for; a player on the other side of the wall, alone, lights nothing. **Cited** (spec-0104 §7.4): the outline, once lit, is drawn for every client within entity range, through blocks; a split party with one member in the lit set shows the other the glow. The limitation stands as spec-0104 states it; this spec narrows when the glow is on, not who sees it. Binding line: `highlight binding: H highlight(s) over A pressable(s) — C lit cell(s) in B box(es); R refused`.

In this example the bell and the buttons are pressed on their own; the latch door, the reliquary chest and the sluice lever are boxed in their shut state and act by vanilla in their open one; the latch is released at completion and the chest is not; the toll door's leaf answers from the sealed side through the gate's own block and from the far side through the shortcut's `not_yet`; the boulder is struck through a box that stands in every state.

**`prop`** (`{ block }`, the carrier spec-0093 §6.5 gave the `interact` and the triggers) is **required** on every interaction point — the `interact` objective, the `use` and `strike` trigger at a point anchor, `shortcuts[]` (placed at `unlock`), the `bonfire` effect (at `anchor`), `shops[]` (at `anchor`), a trap's and a timed gate's `disarm` (at `via`) — admitted on no `approach` or `step` (`DW0964`), and admitted on no trigger with `serves: {gate}` anchored inside the gate region (its body is the gate's block, §3a). Its block is any blockstate with an outline (§2b); the block decides its form (§3a): own-form for a bell, a button, or a lever on a pressable with only the open state; boxed for everything else. A block with no outline, and a door named by its upper half, is `DW1059`. The compiler places it at `setup_finish` (§5.1); the written world lays it too (`assembled::shipped_blocks`). `shops[].marker_item` and `stakes[].marker_item` are **removed** (they named the icons); `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint` is **removed** (its body is gone; its one meaning is the gate-answer trigger's `narrate`).

**`release`** (boolean, default `false`) is admitted on every pressable with a settled state whose block is boxed because it acts on its own — the `interact` objective, the `use` trigger, `shortcuts[]`, each `disarm`: at settlement the box does not return and the block is vanilla's thereafter (§3a). A released pressable owes and admits no `done`. On an own-form block, on an unobservable block, and on a pressable with no settled state (a bonfire, a shop, an ungated unserved repeatable trigger) it is refused (`DW1052`, `DW1051`, §6).

**`answers`** is an object carried by every pressable with a shut or settled state the engine can name a presser for — the `interact` objective, the `use` trigger, `shortcuts[]`, the `bonfire` effect, each `disarm` — holding one `Refusal` line per state the pressable has: `not_yet` (shut), `done` (settled — not owed where the pressable is released or has no settled body) and, on an `interact` with `requires_item`, `missing_item` (open, hand empty). On `shortcuts[]`, `not_yet` is what the **sealed door** says to a presser on the far side while it is barred (the unlock itself has no shut state), and `done` is what the unlock says once thrown. Each line is optional in the schema and owed by `DW1051` where the state exists (§6). Keys in the l10n inventory: `obj.<quest>.<obj>.answers.not_yet` / `.missing_item` / `.done`, `trigger.<id>.answers.not_yet` / `.done`, `shortcut.<id>.answers.not_yet` / `.done`, `fx.<path>.answers.not_yet` for a bonfire, `trap.<id>.disarm.answers.done` and `timed_gate.<id>.disarm.answers.done`, kind `Refusal`, each with its `situation` (as the title's rows already carry). **`missing_item_hint` is removed**: its one meaning is `answers.missing_item`, and a second field for the same answer class is the defect CLAUDE.md names. A document carrying a removed field is refused as an unknown field (`deny_unknown_fields`); the content is changed, with no shim.

**`serves`** is an optional object on every `triggers[]` entry naming the thing this press is part of: `{ "objective": <ObjectiveId> }` or `{ "gate": <AnchorId> }` — one field whose referent's kind the object states, never two fields for one binding. It binds the trigger's window to that thing's: with an objective, the trigger is open only while its own gate holds **and** the objective is pending, settled when the objective is; with a gate — a `close-gate` target or a shortcut's `gate` — open only while its own gate holds **and** the gate is sealed, settled while the gate is open, and shut before a `close-gate` has fired (a shortcut is sealed from load and has no shut state). The reference resolves like `after` does for an objective and like `at` does for an anchor; an unknown id is the existing unknown-objective or unknown-anchor refusal; a gate anchor no `close-gate` or `shortcut` seals is `DW1052`'s inert-declaration shape. A `serves: {gate}` trigger whose `at` resolves to a cell **inside** the gate region is the gate's own answer (§3a): its body is the gate's block at that cell, it admits no `prop`, it has no settled body and so owes no `done`; one whose `at` is face-adjacent to the region is a knocker and owes `done` as any trigger with `serves` does. For a shortcut's gate, the gate's own answer is dispatched only to a presser standing in the sealed side's approach cells (`wrongside`), a gate term the compiler derives. `serves` is admitted on every trigger kind, because standing down at a settlement is a property of the trigger, not of how it is pressed; the **answers** of a `serves` trigger are admitted only where the presser can be named (§6).

**`highlight`** (`{ within }`, spec-0104 §7.1) is admitted on every pressable of §3a and on `stakes[]`; its lit set is §3b's.

**What is not changed.** `once`, `audience`, the three gate terms keep their meaning; a presser `use` trigger the author writes as an answer is unchanged in form, and now carries `serves: {gate}` where it speaks for a sealed gate.

## 5. Emission

### 5.1 The body stands for the whole delve

**Authored.** Every pressable's block is placed at `setup_finish`, through one list — `pressable::props`, `trigger_props` widened to every interaction point of §3a, a door contributing both halves — which the written world lays too, so the server's world and the shipped world cannot disagree about a block. Every **boxed** pressable's click box is summoned at `setup_finish` too, through `ClickBox::fitted_to` and nothing else, tagged `dw_fixture` and its owner's tag (`dw_i_<obj>`, `dw_trig_<id>`, `dw_sc_<id>`, `dw_bonfire_<i>`, `dw_shop_<i>`, `dw_trapdis_<trap>`, `dw_tgdis_<gate>`, `dw_gate_<safe>` for a gate's own answer), `response:1b`, `Invulnerable:1b`; nothing else is summoned for any interaction point. The window moves the box where the block acts on its own: `activate_<obj>` kills `@e[tag=dw_i_<obj>]` and `complete_<obj>` re-summons the same box unless `release`; a boxed `use` trigger's box is **reconciled** in `tick` against its own gate — `execute <gate holds> run kill @e[tag=dw_trig_<id>]` and `execute <gate does not hold> unless entity @e[tag=dw_trig_<id>] run summon …` — with `trigger_poll_guards` spelling the gate on both lines, so the box is the gate made solid; `shortcut_open_<id>`, `trap_disarm_<trap>` and `tgate_disarm_<gate>` re-summon their boxes unless released, and `shortcut_open_<id>` and `open-gate` kill the gate's own answer box with the blocks they fill to air. An unobservable block's box and every `strike` box stand untouched. `activation_commands` places the `collect` chest and nothing for an `interact`; `completion_cleanup` kills nothing for an own-form `interact`. The `#act_<obj>` sentinel and `activate_<obj>` remain for the `collect` chest. **The region model** (`plan/path/region.rs`) registers every prop as a write forced from step 0, which is what it now is; the unforced-from-load arm is kept for the `collect` chest alone. **Cited** (`compiler.md`, `interact` row): the kill at completion existed "so a finished objective is not clickable"; under this spec a finished objective is clickable and answers `done`, which is the point — or is released and is vanilla's.

**The cost, stated.** Every campaign's bytes move (`setup_finish` gains the blocks and loses every summon but the NPC's and the assembly's; `complete_<obj>` loses the kill; the region events change; `tick` loses every `interaction`-record poll; the advancements change criterion); `gallery/baseline/` is regenerated once; the `obj_activate_<obj>`, `v04_interact_cleanup`, `souls_shortcut`, `souls_timed_gate_disarm_<id>` and the shop and stake PackTests change what they assert (a block present before activation and after completion; a granted `default_block_use` advancement where a hitbox record was written). No byte-identity claim is made for any existing document. `compiler::clickbox` keeps every click box the engine places and `DW1013` widens to prove each fitted to its block (§6); `ClickBox::column` goes; `compiler::affordance` keeps `DW0545` and loses `DW0420`/`DW0421`; `validation/press-bodies.json` records the block of every press, its form (own or boxed), and for a boxed one the fitted box; `validation/click-box.json` gains, per judged box, the block and the outline it was judged against. The tick gains one reconciliation pair per boxed `use` trigger whose gate can move; the count is stated in the binding line.

### 5.2 The answers, lowered

**Authored.** For each declared answer the compiler synthesizes one presser `use` trigger, `trigger/dw-answer-<owner>-<state>` (`<owner>` the pressable's safe id; `<state>` one of `not-yet`, `missing-item`, `done`), on the owner's block: its `press_<id>` advancement is `block_use_criterion` at the owner's cell where the answered state has no box (an own-form block in any state; a block that acts on its own in its open state), and `player_interacted_with_entity` on the owner's box tag where it has one (every boxed shut and settled state; an unobservable block in every state) — the two criteria `press_dispatch_fn` already dispatches, chosen by the state's body and never typed. The answer's effects are `narrate { style: actionbar, text }` under `Audience::Solo`, followed by `scoreboard players set #ans_<owner> dw.sys <n>` (1 not-yet, 2 missing-item, 3 done) — the trace write every trigger already makes on `#trig_<id>` (`emit/trigger.rs`: "the write is what makes trigger dispatch observable at all"), which is what a PackTest reads, since an action-bar line leaves no game state. **Every answer plays the engine's refusal cue** — `CUE_REFUSED`, `minecraft:block.chest.locked`, the sound the pinned game pairs with its own refused use — through `cue_line`, beside `CUE_ANNOUNCE`, `CUE_COMPLETE` and `CUE_CAMPAIGN`, and `validation/cues.json` states it for the bot. **The gates** are derived, never typed: the interact's `not_yet` carries the negation of its `pending_guard` and the negation of its settled term; `done` carries the settled term; `missing_item` carries the `pending_guard` and the negated hand guard, replacing the tick line that carried the same today (one click, one line, as before). A trigger's `not_yet` carries the negation of `trigger_poll_guards`' flag, state and `serves` terms with the once term not fired; `done` carries the once-fired term or the served objective's settled term or the served gate's open term. A shortcut's and a disarm's `done` carry their sentinels (`#sc_<id>`, `#trapdis_<trap>`, `#tgdis_<gate>`); a bonfire's `not_yet` carries the negation of its armed term. **One authority**: `pending_guard` and `trigger_poll_guards` are read, not restated; the implementation lands an agreement test that drives an objective through its states and asserts, per state, that exactly one of {completion, `not_yet`, `missing_item`, `done`} can dispatch.

### 5.3 `serves`

**Authored.** `trigger_poll_guards` gains, for a trigger with `serves`, the served objective's `pending_guard` terms or the served gate's sealed term as further space-terminated fragments, so every dispatch route — `press_<id>`, `step_<id>`, the assembly's strike clause — gates identically. `path_triggers`' `enabled` and `Flow`'s link liveness read the same fact: a `serves` trigger is performable only while its objective is pending, or its gate sealed, on that walk; the agreement test of §5.2 covers it. After the objective settles the trigger never dispatches again, whatever `once` says, so no write it would have made reaches settled state; after a gate opens its knocker answers `done` and nothing else.

### 5.4 Nothing else moves

The bot's `/trigger dw.i_<obj>` and `/trigger dw.rest` paths, the completion marker channel, the announcement chrome, the journal and the compass (spec-0104), `takes_item` (spec-0114), the harness's `interact` and `trigger` steps: unchanged. A harness step that today right-clicks an `interaction` entity reads the body's form from `validation/press-bodies.json` and, inside the window, activates the block at the cell where the open state has no box and right-clicks the box where it has one (an unobservable block, a `strike`); the step's shape and its allowlist entry do not change. A bot never presses outside a window, so it never reads an answer; the PackTests do.

## 6. Diagnostics

**`DW1059` — a press target that is not a real block** (`dsl::trigger`, beside `DW0426`'s subject and `DW0429`; error; validation tier, exit 1). Quantifier: every interaction point of the primary document — `interact` objectives, `use` and `strike` triggers, `shortcuts[]`, `bonfire` effects, `shops[]`, trap and timed-gate `disarm`. Arms, each with its reading from §2a or §2b in the message: (a) no `prop` where one is required (a point-anchored pressable with nothing at its cell has nothing the player sees); (b) a `prop` whose blockstate has no outline in the measured table — air, a fluid, `structure_void`, `light` (a click box may stand over a real block only, never in the air); (c) a door `prop` naming `half=upper` (a door is named by its lower half, §3a); (d) an `at` that resolves to a gate **region** anchor (a region is many cells; anchor the trigger at the door's cell, where the gate's block is its body). Path: the pressable's `prop` or `at`. Binding line: `press bodies (DW1059): P interaction point(s) — O pressed on their own (B bell, U button, L lever), X boxed (A acting on their own, N unobservable, S struck), R refused`. **Why a new code**: `DW0426` says the anchor resolves to nowhere; `DW0963` judged the assembled world at build for a thing nothing shows; `DW1051` says a press is silent; none says *this body is not a real block*, and that is refused where the prop is typed, with no world built. The excluded entity — the recovery stake's drop, an NPC's body, an assembly — is never a click box's cell by construction: the stake has no compile-time cell, and a pressable at an NPC's stand anchor or inside an assembly keeps the refusals it has (`DW0350`, `DW0359`).

**`DW1051` — a press that would be silent** (`dsl::trigger`, beside `DW0429`, in `press_answer_checks`; error; validation tier, exit 1). Quantifier: every pressable of §3a with a shut or settled state in the primary document. Owed and missing: `not_yet` on every `interact`, and `done` on every `interact` not released; `missing_item` on an `interact` with `requires_item`; `not_yet` on a `use` trigger with any gate term or a `serves` whose referent has a shut state; `done` on a `use` trigger with `once: true` or a `serves`, unless released or unless it is a gate's own answer (no settled body); `not_yet` on every shortcut (its sealed door, pressed from the far side) and `done` on every shortcut and every disarm not released; `not_yet` on a bonfire whose arming beat is not at step 0. A further arm, the silent block: `release` on an unobservable block (the released block would answer nothing, and no line can be written for it). Path: the pressable's `answers` or `release`. Message: the pressable, the state, what a player would meet there (a press that does nothing and says nothing), and the remedy — write the line, in the campaign's own words; the answer's l10n key. Binding line: `press answers (DW1051): P pressable(s) — A answer(s) owed, D declared, R refused; G gate answer(s) judged by DW0429; E released`. A `use` trigger with no gate, no `once` and no `serves`, a shop, and a bonfire armed at step 0 have only the open state and owe nothing; each is counted. A `strike` is never owed an answer (`DW0427`, §11).

**`DW1052` — an answer for a state the press never has** (same module; error; validation tier). `missing_item` without `requires_item` (today's `DW0437`, retired into this code); `not_yet` on a `use` trigger with no gate term and no `serves`, or with `serves: {gate}` on a shortcut's gate (sealed from load); `done` on a `use` trigger with `once: false` and no `serves`; `done` on a shop; `done` on a released pressable (the block is vanilla's; there is no settled press to answer); `done` on a gate's own answer (its body is filled to air with the gate); `serves: {gate}` naming an anchor no `close-gate` or shortcut seals; `release` on an own-form block (it was never boxed) or on a pressable with no settled state; `prop` on a trigger anchored inside the gate region it `serves` (the gate names its block; a second declaration of one cell is the second authority). An inert declaration is refused, never ignored (spec-0093 §7's rule). Remedy: drop the line or the field, or give the trigger the gate, `once` or `serves` that creates the state.

**`DW0427`, widened under its own code.** `answers` on a trigger whose press vanilla cannot attribute — `strike-npc`, `strike-assembly`, `approach`, `step` — is refused with the message `audience: presser` already gets: a reply needs a presser, and a left-click has none. `serves` on those kinds is admitted (it is a gate, not a reply); a `serves` `strike-assembly` stands down and says nothing, which is recorded as the gap it is (§11).

**`DW0429`, widened under its own code and moved to the build tier** (`compiler::plan`, where regions and anchors are resolved; exit 3). Every `close-gate` target and every shortcut `gate` owes an **answer trigger**: a `use` trigger with `serves: {gate: <that anchor>}` whose `at` resolves either to a cell **inside** the region that holds a block with an outline while sealed (the gate's own answer — its body is that block under a click box, §3a) or to a cell **face-adjacent** to the region holding its own `prop` (a knocker) — for a shortcut, in either form, reachable from the sealed side: the door cell's sealed face, or a knocker among the sealed side's approach cells (`wrongside`). A sealed gate with none is refused with the reading (a push on a sealed door is otherwise silent) and the remedy, naming the gate's own block first (declare the answer trigger at the door's cell) and the knocker second. A trigger anchored inside the region at a cell the seal leaves as air is refused here naming the cell. `answers_press_at` reads the answer trigger in either form. **`DW0425`** (a shortcut with no wrong-side answer) reads the same trigger and is otherwise unchanged. **`DW0372`** (a `close-gate` on a shortcut gate) is unchanged.

**`DW0896`, re-stated over blocks.** Two pressables whose cells coincide and whose props differ are refused naming both; two that agree share the block and each keeps its own advancement, and, where the block is boxed, share one click box carrying both tags.

**`DW1013`, widened under its own code and kept at the build tier** (`compiler::clickbox::check`, exit 3). Quantifier: every `summon minecraft:interaction` in every shipped function, as today; the placed kind is now judged **against the block it stands over**, not against the block grid. For each placed box the check reads the block at the box's cell from the shipped `setup_finish` (the one `setblock` list the written world also lays; a door's two cells from its lower half) and that blockstate's outline from `blockshape::outline`, and proves two things with the box as the server reads it (position as a double, `width` and `height` as the `f` floats written): every box of the outline lies inside the click box by at least `MIN_PROTRUSION` on every face, and the click box lies inside the body's cells expanded by `CLICK_MARGIN` on every face. Binding line: `DW1013 binding: J of S interaction summon(s) placed at a cell and judged against its block (families…); K fitted to a two-cell body; B borne, R at a runtime position — every judged box encloses its block's outline by at least MIN_PROTRUSION and lies within its cells by at most CLICK_MARGIN`. **The perturbation** only this check catches: a box built one margin short on one face of a lever's outline — faces off the grid, so the old check passes it — reds here naming the face and the sixteenth it fails to cover.

**Retired**, declared nowhere, listed nowhere, held bidirectionally by `check-dw-codes.py`: **`DW0437`** (into `DW1052`); **`DW0420`** and **`DW0421`** (their subject, the visible hardware, ceases to exist; the rule they protected — a press target the player can see — is `DW1059`'s, because a block is visible by being a block); **`DW0963`** (into `DW1059`: a prop is required and is the visible thing; a `strike` at a place is refused before any world is built). Their tests move to the codes that inherit them.

The row for `docs/demo-levels.md`: **Three Buttons and a Bell** — one room off a jetty: a bell on a post that an objective opens only after the keeper has spoken (`not_yet`: the rope is tied up; `done`: the bell has been rung), a latched **door** with `requires_item` that will not open before its step (`not_yet`: barred from within; `missing_item`: locked, the keeper's key) and is released once unbarred — a door thereafter, opened by hand — a **lever** behind the keeper that does not flip while chained (`not_yet`) and, once thrown, stays thrown and says so (`done`), a **chest** that will not lift its lid while the keeper watches and answers *empty* ever after, three buttons whose presses count into a datum the bolt objective reads, each `once: false` with `serves` and both answers, so that pressing a fourth time after the bolt draws says *the bolt is already drawn* and moves nothing, a **campfire** that is the bonfire's rest point and answers *not yet* before the arming beat, a boulder struck with a hand, and a sealed shortcut door whose own leaf answers *the door does not open from this side* from the sealed side and *barred; the bar is on this side* from the far side, with its unlock button on the far side lit only when a player stands on that side within six blocks. A player presses every target in every state and reads one action-bar line and hears one cue each time, and sees no door open, no lever flip and no chest screen before its turn; a second player standing beside them reads nothing; a player on the wrong side of the shortcut sees no glow through the door. The look is what no machine decides: whether the line reads at the crosshair before it fades, whether the cue reads as a refusal and not a click, whether a button's own click and spring beside the line confuse or confirm, whether a door that does not budge under the hand reads as barred or as broken, and whether the released door reads as the same door.

## 11. What stays open

- **Objective state as a gate term.** `serves` is one binding of a general fact; the one gate reading an objective's completion (as `after` does, for objectives only) and a gate's sealed state would let any gate consumer say it, and would make `serves` a special case. Proposed, not done.
- **The recovery stake's block form.** The marker's cell is chosen at runtime, and a block-use criterion is held to a cell (§2a). Two forms are candidates and neither is taken here: a **post** — the placement table's degenerate branch resolves to the nearest declared post on the route back, so every marker stands at a compile-time cell and is a bell there; or **vanilla's own death drop** — an item entity that never despawns (`Age` at its floor) and only its owner may pick up (`Owner`), collected by walking over it and read through `inventory_changed`. Until one is specified the marker keeps its entity, with no permanent glow (§3a, the declared loosening).
- **A `strike` that stands down and says nothing — and the ground under `DW0427` is narrower than it says.** A `serves` `strike-assembly`, a `once` `strike` at a place, is disarmed at settlement and answers no one; `DW0427` refuses `answers` on every left-click because "vanilla names no left-clicker". **Cited** (§2b): that is true of a block — the pinned game reports no left-click on any block — and **false of a click box**: `Interaction.skipAttackInteraction` writes the striker's UUID into the `attack` record and fires `CriteriaTriggers.PLAYER_HURT_ENTITY` for the striking player, which an advancement with an `entity` predicate on the box's tag can hold, naming the striker exactly as `player_interacted_with_entity` names a presser. Every struck body in the engine — a `strike` at a place, `strike-npc` on the NPC's hitbox, `strike-assembly` on the assembly's body — is an `interaction` entity, so every one of them can name its striker. **Recommended, not done here**: a follow-up amendment admitting `answers` on `strike`, `strike-npc` and `strike-assembly` through `player_hurt_entity`, and retiring `DW0427`'s left-click arm; this amendment changes no diagnostic's quantifier beyond what it was asked.
- **A push on scenery.** A boulder that answers a blow or a wall that answers a push now has a carrier — a real block under a click box, in every state — and is no longer open; a `use` on an unobservable block and a `strike` on any block are what the carrier gives. What stays open is the answer to the blow (above).
- **Vanilla's `lock` component** as a body for a locked container (the pinned game's own refused use, with its own line and sound; `ChestBlock.useWithoutItem` reports the press whatever the lock did) is a different carrier for the same class and is not taken here; it would carry vanilla's own wording beside the campaign's.
- **A per-viewer glow.** Spec-0104 §7.4's limitation stands; §3b narrows when a highlight is on, not who sees it.
