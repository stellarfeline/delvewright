- **What it is for**: a player who presses a thing and is told what happened, on a thing that is really there. In a playtest, a prop that an objective would later open was clicked before its step and said nothing; a puzzle solved by several presses went on accepting presses after it was solved and punished one of them; and in a second playtest a shortcut's far-side unlock — a glowing lever icon the compiler summons beside an invisible hitbox — was seen glowing through the shut door from the wrong side and pressed from there, giving the shortcut away before the party had earned it. The general form of all three: every click target is a block the world has, drawn and occluded like the world, and it answers every press — *not yet* before its step, and no change to settled state after. The engine today summons an invisible `minecraft:interaction` for every interaction point and, for several of them, a permanently glowing `minecraft:item_display` icon; it spends a click that arrives outside the window in silence, and never stands a repeatable trigger down because an objective it feeds has completed. The engine already has the whole mechanism for a press on a block (`default_block_use` at the block's cell, spec-0093 §6.5), for a press answer (a `use` trigger addressed to its presser, a `narrate` on the action bar, an advancement that observes without consuming) and for a thing lit only when a player is near (spec-0104 §7). This spec makes the block the only body of an interaction point and extends the answer to every pressable.
- **Research**: `docs/reference/press-answers.md` is the record for the answer; its synthesis is §2. Spec-0104 §2.4 is the record for how a usable thing is marked without breaking the world (the object is changed until it reads; a highlight lights only near, never as a beacon), and this spec cites it rather than researching the question again. The pinned game's own report of a press on a block is read in §2a, with read sites. Each statement below is marked **cited** (a line of the tree at the ground revision, a reading of the pinned jar or pinned data, a published source on a record page, or a constitution rule) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0117`. Three diagnostics: `DW1051` (a press that would be silent in a state a player can press it in, §6), `DW1052` (an answer declared for a state the press never has, §6) and `DW1059` (a press target that is not a block the pinned game reports the use of, §6). `DW0437` is retired into `DW1052` (§6); `DW0427` widens what it judges under its own code (§6); `DW0429` and `DW0425` widen what they read under their own codes (§6); `DW0420`, `DW0421` and `DW0963` are retired (§6). The surface change is stage 5 and takes the unreleased `dsl_version` minor other unreleased surface changes share; this spec states no version literal.
- **Non-goals**: an engine-written default wording or default cue for a refused press (§3, `DW0429`'s rule); an answer addressed to the party (§3); an answer on a left-click (`strike-npc`, `strike-assembly` — vanilla cannot name the striker, §6); an objective's state as a term of the one gate (§11); any change to what a press *does* while its window is open; the NPC dialogue hitbox and the assembly's struck body (each is a creature or a thing that is hit, not an interaction point, §3a); a journal or log of refusals (spec-0104 §8 lists what is open; a refusal is the moment, not the record); the recovery stake's block form (§3a, §11: its cell is chosen at runtime, which no block-use criterion can be held to); a per-viewer glow (spec-0104 §7.4).

## 1. What the tree does today, re-verified

Each claim the finding's record made is re-read at the ground revision; none has changed. One fact the record did not name is added last, because it decides the shape of this spec.

- **Cited** (`emit/functions.rs`, the activation driver; `emit/objective.rs`, `activation_commands`): an `interact`'s `minecraft:interaction` body — or, for a prop vanilla reports the use of, the prop block alone — is placed by `activate_<obj>` the tick the objective's `pending_guard` first holds, once, behind the `#act_<obj>` sentinel. **Cited** (`completion_cleanup`): `complete_<obj>` runs `kill @e[tag=dw_i_<obj>]`. So the body exists exactly while the objective is pending; a prop block placed at activation stays as scenery. **Cited** (`plan/path/region.rs`): the region model registers that prop as a write sealed from load and forced from the completion step, because "activation has no step of its own".
- **Cited** (`emit/functions.rs`, the `interact` tick lines): the completion line runs under `pending_guard` and the hand guard; the `missing_item_hint` line runs under `pending_guard` and the negated hand guard; the reset `scoreboard players reset @s dw.i_<obj>` runs unconditionally. A click that arrives while the objective is not pending is spent and nothing is said. For a hand-pressed prop the `i_<obj>` advancement fires on `default_block_use` at the cell in every state, so a lever the piece authored is flipped before its step and spent in the same silence.
- **Cited** (`quest/objective.rs`, `l10n.rs`): `missing_item_hint` is the one authored answer an `interact` carries, inventoried as `obj.<quest>.<obj>.missing_item_hint`, kind `Refusal`; no field answers a press before the step or after it.
- **Cited** (`emit/trigger.rs`, `trigger_poll_guards`): a trigger's dispatch gate is its `once` sentinel, its `forbids_flags`, its `requires_flags` and its `requires_state`, and nothing else; no term reads an objective. A `once: false` trigger whose writes feed an objective's gate fires after that objective completes exactly as before.
- **Cited** (the campaign, counted): 30 objectives, 16 `interact` (3 with `requires_item`, all 3 with a `missing_item_hint`); 41 triggers, 14 `use`, every one `once: false`, 4 gated by flags, 10 addressed to the presser, 6 of them answers to sealed things. The repeated-press shapes are seven repeatable strike triggers each counting into a datum and standing down on a flag they set themselves, and three repeatable, ungated `use` triggers each setting a datum. The puzzle's own stand-down read a flag set later than the solve; the engine had no way to say *this press belongs to that objective*.
- **Cited — what the record did not say** (`compiler.md`, the press-answer rows; `trigger.rs`, `DW0429`; `plan/trigger.rs`, `collect_press_answers`): a press answer is already "not a mechanism — it is an ordinary trigger": `EnvTrigger { on: use, audience: presser, once: false, effects: [narrate { style: actionbar, text }] }`, authored, or synthesized by the compiler from a `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint`; dispatched by a `player_interacted_with_entity` advancement whose reward revokes itself so the object answers every press; gated through `trigger_poll_guards`; emitted, inventoried, proven and claimed (`DW0811`) by the same code every authored click uses. A sealed body the campaign leaves silent is refused (`DW0429`) and the compiler bakes no default, "one rule for the whole pressable class". `NarrateStyle::Actionbar` is documented as "the channel a *reply* uses".

## 2. What practice does

**Cited** (`press-answers.md` §1–§3): feedback within a reasonable time is how a user learns the outcome of a prior interaction and decides the next step (Nielsen Norman #1); an error says in plain language what is wrong and what to do (#9; `game-writing.md` F2); essential information is conveyed neither by sound alone nor by text alone (Game Accessibility Guidelines); the pinned game's own refused use — a locked container pressed without its key — plays a sound and writes one line on the action bar, and `minecraft:block.chest.locked` is that sound in the pinned registry.

**Cited** (spec-0104 §2.4): a thing that does not read as usable is first repaired by the author — a better block, a better scene; where the scene keeps the block, a highlight is declared and lights only near, "a cue on arrival and never a beacon from afar"; the glow outline "can be seen through blocks", so a permanent one is a beacon.

**Authored — the synthesis.** A refused press is answered on the action bar, to the presser, on every press, with a line the author wrote and a sound the engine plays; the engine never writes the words; a pressable that would be silent in a state a player can press it in is refused at build, as a sealed body is. The thing pressed is a block the world draws and occludes; nothing stands in for it; it glows only near, only while the press can act, and only from the side the press is made from.

## 2a. What the pinned game reports of a press on a block

Every statement is **cited** from the pinned server jar named in the ground line unless marked otherwise.

- **The one place a press on a block is reported.** `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn(ServerPlayer, Level, ItemStack, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)` reads the block at the hit position; in spectator mode it opens a menu or passes; otherwise it computes `flag` (either hand holds an item) and `flag1` (the player is sneaking **and** `flag`), copies the stack, and, when `flag1` is false, calls the block's `useItemOn`; if that result `consumesAction()` it fires `CriteriaTriggers.ITEM_USED_ON_BLOCK` and returns; if the result is `TryEmptyHandInteraction` and the hand is `MAIN_HAND` it calls the block's `useWithoutItem`, and if **that** `consumesAction()` it fires `CriteriaTriggers.DEFAULT_BLOCK_USE.trigger(player, pos)` and returns; otherwise it falls through to the item's own `useOn` (firing `ITEM_USED_ON_BLOCK` on a consumed result) or returns `PASS` (bytecode offsets 0–320). `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.handleUseItemOn` fires `CriteriaTriggers.ANY_BLOCK_USE` **only when** `useItemOn`'s result `consumesAction()` (offsets 272–296). So: a right-click on a block whose use nothing consumes fires **no advancement criterion at all**; `minecraft:default_block_use` is exactly "the block's own empty-hand behaviour ran", held by `location` predicates to a block id and a position (`block_use_criterion`); `minecraft:any_block_use` adds nothing a pressable needs. A sneaking press with an item in either hand bypasses the block entirely and is never reported; so is an off-hand press.
- **The defaults.** `BlockBehaviour.useWithoutItem` returns `PASS`; `BlockBehaviour.useItemOn` returns `TRY_WITH_EMPTY_HAND`. A block that overrides neither is **unobservable**: stone, iron bars, a barrier, a wall, a sign that is waxed (its body was not read; stated from the class list and the override alone).
- **The bell.** `BellBlock.useWithoutItem` → `onHit(level, state, hit, player, true)` → `SUCCESS` when it rang, else `PASS`. `onHit` rings only for a **proper hit** (`isProperHit`): never on a `Y` face, never above `0.8124` of the block's height, and by attachment — floor: the face lies on the bell's `facing` axis; single or double wall: the face lies off that axis; ceiling: any side. `attemptToRing` calls the block entity's `onHit(direction)`, plays `SoundEvents.BELL_BLOCK` (volume 2, pitch 1), emits `GameEvent.BLOCK_CHANGE`, and **writes no block state**. A ring is an animation and a sound that end by themselves.
- **The button** (14 ids in the pinned registry, every `*_button`: acacia, bamboo, birch, cherry, crimson, dark oak, jungle, mangrove, oak, pale oak, polished blackstone, spruce, stone, warped). `ButtonBlock.useWithoutItem`: when `powered` is already true it returns `CONSUME` — the press is **reported and changes nothing**; otherwise `press` sets `powered=true`, updates neighbours, schedules its own tick after `ticksToStayPressed` (**20** for the stone and polished blackstone buttons, **30** for every wooden one — the constructor constants in `Blocks`), plays the click and emits `BLOCK_ACTIVATE`; `SUCCESS`. A press is a pulse that reverts by itself; during the pulse a second press is reported and does nothing.
- **The lever.** `LeverBlock.useWithoutItem` → `pull`: `powered` toggled and written, neighbours updated, the click played, `BLOCK_ACTIVATE`/`BLOCK_DEACTIVATE`; `SUCCESS`. A press is a **persistent** state change the world shows until the next press.
- **The door.** `DoorBlock.useWithoutItem`: when the door's `BlockSetType.canOpenByHand()` is false it returns `PASS` — the iron door (`iron`: hand `false`) is **unobservable**; otherwise `open` is toggled and written, the sound played, `BLOCK_OPEN`/`BLOCK_CLOSE` emitted; `SUCCESS` — a wooden or copper door (`oak`, `stone`, `copper`: hand `true`) **opens**. The trapdoor and the fence gate override `useWithoutItem` and read the same `BlockSetType` (**unsupported**: their bodies were not read; the claim is from the override list and the shared type).
- **The campfire.** `CampfireBlock` overrides only `useItemOn`, which consumes the action only for an item the `CAMPFIRE_INPUT` recipe set accepts (`placeFood`); an empty hand falls to the default `useWithoutItem` → `PASS`. A campfire is **unobservable** as a press target.
- **The chest.** `ChestBlock.useWithoutItem` → `openMenu` on the server and returns `SUCCESS` **whatever happened in the menu** — a container carrying a `lock` the hand does not match is still reported (the pinned game's own refused use, press-answers §3, is this path). A chest's press is reported, and its effect is a screen.
- **Overriding one or both methods** (headers read, bodies not): the barrel, lectern, note block, decorated pot, chiseled bookshelf, cauldron, candle, flower pot, jukebox, bed, cake, crafting table, shelf, copper golem statue, repeater, comparator, daylight detector, enchanting table, anvil. Each one's empty-hand use either opens a screen or writes a state the world keeps (**unsupported** per block beyond the override; the kinds are excluded on their class, §3a).

## 3. The decision: one pressable class, one press-answer mechanism, three states

**Authored — the object class.** The thing a player right-clicks is a **pressable** — `compiler::pressable` is already the one authority for its body, for an `interact` objective and for a `use` trigger alike, and §3a makes it the authority for every interaction point the engine arms. A pressable is in one of three states at any tick:

| State | An `interact` | A trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **open** — the press acts | its `pending_guard` holds (quest active, every `after` complete, its gate, not complete) | its own gate holds and, if `once`, it has not fired; with `serves` (§4), also the served objective is pending or the served gate is sealed |
| **settled** — the act is done | `dw.o_<obj>` is 1, or its quest is complete | `once` and fired; or `serves` and the served objective is settled or the served gate is open |
| **shut** — not open and not settled | any other tick | any other tick |

An `interact` with `requires_item` has a fourth answer inside **open**: the hand lacks the item (today's `missing_item_hint`). The other pressables of §3a have the same three states: a shortcut unlock and a disarm are open while armed and settled once spent; a bonfire and a shop are shut before their arming beat (the bonfire) and open ever after; neither settles.

**Authored — the answer is the existing press-answer trigger, synthesized from a declared wording.** Exactly as `sealed_hint` was lowered by `collect_press_answers` into a presser `use` trigger riding the seal's body, each answer declared here is lowered into a presser `use` trigger on the pressable's own block, gated to the one state it answers, carrying `narrate { style: actionbar, text }` under `Audience::Solo`. Nothing is added to the emitter but the gate derivation; the dispatch, the l10n path, the `env_trigger_<id>` PackTest, the `DW0811` claim and the `dw-` id namespace (`DW0428`) are the ones the seal answer already has. Two consequences follow and are taken: the block of a pressable must exist in every state a player can press it in, so it stands from `setup_finish` to the end of the delve (§5.1); and the states a press can be in are what decide which answers are owed (§6).

**Authored — no baked default, so silence is refused.** `DW0429`'s reasoning is adopted whole: a default wording or a default-only cue would be the compiler making a design statement about this door on the author's behalf. A pressable that would be silent in a state a player can press it in is `DW1051`, the sibling of `DW0429` over the pressables it did not cover. The engine's half — the block, the dispatch, the state, the sound — is supplied; the words are the campaign's. This is a tightening against the engine before this spec, in those words: an `interact` that answers nothing before or after its step, which nothing refused, is refused.

**Rejected, authored.**

- *A default line from compiler chrome, hidden by a switch.* The quest chrome (`New objective:`) names a thing the engine owns; the words a shut door says are the campaign's tone, which `DW0429` already refuses to invent.
- *An answer that is an effect bundle.* It would let a not-yet answer write a flag or a done answer move the world — the very writes the finding forbids after a step settles — and would need a refusal for writing verbs that the string form makes unnecessary. A reply is one `Refusal` line; a beat is a trigger the author writes.
- *A party-addressed answer.* A reply to a press tells the one who pressed (`trigger.rs`: "broadcasting it tells four players about a door three of them are nowhere near").
- *Refusing a `once: false` trigger that feeds an objective and is not stood down.* A bell that can be rung forever and tallies its rings for an ending is a legitimate design; which of two readers of a datum is "the" objective a press serves is a candidate, not a match (CLAUDE.md, a resolve-by-name over a non-unique scope). The author states it (§4 `serves`), and the skill says when to.
- *Objective state as a term of the one gate* (`requires_flags` / `forbids_flags` / `requires_state` gaining an objective term). The general form, and the right home in the long run; it touches every gate consumer and every flow proof, and this finding needs one binding, not a fourth gate term. Recorded open (§11).

## 3a. The body is a block the world has

**Authored — the rule.** An interaction point — a thing the delve asks a player to right-click — is a **block at a cell**, and nothing else: no `minecraft:interaction` stands for it, no `item_display` marks it. The block is the body, the pinned game's own report of its use (§2a, `default_block_use` at the cell) is the detector, and the block is drawn and occluded exactly as the world is. A body a player strikes or speaks to — an NPC's dialogue hitbox, the assembly a player hits — is a **creature or a thing that is hit**, not an interaction point; it keeps its fitted `interaction` entity, because it is the body's own shape and the pinned game names no striker through a block.

**Authored — what becomes of `compiler::pressable::Body`.** `Block { cell, block }` is the body of every interaction point. `Rides { tag, owner }` remains for exactly two owners — an NPC's hitbox (a `strike` at its stand anchor, `strike-npc`) and an assembly's body (`strike-assembly`) — and for nothing else. `Point` and `Region` are **removed**: a point anchor with no hand-pressed prop, and a gate region, are not blocks the pinned game reports (`DW1059`, §6). `Nothing` stays (`DW0426`: the anchor resolves to nowhere).

**Cited, then authored — which blocks can be a body.** From §2a, a block is a body when its empty-hand use is **reported** (the block's `useWithoutItem` consumes the action) **and** that use **changes nothing the world keeps**, so that a press while it is not the pressable's turn has no effect beyond the pinned game's own acknowledgement that the press landed. The kinds:

| kind | reported | what a press changes | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| bell | yes, on a proper face | a ring that ends by itself | **a body in every state** |
| the 14 buttons | yes, in every state | a pulse of 20 or 30 ticks that reverts by itself | **a body in every state** |
| lever | yes | `powered`, kept until the next press | **a body only where every press acts** — an ungated, repeatable, unserved `use` trigger, a bonfire, a shop — because there its flip *is* the act, and nowhere a shut or settled state exists, where a flip before or after the turn would show a state the story does not have |
| wooden or copper door, trapdoor, fence gate | yes | it opens | excluded: the press has an effect |
| iron door, iron trapdoor, campfire, every block with the default `useWithoutItem` | no | nothing | excluded: unobservable |
| chest, barrel and every container or workstation that opens a screen | yes | a screen | excluded here; the locked container is a different carrier (§11) |
| note block, repeater, comparator, daylight detector, candle, flower pot, jukebox, bed, cake, chiseled bookshelf, decorated pot, cauldron, shelf, copper golem statue | yes or by item | a state the world keeps, or an item moved | excluded |

`dsl::blockshape::is_hand_pressed` (lever, bell, every `*_button`) already names the three reported kinds whose use is not a screen; this spec adds the lever's one-state rule on top of it, and no other list.

**Authored — the acknowledgement is not an effect.** A button's pulse and a bell's ring are the pinned game saying *the press landed* — the same role press-answers §2 gives the engine's cue — and both are over within a second and a half, leaving the world as it was. A lever's flip is a statement about the world that stands until someone changes it; a door's opening is the act the story was keeping. That is the line, and it is drawn by what the block keeps, not by what it shows for a moment.

**Authored — every consumer, and the block it gets.** Enumerated from §1a.

**Authored — the knocker.** A sealed gate's blocks cannot be pressed observably (§2a: a door that opens is not sealed, an iron door and a wall report nothing), so the answer a sealed door owes rides a pressable **beside** it: a bell-pull, a knocker, a latch — a `use` trigger whose prop stands face-adjacent to the gate region, on the side it answers for, with `serves: {gate: <gate anchor>}` so it is open exactly while the gate is sealed and answers `done` once the way is open. No new field carries it; it is the authored press answer the record already describes, given a block and a window.

**One cell, one block.** Two pressables whose cells coincide declare the same block or are refused (`DW0896`, re-stated over blocks, §6); one press on the shared block fires every advancement whose criterion names that cell, each as its own listener, so two `interact` objectives on one bell in sequence build.

**Declared as a loosening, in those words:** this spec's class is block-only **except the recovery stake marker**, which keeps its entity body until its block form is specified (§11); the ruling it executes admits no exception, and this spec records one.

**Declared as a removal:** a `strike` trigger at a place that is not an NPC's stand anchor can no longer be expressed; the pinned game reports no left-click on a block, the only vanilla carrier was an invisible hitbox fitted over the block, and that is the class this spec removes. What is left: `strike-npc`, `strike-assembly`, a `strike` on an NPC's stand anchor, and a `use` on a knocker.

## 3b. Visibility — drawn like the world, lit only near and only from the right side

**Authored.** A block body is drawn and occluded by the client exactly as every block is; nothing the compiler summons marks it. Every `Glowing:1b` the compiler emits for an interaction point is gone with its icon (`affordance_hardware` and every caller; the `dw_marker` and `dw_hw_<tag>` tags; the stake marker's flag). The one glow mechanism is spec-0104 §7's `highlight { within }`, admitted on every pressable of §3a (the `interact`, the `use` trigger, the shortcut unlock, the bonfire, the shop, each disarm) and on `stakes[]`, so a creator who wants a thing lit declares it per act, and a campaign that declares none states its zero. This folds in the defect recorded as the issue titled *guidance: compiler-summoned hardware glows through walls and gives a shut way away from the wrong side*: the far-side lever icon that glowed through the door is gone, and its block is a button or a bell the wall hides.

**Authored — the lit set.** A highlight is lit while the pressable is **open** (§3) and a player stands in its **lit set**: the walkable cells within `within` of the block from which the block's pressed face is in sight — line of sight by `body.rs` `blocks_camera`, the traversal spec-0104 §2.6 cites — computed at build and emitted as the union of boxes the tick tests, instead of §7.3's sphere. A shortcut unlock's lit set lies on the far side; a knocker's on the side it answers for; a player on the other side of the wall, alone, lights nothing. **Cited** (spec-0104 §7.4): the outline, once lit, is drawn for every client within entity range, through blocks; a split party with one member in the lit set shows the other the glow. The limitation stands as spec-0104 states it; this spec narrows when the glow is on, not who sees it. Binding line: `highlight binding: H highlight(s) over A pressable(s) — C lit cell(s) in B box(es); R refused`.

**`prop`** (`{ block }`, the carrier spec-0093 §6.5 gave the `interact` and the triggers) is **required** on every interaction point — the `interact` objective, the `use` trigger, `shortcuts[]` (placed at `unlock`), the `bonfire` effect (at `anchor`), `shops[]` (at `anchor`), a trap's and a timed gate's `disarm` (at `via`) — and admitted on no `approach` or `step` (`DW0964`). Its block is a bell or a button, or a lever where the pressable has only the open state (§3a); anything else is `DW1059`. The compiler places it at `setup_finish` (§5.1); the written world lays it too (`assembled::shipped_blocks`). `shops[].marker_item` and `stakes[].marker_item` are **removed** (they named the icons); `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint` is **removed** (its body is gone; its one meaning is the knocker's `narrate`).

**`answers`** is an object carried by every pressable with a shut or settled state the engine can name a presser for — the `interact` objective, the `use` trigger, `shortcuts[]`, the `bonfire` effect, each `disarm` — holding one `Refusal` line per state the pressable has: `not_yet` (shut), `done` (settled) and, on an `interact` with `requires_item`, `missing_item` (open, hand empty). Each line is optional in the schema and owed by `DW1051` where the state exists (§6). Keys in the l10n inventory: `obj.<quest>.<obj>.answers.not_yet` / `.missing_item` / `.done`, `trigger.<id>.answers.not_yet` / `.done`, `shortcut.<id>.answers.done`, `fx.<path>.answers.not_yet` for a bonfire, `trap.<id>.disarm.answers.done` and `timed_gate.<id>.disarm.answers.done`, kind `Refusal`, each with its `situation` (as the title's rows already carry). **`missing_item_hint` is removed**: its one meaning is `answers.missing_item`, and a second field for the same answer class is the defect CLAUDE.md names. A document carrying a removed field is refused as an unknown field (`deny_unknown_fields`); the content is changed, with no shim.

**`serves`** is an optional object on every `triggers[]` entry naming the thing this press is part of: `{ "objective": <ObjectiveId> }` or `{ "gate": <AnchorId> }` — one field whose referent's kind the object states, never two fields for one binding. It binds the trigger's window to that thing's: with an objective, the trigger is open only while its own gate holds **and** the objective is pending, settled when the objective is; with a gate — a `close-gate` target or a shortcut's `gate` — open only while its own gate holds **and** the gate is sealed, settled while the gate is open, and shut before a `close-gate` has fired (a shortcut is sealed from load and has no shut state). The reference resolves like `after` does for an objective and like `at` does for an anchor; an unknown id is the existing unknown-objective or unknown-anchor refusal; a gate anchor no `close-gate` or `shortcut` seals is `DW1052`'s inert-declaration shape. `serves` is admitted on every trigger kind, because standing down at a settlement is a property of the trigger, not of how it is pressed; the **answers** of a `serves` trigger are admitted only where the presser can be named (§6).

**`highlight`** (`{ within }`, spec-0104 §7.1) is admitted on every pressable of §3a and on `stakes[]`; its lit set is §3b's.

**What is not changed.** `once`, `audience`, the three gate terms keep their meaning; a presser `use` trigger the author writes as an answer is unchanged in form, and now carries `serves: {gate}` where it speaks for a sealed gate.

## 5. Emission

### 5.1 The body stands for the whole delve

**Authored.** Every pressable's block is placed at `setup_finish`, through one list — `pressable::props`, `trigger_props` widened to every interaction point of §3a — which the written world lays too, so the server's world and the shipped world cannot disagree about a block; nothing is summoned for any interaction point; `activation_commands` places the `collect` chest and nothing for an `interact`; `completion_cleanup` kills nothing for an `interact`; `shortcut_open_<id>`, `trap_disarm_<trap>` and `tgate_disarm_<gate>` kill nothing. The `#act_<obj>` sentinel and `activate_<obj>` remain for the `collect` chest. **The region model** (`plan/path/region.rs`) registers every prop as a write forced from step 0, which is what it now is; the unforced-from-load arm is kept for the `collect` chest alone. **Cited** (`compiler.md`, `interact` row): the kill at completion existed "so a finished objective is not clickable"; under this spec a finished objective is clickable and answers `done`, which is the point.

**The cost, stated.** Every campaign's bytes move (`setup_finish` gains the blocks and loses every summon but the NPC's and the assembly's; `complete_<obj>` loses the kill; the region events change; `tick` loses every `interaction`-record poll; the advancements change criterion); `gallery/baseline/` is regenerated once; the `obj_activate_<obj>`, `v04_interact_cleanup`, `souls_shortcut`, `souls_timed_gate_disarm_<id>` and the shop and stake PackTests change what they assert (a block present before activation and after completion; a granted `default_block_use` advancement where a hitbox record was written). No byte-identity claim is made for any existing document. `compiler::clickbox` and `DW1013` narrow to the two struck bodies; `compiler::affordance` keeps `DW0545` and loses `DW0420`/`DW0421`; `validation/press-bodies.json` records the block of every press.

### 5.2 The answers, lowered

**Authored.** For each declared answer the compiler synthesizes one presser `use` trigger, `trigger/dw-answer-<owner>-<state>` (`<owner>` the pressable's safe id; `<state>` one of `not-yet`, `missing-item`, `done`), on the owner's block: its `press_<id>` advancement is `block_use_criterion` at the owner's cell, exactly as a hand-pressed prop's answer is lowered today, through `press_dispatch_fn`. The answer's effects are `narrate { style: actionbar, text }` under `Audience::Solo`, followed by `scoreboard players set #ans_<owner> dw.sys <n>` (1 not-yet, 2 missing-item, 3 done) — the trace write every trigger already makes on `#trig_<id>` (`emit/trigger.rs`: "the write is what makes trigger dispatch observable at all"), which is what a PackTest reads, since an action-bar line leaves no game state. **Every answer plays the engine's refusal cue** — `CUE_REFUSED`, `minecraft:block.chest.locked`, the sound the pinned game pairs with its own refused use — through `cue_line`, beside `CUE_ANNOUNCE`, `CUE_COMPLETE` and `CUE_CAMPAIGN`, and `validation/cues.json` states it for the bot. **The gates** are derived, never typed: the interact's `not_yet` carries the negation of its `pending_guard` and the negation of its settled term; `done` carries the settled term; `missing_item` carries the `pending_guard` and the negated hand guard, replacing the tick line that carried the same today (one click, one line, as before). A trigger's `not_yet` carries the negation of `trigger_poll_guards`' flag, state and `serves` terms with the once term not fired; `done` carries the once-fired term or the served objective's settled term or the served gate's open term. A shortcut's and a disarm's `done` carry their sentinels (`#sc_<id>`, `#trapdis_<trap>`, `#tgdis_<gate>`); a bonfire's `not_yet` carries the negation of its armed term. **One authority**: `pending_guard` and `trigger_poll_guards` are read, not restated; the implementation lands an agreement test that drives an objective through its states and asserts, per state, that exactly one of {completion, `not_yet`, `missing_item`, `done`} can dispatch.

### 5.3 `serves`

**Authored.** `trigger_poll_guards` gains, for a trigger with `serves`, the served objective's `pending_guard` terms or the served gate's sealed term as further space-terminated fragments, so every dispatch route — `press_<id>`, `step_<id>`, the assembly's strike clause — gates identically. `path_triggers`' `enabled` and `Flow`'s link liveness read the same fact: a `serves` trigger is performable only while its objective is pending, or its gate sealed, on that walk; the agreement test of §5.2 covers it. After the objective settles the trigger never dispatches again, whatever `once` says, so no write it would have made reaches settled state; after a gate opens its knocker answers `done` and nothing else.

### 5.4 Nothing else moves

The bot's `/trigger dw.i_<obj>` and `/trigger dw.rest` paths, the completion marker channel, the announcement chrome, the journal and the compass (spec-0104), `takes_item` (spec-0114), the harness's `interact` and `trigger` steps: unchanged. A harness step that today right-clicks an `interaction` entity activates the block at the same cell instead; the step's shape and its allowlist entry do not change. A bot never presses outside a window, so it never reads an answer; the PackTests do.

The look is what no machine decides: whether the line reads at the crosshair before it fades, whether the cue reads as a refusal and not a click, whether a button's own click and spring beside the line confuse or confirm, and whether a knocker reads as the door's voice.

## 11. What stays open

- **Objective state as a gate term.** `serves` is one binding of a general fact; the one gate reading an objective's completion (as `after` does, for objectives only) and a gate's sealed state would let any gate consumer say it, and would make `serves` a special case. Proposed, not done.
- **The recovery stake's block form.** The marker's cell is chosen at runtime, and a block-use criterion is held to a cell (§2a). Two forms are candidates and neither is taken here: a **post** — the placement table's degenerate branch resolves to the nearest declared post on the route back, so every marker stands at a compile-time cell and is a bell there; or **vanilla's own death drop** — an item entity that never despawns (`Age` at its floor) and only its owner may pick up (`Owner`), collected by walking over it and read through `inventory_changed`. Until one is specified the marker keeps its entity, with no permanent glow (§3a, the declared loosening).
- **A `strike` that stands down and says nothing.** A `serves` `strike-assembly` is disarmed at settlement and answers no one, because vanilla names no left-clicker (`DW0427`). The assembly's own clip is the author's answer there.
- **A press on scenery.** The pinned game reports no left-click on a block and no right-click on a block whose use nothing consumes (§2a); a boulder that answers a blow, or a wall that answers a push, has no block carrier. The knocker and `approach` are what is left; a vanilla primitive would re-open the question.
- **Vanilla's `lock` component** as a body for a locked container (the pinned game's own refused use, with its own line and sound; `ChestBlock.useWithoutItem` reports the press whatever the lock did) is a different carrier for the same class and is not taken here; it would carry vanilla's own wording beside the campaign's.
- **A per-viewer glow.** Spec-0104 §7.4's limitation stands; §3b narrows when a highlight is on, not who sees it.
