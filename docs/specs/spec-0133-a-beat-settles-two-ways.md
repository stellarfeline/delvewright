# spec-0133: A beat settles two ways — an objective with a `limit` is done or expired, expiry is a story outcome the worlds enumerate and the bot waits out, and the clock is game ticks on the server's own schedule

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against engine `ee036a0cc` (`main`, `delvec 1.16.0`, `dsl 0.70.0`), read whole for: `Objective` and `Act` in `crates/dsl/src/quest/objective.rs`; `Verb` in `crates/dsl/src/quest/verb.rs`; `for_each_effect_root` and `nested_effect_lists` (`crates/dsl/src/effects.rs`, `fight.rs`, `l10n.rs`); `Flow`, `ReplayState`, `solve_with`, `state_gates`, `armed_objectives` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/flow.rs` and `Elective`, `StoryWorld`, `Target`, `walk_for_performing`, `story_playthrough` in `flow/story.rs`; `plan::firing_of` and `effect_writes` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/plan/path/region.rs`; `told_guard` and `pending_guard` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit/quest.rs`; `emit_packtest` in `emit/packtest.rs`; the `removal_lines` the rest re-seat uses in `emit/npc.rs`; `dsl::chrome::ALL`; the harness contract `harness/src/critical-path.ts` (`STEP_ACTIONS`, format `5`) and `harness/src/mock.ts`; `docs/reference/compiler.md` (the stage-5 table, the `timed_gates[]` and `sequence` rows, *objective lifecycle*, the `critical-path.json` contract), `docs/reference/delvec/compiler/flow.md` (*Story worlds*), `delvec/compiler/nav.md` (`DW0379`, `DW0378`, the 4 t/block sprint model), `playtest-methodology.md`; spec-0051, spec-0108 (§6.4–§6.5), spec-0114, spec-0016 §4.
- **What it is for**: a level script in which every daytime task is a soft failure — a limited time to fetch the crates, to herd the goats, to hit the bell, to catch the fox — where a task not done in time still lets the day go on, and the night reads which tasks were done. The engine has no beat that settles any way but by its act: an objective completes by what the player does and otherwise stays open forever; `sequence` is the only clock and dates every step of a timeline at its root's firing in every story world; an optional quest is performed whole or skipped, never started and lost. Six of the script's sixteen missing items are this one mechanism. This spec gives an objective a second settlement, **expired**, that the proof enumerates as a story outcome and the bot proves by waiting.
- **Research**: none owed. The clock is the mechanism the `timed_gates[]` row of `compiler.md` already runs on the pinned server — `schedule … replace` with no per-tick cost — and the bodies a task was about are removed by the one removal the rest re-seat already performs. Every rule below is marked **[cited]** with the record or constitution line it rests on, or **[authored]** where this spec decides.
- **Numbers**: spec `0133`; `dsl_version` `0.72.0` (the objective gains a field, the verb set gains one, the stack of a `critical-path.json` step gains a kind); `DW1084`, `DW1085`, `DW1086` used (§7); `DW1087` unused and returned.
- **Non-goals**: a limit on a quest or on a trigger (§1 says why the objective is the class); a clock the player cannot see (§3 refuses it); a timer that writes a flag and nothing else (ruled: a failure flag fired later to pass a check is forbidden — expiry is a settlement, §4); real time (ADR-0006); a judgement of whether a window is fair beyond the walk (§6); fast-forwarding the bot's wait (§8); the k-of-n quorum and the body census the same script needs (spec-0134); any campaign's task design.

## 1. The object: an objective's settlement **[authored]**

Three things in the engine could carry a limit — the quest, the trigger, the objective — and the class is decided by what settles. A quest completes when its objectives do; it has no act of its own and settles only through them. A trigger fires, once or again, and has no open state a clock could close. An objective is the one thing that is **open** — activated by its quest and its `after`, told to the player, pending until its act — and closing it is what a limit does. So the limit is a field of the objective, keyed to every one of the five kinds, and a quest or a line of dialogue is limited by limiting the objective that carries it (a `talk-to` completes by a dialogue option, backward; its limit closes that option).

**[cited]** (spec-0114 §4; `compiler.md` *objective lifecycle*): an objective settles today in exactly one way — **done**, by the act its kind names, which sets `dw.o_<obj>`, broadcasts `[dw:complete …]`, and fires its completion bundle. **[authored]** From this spec it settles in one of two: **done**, unchanged, or **expired** — the clock ran out while it was pending. Both are settlements: an objective settled either way lets the objectives `after` it activate, counts toward its quest's completion, closes its announcement, its compass, its highlight and its press answers, and is never settled again. They differ in what fires: done fires the completion bundle; expired fires `on_expire` and nothing of the completion bundle. A quest whose objective expired still completes when every objective of it is settled, and its `on_complete` fires — the day goes on, which is the whole point; what the night reads is the flags the done and the expired bundles wrote.

## 2. The surface **[authored]**

Every objective kind takes an optional **`limit`**:

```json
"limit": {
  "ticks": 6000,
  "from": "activation" | "quest-start" | {"objective": "obj/<id>"},
  "on_expire": [ <effect>, … ]
}
```

- `ticks` is the window in **game ticks**, `20..=720000` (one second to the ten-hour ceiling of a delve), refused outside the bound by the exported schema (`DW0100`). Ticks, not seconds, because the engine's clock vocabulary is ticks (`sequence.at_ticks`, `timed_gates[].open_ticks`) and a second spelling of one quantity is a second conversion to keep honest; the player sees minutes and seconds (§3).
- `from` is the event the clock starts at. Default `activation`: the tick the objective's told guard first holds (`emit::quest::told_guard`). `quest-start`: the tick the objective's quest activates. `{"objective": id}`: the activation of an earlier objective of the same quest, so one clock can run over a chain — the fox is chased past four hides and the fireworks fetched, all inside one window that opened when the chase began. The named objective must be an `after`-ancestor of the limited one in the same quest (`DW1086`, §7).
- `on_expire` is the effect bundle expiry fires: the sinking of the crates, the shepherd's walk, the line that tells the party the task is over, the flag the ending reads. Optional; when present, at least one effect (`minItems: 1`, `DW0100`). It is an **effect root** — the tenth, visited by `for_each_effect_root` and so by every walk defined over the roots (l10n, the producer scans, the checkpoint collector, nav's region writes, emission); `tools/ci/check-effect-roots.py` holds every hand-written enumeration to it. Its audience is the party (`Audience::Party`): the clock fires with no acting player, so `audience: actor` inside it is `DW0503`, unchanged, and a `give-item` with `carrier: one` is `DW0357`, unchanged.

**`despawn-wave{wave}`** — the verb the expiry of a task about bodies needs: the goats not herded by dusk are taken home by the shepherd, and the goats that were running on the terraces are gone. A wave is the one body class the engine seats with AI and the one it cannot yet remove by declaration; `despawn-actor` and `despawn-npc` exist. Emission is the removal `wave_reseat_<wave>` already performs (`removal_lines`: every survivor carrying `dw_wave_<id>` removed unseen, any `drops` declaration stripped first, so nothing is seen to die and nothing is left behind). A wave no beat seats cannot be despawned (`DW0913`); a `kill` objective on a wave a reachable `despawn-wave` can remove before it completes is the ordinary `DW0204` of the world that performs it. Keyed to the wave, usable in any bundle.

## 3. The clock, and the player who can see it **[authored, on cited mechanism]**

**[cited]** (`compiler.md`, `timed_gates[]` and `timed-gate`): a `schedule` in replace mode is a clock with zero per-tick cost that cannot double up, and the pinned server keeps it across a restart. **[authored]** Emission per limited objective:

- At the start event (`activate_<obj>`, the quest's activation function, or the named objective's `activate_`), `schedule function <ns>:expire_<obj> <ticks>t replace`.
- `expire_<obj>`: `execute if score #o_<obj> dw.obj matches 0 if <pending_guard> run function <ns>:settle_expired_<obj>` — the clock settles only an objective still pending; one that was done first is left alone. `settle_expired_<obj>` sets `dw.x_<obj>` = 1, broadcasts the anchored marker `[dw:expired <campaign_id> obj/<id>]` (the completion-marker channel of `compiler.md` §4, a second token), prints `Objective expired: <title>` with the cue `block.note_block.bass` for an announced objective, runs the objective's cleanup (the same despawn of its hitbox and highlight completion runs), then the `on_expire` bundle.
- At completion (`complete_<obj>`), `schedule clear <ns>:expire_<obj>`.
- **Born expired.** A clock that starts before its objective opens — at the quest's start, or at an ancestor's activation — can run out while the objective is still closed. The clock then marks it run out (`#lx_<obj>`, set by `expire_<obj>` whenever the objective is not done), and the objective is born expired: the tick it opens, its own pending guard holding, it settles expired exactly as above — `on_expire` fires and the story goes on. An objective is never left open with no window because its clock ran before it. An objective clocked from its own activation cannot open after its clock ran out, and needs no such mark.
- **A world nobody has entered runs no clock.** A `quest-start` clock on a quest active at world load (a `campaign-start` quest) starts at the first player's arrival — the first-join hook the engine already places players by (`join_place`, the `dw_joined` tag), or, in a campaign that places nobody, the first player online — never at the first tick of an empty world.
- The `pending_guard` every objective driver and every dialogue option reads gains `unless score #x_<obj> dw.obj matches 1`, so an expired objective's act does nothing — the chest can be opened, the bell rung, the option chatted, and nothing completes. The guard has one definition and every consumer goes through it (`emit::quest::pending_guard`).
- **The countdown is shown.** While the clock runs, a bossbar `dw:limit_<obj>` is shown to every player whose objective it is (`@a`, the announcement's audience), named `<title> — <m:ss>`, its value the seconds left, driven by `limit_tick_<obj>` every 20 ticks through the same replace-mode `schedule`, removed at either settlement. The chrome strings `limit.countdown` (`{title} — {time}`) and `limit.expired` (`Objective expired: {title}`) enter `dsl::chrome::ALL` and every language table, so a `--lang` bake carries them.

Ruled: a hazard the player cannot see is refused, never hidden — danger is visible or the engine refuses it. A limit on an objective that is not announced (no `title`, or a resolved `announcement` of `hidden`) is therefore `DW1085` (§7): the clock would run on something the player was never told exists.

A death does not stop a clock, a checkpoint does not reset one, a bonfire rest does not pause one: the clock is the story's, as a `timed-gate`'s is.

## 4. What expiry does, kind by kind **[authored]**

| kind | pending until | expired: what stays | expired: what is refused after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `talk-to` | the completing dialogue option | the NPC and its other lines | the option, by the guard |
| `reach-anchor` | the body in the box | the place | the box, by the guard |
| `kill` | the wave's countdown at 0 | the wave's survivors, unless `on_expire` carries `despawn-wave` | the countdown's completion |
| `collect` | the stack in the inventory | the chest and its contents (a conjured chest stays, an adopted holder keeps its stack) | the `inventory_changed` completion |
| `interact` | the press | the block; the `done` answer is replaced by the `not_yet` answer, which now reads as the thing being closed | the press's completion |

A limited `collect` whose `on_expire` is meant to take the stack back writes a `clear-region` or `set-block` at the holder: the crate sinks, the engine does not reach into inventories. An `interact` with `release: true` that expired stays a door the player can use: expiry settles the objective, not the block.

## 5. The expired world **[authored, on the cited story model]**

**[cited]** (`flow.md` *Story worlds*): a story world is a dialogue selection and a participation — the set of elective acts performed — solved with the producer table restricted to it, run as an ordered replay, and read at each final scene; a performed elective ambient act writes at its entry deed; worlds are the *all* world, the *none* world, and one witness per target of a final scene's inputs.

**[authored]** A limited objective's expiry is a new elective class, an **outcome**: `flow::story::Elective` gains a kind, `Expiry(obj)`, beside the optional quest and the ambient root. A world that performs it has the objective expire; one that does not has it done. In a world where it expires: the objective's completion producers credit nothing; its `on_expire` producers credit, dated at the objective's own position in the run (its entry deed is the step where the all world completes it; the replay performs the expiry there instead); its `after`-dependents and its quest's completion proceed as in §1. In every other world the objective is done and `on_expire` writes nothing. A forced act stays forced: expiry is never forced, because the all world completes everything.

**Every expiry is a world.** Beyond the witnesses the final scenes ask for, one **expiry witness** per limited objective is enumerated — the all world with that one objective expired — whether or not an ending reads anything it changes. Ruled: the proof enumerates the expired branch and the bot walks it; a limit nothing reads still closes a task, and a party on that path must still reach an ending. The expiry witness is a row of `validation/branch-plan.json` and a `validation/world-path-<k>.json` of its own, walked by `branch-runs.sh` at the tier that walks branches. Worlds remain deduplicated on (selection, participation) and capped at `MAX_WORLDS`, and a world past the cap is counted as the existing enumeration counts it — never silently dropped from the binding line.

`DW0204`, `DW0879`, `DW1029` and `DW1030` read each expiry witness as they read every story world: an expiry whose world cannot reach an ending is that world's `DW0204` (or `DW1030` for the ending it starves), naming the world by its expired objective. No new code: a story world that cannot complete is already refused, and this spec adds worlds, not a second reader.

## 6. The window, measured against the walk **[authored, on the cited model]**

**[cited]** (`nav.md`, `DW0379`, `DW0378`): the engine prices a walk at the 4 t/block sprint model `DW0355` uses, over the same assembled nav model every proof reads. **[authored]** For each limit, the engine computes the **walk ticks**: the proven route from the cell the party stands on at the clock's start event — the critical path's position at that step (for `quest-start`, the step whose completion triggers the quest; for a named objective, that objective's activation) — through every objective the limited one is `after` and not yet settled at that moment, to the limited objective's own completion cell, at the sprint model. A window shorter than its walk is a task no party can do: `DW1084` (§7). The engine states, on every validate, `limit binding (DW1084): <n> objective(s) carry a limit; walk <w> of <ticks> tick(s) (<pct>%) — <obj>; …; <k> expiry world(s) exported`.

The walk is a floor, not a judgement: a fight's length, a shot's practice, a search, are activity, which the window must leave room for beyond the walk. The engine prices activity for the pacing figure (spec-0131) and does not read it here: a window is the author's call, as a monster's difficulty is, and the playtest judges it. The binding line prints the percentage so the author sees how much of the window the walk alone takes.

**Dating.** `on_expire` is an effect list like any other to `plan::firing_of` and `effect_writes`: in the expiry witness its region writes are dated at the objective's step; in every other world they do not fire. A `clear-region` that sinks the crates therefore closes nothing on the all world's route, and in the expiry witness the route proof walks a world where the crates are gone — which is what the party on that path sees.

## 7. The rules **[authored]**

- **`DW1084` — a limit shorter than the walk it allows.** `delvec::compiler::nav` (build tier, exit 3). Quantifier: every limited objective on the critical path and on every expiry witness's path. Refused when the walk ticks of §6 exceed `ticks`. Names the objective, the start event and its cell, the walk in ticks and blocks, the window, and the remedy: lengthen the window or shorten the route. Not refused: a walk the window equals (the party sprints it exactly); a limited objective off every path (counted as unwalked in the binding line, never judged).
- **`DW1085` — a limit the player is not told about.** `delvewright_dsl::quest::check` (validation tier, exit 1). Quantifier: every objective with a `limit`. Refused when the objective is not announced (spec-0093: no `title`, or `announcement` resolving to `hidden`). Path: `/content/quests/<i>/objectives/<j>/limit`. Remedy: give the objective a title and show its announcement, or remove the limit.
- **`DW1086` — a clock that starts after the thing it closes.** `delvewright_dsl::quest::check` (validation tier, exit 1). Quantifier: every `limit.from` naming an objective. Refused when the named objective is the limited one, is in another quest, or is not an `after`-ancestor of it (transitively, over the quest's own `after` relation; `DW0140` has already refused a cycle). Names both objectives and the remedy: name an ancestor, or use `activation`.
- **`DW1087`**: unused; returned to the planner.

Reused, not duplicated: `DW0100` for the bounds and the empty bundle; `DW0503` and `DW0357` for an acting player the clock does not have; `DW0913` for a `despawn-wave` on a wave nothing seats; `DW0204`, `DW0879`, `DW1029`, `DW1030` over the expiry witnesses; `DW0481` demands a `happening` of the story-node verbs inside `on_expire` as inside any bundle.

## 8. The proof **[authored, on cited machinery]**

**The bot waits.** `critical-path.json` and every `world-path-<k>.json` gain the step kind **`expire`** (`STEP_ACTIONS`; the contract's `format_version` moves with it in both constants — `plan::path::CRITICAL_PATH_FORMAT_VERSION` and the harness's — numbered by the planner — this spec consumes no format number): `{"action": "expire", "objective": "obj/<id>", "wait_ticks": n, "stand": [x, y, z]}`. The step stands where the all world's step for that objective stands in the order; `wait_ticks` is the window minus the ticks the replay has already spent since the start event, as a ceiling the harness converts to its own timeout with the ordinary grace; `stand` is a standable cell of the walk region the compiler proved outside the objective's own completion volume, outside every `keep_out` volume armed at that step, and — for a `kill` — outside the aggro sphere of the wave (the forced-solid spheres `nav/wave.rs` already draws, `DEFAULT_FOLLOW_RANGE` where none is declared). The harness walks to `stand`, performs nothing, and passes the step on the anchored `[dw:expired …]` marker alone; a `[dw:complete …]` for the same objective arriving first fails the step by name (the bot completed what it was to let lapse — the stance was wrong, and the plan missed it). The wait is real: **[cited]** (CLAUDE.md, *Debug doctrine*): never weaken a check to get green; a mocked clock would prove the engine's function, not the schedule the player lives under. The engine states the sum of every expiry witness's `wait_ticks` in the `limit binding` line, beside the count, so the ladder's cost is read before it is paid (CLAUDE.md, *Host hardware never caps a capability*: the cost is computed and stated, and the hosting side meets it).

**PackTest proves the arithmetic on every pull request.** Per limited objective the build emits two templates: `limit_<obj>_expires` — the quest activated to the objective by the engine's own functions (the shape `v06_arrive_handoff` uses to seal and open), `ticks + 1` ticks stepped, then asserts `dw.x_<obj>` = 1, `dw.o_<obj>` = 0, the first producer `on_expire` writes (a flag's score, a datum's value) holding, and the objective's completion function a no-op after; and `limit_<obj>_done` — the same activation, the completion run before `ticks`, `ticks + 1` stepped, then asserts `dw.o_<obj>` = 1 and `dw.x_<obj>` unset. The gallery's window is short so these run inside the required `tier 2` job; a campaign's are as long as its windows, which is why the PackTest and not the bot is the per-pull-request proof.

**Vacuity.** A limit whose expiry witness is a world the enumeration dropped at its cap is named in the binding line as unproven, never counted as walked. A `limit binding` line of zero limits is the measured zero.

## 9. The gallery **[authored, under spec-0039]**

The primary binds one `limit` on an announced `reach-anchor` of a mandatory line (`from: activation`, a window of a few hundred ticks, `on_expire` carrying a `narrate`, a `set-flag` the ending reads, and a `despawn-wave` of a wave the line seats and no `kill` requires); an overlay binds `from: quest-start` and `from: {objective}` on a two-objective line. The ending's final scene reads the expiry flag, so the expiry witness is also an ending witness and the ending proofs bind. Probes: `a-clock-on-a-silent-objective` (`DW1085`), `a-clock-that-starts-after-its-objective` (`DW1086`), `a-window-shorter-than-the-walk` (`DW1084`). The coverage gate reports `limit`, `limit.from`'s three arms, `limit.on_expire` and `despawn-wave` bound.

## 11. What it leaves out, by name **[authored]**

A **pause**, a **reset** or an **extension** of a running clock: a clock settles once; a task given a second chance is a second objective. **Real-time** and **wall-clock** limits (ADR-0006). A limit on a **trigger** or a **quest** (§1). A **player-scoped** clock (each player's own window): the objective is party state and so is its clock. **Fast-forward** in the ladder (§8). A **fairness** judgement of the window beyond the walk (§6). The **k-of-n quorum** and the **census** the same script needs are spec-0134's, which builds on this spec's outcome class.

## 12. Acceptance criteria

1. **The surface.** `delvec schema --stage quest-expansion` exports `limit {ticks, from?, on_expire?}` on all five objective kinds, `ticks` bounded `20..=720000`, `from` with exactly the three arms of §2, `on_expire` with `minItems: 1`; and `despawn-wave {wave}` among the verbs. `crates/dsl/tests/it/gate_consumers.rs` is unchanged in count: a limit is not a gate. Every document the repository holds parses at `dsl_version` 0.72.0.
2. **Two settlements.** `crates/delvec/tests/it/limit.rs`: on a fixture of one limited objective, the emitted `activate_<obj>` schedules `expire_<obj>` at `ticks` in replace mode; `complete_<obj>` clears it; `settle_expired_<obj>` sets `dw.x_<obj>`, broadcasts `[dw:expired …]` before the first `on_expire` line, and prints the chrome line for an announced objective; the `pending_guard` of every driver of that objective, and of every dialogue option that completes a `talk-to` with a limit, carries the `unless … dw.x_<obj>` clause; an objective `after` the limited one is activatable once `dw.x_<obj>` is 1; the quest's completion counts the expired objective as settled. Perturbation: dropping the `schedule clear` line from the emitter reds the test that asserts a done objective is never expired.
3. **The three starts.** The same file: `from: activation` schedules in `activate_<obj>`; `from: quest-start` in the quest's activation; `from: {objective}` in the named objective's activation; the emitted tick is the declared `ticks` in each.
4. **The countdown.** The bossbar `dw:limit_<obj>` is created at the start event, named with the chrome string and the objective's title, set from `limit_tick_<obj>` every 20 ticks through a replace-mode `schedule`, and removed in both `settle_expired_<obj>` and `complete_<obj>`; the two chrome keys are in `dsl::chrome::ALL`, every language table carries them, and the l10n inventory test reds when one is missing.
5. **The outcome world.** `crates/delvec/tests/it/limit_worlds.rs`: a campaign with one limited objective on a mandatory line enumerates the all world plus one expiry witness; the witness's `world-path-<k>.json` carries an `expire` step at the objective's position with `wait_ticks` and a `stand` outside the objective's volume; in that world the objective's completion producers are credited to nothing and its `on_expire` flag is credited at the objective's step; an ending whose final scene reads that flag fires in the witness and not in the all world (`validation/endings.json`); two limited objectives give two witnesses; a limit in an optional quest gives a witness per participation that performs the quest. Perturbation: declaring the flag's write inside the completion bundle instead of `on_expire` moves the credit to the all world and the witness no longer fires the ending.
6. **The window.** `limit.rs`: a window longer than the walk is green and the binding line states walk, window and percentage; a window shorter than the walk is `DW1084` naming the objective, the start cell and both tick counts; `from: quest-start` measures from the triggering step's cell; a limited objective on no path is counted and not judged. Perturbation: lengthening the route (moving the anchor) past the window turns green to red with no other change.
7. **`DW1085` and `DW1086`.** `crates/dsl/tests/it/limit.rs`: a limit on an objective with no title, and on one with `announcement: hidden`, is refused at the objective's `limit` path; `from` naming the objective itself, an objective of another quest, and a non-ancestor of the same quest is refused; an ancestor two `after` hops up is green. `DW1087` is declared nowhere and `tools/ci/check-dw-codes.py` is green with no `PENDING` entry.
8. **The harness.** `harness/test/critical-path.test.ts`: an `expire` step parses with its three fields and is refused without any one of them; the executor's `expire` passes on the `[dw:expired …]` marker, fails by name on a `[dw:complete …]` for the same objective, and never sends a chat, a use or an attack during the wait (the mock bot's log is empty but for the walk); the format constant moved in both languages and the harness refuses the previous number.
9. **PackTest.** The build of the gallery primary emits `limit_<obj>_expires` and `limit_<obj>_done` for its limited objective; both run green inside `tier 2`; `limit.rs` asserts the expires template steps `ticks + 1` and asserts the three facts of §8, and the done template asserts the two.
10. **`despawn-wave`.** `limit.rs`: its emission equals the removal lines of `wave_reseat_<wave>` for the same wave, from one function; a wave no beat seats is `DW0913` at the verb's path.
11. **The gallery.** Criterion 9's elements are bound in the primary and the overlay as §9 states; the three probes are refused with their named codes; `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py` reports every unit of §9 bound; the gallery's expiry witness is a row of its `branch-plan.json` and `branch-runs.sh` walks it green at the tier that walks branches, the run report naming the wait it spent.
