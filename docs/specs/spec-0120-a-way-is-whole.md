# spec-0120: A way is whole — a fixed structure never blocks part of a door, a stair or a road; the clear width the author declared is clear along the whole way in every state its cells can take, nothing standing in it is excused by a declaration, and the engine refuses the rest

Read beside it: spec-0113 at `origin/feature/reachable-surfaces` (`15f01acb6`, unmerged) for the reach graph; spec-0118 for the shut-way craft advice this must keep satisfiable; the content demo `three-ways-shut` at `86e6b7ac4` of the content repository (`campaigns/three-ways-shut/`, `prefabs/three-ways-shut-yard.json`, `design/build_pieces.py`) for the geometry of the first finding. **Amended** (§0) against engine `57f0e7026e9d5c4f2df8529a0b4b49b041424eb1` (`integrate/batch-c`, the first implementation of this spec, `dsl_version` 0.59.0) and the content demo `the-hearth-door` at `efa28499d54aed83a32365d28e794a411f0150a3` (`demo/the-hearth-door`), both read only.
- **What it is for**: a stair that is as wide at its foot as the author said it was. In a playtest of a demo level, a shut way into an old cart road sat at the foot of a stone stair three cells wide; before it opened, the barricade's wood stood over one of the stair's three columns and the mouth's jamb over another, and after it opened the jamb still did. One column stayed free, so no reachability proof failed and no check fired. Nobody builds a fixed structure across half a door, a stair or a road; every building code treats the width of a way as a thing that is **not diminished along the way**, and the engine before this spec proved only that a way is *passable* — one column of a body's width is enough for every proof it had. This spec makes the engine confirm the clear width the author declared, along the whole way, in every state its cells can take. In the playtest of the second demo level (§0) the same stair read as blocked by a post the engine had been told was meant, so the spec now admits no such telling: **a declared way's clear width is clear**, and a thing meant to stand beside a way stands outside its declared width.
- **Research**: `docs/reference/clear-width.md` is the record — the codes' rules for door, stair and corridor width, what they do not give, and how a cell grid carries them. Each statement below is **cited** (a reading of the tree at the ground revision, a constitution rule, that research page, or a confirmation — marked *owner-confirmed*) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0120`. One diagnostic: **`DW1057`** (a way narrowed, §6.1). `DW1058` (a declared narrowing the bytes do not show) is **withdrawn** with the surface it judged (§0, §4): its row, its tests, its probes and its ledger counts leave with this amendment, and the number is not re-used. `dsl_version` **0.59.0** introduced `boxes[].way` and the three `narrowed[]` surfaces; the amendment removes `narrowed[]` from the site plan's seam, the site plan's box and the prefab contract's edge and takes `<dsl-next>` (the planner allocates). The contract's grammar surface takes the engine version of the release that lands it, as `climb` took its own; no other number is consumed.
- **Non-goals**: a threshold on how much narrowing is acceptable — the codes name no fraction for a *fixed* obstruction and the constitution refuses an invented one, so a way is **whole** or **narrowed** and nothing in between is judged. A way fully shut — that is the gate's own state and the gate's own proofs (`DW0836`, `DW1011`). The height of a way above a body's head (a beam a body walks under blocks nothing). Whether a body can *squeeze* past — the nav model's one predicate decides what a body can be in, and this spec reads it and does not widen it. A way class with a calibrated width range (spec-0053 §3): a way place declares its width as a number (§1.4), so no class, no table entry and no gym walk is needed by this rule. The hiding structure spec-0118 §9 advises — it stays legitimate exactly where §9 of this spec says.

### 0.2 The tightening, in those words

**Owner-confirmed**, from the playtest of the demo level: a stair three wide with a post standing in one of its three columns is a blocked stair, whatever the document says about the post. **This amendment is a tightening**: everything the rule refused before, it refuses still; what it admitted on a declaration — a narrowing with a `narrowed[]` entry confirmed cell for cell — it now refuses as `DW1057` like any other. No criterion asserts less than before; the criteria that asserted the excuse surface (its schema, its confirmation, its stale-excuse code) are struck, not loosened, because the surface they described no longer exists.

**What replaces the newel and the lamp post.** A declared way's width is the author's; a thing meant to stand *beside* the way stands **outside** that width — in the next column over, where §3(c) already puts a rail and a banister. The newel at the foot of a stair three wide stands on the floor beside the bottom tread, in the column past the opening's third, as a real newel does at the end of a real handrail; the lamp post on a street four wide stands on the verge, in a column of the box's extent that `way.clear` does not claim (`way.at` and `clear` leave the verge where the author puts it). An author who wants the stair two wide with a post in the third declares the opening two wide and builds two treads and a wall column: `DW0844` and `DW0836` hold the piece to the seam either way, and the post then stands in a wall, not in a way.

**Why no declaration can stand in for a clear column.** §1.1 of the original text read the confirmation as leaving *room to declare an exception explicitly*; the playtest showed the exception as the player meets it, and withdrew it. The constitution's rule that a check confirms intent and never infers one is met the way §1.2 always met it: the declared width is the intent, the check confirms that width is free, and nothing about *why* a column is taken is inferred — or asked. A way narrower than its declaration is corrected in the world or in the declaration of the width, never in a third document that says the narrowing is meant.

## 1. What a way is, and why confirming its width is not inferring an intent

**Settled first, as the brief asks.**

### 1.1 The confirmation and the two rules it must meet

**Owner-confirmed.** A fixed (decorative or structural) structure may not block part of a door, a stair or a road; this is a check, not advice. **Owner-confirmed** (§0.2): a declaration that a structure inside a way's width is meant does not make the way whole; the structure moves out of the width.

**Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *A check confirms intent; it never restricts*): a check judges what the author declared and never infers an undeclared intent in order to refuse. **Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *Vacuity*): an opt-out is secured by a property the defect cannot supply; where none exists, there is no opt-out.

### 1.2 The width of every way is already a declaration

**Cited** (`metrics.rs`, `Opening`): a seam's `opening` is *clear width* and *clear height*, in cells — the author names a standard (`door` 1×2, `arch` 2×3, `passage` 3×3, `gateway` 5×5) or writes `{width, height}` (`compiler.md`, `seams[].opening`: *the author's own opening*). **Cited** (`blockout.rs`, `tread`): a stair's treads are laid across *the opening's own width, clipped to the host — a stair the width of its doorway is what a body can actually walk up*. **Cited** (`compiler.md`, `seams[].contact`): a contact's span is *the author's*. **Cited** (`grammar.md`, the contract edge table): a piece's `walk`, `stair`, `climb` or `drop` edge names its `via` region — *the treads belong to the edge* — and a `barred` edge its `bar` region, each a set of declared boxes.

So the engine never measures the bytes to learn how wide a way is: **the author said**. What the engine measures is how much of that declared width is **free**. That is the architectural sense of *clear width* (`clear-width.md` §1): the opening a body passes, not the opening that was drawn. A check that says *you declared three clear and built two* confirms a declared intent — the width — and infers nothing about why the third column is taken. **This is the reconciliation**: the confirmation is executed on the declared width; the width is the only declaration there is; nothing is inferred.

### 1.3 The objects the rule binds, and how each hands its width and its axis

**Authored.** A **way** is one of five declared objects; each hands its cross-section and its travel axis, so no consumer guesses either from the region's shape (the rule `DW1011` already follows for a seam gate's axis).

| Way | Declared by | Cross-section | Travel axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **portal** — a `walk`, `stair`, `climb` or `drop` seam with an `opening` | the site plan | the opening's `width × height` at `at` on the face | the seam's normal (`PlacedSeam::bar_axis`) |
| **stair run** — a `stair` seam's treads | the plan's seam and host (`stair_in`); laid by the stand-in (`LaidRun`) or by the piece's contract `stair` edge whose `via` the contract's own graph joins to the face answering the seam | the opening's width across the run, one column per cell, at every course | `StairRun::run_axis`; for a piece flight, the axis on which its `via` region separates its two spaces |
| **contact** — a seam with a `contact` | the site plan (spec-0053 §4) | the span's columns on the plane | the face's normal |
| **piece way** — a contract edge of class `walk`, `stair`, `climb`, `drop` (its `via`) or `barred` (its `bar`) that answers **no** plan seam | the piece's spatial contract | the region's columns across its travel axis | the horizontal axis on which the region separates its `a` space from its `b` space (or from the face it reaches, for `exterior`); `y` where no horizontal axis does (a climb) |
| **way place** — a road, a street, a bridge, a causeway: a box that declares `way` (§4.1) | the site plan's box | `clear` columns across the box, starting `at` on the cross axis, at every station along the run | the declared `axis` |

A piece contract edge whose region realizes a plan seam — the face opening `DW0844` aligns, or the flight that joins to it — **is** that seam's way and is judged once, under the seam. A stair seam answered by a piece whose contract declares no flight joining to the face is counted *run undeclared by the piece* and its approaches are still judged.

### 1.4 Doors, stairs — and roads

A **door** is a portal; a **stair** is a stair seam's run with its portal; both are declared with their width. A **road** is a place (`compiler.md`, `nodes[]`: *a stretch of shore, a cavern, a road*); **cited** (the creator guide, `references/reference-works.md`, *A connector is a place*): a street, a bridge or a stair of its own is a box with its seams at each end, in the site plan and the layout graph. A place's `intent` is *a free non-empty label no check keys on*, and **cited** (measured at the ground revision: `way_class` and `way-class` occur in no file under `crates/dsl/src`) spec-0053 §3's way class is written and not built — so at the ground revision the engine held no declared clear width for a road's own run, and this spec does **not** infer one from a box's shorter extent or from the word `connector`, which would be the inferred intent the constitution forbids. **Authored**, executing the confirmation at its full scope: the place **declares** that it is a way — its travel `axis` and its `clear` width, as a number, on the site plan's box (§4.1) — and its run is then judged along its length like every other way, column by column at every station. The mouths at its ends are portals and its fronts are contacts, judged as such; the run between them is the place's own. The width is a number the author writes, not a class a table ranges (spec-0053 §3's shape), because this rule needs the declared width and nothing about whether the place is a *corridor* or a *road* — that kind is the author's word in `form` and `intent`, and no check keys on it.

## 3. What blocks part of a way

**Authored**, from `clear-width.md` §4.

- **The corridor.** A way's cross-section swept along its travel: for a portal or a contact in a wall, the plane's cells and **one approach cell** on each side at the sill (the cell a body stands in to pass — the whole-cell floor of the landing and manoeuvring-clearance figures the codes give, `clear-width.md` §3); for a stair run, the body cell over every tread at every course plus the foot's approach row at the host floor and the head's approach beyond the plane; for a seam through a floor, the hole's columns from two cells above the upper floor down to the lower floor; for a piece way, its region's columns and one approach cell in each endpoint space; for a way place, its `clear` columns at every station along its `axis` from one end of the box's play space to the other — a station a seam's approach already covers is judged once, under the seam.
- **Columns.** The corridor is judged one **column** at a time — one per cell across the width, at each station along the travel. A column is **clear** when a body can be in its body cell — the cell whose floor is the way's walk plane there (the sill, the tread's top as `solid_top_16` reads it, the rung; on a way place, the standable cell the nav model finds in the column over the place's floor, so a bridge's deck and a street's paving are each their own plane) — by the nav model's one predicate (`can_be`), with the headroom that predicate demands. So a beam over a body's head blocks nothing; a slab a body steps onto blocks nothing; a `Thin` block blocks nothing; a `TallBarrier`, a `FullCube`, a raised `PartialFloor` a body cannot step onto, a closed hatch, each blocks its column.
- **States.** A cell has the states it can take: its as-built block, and every block a quest-layer write puts there (`open-gate` to air, `close-gate` to its fill, a shortcut's unlock, a clock's, a `way`'s `laid` block or `cleared` void, a `set-block`). A column is clear only when it is clear in **every** state of every cell in it. This is what *shut and open* means here: an operated structure that takes part of a way while it stands has taken part of the way.
- **Whole, narrowed, shut.** A way is **whole** when every column of its corridor is clear; **narrowed** when some are and some are not; **shut** when none is — at a station, in a state. Shut is never this spec's finding: a shut plane is the gate's state (`DW0836` where the bar should not be there, `DW1011` for what it separates), and a shut approach is a wall the route proofs refuse. Narrowed is exactly the shape every existing proof passes, and **narrowed is refused** (§4).
- **Excluded, and why.** (a) The way's **own** gate region (`anchor/seam-<edge>`, the piece's `bar`) and its own `way` region: they are its shut state, judged by the proofs that own it. (b) The portal's **plane** columns: `DW0836` already refuses any allocated cell not passable, so this check counts them *left to `DW0836`* and never refuses twice (a contact's plane is **not** excluded — `DW0836` admits a contact with one passable column, which is this spec's finding). (c) Cells **outside** the cross-section: the frame of an opening, the lintel above its height, the wall beside a stair's treads, the verge beside a street's `clear`. A rail, a banister, a newel, a lamp post that is part of a way stands in that outside — the next column over from the declared width — and is never judged; one inside the declared width takes a column of it, because the engine's fence is a whole cell (`clear-width.md` §3), and is **moved**.
- **Included, by name** (the brief's three classes). **Prefab bytes**: everything a bound piece's template puts in a corridor column. **The stand-in's own blocks**: a frame ring that reaches an approach, a tread run laid for one seam that enters another's corridor in the same host, a station landed in one. **Detail and props seated by the engine**: a holder, an `interact` prop, a `prop` block, a relight fixture, a world edit, a `set-block` — anything the assembled world the geometry proofs read contains or the quest layer writes. A gate region of **another** way is included in every state it takes (b above excludes only the way's own).

## 4. The decision, and the surface

**Two sentences, authored.**

1. **Every way is judged whole**, over the assembled world, in every state its cells can take, by the columns of §3; a way narrowed by anything is refused (`DW1057`).
2. **Nothing excuses a narrowing.** No flag, no acknowledgement, no entry, no second field: a column of a declared way is clear, or the way is refused, and the remedy is in the world (move the structure) or in the width (declare the way as wide as it is built, and build it so).

**Rejected, authored.**

- *A `narrowed[]` declaration on the way* (the surface this spec carried at `dsl_version` 0.59.0: `{side, across, course?, block, what}` on a seam, `{along, across, block, what}` on a way place, `{cells, block, what}` on a contract edge, confirmed cell for cell with a stale-excuse code). The hatch's security was that the author must locate the structure in the way's own frame and name its block; §0.1 shows the structure nobody meant is located and named as readily as the one somebody did, and the playtest shows the player meets the post either way. Withdrawn.
- *An `ack` flag on the way.* The opt-out secured by a property the defect can supply, in its plainest form.
- *Declaring the narrowed width instead* (`passage` → `{width: 2}`) **with the treads left three wide**. `DW0844` and `DW0836` refuse the piece for being wider than its seam; the author would be narrowing a true declaration to hide a false structure. Declaring two **and building two** is not rejected — it is the remedy, and the post then stands in a wall column (§0.2).
- *Admitting fences by class.* A fence inside a doorway is a fence inside a doorway; the codes admit a rail's 4½ inches, not a 1-metre post (`clear-width.md` §3). The rail that is part of a way stands beside its width.
- *Reading the width from the reach graph.* Where a body can be (spec-0113) is the **result** the defect produces — a narrowed stair is reachable in one column — so a width read from it would measure the defect and find nothing. The reach graph is this check's predicate for *clear* (§9), never its source for *wide*.

**`boxes[].way`** — optional, `{axis, clear, at?}`: the place is a way whose run travels along `axis` (`x` or `z`), `clear` columns wide across it, the columns starting `at` cells from the box's low corner on the cross axis — defaulting to centred, `max(0, (extent − clear) div 2)`, exactly as a seam's `at` defaults. A `clear` wider than the box's cross extent, or an `at` that puts a column outside it, is refused at validation under `DW0826`'s *something the plan places leaves the region*, widened by one shape (a way wider than its place). A box without `way` is a room: its floor holds whatever its piece puts there, and this check never enters it. The columns of the box's extent that `clear` does not claim are the **verge**: the lamp post, the bollard, the bench stand there.

**Removed**: `boxes[].narrowed[]` and `seams[].narrowed[]`. A document that carries either is refused as an unknown field (`DW0100`), the ordinary refusal for a key the stage does not have; `DW0824` and `DW0826` keep their original quantifiers (the shapes the entries widened them by leave with the entries).

### 4.2 The spatial contract — the piece

**Removed**: `edges[].narrowed[]` on a contract edge. A contract that carries it is refused by the contract's own gate as an unknown field, in the words the grammar uses for one. `DW0888`'s quantifier returns to its original (the *narrowing on an edge that realizes a plan seam* shape leaves with the field).

**What does not move.** `opening`, `contact`, `form`, `stair_in`, `boxes[].way`, every class, every proof the derivation and the blockout battery already run.

## 5. The analysis — `compiler::nav::width`

Build tier, over the assembled world the geometry proofs read, from the call site in `emit::routes::prove_world` where `DW0450` runs — after the pieces, the stand-ins, the holders, the props and the edits are in, and with the plan's seams, the laid runs and the contract graphs in hand. **One authority**: a column is clear by `can_be`; a cell's states are the region-write model's; a seam's axis is `bar_axis`; a run's columns are `LaidRun`'s or the contract flight's `via`; the way's own regions are `PlacedSeam::bar` and the contract's `bar`/`way`. Nothing here is a second copy of the nav model or the derivation.

### 5.1 The population

Every way of §1.3, enumerated from the plan's seams (`P` portals, `S` stair runs, `C` contacts), the plan's boxes declaring `way` (`Y` way places) and every bound piece's contract edges that answer no seam (`K` piece ways); the denominators are the plan's traversal seams, the plan's boxes and the contracts' traversal edges. The population is also what spec-0118 cuts a branch at (`nav::width::plan_cross_sections`), unchanged by this amendment.

### 5.2 The verdict per way

For each way: its corridor's columns (§3) and, per column, its states; the count **clear** / **taken**, per station (each approach, the plane, each course). A way is *whole*, *narrowed* or *shut* at each station. **Narrowed at any station in any state is `DW1057`**; the record names every taking cell with its block and the state that takes it. There is no third verdict.

### 5.3 The ledger and the binding line

`validation/clear-width.json` carries one row per way: its kind, the seam or the piece and edge, the declared width, each station with its columns clear and taken (world cells, block, the state that takes it), and its verdict (`whole` or `narrowed`). Written on every build that is not refused; a campaign with no ways writes it with its zeros.

The binding line, on every build: `clear width (DW1057): W way(s) of T traversal seam(s), B box(es) and E contract edge(s) — P portal(s), S stair run(s), C contact(s), Y way place(s), K piece way(s); M column(s) at N station(s) judged over V state(s); H way(s) whole, U narrowed; L plane column(s) left to DW0836; R stair run(s) undeclared by their piece`. Every count is a number; `0` is written as `0`. `D`, `G`, `F` and `X` leave the line with the surface they counted.

## 9. Interaction with the reach graph, guidance and spec-0118

- **The reach graph** (spec-0113). *Clear* is the nav model's predicate for where a body can be; when spec-0113 lands, that predicate is the reachable-surface graph's, and this check follows it with no change of its own — one authority for *can a body be here*. *Wide* is never read from it (§4, rejected).
- **Guidance** (spec-0118 §9, craft advice for the skill): *hide the shut way with structure that belongs to the setting* stays satisfiable, and this spec says how: the hiding structure is the shut way's **own gate** — cells of its `anchor/seam-<edge>` region or of a gate station cleared by the same beat — standing in that way's own corridor across its **whole** width, and in no other way's corridor. A fireplace hiding a passage hides a way without blocking part of one — the hearth is built around the opening's declared width (cheeks beside, mantel above), and what stands inside the width is the way's own gate, cleared when the passage opens. The barricade of the first finding was the right idea in the wrong cells: across the cart road's mouth it was that way's gate; across the stair's foot it was a structure in another way. The craft line for `placement.md`: **a structure that hides a shut way stands only in that way's own width; it never enters the width of a door, a stair or a road beside it.** What that hiding gate must be made of so that it hides is spec-0118's (as amended, §0 there): a gate the eye looks through hides nothing.
- **spec-0118's declarations** (`learned`) read nothing this spec changes; its cuts read this spec's cross-sections (`plan_cross_sections`), which the amendment leaves as they are.

## 11. What stays open

- **A way class.** A way place declares its width as a number; spec-0053 §3's class — a named range the gym calibrates — stays unbuilt, and nothing in this rule needs it. If a later brief wants `way_class.road` to range a declared `clear`, the class is a check over the number this spec already has, never a second declaration of the width.
- **The approach is one cell.** The codes' landing and manoeuvring figures are a cell and a fraction (`clear-width.md` §3); one cell is the floor. A two-cell approach is the recorded next shape, decided on the demo level and on playtests, never by a number without a source.
- **A stair's landing beyond the head.** The head's approach is one cell beyond the plane; a landing as deep as the stair is wide (the codes' figure) is the same open item as the approach depth.
- **Vertical ways.** A climb's corridor is the rung column and the hole; a body beside a ladder on a two-wide hole is modelled by `holds_body`, and whether the second column of a wide ladder hole is a way or a shaft is decided on the demo level before any rule narrows it.
