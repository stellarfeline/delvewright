## 1. What a way is, and why confirming its width is not inferring an intent

**Settled first, as the brief asks.**

### 1.1 The ruling and the two rules it must meet

**Owner-confirmed.** A fixed (decorative or structural) structure may not block part of a door, a stair or a road; this is a check, not advice; the author must have room to declare an exception explicitly.

**Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *A check confirms intent; it never restricts*): a check judges what the author declared and never infers an undeclared intent in order to refuse. **Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *Vacuity*): where an opt-out is a choice among kinds, the object determines the kind; an opt-out is secured by a property the defect cannot supply.

### 1.2 The width of every way is already a declaration

**Cited** (`metrics.rs`, `Opening`): a seam's `opening` is *clear width* and *clear height*, in cells — the author names a standard (`door` 1×2, `arch` 2×3, `passage` 3×3, `gateway` 5×5) or writes `{width, height}` (`compiler.md`, `seams[].opening`: *the author's own opening*). **Cited** (`blockout.rs`, `tread`): a stair's treads are laid across *the opening's own width, clipped to the host — a stair the width of its doorway is what a body can actually walk up*. **Cited** (`compiler.md`, `seams[].contact`): a contact's span is *the author's*. **Cited** (`grammar.md`, the contract edge table): a piece's `walk`, `stair`, `climb` or `drop` edge names its `via` region — *the treads belong to the edge* — and a `barred` edge its `bar` region, each a set of declared boxes.

So the engine never measures the bytes to learn how wide a way is: **the author said**. What the engine measures is how much of that declared width is **free**. That is the architectural sense of *clear width* (`clear-width.md` §1): the opening a body passes, not the opening that was drawn. A check that says *you declared three clear and built two* confirms a declared intent — the width — and infers nothing about why the third column is taken. The only intent it cannot see is whether the narrowing was meant, and for that it does not guess: it asks (§3). **This is the reconciliation**: the ruling is executed as a confirmation of the declared width; the exception is a declaration too; nothing is inferred.

### 1.3 The objects the rule binds, and how each hands its width and its axis

**Authored.** A **way** is one of five declared objects; each hands its cross-section and its travel axis, so no consumer guesses either from the region's shape (the rule `DW1011` already follows for a seam gate's axis).

| Way | Declared by | Cross-section | Travel axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **portal** — a `walk`, `stair`, `climb` or `drop` seam with an `opening` | the site plan | the opening's `width × height` at `at` on the face | the seam's normal (`PlacedSeam::bar_axis`) |
| **stair run** — a `stair` seam's treads | the plan's seam and host (`stair_in`); laid by the stand-in (`LaidRun`) or by the piece's contract `stair` edge whose `via` the contract's own graph joins to the face answering the seam | the opening's width across the run, one column per cell, at every course | `StairRun::run_axis`; for a piece flight, the axis on which its `via` region separates its two spaces |
| **contact** — a seam with a `contact` | the site plan (spec-0053 §4) | the span's columns on the plane | the face's normal |
| **piece way** — a contract edge of class `walk`, `stair`, `climb`, `drop` (its `via`) or `barred` (its `bar`) that answers **no** plan seam | the piece's spatial contract | the region's columns across its travel axis | the horizontal axis on which the region separates its `a` space from its `b` space (or from the face it reaches, for `exterior`); `y` where no horizontal axis does (a climb) |
| **way place** — a road, a street, a bridge, a causeway: a box that declares `way` (§4.1) | the site plan's box | `clear` columns across the box, starting `at` on the cross axis, at every station along the run | the declared `axis` |

A piece contract edge whose region realizes a plan seam — the face opening `DW0844` aligns, or the flight that joins to it — **is** that seam's way and is judged once, under the seam. A stair seam answered by a piece whose contract declares no flight joining to the face is counted *run undeclared by the piece* and its approaches are still judged.

### 1.4 Doors, stairs — and roads

A **door** is a portal; a **stair** is a stair seam's run with its portal; both are declared with their width. A **road** is a place (`compiler.md`, `nodes[]`: *a stretch of shore, a cavern, a road*); **cited** (the creator guide, `references/reference-works.md`, *A connector is a place*): a street, a bridge or a stair of its own is a box with its seams at each end, in the site plan and the layout graph. A place's `intent` is *a free non-empty label no check keys on*, and **cited** (measured at the ground revision: `way_class` and `way-class` occur in no file under `crates/dsl/src`) spec-0053 §3's way class is written and not built — so at the ground revision the engine holds no declared clear width for a road's own run, and this spec does **not** infer one from a box's shorter extent or from the word `connector`, which would be the inferred intent the constitution forbids. **Authored**, executing the ruling at its full scope: the place **declares** that it is a way — its travel `axis` and its `clear` width, as a number, on the site plan's box (§4.1) — and its run is then judged along its length like every other way, column by column at every station. The mouths at its ends are portals and its fronts are contacts, judged as such; the run between them is the place's own. The width is a number the author writes, not a class a table ranges (spec-0053 §3's shape), because this rule needs the declared width and nothing about whether the place is a *corridor* or a *road* — that kind is the author's word in `form` and `intent`, and no check keys on it.

## 3. What blocks part of a way

**Authored**, from `clear-width.md` §4.

- **The corridor.** A way's cross-section swept along its travel: for a portal or a contact in a wall, the plane's cells and **one approach cell** on each side at the sill (the cell a body stands in to pass — the whole-cell floor of the landing and manoeuvring-clearance figures the codes give, `clear-width.md` §3); for a stair run, the body cell over every tread at every course plus the foot's approach row at the host floor and the head's approach beyond the plane; for a seam through a floor, the hole's columns from two cells above the upper floor down to the lower floor; for a piece way, its region's columns and one approach cell in each endpoint space; for a way place, its `clear` columns at every station along its `axis` from one end of the box's play space to the other — a station a seam's approach already covers is judged once, under the seam.
- **Columns.** The corridor is judged one **column** at a time — one per cell across the width, at each station along the travel. A column is **clear** when a body can be in its body cell — the cell whose floor is the way's walk plane there (the sill, the tread's top as `solid_top_16` reads it, the rung; on a way place, the standable cell the nav model finds in the column over the place's floor, so a bridge's deck and a street's paving are each their own plane) — by the nav model's one predicate (`can_be`), with the headroom that predicate demands. So a beam over a body's head blocks nothing; a slab a body steps onto blocks nothing; a `Thin` block blocks nothing; a `TallBarrier`, a `FullCube`, a raised `PartialFloor` a body cannot step onto, a closed hatch, each blocks its column.
- **States.** A cell has the states it can take: its as-built block, and every block a quest-layer write puts there (`open-gate` to air, `close-gate` to its fill, a shortcut's unlock, a clock's, a `way`'s `laid` block or `cleared` void, a `set-block`). A column is clear only when it is clear in **every** state of every cell in it. This is what *shut and open* means here: an operated structure that takes part of a way while it stands has taken part of the way.
- **Whole, narrowed, shut.** A way is **whole** when every column of its corridor is clear; **narrowed** when some are and some are not; **shut** when none is — at a station, in a state. Shut is never this spec's finding: a shut plane is the gate's state (`DW0836` where the bar should not be there, `DW1011` for what it separates), and a shut approach is a wall the route proofs refuse. Narrowed is exactly the shape every existing proof passes.
- **Excluded, and why.** (a) The way's **own** gate region (`anchor/seam-<edge>`, the piece's `bar`) and its own `way` region: they are its shut state, judged by the proofs that own it. (b) The portal's **plane** columns: `DW0836` already refuses any allocated cell not passable, so this check counts them *left to `DW0836`* and never refuses twice (a contact's plane is **not** excluded — `DW0836` admits a contact with one passable column, which is this spec's finding). (c) Cells **outside** the cross-section: the frame of an opening, the lintel above its height, the wall beside a stair's treads. A rail or banister that is part of a way stands in that outside — the next column over from the declared width — and is never judged; one inside the declared width takes a column of it, because the engine's fence is a whole cell (`clear-width.md` §3), and is declared (§4) or moved.
- **Included, by name** (the brief's three classes). **Prefab bytes**: everything a bound piece's template puts in a corridor column. **The stand-in's own blocks**: a frame ring that reaches an approach, a tread run laid for one seam that enters another's corridor in the same host, a station landed in one. **Detail and props seated by the engine**: a holder, an `interact` prop, a `prop` block, a relight fixture, a world edit, a `set-block` — anything the assembled world the geometry proofs read contains or the quest layer writes. A gate region of **another** way is included in every state it takes (b above excludes only the way's own).

## 4. The decision, and the surface — `narrowed[]`

**Three sentences, authored.**

1. **Every way is judged whole**, over the assembled world, in every state its cells can take, by the columns of §3; a way narrowed by anything the author did not declare is refused (`DW1057`).
2. **A narrowing the author means is declared on the way**, naming where across the width it stands, which block stands there and what it is; the declaration is confirmed cell for cell against the bytes, and one that the bytes do not show — missing, moved, a different block, a clear column named — is refused (`DW1058`).
3. **Nothing else excuses a narrowing.** No flag, no acknowledgement, no second field: one hatch per gate, and the hatch demands the geometry.

**Why a declaration and not a rule.** A ruin's fallen lintel across half an arch, a newel post at the edge column of a broad stair, a hearth's cheek inside a wide fireplace opening a passage hides behind — each is a structure a creator may mean inside a declared width. A rule that admitted them by their block, their position or a fraction would be inferring what the author meant from what the bytes hold (§1.1), and a rule that admitted none would refuse legitimate designs. A declaration admits exactly the ones the author names.

**Why the hatch is one the defect cannot supply.** The defect is a structure nobody meant where it stands. To excuse it the author must write **where it stands in the way's own frame** (not the world cells the message prints — the message hands `[x, y, z]` and the declaration takes *side and columns*, so the excuse is not a paste), **which block** it is and **what it is**; the engine then confirms that the named block stands in exactly the named columns and that no other column of the corridor is taken. A declaration that excuses more than the bytes show, or less, or a different stone, is refused. A structure the author can locate in the design, name by its block and say what it is, is a structure the author has looked at — which is the only property that separates a meant narrowing from an accident, and the only one a cell grid can demand.

**Rejected, authored.**

- *An `ack` flag on the way.* The opt-out secured by a property the defect can supply: the finding's stair would carry `ack: true` and go green.
- *Declaring the narrowed width instead* (`passage` → `{width: 2}`). The treads are three wide and the plane is three wide; `DW0844` and `DW0836` would then refuse the piece for being wider than its seam, and the author would be narrowing a true declaration to hide a false structure.
- *Admitting fences by class.* A fence inside a doorway is a fence inside a doorway; the codes admit a rail's 4½ inches, not a 1-metre post (`clear-width.md` §3). The rail that is part of a way stands beside its width.
- *Reading the width from the reach graph.* Where a body can be (spec-0113) is the **result** the defect produces — a narrowed stair is reachable in one column — so a width read from it would measure the defect and find nothing. The reach graph is this check's predicate for *clear* (§9), never its source for *wide*.

**`boxes[].way`** — optional, `{axis, clear, at?}`: the place is a way whose run travels along `axis` (`x` or `z`), `clear` columns wide across it, the columns starting `at` cells from the box's low corner on the cross axis — defaulting to centred, `max(0, (extent − clear) div 2)`, exactly as a seam's `at` defaults. A `clear` wider than the box's cross extent, or an `at` that puts a column outside it, is refused at validation under `DW0826`'s *something the plan places leaves the region*, widened by one shape (a way wider than its place). A box without `way` is a room: its floor holds whatever its piece puts there, and this check never enters it. **`boxes[].narrowed[]`** — optional on a way place and refused on a room; each entry `{along, across, block, what}`, `along` the station counted in cells from the box's low corner on `axis`, `across` the inclusive span of columns counted from the way's first column; `block` and `what` as below.

**`seams[].narrowed[]`** — optional, a list; each entry `{side, across, course?, block, what}`. `side` is where along the travel: `a` (the approach in the `a` place), `b` (the approach in `b`), `plane` (a contact's span; refused on a portal, whose plane is `DW0836`'s), or `run` with `course` (a stair run's course, counted from the top as `LaidRun` counts it). `across` is the inclusive span of columns, counted from the opening's low corner on the face's in-plane horizontal axis (the `at` axis), so it reads as `at` reads. `block` is the block id with its state, as the piece palette writes it. `what` is a few words, like `form` — the creative statement, never player-facing. An entry whose `across` leaves the width, whose `side` the way does not have, or whose `what` is empty is refused at validation under the site plan's own codes (`DW0824`'s *the graph and the plan do not agree exactly* widens by one shape: a narrowing on a seam that is not a way).

### 4.2 The spatial contract — the piece

```json
{ "a": "room", "b": "landing", "class": "stair", "rise": 5, "via": "keeper-flight",
  "narrowed": [
    { "cells": [[10, 1, 14], [10, 2, 14], [10, 3, 14]], "block": "minecraft:stone_bricks", "what": "the jamb of the cart road's mouth" }
  ] }
```

**`edges[].narrowed[]`** on a contract edge of class `walk`, `stair`, `climb`, `drop` or `barred` — optional; each entry `{cells, block, what}`, the cells in the piece's own frame, as every box of the contract is. **The object determines the frame**: a seam speaks in sides and columns, a way place in stations and columns, and the piece has only cells. The fields that mean the same thing — `block`, `what` — are one type in both. A contract edge that realizes a plan seam (§1.3) carries no `narrowed`: the seam's declaration is the one authority, and a `narrowed` on such an edge is refused by the contract's own gate (`DW0888`'s *a declaration its own bytes deny* widens by one shape, since the piece's bytes answer the plan's seam there).

**What does not move.** `opening`, `contact`, `form`, `stair_in`, every class, every proof the derivation and the blockout battery already run.

## 5. The analysis — `compiler::nav::width`

Build tier, over the assembled world the geometry proofs read, from the call site in `Plan::build` where `DW0450` runs — after the pieces, the stand-ins, the holders, the props and the edits are in, and with the plan's seams, the laid runs and the contract graphs in hand. **One authority**: a column is clear by `can_be`; a cell's states are the region-write model's; a seam's axis is `bar_axis`; a run's columns are `LaidRun`'s or the contract flight's `via`; the way's own regions are `PlacedSeam::bar` and the contract's `bar`/`way`. Nothing here is a second copy of the nav model or the derivation.

### 5.1 The population

Every way of §1.3, enumerated from the plan's seams (`P` portals, `S` stair runs, `C` contacts), the plan's boxes declaring `way` (`Y` way places) and every bound piece's contract edges that answer no seam (`K` piece ways); the denominators are the plan's traversal seams, the plan's boxes and the contracts' traversal edges.

### 5.2 The verdict per way

For each way: its corridor's columns (§3) and, per column, its states; the count **clear** / **taken**, per station (each approach, the plane, each course). A way is *whole*, *narrowed* or *shut* at each station. For a narrowed station the check reads the way's `narrowed[]`: the declared columns (an entry's `across` on its `side` or at its `along`, or the entry's `cells` mapped to columns) with their block, against the taken columns with the block that takes them in the state that takes them. **Confirmed** when the two sets are equal and every block matches; **undeclared** where a taken column has no entry (`DW1057`); **not shown** where an entry names a column that is clear, a block that is not the one standing, a side or a course the way does not have (`DW1058`).

### 5.3 The ledger and the binding line

`validation/clear-width.json` carries one row per way: its kind, the seam or the piece and edge, the declared width, each station with its columns clear and taken (world cells, block, the state that takes it), its verdict, and every `narrowed[]` entry with its confirmation. Written on every build, green or red; a campaign with no ways writes it with its zeros.

The binding line, on every build: `clear width (DW1057/DW1058): W way(s) of T traversal seam(s), B box(es) and E contract edge(s) — P portal(s), S stair run(s), C contact(s), Y way place(s), K piece way(s); M column(s) at N station(s) judged over V state(s); H way(s) whole, U narrowed undeclared, D narrowed as declared (G column(s) excused by F declaration(s)), X declaration(s) not shown; L plane column(s) left to DW0836; R stair run(s) undeclared by their piece`. Every count is a number; `0` is written as `0`.

## 6. Diagnostics

### 6.1 `DW1057` — a way narrowed by something the author did not declare

Error, build tier (exit 3), `compiler::nav::width`, raised once per way at the first station and state it is narrowed without a declaration. Quantifier: every way of §1.3. The message names the way (the connection with its class, the seam's `form`, the way place by its node, or the piece and its contract edge), the station (*the approach in `node/yard`*, *course 3 of the run*, *the span*, *12 cells along `node/causeway`*), the declared clear width, the **free width** as *N of W columns clear*, and every taking cell as `[x, y, z]` with its block and the state in which it takes the column (*as built*; *while `anchor/barricade` is shut*). Remedy, in the message: move the structure out of the way's W columns; or, where it is meant, declare it — the `narrowed[]` entry the author would write, with `side` and `across` **filled from the way's frame** and `block` filled, and `what` left for the author, so the paste is not complete without the one word the engine cannot write. Remedy rows in `remedy_reachability.rs`: the structure moved one column over (green); the declaration written (green); the declaration written with the wrong block (red under `DW1058`).

### 6.2 `DW1058` — a declared narrowing the bytes do not show

Error, build tier (exit 3), same module. Quantifier: every `narrowed[]` entry on every way. Three shapes under one code, by what disagrees: *declared across `[u0, u1]` on side `a`, but column `u` there is clear*; *declared `minecraft:stone_bricks`, but `minecraft:oak_planks` stands at `[x, y, z]`*; *declared on a side, a course or a plane this way does not have*. Remedy, always the word's or the world's: correct or remove the entry, or put the structure back. A declaration that is **stale** — the piece re-detailed, the post gone — is this code, so an excuse never outlives the thing it excused.

### 6.3 Why two codes

The two findings have opposite remedies and opposite directions: `DW1057` says *the world narrows a way you did not declare narrowed* — fix the world or declare; `DW1058` says *you declared a narrowing the world does not have* — fix the declaration. A reader filtering a build by code must tell them apart, and the vacuity test of `CLAUDE.md` needs a perturbation only the stale-excuse rule can catch (§7's second probe reds nothing else). One code with two meanings would be `DW1054`'s shape without `DW1054`'s reason: there the three words share one object and one remedy sentence; here the object differs (a structure; an entry).

### 6.4 Not new

`DW0836` is unchanged and this check defers a portal's plane to it (§3); `DW0824`, `DW0826` and `DW0888` each widen by one shape (§4); `DW1011`, `DW0837`, `DW0986`, `DW0844` are unchanged in population and verdict.

## 9. Interaction with the reach graph, guidance and spec-0118

- **The reach graph** (spec-0113). *Clear* is the nav model's predicate for where a body can be; when spec-0113 lands, that predicate is the reachable-surface graph's, and this check follows it with no change of its own — one authority for *can a body be here*. *Wide* is never read from it (§4, rejected).
- **Guidance** (spec-0118 §9, craft advice for the skill): *hide the shut way with structure that belongs to the setting* stays satisfiable, and this spec says how: the hiding structure is the shut way's **own gate** — cells of its `anchor/seam-<edge>` region or of a gate station cleared by the same beat — standing in that way's own corridor across its **whole** width, and in no other way's corridor. **Owner-confirmed** example: a fireplace hiding a passage hides a way without blocking part of one — the hearth is built around the opening's declared width (cheeks beside, mantel above), and what stands inside the width is the way's own gate, cleared when the passage opens. The barricade of the finding was the right idea in the wrong cells: across the cart road's mouth it was that way's gate; across the stair's foot it was a structure in another way. The craft line for `placement.md`: **a structure that hides a shut way stands only in that way's own width; it never enters the width of a door, a stair or a road beside it.**
- **spec-0118's declarations** (`learned`) read nothing this spec changes; `seen` walks a sight line to the bar, which a narrowing beside the bar neither helps nor hinders. The two specs share `three-ways-shut`: its re-detailing under this gate (§8) keeps every `learned` word true, which `DW1054` confirms on the same build.

## 11. What stays open

- **A way class.** A way place declares its width as a number; spec-0053 §3's class — a named range the gym calibrates — stays unbuilt, and nothing in this rule needs it. If a later brief wants `way_class.road` to range a declared `clear`, the class is a check over the number this spec already has, never a second declaration of the width.
- **The approach is one cell.** The codes' landing and manoeuvring figures are a cell and a fraction (`clear-width.md` §3); one cell is the floor. A two-cell approach is the recorded next shape, decided on the demo level and on playtests, never by a number without a source.
- **A stair's landing beyond the head.** The head's approach is one cell beyond the plane; a landing as deep as the stair is wide (the codes' figure) is the same open item as the approach depth.
- **Vertical ways.** A climb's corridor is the rung column and the hole; a body beside a ladder on a two-wide hole is modelled by `holds_body`, and whether the second column of a wide ladder hole is a way or a shaft is decided on the demo level before any rule narrows it.

## Acceptance criteria

Each is a test or a gate run, named; a criterion the tree cannot yet satisfy is a recorded debt, never a pass. `delvec` is the implementing tree's binary, force-rebuilt before any comparison.

1. **Surface.** `delvec schema --stage site-plan` exports `boxes[].way` as `{axis, clear, at?}` with `axis` the closed set `x | z`, `boxes[].narrowed[]` as `{along, across, block, what}`, and `seams[].narrowed[]` as `{side, across, course?, block, what}` with `side` the closed set `a | b | plane | run`; the contract schema exports `edges[].narrowed[]` as `{cells, block, what}` on the five traversal and barred classes and on nothing else; an empty `what`, an `across` leaving the width, a `plane` on a portal, a `clear` wider than its box, a `narrowed` on a box without `way`, or a `narrowed` on a contract edge that realizes a plan seam is refused at validation with the code §4 names for each; `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py` reports every field bound.
2. **The population.** A test over a fixture with one of each kind asserts the binding line's `P`, `S`, `C`, `Y`, `K` against `T`, `B` and `E`; a campaign with no site plan and no contract ways writes `validation/clear-width.json` with every count `0`; a stair seam answered by a piece with no flight joining to its face counts into `R` and its approaches are still judged (asserted).
3. **One predicate.** The column-clear test calls `nav::separate::can_be` (or the function it is extracted to) and no private standing rule; an agreement test drives `can_be` and the column test over generated worlds and asserts they never disagree on a body cell.
4. **States.** A fixture with a gate region of another way standing in a way's approach is refused `DW1057` naming the region and *while `anchor/…` is shut*, and is green when the region is the way's own; a `set-block` into an approach column is refused the same way; a fixture whose only narrowing is a `Thin` block or a bottom slab a body steps onto is whole (asserted).
5. **The finding's shape.** A fixture reproducing the yard's foot — a three-wide run, one approach column under fixed masonry, one under a foreign gate region — is refused `DW1057` with *1 of 3 columns clear* in the shut state and *2 of 3* in the open, naming both cells with their blocks and states; the same fixture with the masonry and the region moved one column outward is whole.
6. **Confirmed declarations.** The fixture of criterion 5 with the masonry declared `narrowed` on the seam (`side: a`, the column, the block, a `what`) and the region moved is green and counts into `D` and `G`; the entry removed reds `DW1057`; its `block` changed reds `DW1058`; its `across` moved reds `DW1058`; the masonry removed with the entry left reds `DW1058` (the stale excuse).
7. **The `DW0836` pair.** A portal with a solid cell in its plane reds `DW0836` and this check counts the column into `L` and raises nothing; a contact with half its span walled is green under `DW0836` and refused `DW1057` — the pair test asserts both verdicts over one build.
8. **The stand-in.** A two-seam fixture with no pieces, where one seam's laid run enters the other's approach column, is refused `DW1057` naming the tread cells; moving the stair seam's `at` by one is green — the probe's shape, taken in test.
9. **Piece ways.** A fixture piece whose contract `stair` edge's `via` is three wide and whose foot approach holds one solid cell is refused `DW1057` in the piece's frame; its `narrowed` with `cells` naming that cell is green; a piece flight that realizes a plan stair seam is judged once (the ledger holds one row, asserted).
10. **Engine-seated structure.** A holder, an `interact` prop and a relight fixture each placed in an approach column are refused `DW1057` naming the object; the same objects one column outward are green.
10a. **A way place.** A fixture box declaring `way: {axis, clear}` with one solid cell in a middle column half-way along its run is refused `DW1057` naming the station in cells along the box and *clear − 1 of clear columns clear*; the cell declared `boxes[].narrowed[]` with its `along` is green and counts into `D`; the same cell one column outside the declared `clear` is green; the box with `way` removed is a room and the cell is not judged (the ledger holds no row for it, asserted); a station a seam's approach covers appears once in the ledger, under the seam.

13. **Gallery and probes.** The causeway is a way place with its lamp post bound and confirmed, the undercroft newel is bound and confirmed on both overlays, a contract-edge `narrowed` is bound on a piece way, the perturbations of §7 red and green as stated, the four probes are refused with their codes by `check-gallery-coverage.py`; `gallery/baseline/` moves by the newel's and the lamp post's cells, the declarations, the ledger and the binding line, attributed row by row in the commit body.
