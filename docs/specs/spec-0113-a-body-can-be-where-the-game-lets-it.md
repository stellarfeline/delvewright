# spec-0113: A body can be where the game lets it — one reachable-surface graph, simulated tick by tick under the pinned physics, read by every proof that asks where a player can be, and a declared jump proven makeable

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against engine `85c24c174` (`origin/main`). Read: `crates/dsl/src/metrics.rs` (`JUMP_REACH`, `jump_max_gap`, `step_allowed`, `WATER_CLIMB_OUT_RISE`, the fall and climb constants), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/world/{body,climb,mod}.rs` (`neighbors_fp`, `moves_of`, `settle_fp`, `fatal_step_off`, `mob_moves`, `reachable_walkable_rooted`), `nav/leave.rs` (`DW0921`), `nav/horizon.rs` (`DW0322`), `nav/sea.rs`, `nav/route/{mod,region}.rs`, every call site of the movement relations across `crates/delvec/src/compiler` (`blind`, `lethal`, `light`, `sculk`, `strand`, `surround`, `stake`, `reach`, `engage`, `daylight`, `blocking`, `emit/{mod,routes,proofs}`, `edit`, `blockout`, `grammar/contract`), `harness/src/movement.ts`, mineflayer-pathfinder 2.4.5 `lib/movements.js` and prismarine-physics 1.11.1 `index.js` at the harness lockfile's pins, `docs/notes/jump-arc-model.md`, and the pinned 1.21.11 server and client jars by mapped name.
- **What it is for**: two demo-level playtests left the map by movements no proof judged. In The Bell Changes the Tune a player sprint-jumped out of the site and fell out of the world; in The Thing Across the Water a player jumped off the land and was stranded in the sea with no way back. The leave proof (`DW0921`) and the stranding proof (`DW0322`) judged a smaller region than a body can reach, so both were green. This spec makes **where a vanilla body can be** one computed graph, over-approximated in the direction that can only turn a proof red, consumed by every proof that asks the question, and makes the same simulator prove, in the other direction, that a jump a creator declares is makeable.
- **Research**: `docs/reference/body-movement.md` is the record — every rule of the pinned game this spec rests on, cited to its read site, with the rig measurements two independent implementations agree on. Nothing from it is restated here; this document says what the engine does with it.
- **Numbers**: spec `0113`. Three diagnostics: `DW1041` (§5.2), `DW1042` (§5.3), `DW1043` (§5.4). `DW0921` and `DW0322` keep their codes under a wider quantifier each (§5.1), which can only turn a proof red. **`dsl_version` moves to `0.50.0`**: the world stage gains `jumps[]` (§6).
- **Non-goals**: the mob relation (`World::mob_moves` stays the model of vanilla's ground pathfinder, a different object from a player body, §4.6); scaffolding and the open-trapdoor ladder (spec-0099 §3.3–3.4); bubble columns, elytra, riptide, wind charges, ender pearls, chorus fruit and every item or effect that changes a body's movement (refused by name, §5.4, until a later spec takes them); the server's movement correction, which never undoes a move the client's physics allows (`body-movement.md` §8); glitches that depend on packet timing or client settings (`sprintWindow`, auto-jump), which no simulation of one client's tick reproduces and which this model names as outside it.

## 1. The thing, and the object it belongs to

**Authored.** Where a body can be is a fact about **the pinned game's body under the pinned physics over the assembled world**, and about nothing a verb or a proof decides. So:

- *How the body moves in one tick* — input, acceleration, friction, gravity, collision, stepping, jumping, swimming, the poses — belongs to one simulator in `delvewright_dsl::metrics::body` (the crate that already holds the body's measured facts), parameterised by the attribute set the body carries and taking a collision world through a trait.
- *Where a body can rest, and which rest places one movement joins* belongs to the navigation world (`compiler::nav::world`), as the **reach graph**: nodes are rest states, edges are movements proven by forward-simulating the body from a launch state to a rest state. It is built from the assembled occupancy model and the premises every nav world already carries (lethal volumes, furniture, gates, ambient), once per quest configuration a proof asks about, and cached (§7).
- *What asks the graph* is every proof whose question is "can a player be here" (§5.1); *what asks the walk relation* is every proof whose question is "can the party walk this on cue" (§4.5). Two relations, two questions, no third.

## 2. What the current model does, and where it is wrong

**Measured against the tree.** `World::body_moves` (`nav/world/body.rs` `moves_of`) is a cell relation: the walk step (`step_allowed` over sixteenths), a fall in the same column settled on the first floor within the survivable depth, a cardinal gap jump admitted by `metrics::JUMP_REACH` when a three-cell band is clear over the launch and every gap column, wading, swimming on the surface, the one-block climb-out, and the climb of spec-0099. Each of the following is a movement the game allows and the relation does not, and each can hide a place a body reaches:

1. **Only cardinal jumps from cell centres.** A body jumps at any yaw and from anywhere its box is supported; a landing one cell off the axis is unseen.
2. **Only onto standable cells.** A body lands on a fence or wall top it jumped down onto (`tall` is never floor), on a partial block the occupancy model treats as a full cube (stairs, trapdoors, a bottom slab's lip), and on the half-block of a honey or soul-sand block.
3. **`JUMP_REACH` is the minimum over runways** of what the harness bot's physics measured, and `jump_max_gap` reads the shallower row for a partial rise. Both choices under-approximate, which is the right direction for the route proof the table was built for and the wrong one for the leave and stranding proofs that read it.
4. **The clear band.** Requiring three cells of air over launch and gap refuses the head-hitter arcs the game allows under a lid three cells up (`body-movement.md` §5).
5. **A fall lands in its own column.** A body leaving a ledge at sprint speed drifts up to three columns while it falls twenty blocks; the relation settles it where it stepped off.
6. **A sprint across one column** without a jump is a walk the game allows and the walk relation refuses.
7. **No pose.** A body crouches under 1.5 blocks of headroom and crawls under one; the model needs two cells of air everywhere.
8. **No dive.** A swimmer that sinks walks the sea floor and swims through any water cell; the relation keeps it on the surface and refuses capped water.
9. **No horizontal reach in water.** A body in the sea at the bottom of a one-block step climbs out by the `0.3` impulse; the relation's rim rule is a cell rule derived from one measurement.

Every item is in the direction that lets something ship. The repair is not nine more arms: it is one simulator and one graph.

## 3. Decisions

1. **Over-approximate reach; under-approximate a declared jump.** The graph answers "where can a body be" with every control the body has, from the most favourable launch state the geometry allows (§4.3): a proof reading it can only turn red where the old relation was green. A declared jump (§6) is proven in the other direction: every state in an authored window must land (§4.4). The two bounds are two parameter sets of one simulator, never two simulators.
2. **The simulator is the engine's own Rust, held to the jar by three instruments** (§8): its arithmetic reproduces prismarine-physics tick for tick over a committed case set; the ballistic half (gravity, drag, collision, slime, water) is held to the pinned jar's own code by a PackTest that watches a mob with no AI fall, bounce and sink; and the input half is held to the live measurements already in the record. A fourth instrument — Carpet fake players on the toolserver, which would run the jar's own player movement — is named as the follow-up that closes the input half against the jar directly.
3. **Templates propose, simulation disposes** (§4.3, §7). The arcs a body can fly from a flat launch in free air are computed once per launch class and stored with the cells each sweeps; a world cell checks the template's swept cells and simulates only the arcs an obstacle touches. This is the cost answer, and the budget is stated and refused rather than truncated (`DW1042`).
4. **One cell relation remains, for walking on cue.** `World::neighbors_fp` (walk, step, climb) stays the route relation the bot drives. Its step rule is asserted against the simulator by test rather than restated, and it gains nothing a bot does not do on cue.
5. **The mob relation stays a cell relation** (§4.6): vanilla moves a mob by its ground pathfinder, not by a player's inputs; `mob_moves` models that object and is not this spec's.
6. **A declared jump is a DSL surface now** (§6): `world.jumps[]`, proven by `DW1041`, taken as a leg move by the route relation, exported with the waypoints, and driven by the bot through its pathfinder's parkour move. Without a consumer the under-approximation would be an unrun helper, and the demo level's course needs the declaration.
7. **Unmodelled movement is refused by name** (`DW1043`): a kit, `give-item`, loot or shop item whose effect or component changes how the body moves is refused while the model does not take it. A kit that changes the body's fall-damage arithmetic is admitted, and every fall becomes survivable to the over-approximation (§4.2).
8. **`JUMP_REACH`, `jump_max_gap` and `WATER_CLIMB_OUT_RISE` are deleted**, with the `spike-jump-arc` citations that name a tool the tree no longer has; `metrics` keeps the body's facts and gains the simulator's constants with their read sites.

## 4. The model

### 4.1 Rest states (authored)

A node of the graph is a cell and a state: **standing** (the body's box rests on a collision top inside the cell, in one of the pose classes the headroom allows — standing, crouched, crawling), **afloat** (a water surface cell), **submerged** (a water cell with water over it), **holding** (a climb cell, spec-0099 §4.1). A cell a declared killing volume reaches, or whose support is furniture, is no rest state (the premises every nav world carries). A fence, wall or gate top is a standing rest state at its own height for a body that arrives from above; it is still never a floor the walk relation stands a body on.

### 4.2 The body (authored over cited attributes)

The simulator takes an attribute set: width, standing and crouched and crawling heights, movement speed, sprint multiplier, step height, gravity, jump strength, safe fall distance, fall-damage multiplier, max health, water-movement efficiency. The graph is built for **the vanilla player body** (`body-movement.md` §8, the `Player.createAttributes` defaults). Food is assumed sufficient to sprint. A campaign whose kits carry a damage-reducing armour enchantment, a max-health change or a fall-damage multiplier is built with an unbounded survivable fall, stated in the binding line; anything else that moves the body differently is `DW1043`.

### 4.3 Launch states and the over-approximating edge set (authored)

From a standing rest state, the body may leave its cell by **walking off, sprinting off, or jumping**, in any direction. The launch set per cell:

- **Facing**: every yaw at one-degree steps through the game's own sine table, with strafe `−1`, `0`, `+1` and input magnitude `1.0` (the diagonal's magnitude, the body's most favourable, `body-movement.md` §2).
- **Speed**: a saturated sprint, regardless of the run-up the cell actually offers. Momentum carried across successive jumps never exceeds it (`body-movement.md` §5), so the terminal run-up speed bounds every chain.
- **Position**: the body's box is placed as far toward the exit edge as the support allows, plus the one tick of lagged `onGround` the game grants, both taken from the simulator's own approach rather than assumed.
- **Press**: every tick of the supported window, jump held or not.
- **Pose**: the tallest pose the headroom allows at launch; the game's pose rule decides en route.

Each launch is simulated under the pinned tick until the body comes to rest, dies, or leaves the window (§4.7). A landing is credited when the body's box, **widened by `0.1` on each side for this test only**, overlaps the rest cell's support at touchdown; collision uses the exact box. The simulation runs over the assembled world with every premise applied: a killing volume the arc enters ends the body, a lethal fall ends it, a shut gate is solid, an open or openable one is passable at the state the configuration gives it.

The **template**: for each launch floor class (the collision-top sixteenths the world contains) and each ceiling class over the launch cell, the free-air arcs above are computed once, deduplicated by their swept cell set, and stored with their landing offset. At a world cell, an arc whose swept cells hold no collider and no fluid is credited from the template without simulation; an arc that touches one is re-simulated over the real cells from that launch state. The convergence of the facing step is an acceptance criterion (A7), not an assumption.

Water: a standing body steps or falls into water as the simulator says; afloat and submerged bodies move cell to cell through 6-adjacent water (swimming in any direction, sinking, the `+0.04` rise), climb out by the simulated `0.3` impulse onto any support within reach, and jump from a floor under water no deeper than the fluid-jump threshold. Air supply is **not** a bound on the graph: a bound could only remove reach. Current (`0.014` per cell of flow) is applied as the simulator computes it.

Climbing: spec-0099's holding arms are kept and the catch (spec-0099 §3.5) is a consequence of the simulator rather than a separate rule; a holding body may also launch (let go, jump off) under the same launch set.

### 4.4 The under-approximating edge: a declared jump (authored margin over the same simulator)

A jump is **makeable** when every launch in an authored window lands with the exact box overlapping the landing support by at least `0.1`: facing within `±5°` of the straight line between the two anchors' cells, strafe `0`, input magnitude `0.98`, sprinting when declared, pressed on **any of the last two supported ticks** before the edge, from the run-up the course gives — the straight walkable cells behind the launch cell along the jump's axis, capped at six, measured off the world and printed. A jump whose run-up is shorter than the one its distance needs fails here, and the remedy is a longer run-up, which the message names. The window's three figures are this spec's own (the two-tick press is the "timing read, not coin flip" standard spec-0016 §4 holds a timed gate to), and they live in `metrics` beside the body's facts, flagged `authored`.

### 4.5 What stays on the walk relation (authored)

`World::neighbors_fp` keeps the route proofs (`DW0311` and family, `DW0314`), `move-npc`/`move-actor` routing (`DW0307`), lanes (`DW0386`), checkpoints and respawns (`DW0315`, `DW0316`), pacing (`DW0379`, `DW0822`), the stealth-onset sprint (`DW0355`), the shortcut and ambush proofs that route a party, and the exported waypoints. A proven declared jump (§6) is the one edge the route relation gains, and it is driven, never discovered. `metrics::step_allowed` is unchanged and is asserted against the simulator (A3).

### 4.6 Mobs (authored)

`World::mob_moves` is unchanged: the proofs that ask where a wave can get (`DW0922`, `DW0923`, `DW0924`, the party-and-mob region of `strand`) model vanilla's ground pathfinder, which plans no leap and no dive. A mob pushed or knocked through the air is not modelled; the direction is stated: those proofs can under-report a mob's reach, and nothing here changes it.

### 4.7 The window and the ambient (authored)

The graph is bounded by the nav world's built volume grown by the farthest simulated arc, and by the open-sea rule `DW0322` already has: an afloat or submerged body that reaches the window's edge in an `ocean` ambient is in the open sea, every such body is one, and under a `void` ambient a body that leaves the built volume with nothing below has left the world — a rest state the proofs name as such.

## 5. The proofs

### 5.1 Consumers, and the order they move in

Every current reader of `body_moves`, `reachable_walkable`, `reachable_walkable_rooted` or `fatal_step_off` was classified by its question. **Where can a player be** moves to the graph; **can the party walk this on cue** stays (§4.5). The moves, in three waves, each wave one commit with its binding lines:

1. **The two escapes.** `nav/leave.rs` (`DW0921`: the flood from the route cells becomes the graph's reach from them, per configuration; `movement_words` names the edge kind — "a sprint jump north-east up one", "a fall of nine, drifting two"); `nav/horizon.rs` (`DW0322`: the enterable-column heuristic is deleted; a body is in the sea exactly when the graph puts it in an afloat or submerged state, and stranded when no path in the graph leads from that state to a standing state outside the sea or to the open sea's exit rule of §4.7). Both binding lines gain the graph's counts (§5.5).
2. **The other "where a body can be" readers**: the lethal keep-out population (`lethal.rs`, `DW0891`), the blinding reach (`blind.rs`, `DW0943`, whose `fatal_step_off` becomes a graph query), the sculk catalyst closure (`sculk.rs`, `DW1001`), the relight survey and darkness grants (`light.rs`, `DW0210` — a ledge a body jumps to is a cell that must be lit), the sea-seepage region (`nav/sea.rs`, `DW0851`), the seated-party region (`strand.rs`), the gap-floor region of a surround (`surround.rs`), the per-area emission regions (`emit/mod.rs`, `emit/routes.rs`, `emit/proofs.rs`), the edit battery's walk region (`edit.rs`), the daylight and blocking regions (`daylight.rs`, `blocking.rs`), and `reach.rs`'s footing search for a `reach` objective's completion radius.
3. **Design-time joins**: the blockout battery's "two places joined where no seam was allocated" (`blockout.rs`, `DW0838`) and the grammar contract walks (`grammar/contract.rs`) read cells of a box, not an assembled world; they move when the graph can be built over a box, and the gap is recorded as a row in the ledger rather than left as a doc line.

After wave 1 the old gap-jump arm is deleted from `moves_of`; after wave 2 `body_moves` is deleted, and `reachable_walkable` keeps only its walk-relation readers under a name that says so (`walk_region`).

### 5.2 `DW1041` — a declared jump is not makeable (build tier, `compiler::nav`)

For each `world.jumps[]` entry: `from` and `to` resolve to standing rest states one jump apart (not walk-connected: a declared jump across a gap the walk relation already crosses is reported in the binding line as `0 proven, 1 not a jump`, and refused — a declaration that confirms nothing is the author's intent misread); the window of §4.4 lands from the measured run-up. The refusal names the entry, the two cells, the run-up measured and the run-up needed, the first facing and press tick that miss, and where the body ended. Derived by simulation, never by table.

### 5.3 `DW1042` — the reach budget is exceeded (build tier, about the engine, `compiler::nav`)

The graph's work is bounded: launch states simulated plus template lookups, per configuration, against a ceiling `metrics` states (`REACH_BUDGET_STATES`, authored from the measurements of §7). A build whose graph would exceed it is refused with the count, the ceiling and the configuration named, never truncated: an untraversed graph is the vacuity mode `CLAUDE.md` names, and a green over a partial graph is not a pass.

### 5.4 `DW1043` — a movement the model does not take (build tier, `compiler::nav`)

Every item the engine puts in a player's hands — a kit item, a `give-item`, a container's loot, a shop offer — is read for: a potion whose named or custom effects include `speed`, `slowness`, `jump_boost`, `slow_falling`, `levitation` or `dolphins_grace`; an `attribute_modifiers` component touching `movement_speed`, `jump_strength`, `step_height`, `gravity`, `safe_fall_distance`, `fall_damage_multiplier`, `water_movement_efficiency`, `movement_efficiency` or `sneaking_speed`; `elytra`; a `trident` with `riptide`; `wind_charge`; `ender_pearl`; `chorus_fruit`; `depth_strider`, `swift_sneak` or `soul_speed`. Each is refused naming the item, the place it is given and the rule it would change. The binding line counts items examined. This is the exclusion `CLAUDE.md` prescribes for a capability the engine cannot yet prove; the follow-up that takes attribute sets per kit lifts it.

### 5.5 Binding lines and the artifact

Every walked build prints `reach binding: K configuration(s); N rest state(s) (S standing, A afloat, U submerged, H holding); E edge(s) (W walk, F fall, J jump, D dive, C climb, X exit); L launch state(s) simulated, T template arc(s) credited; B of budget; survivable fall F blocks (unbounded: yes/no); run time` and writes the same counts, with the per-kind edge counts and the sha256 of the occupancy model it was built over, to `validation/reach-graph.json`. `DW1041`'s line: `jumps binding: J declared, P proven, R refused, Q not a jump; run-ups measured a..b`. `DW1043`'s: `movement items: I examined, R refused`. `DW0921` and `DW0322` keep their lines with the reached count now the graph's.

## 6. The surface for content

`world.jumps[]` (world stage, `dsl_version 0.50.0`): `{ id, from, to, sprint }` — `id` a `jump/` identifier, `from` and `to` anchors that resolve to standing rest states, `sprint` a boolean defaulting to `true`. The engine proves each (§5.2), the route relation takes a proven jump as a leg move between its two cells, each exported leg carries `jumps[]` beside spec-0099's `climbs[]` (`from`, `to`, `sprint`, the measured run-up), and the harness drives such a hop with mineflayer-pathfinder's parkour move (`allowParkour` on for that hop only, off for every other leg so the bot walks what the proof proved) and refuses by name a jump outside the pathfinder's own table (forward beyond four columns, up at four, down without a clear lid where the table needs one) as a recorded harness gap, exactly as spec-0099 §6 refuses a climbable its physics cannot climb. Nothing else is declared: a gap a creator does not declare is judged only by the over-approximation, and the engine infers no intent from geometry.

## 7. Cost and caching

**Baselines on the tree** (`DW0921 binding` lines, the current cell relation): the gallery `15,372` cells reached over `21` configurations; The Stranding's scene *the beached thing* `31,137` over `1`; The Treehouse Camp `39,700` over `2`; Vesperhold `316,580` over `6` (ledger row `vh-16`). The graph has more edges per node (the launch set) and the same order of nodes. The template credits every arc over open ground without simulation; the arcs simulated are those an obstacle touches, which on an open map is a fraction of the launch set at the fraction of cells beside walls, lips and ledges. Per configuration the graph is memoised on the sha256 of the occupancy model plus the configuration's gate state, so two configurations that differ in no cell share one graph, and a stage-8 edit batch rebuilds only the configurations whose cells moved. The acceptance criterion (A10) is a measured ratio on two named builds, not a promise; `REACH_BUDGET_STATES` is set from those measurements with headroom and stated in the commit body that lands them.

## 8. How the simulator is held to the game

1. **Against prismarine-physics 1.11.1** (MIT, the harness pin): `harness/test/physics-cases.test.ts` drives the library over the rigs of `body-movement.md` §9 plus one case per movement class of §4.3 (slab launch, head-hitter, fence top, honey, soul sand, ice, slime bounce, water sink and rise, climb-out, current) and writes every tick's position and velocity to `crates/dsl/tests/fixtures/movement/cases-1.21.11.json`; `metrics::body::tests::the_simulator_matches_the_bot_physics` replays each case and asserts equality within `1e-9` per tick. The two share no code and no configuration but the case file.
2. **Against the jar's own ballistic code**: a PackTest (`packtest/` template `movement_ballistics`) summons `minecraft:zombie{NoAI:1b}` bodies with declared `Motion` over stone, slime, water and a slab lip, reads `Pos` every tick for sixty ticks, and asserts each against the trajectory the simulator computed for a body of that mob's dimensions, committed beside the template. This holds gravity, drag, collision, the slime bounce and the water sink to the pinned server with no player input.
3. **Against the live record**: `docs/notes/jump-arc-model.md` §2's thirty-three verdicts and two landing positions are a committed case the simulator reproduces (`the_simulator_reproduces_the_live_spike`), as `JUMP_REACH`'s own check once did.
4. **Named follow-up**: Carpet fake players on the toolserver (MIT; ADR-0003 names them) would drive the jar's own `ServerPlayer` movement with inputs and close the input half against the jar directly; it is not adopted by this spec.

## 10. Decisions, in one line each

1. One simulator, two parameter sets: over-approximate reach, under-approximate a declared jump (§3.1).
2. The simulator is Rust in `dsl::metrics::body`, held to the game by three instruments (§8); Carpet fake players are the named follow-up.
3. Templates propose, simulation disposes; the budget is refused, never truncated (`DW1042`).
4. The walk relation stays for walking on cue, and gains only the proven declared jump.
5. Mobs keep their cell relation.
6. `world.jumps[]` now, `dsl_version 0.50.0`; the bot drives a declared jump through its pathfinder's parkour move and refuses what that move cannot express.
7. Unmodelled movement items and effects are `DW1043`; fall-damage reduction makes every fall survivable to the over-approximation.
8. `JUMP_REACH`, `jump_max_gap`, `WATER_CLIMB_OUT_RISE` and the spike citations are deleted; `metrics` holds the simulator's constants with read sites; its fall-damage comment is corrected to the jar's `floor`.

## Acceptance criteria

Each is checked against the tree before it is written; on `85c24c174` every one but A1's reference page is **debt** — the implementation the planner dispatches on this branch owes it.

1. `docs/reference/body-movement.md` exists and cites every rule the simulator implements by class and method; `tools/ci/check-doc-dupes.py` and `check-unsanctioned-identifiers.py` pass over it. *Vacuous if* the page restated numbers a test holds: it carries read sites and rig results only, and `metrics` carries the constants.
