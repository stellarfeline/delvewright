## 1. The defect, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (the tree at `446c3a1fd`, re-measured for this spec; the brief's three readings at `9595285e` hold here). A grammar program writes no block entity (`prefab-procedure.md` §6: *No block entities and no NBT. No chest loot, no sign text*). No quest effect writes sign text: `Verb::SetBlock { anchor, block }` and `Prop { block }` take a block id with a blockstate suffix and nothing else, and the suffix parser passes `[…]` to `setblock` verbatim. Of the content repository's templates at `a5f546138` — **190** `.nbt` files, every one read through the local LFS object store (190 resolved, 0 missing) — **0** carry `front_text` and **0** carry a sign block in their palette. The gallery has one sign, `minecraft:oak_sign[rotation=8]`, the `prop` of `trigger/read-the-label`; the trigger narrates in chat *The label under the case reads: every surface, written once*, because the label cannot read anything, and spec-0112 §11's measurement of the gallery's written load world counts *one blank sign*. The demo level The Lever and the Keeper (content `demo/the-lever-and-the-keeper` at `a5f546138`) designs *a lettered sign* over its lever (`DESIGN.md`, *Layout*), its keeper's hint says *under the sign* in two languages, and its `GENERATION.md` closes with the finding *a campaign cannot letter a sign — it blocks this level*.

**The object the words belong to is the sign block entity** — `SignBlockEntity`'s `front_text`, `back_text` and `is_waxed` (§2.1) — wherever and whenever the sign block comes to stand. A sign block enters a delve's world by three routes the engine already has: a piece's template places it at load, a `prop` places it in `setup_finish` (a trigger's) or in `activate_<obj>` (an `interact` objective's), and a `set-block` (or a `fill-region`) places it at a beat. The words are not the block's state — vanilla keeps `facing`, `rotation`, `attached` and `waterlogged` in the blockstate and the text in the entity — and they are not the piece's: a piece is a library item any campaign may bind, and its words would be a design decision about what it is for (CLAUDE.md, *This is a general engine*). They are the campaign's, and the campaign addresses a block the world holds by **anchor** — the rule `prefab-procedure.md` §6 already states for every payload (*bound by the campaign against an anchor, never by the piece. So the piece must declare an anchor for every such thing*) and the shape spec-0021's `loot[]` already has: contents for a block the prefab placed, proven really there (`DW0431`), claimed once (`DW0435`). A sign's text is that shape with a text payload, and the one new thing a text payload brings — it is player-facing, so it is localised, measured and drawn — is what §2.3–§2.5 settle.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 The pinned sign, server side

**Cited — `SignBlockEntity`, `SignText`, `HangingSignBlockEntity`, bytecode.** The entity saves three keys: `front_text` and `back_text`, each a `SignText` through `SignText.DIRECT_CODEC`, and `is_waxed`, a boolean read with default `false`. A `SignText` is `messages` (a list the codec holds to **exactly four** components, `Util.fixedSize(list, 4)`; the default face is four `CommonComponents.EMPTY`), `filtered_messages` (lenient optional), `color` (a `DyeColor`, default `black`) and `has_glowing_text` (default `false`). The sixteen dye names, in `DyeColor`'s order: `white`, `orange`, `magenta`, `light_blue`, `yellow`, `lime`, `pink`, `gray`, `light_gray`, `cyan`, `purple`, `blue`, `brown`, `green`, `red`, `black` — not the chat palette (`dark_purple`, `light_purple` and `dark_gray` are not dyes). `SignBlockEntity.getMaxTextLineWidth()` returns **90** and `getTextLineHeight()` **10**; `HangingSignBlockEntity` overrides them to **60** and **9**. The pinned block registry holds **48** sign blocks: twelve woods (`oak`, `spruce`, `birch`, `jungle`, `acacia`, `dark_oak`, `mangrove`, `cherry`, `pale_oak`, `bamboo`, `crimson`, `warped`) × {`_sign`, `_wall_sign`, `_hanging_sign`, `_wall_hanging_sign`}; the two hanging kinds (`CeilingHangingSignBlock`, `WallHangingSignBlock`) make a `HangingSignBlockEntity`, the other two a `SignBlockEntity`. So **the face's width is the block's kind**, read from the block's name as `is_hand_pressed` reads a lever's: a name ending in `_sign` is a sign; one containing `hanging` is the narrow face.

### 2.2 Who may change the words, and what `is_waxed` does

**Cited — `SignBlock.useWithoutItem` / `useItemOn`, `SignBlockEntity.updateSignText`, `GameType.updatePlayerAbilities`, `SignApplicator`, bytecode.** A right-click with an empty hand runs the face's click commands first, then: if `isWaxed()` the server plays `getSignInteractionFailedSoundEvent()` (`WAXED_SIGN_INTERACT_FAIL`, or the hanging sign's own) at the sign and returns `SUCCESS_SERVER`; else, if a click command ran, `SUCCESS_SERVER`; else the edit screen opens only when nobody else is editing **and `player.mayBuild()`** and every line of the face is empty or `PlainTextContents` (`hasEditableText`); otherwise `PASS`. A right-click holding a `SignApplicator` (a dye, an ink sac, a glow ink sac, a honeycomb) applies only when `player.mayBuild()` and the sign is not waxed. The packet path `updateSignText` refuses, logging *just tried to change non-editable sign*, unless the sign is unwaxed **and** the player is the one `playerWhoMayEdit` names. `GameType.updatePlayerAbilities` sets `mayBuild = !isBlockPlacingRestricted()`, and `isBlockPlacingRestricted()` is true for `ADVENTURE` and `SPECTATOR`: **in adventure mode no player can open a sign, dye it or glow it, waxed or not.**

**Authored.** The engine writes `is_waxed: 1b` on every sign it letters, and there is no field for it. Under the product's one game mode (CLAUDE.md: adventure mode) a `waxed` field could change nothing a player meets, so it would be an inert unit the gallery could only bind vacuously; what the flag does carry is the vanilla statement that the text is final under **every** mode — a creator standing at the sign in creative during a playtest, and a `--lang` bake, whose lines are literal `PlainTextContents` that `hasEditableText` would otherwise admit. Its one audible consequence is stated rather than hidden: a player who right-clicks a lettered sign with an empty hand hears the waxed-sign tick. Where a click box stands over the sign — a `use` trigger's prop, the gallery's label — the click is the trigger's: the box is strictly nearer than the block (`DW1013`, **cited**) and the block's use is never reached.

### 2.3 How the client draws a face, and what happens to a line that is too long

**Cited — `AbstractSignRenderer`, `SignRenderer`, `HangingSignRenderer`, `AbstractSignEditScreen`, bytecode.** Each face's four lines are `SignText.getRenderMessages(filtered, c -> Font.split(c, state.maxTextLineWidth))` taking the **first** split line (or `EMPTY` when there is none), where `state.maxTextLineWidth` is the entity's `getMaxTextLineWidth()`. `Font.split` is `StringSplitter.splitLines`: it breaks at whitespace, or mid-word when no whitespace fits — so **a line wider than the face shows its first wrapped segment and drops the rest**; nothing shrinks, nothing wraps onto a second visible line, and no diagnostic says so. Each line is drawn centred (`-width / 2`, Java integer division) at `line_height` spacing from a half-height offset. A non-glowing face draws in `ARGB.scaleRGB(dye.getTextColor(), 0.4)`; a glowing one draws the dye's full `getTextColor()` with an eight-direction outline (`Font.drawInBatch8xOutline`) in `getDarkColor()` — `0xF0EBCC` for black, else the 0.4-scaled dye — drawn within `OUTLINE_RENDER_DISTANCE = 16²` of the camera. A line's own component style (a `color` on a span, spec-0096) is drawn as the font draws any styled component: it overrides the dye on that span. The edit screen's own acceptor is the same measurement — `font.width(line) <= sign.getMaxTextLineWidth()` — so **a line fits when its rendered width in font pixels is at most 90 (a standing or wall sign) or 60 (a hanging sign)**.

### 2.4 How wide a line is

**Cited — `Font.width` → `StringSplitter.stringWidth` → `GlyphInfo.getAdvance(boolean)`, `BitmapProvider`, `UnihexProvider`, `SpecialGlyphs`, the client's `assets/minecraft/font/default.json`, bytecode and assets.** A line's width is the sum over its characters of `getAdvance() + (bold ? getBoldOffset() : 0)`. A bitmap glyph's advance is `(int)(0.5 + inkWidth · scale) + 1` with bold offset `1.0`; a unihex glyph's is `width / 2 + 1` (integer division, `width = right − left + 1`) with bold offset `0.5`; the missing glyph's is `5 + 1 = 6` with bold offset `1.0`; a space is the `space` provider's `4`. **The engine already holds this instrument**: `compiler::textfit::default_font_width` (the 95 printable-ASCII advances measured from the pinned jar's `font/ascii.png` by `tools/maintenance/extract-font-metrics.py`, `FULL_WIDTH_ADVANCE = 9` from `default.json`'s `size_overrides`, `TYPICAL_ADVANCE = 6` = the missing glyph, eight measured punctuation rows) and `textfit::bold_widening` (one pixel per bold character — exact for a bitmap glyph, an overestimate for a unihex one, so it can only red), the two `DW0330` and `DW0331` already read. Lumenwright measures the same quantity by a second method sharing no table with it: `Font::add_bitmap` derives each advance from the jar's bitmap at run time (`(0.5 + width · scale).floor() + 1`), `Font::advance` adds `1` for bold and answers `6` for an unknown character, and `text::layout_line` cuts at `max_width` as the renderer does. The two agree by construction on ASCII and on the missing glyph; §5.4 makes the agreement a test.

In characters, stated to a translator as a derivation (§3.4): a 90-pixel face holds **15** typical Latin letters (6 px) or **10** Han glyphs (9 px); a 60-pixel face **10** or **6**. With the client's Force Unicode Font on, Latin drops to mostly 4 px and the Latin budget widens; the default font is the stricter side and the one the check measures (`textfit`'s own quantified caveat, **cited**).

### 2.5 How the engine localises a line, and how the two readers resolve it

**Cited — the tree.** Every inventoried string is tagged once (`dsl::l10n::tag_translatables`) and reaches the tree only through `emit::tr` / `tr_with` / `snbt_component` / `snbt_text_component` (`DW0185`), which lower it to `{"translate": "delve.<campaign_id>.<key>", "fallback": "<English>"}` with spans as `with` (spec-0096); the SNBT form (`emit::snbt_component`, the one an NPC's `CustomName` takes) is the same compound through `emit::text::snbt_of`. `--lang` swaps the strings before emission and ships no lang file. Every string field of every stage schema is classified or CI is red (`crates/dsl/tests/l10n_surface.rs`); every key has a `TextKind` (`key_kind`) and a situation (`key_situations`), which `delvec l10n-inventory` hands `tools/creator/i18n-translate.py`; a sidecar row's spans are held to the English's (`DW0976`); the width gates read the English and every sidecar. **The server** stores a sign line as a component and the client draws the component, so a `translate` line on a sign is resolved by the client's language table from the pack the delve serves, falling back to the English on the component — the same path every other player-facing line takes. **Lumenwright** reads `front_text.messages` / `back_text.messages`, flattens a `translate` component through `Lang::format(key, fallback, with)` over the `lang/<code>.json` tables of the jar and every resource pack it is handed (spec-0112 §2.2 item 5 hands it the build's `resourcepack.zip`), and lays out the line with the glyph advances of §2.4; a bare string is a literal line. So a frame of a lettered sign shows the words a client in that language shows.

### 2.6 How the tree already binds a payload to a block a piece placed

**Cited — `crates/dsl/src/loot.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/loot.rs`, `prefab-procedure.md` §6, §9.** `loot[] {id, anchor, items[]}` is contents for a container the prefab already placed: the compiler never places the container, `container_anchors` enumerates the anchors whose cell holds one, `check_loot_containers` refuses an anchor whose cell does not (`DW0431`) with a remedy that lists the anchors that do and says in words that the fix is the piece, never a `set-block` and never a hand-patched `.nbt`; two fills of one anchor are `DW0435`. An anchor is `{pos?, facing?, role?, region?, …}`, one object class for a point, a gate region and a trap's hardware; a generator writes it beside the blocks it lays (`mark`, `grammar.md` §2b), and `rotate_state` turns a sign's `facing` and sixteen-step `rotation` with its piece.

## 3. The surface

### 3.1 Where the words are declared, and what each other place fails to reach

**Authored, on §1 and §2.6.** The words are a stage-5 declaration keyed by anchor, beside `loot[]`. The other three places were weighed and each fails to reach something the levels of §2.7 need:

| Declared on | Fails to reach |
|---|---|
| the piece — a `front_text` compound in the `.nbt`, or a grammar program's paint | localisation: `/place template` lays the bytes as one unit, so no `--lang` swap, no inventory row, no sidecar, no width gate reaches a line in it (`l10n_surface.rs` enumerates DSL string fields, not NBT); a sign a `prop` or `set-block` places later; a piece's reuse — one campaign's words would ship in every campaign that binds the piece, and a grammar export writes no NBT at all |
| the place — a site-plan node or a detail-plan row | the placement is designed before the story that reads the sign exists (stage order, ADR-0002), and a place has no vocabulary for one cell but its anchors — so it would reach the anchor anyway, one document earlier than the story that owes the words |
| the verb — a `text` on `set-block` and on `prop` | a sign the piece placed at load, which is the first level's case: every lettered sign would have to be placed twice (template, then `setblock`) so that a verb could carry its words, and the capability would hang on two verbs rather than on the object (CLAUDE.md: *a capability belongs to the object class it acts on, not to the verb that first needed it*) |
| **the anchor — `signs[]`, a declaration the engine applies wherever and whenever the cell comes to hold a sign** | a sign at a cell no anchor names (a program's `scatter`). By design: it is scenery, and content is addressed by anchor (`prefab-procedure.md` §6) |

### 3.2 `signs[]`

**Authored.** Stage 5 (`quests.json`) gains one list:

```json
"signs": [
  {
    "id": "sign/the-label",
    "anchor": "anchor/label",
    "front": { "lines": ["EVERY SURFACE", "", "written once"] },
    "back":  { "lines": ["", "[[bold|turn back]]"], "color": "red", "glowing": true }
  }
]
```

1. **`id`** — `sign/<kebab>` (`DW0110`), unique (`DW0111`). The handle a beat letters by (§3.3).
2. **`anchor`** — the anchor whose cell holds, or comes to hold, the sign block: a point anchor, or a `furniture` region resolving to its `from` cell as everywhere a point is asked of it (`prefab-procedure.md` §9, **cited**). The cell is proven to hold a sign block in at least one configuration the party can be in (§6.2, `DW0431`); one row per anchor (`DW0435`).
3. **`front`** (required) and **`back`** (optional) — each a **`Face`**: `lines` (one to four strings, top to bottom; `""` leaves a line blank; a face shorter than four lines is blank below; a fifth line is `DW0100` by schema), `color` (one of the sixteen dye names of §2.1, default `black`; another word is `DW0100`), `glowing` (default `false`). A line is a player-facing string like any other: it enters the inventory, may carry styled spans, and is held to the English's spans in every sidecar. A face with no visible glyph on any line is `DW1048` (§6.1): a blank face is what the piece already gives, so declaring one is a mistake, not a design.
4. **The engine writes `is_waxed: 1b`** on every lettered sign (§2.2). There is no field.

### 3.3 `letter-sign` — a beat rewrites the words

**Authored.** An effect, beside `set-block` and `set-time`, usable from every effect root:

```json
{ "type": "letter-sign", "sign": "sign/the-label", "front": { "lines": ["", "SOLD"], "color": "red" } }
```

`sign` names a declared row (`DW0112` otherwise); `front` and `back` are `Face`s, at least one present (`DW0100` by schema); a face not given keeps its words. It fires where its root fires, like every effect, and writes the whole face: a `letter-sign` is a statement of what the sign says from that beat, never a diff. The Stargazer's Roof's four sentences are one `signs[]` row and three `letter-sign`s on the three rings. A `letter-sign` at a beat in whose configuration the cell holds no sign is `DW0431` (§6.2).

### 3.4 The words are translated, with the face in the translator's hand

**Authored, on §2.5.** Each non-blank line is one inventory row — `sign.<s>.front.<i>` / `sign.<s>.back.<i>` for a declaration, `fx.<root>.<i>.front.<j>` / `.back.<j>` for a `letter-sign` (position-derived like every effect leaf; `effect_roots` and `effect_roots_mut` enumerate it with the other leaves, so the glyph, fit and inventory scans cannot drift) — with `<i>` the line's index on its face. `TextKind` gains **`SignLine`** (*a line on a sign: a few words read at a glance from a board, never a sentence*). Its `key_situations` carry, as procedural derivations: the face's other lines in order (*Line 1 of 3 on the front face; the others read: …*), **the budget in characters** — `about 90 font pixels: 15 Latin or 10 Han characters` or the 60-pixel figures, derived from the kind of sign block that stands or will stand at the anchor — and the place the anchor lies in. `tools/creator/i18n-translate.py` states the budget in its prompt from the situation it is handed, as it already states the option-label budget; a line that still overflows is `DW1048` from the closing `delvec validate`, named with its language, and fixed by hand like any refused row. A blank line is not a row.

### 3.5 Words typed into a block are refused

**Authored.** Vanilla's `block_state` argument accepts `id[props]{nbt}`, so a creator could write `"block": "minecraft:oak_sign[rotation=8]{front_text:…}"` on a `set-block`, a `prop` or a `fill-region`. That is raw NBT authored by an LLM, outside every l10n and fit gate — the type specimen of the hack this spec exists to make unnecessary. `split_blockstate` (the one block-string parser, **cited**) refuses a `{` anywhere in a block string as `DW0193`, with a remedy naming `signs[]`; the rule holds at every campaign block field through the one function.

## 4. Emission

### 4.1 One compound, two readers

**Authored, on §2.1 and §2.5.** The compiler builds one value per lettered sign per configuration, `SignCompound { front: Face, back: Face (the default face when undeclared), waxed: true }`, rendered once:

```
{front_text:{messages:[C₀,C₁,C₂,C₃],color:"<dye>",has_glowing_text:<0b|1b>},back_text:{…},is_waxed:1b}
```

`Cᵢ` is `emit::snbt_component` of the tagged line — `{translate:"delve.<id>.sign.<s>.front.<i>",fallback:"…"}` with its spans as `with`, a bare `""` for a blank line, the literal under `--lang` — through the one SNBT formatter, so the quoting, the booleans and the key order are the engine's one rule and the `text_opacity:255b` class of defect (`compiler.md`, *Pass order* — **cited**) cannot recur. **The same value** is what the emitted command carries and what the written world lays as the cell's block entity (§4.3): one producer, so the line and the world cannot disagree (the shape spec-0112 §4.1 gives every placed object; this spec adds the sign's compound as a campaign row where §4.1 named only the template's).

### 4.2 The lines

**Authored.** The command is always `data merge block <x> <y> <z> <compound>` (in the pinned tree, **cited**); it is never a `setblock … {nbt}`, so the write that places the block and the write that letters it are one shape each. It is emitted:

- in `setup_finish`, once, for a sign whose cell the **load world** holds as a sign block (a template's);
- **immediately after every emitted write that puts a sign block into a lettered cell**: the `setblock` of a trigger's `prop` in `setup_finish`, of an `interact`'s `prop` in `activate_<obj>`, of a `set-block` in its bundle, and a `fill-region` whose block is a sign and whose box covers the cell — one function, `sign::after_write(cell, block)`, asked by the one block-write emitter after each line it writes, so a fourth placer inherits the lettering;
- for a `letter-sign`, in its root's bundle at its position, with the faces the effect states over the faces the sign had.

A campaign that declares no sign emits none of these and is byte-identical. Every line is walked against the pinned command tree as every emitted line is. **Determinism** (ADR-0006 — **cited**): the compound is rendered from `BTreeMap`-ordered faces by the one formatter; the keys are position-derived; two builds are byte-identical, asserted by the existing double-build gate once the gallery declares a sign.

### 4.3 The written world, the save, and the frame

**Authored, on spec-0112 §5.4, §8.2 and §2.2 (cited).** The written world of a configuration carries, in the chunk's `block_entities`, the `SignCompound` of every lettered sign whose cell holds a sign block in that configuration, with `id`, `x`, `y`, `z` written as the game writes them, after the latest `letter-sign` fired before the arrival (the latest-write-per-cell rule the region model already keeps); a `set-block` sign is lettered in every configuration from its write on. `block_entities_omitted` stops counting a cell whose compound the world holds, so a lettered sign leaves the *drawn blank* count and the binding line says so. The written compound is held to the server's save key by key — `front_text`, `back_text`, `is_waxed` — by `tools/ci/check-written-world.py`'s object comparison (spec-0112 §8.2, `DW1040`): a sign is in no bookkeeping class, so any difference reds. The review frame draws the compound through Lumenwright (§2.5): the sign is a body in the frame's `shows`, its lines are counted in `BlockEntityStats.text_lines`, and a sign the renderer could not draw would be `undrawn` (spec-0112 §8.1) — never a bare wall read as a missing sign.

## 5. Proving it reaches the party

### 5.1 The binding line and the ledger

**Authored.** Every build prints `sign binding: N sign(s), F face(s), L line(s) (B blank); T lettered at load, P after a write (S set-block, R prop, I fill), W re-lettered by letter-sign; widest line W₁ of 90 px (standing) and W₂ of 60 px (hanging) over K language(s)` — every figure computed from the declarations with `N` as the denominator; a campaign with none prints `0 sign(s)`, a measured zero. `validation/signs.json` carries one row per sign: id, anchor, cell, the block and its kind, the budget, the write that places it (or `load`), the configurations it stands lettered in, and per face per language each line with its measured width.

### 5.2 PackTest

**Authored.** Per sign one synchronous template `sign_<s>`: it reaches the sign's placing function the way the generated verb templates do (`setup_finish` for a template or trigger-prop sign; the objective's activation drive for an `interact` prop; the beat's bundle for a `set-block`), then asserts `execute if data block <x> <y> <z> {is_waxed:1b,front_text:{color:"<dye>",has_glowing_text:<b>,messages:[{translate:"delve.<id>.sign.<s>.front.<i>"}]}}` for the first non-blank line of each declared face — the component's key, which is what the server holds; what the client shows for it is the lang file's and is judged at §5.4. Under a `--lang` build the assertion is the literal. Per `letter-sign` one template drives the beat and asserts the new first line and the unchanged other face. The templates are registered as `watch::Claim`s so a suite that drives fewer signs than are declared is `DW0811`.

### 5.3 What the bot is not asked

**Authored.** The bot does not read a sign: the server's compound is PackTest's and the save's (`DW1040`), and the letters are the frame's. Nothing new is spoken over the live channel.

### 5.4 The width instrument, cross-checked

**Authored, on §2.4.** A test in the gallery job, where the pinned client jar is cached, asks Lumenwright's `Font` (built from that jar) for the advance of each of the 95 printable ASCII characters and of `textfit`'s eight punctuation rows, bold and plain, and holds `textfit::default_font_width` and `bold_widening` to the same figures; it states the count compared on its own line. Two instruments, one from a committed table measured by a maintenance script and one from the jar's bitmaps at run time, sharing no configuration (CLAUDE.md, *Measurement doctrine* — **cited**). A frame of the gallery's label is then the third reading: `text_lines` equals the label's non-blank lines over both faces.

## 6. Refusals

**Authored**, each raised at the document it is entered in, with the remedy named and a `remedy_reachability.rs` row for each remedy that is a move.

### 6.1 `DW1048` — a sign's text does not fit its face (`compiler::textfit`, beside `DW0330` / `DW0331`; exit 3)

- a line whose rendered width — `textfit::default_font_width` of its visible text plus `bold_widening`, the `DW0331` measurement — exceeds the face's budget, **90** for a standing or wall sign and **60** for a hanging sign, in the English or in any declared sidecar row; the message names the sign, the face, the line, its width, the budget, the language and the kind of block the budget comes from;
- a face with no visible glyph on any of its lines.

Error tier, for `DW0331`'s reason and not `DW0330`'s: the limit is the block's, fixed by the kind of sign that stands there on every client at every window size, so an overrun **is** a cut line (§2.3), a fact and not a guess. The remedies: shorten the line, split it over a blank line, or stand a wider sign — a standing sign where a hanging one hangs. Raised at the build pass that first knows the block at the cell (the assembled model, pass 7), because a template sign's kind is in the world and not in the document; a `prop` or `set-block` sign's kind is in the document, and its line is judged there too, by the same function.

### 6.2 Existing rules, each under a wider quantifier

- **`DW0431`** — content declared for a cell that does not hold the block it needs: a `signs[]` row whose anchor's cell holds no sign block in any configuration a body can arrive in, or a `letter-sign` whose beat fires in a configuration where the cell holds none. The proof is extracted from `check_loot_containers` into one function both loot and sign call, with `DW0431` keeping its declaration site; the remedy lists the anchors whose cell holds a sign, as `container_remedy` lists containers, and says the fix is a piece's anchor or the write that places the sign, never a `set-block` typed to carry words.
- **`DW0435`** — two declarations on one anchor: two `signs[]` rows.
- **`DW0112`** — a `letter-sign` naming a sign no row declares.
- **`DW0193`** — a `{` in any block string (§3.5), remedy `signs[]`.
- **`DW0100`** — a fifth line, a colour outside the sixteen dyes, a `Face` with no `lines`, a `letter-sign` with neither face; **`DW0110` / `DW0111`** — the id.

## 8. Decisions for the owner

Only what she would meet.

1. **A sign's words are the campaign's, declared against the anchor where the sign stands.** What she sees: the same library piece reads one thing in one delve and another in the next, in the player's language. The alternative — words baked into the piece — could not be translated and would travel with the piece into every delve that uses it.
2. **A sign in a delve can never be written on, and clicking it ticks.** What she hears: the waxed-sign tick on an empty-handed right-click. The alternative — an unwaxed sign — behaves identically in adventure mode and differently for a creator in creative or under a single-language bake.
3. **A line that would not fit is refused in every language before anything is built.** What she sees: never a cut line; a Chinese row that runs long is a named refusal at the translation step, not a sign reading half a sentence.
4. **Changing what a sign says is a beat**, written as the whole face. What she sees: the Stargazer's sign reads a new sentence at each ring, whichever order anything else fires in.
