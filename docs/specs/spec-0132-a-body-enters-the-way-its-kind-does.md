# spec-0132: A body enters the way its kind does — an actor's native entrance, the arrival without a puppet, and the bot that arrives alive beside a body it never fights

- **Status**: Approved
- **Ground**: `main` at the revision this branch forks from, read whole for: `crates/dsl/src/actor.rs` (the `Actor` struct and `actor_checks`), `crates/dsl/src/fight.rs` (`unleashed_actors`), `crates/dsl/src/ambush.rs` (the desugaring), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit/actor.rs` (`actor_puppet_summon`, `actor_twin_summon`, `spawn_finalize_nbt`, `aggro_lock_lines`, `actor_fns`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit/removal.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/combat.rs` (`hostile_actors`, `non_combatants`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/presence.rs`, `crates/delvec/src/compiler/framing/shot.rs` (`Presence`, `DW1034`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/plan/mod.rs` (`critical_path_sneak`, `body_point`), `crates/delvec/src/compiler/emit/manifest.rs` (the critical-path document), `harness/src/critical-path.ts` (`CRITICAL_PATH_FORMAT_VERSION`), `harness/src/executor/staging.ts` (`stageAway`), `harness/src/executor/sustain.ts` (`onBotDamaged`, `holdFullHealth`), `harness/src/executor/walk.ts` (`gotoStaged`), `harness/src/sculk.ts` (`SculkEar.onSpawn`, `runFailure`), `harness/src/report.ts` (`STAGES`, `redStages`), `docs/reference/compiler.md` (the `actors[]`, `ambushes[]`, `cutscene`, `unleash-actor` and `critical-path.json` rows), `docs/reference/playtest-methodology.md` *What the ladder asserts at a combat encounter*, `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py`, `gallery/`.
- **What it is for**: a level in which a body enters the world the way its kind does in vanilla — a warden digging out of the ground, a wither charging up — and then hunts the party, and in which the machine proof is a bot that knows the route and walks out alive while that body is loose. The engine has neither: the twin `unleash-actor` summons stands up at once with no entrance, an `unleash-actor` with no puppet standing does nothing and nothing says so, and the ladder removes every body that lands a hit (`playtest-methodology.md`: *Outside a scripted death, a body never kills the bot*), so no leg can prove a route survives a hunter. `maze-craft.md` §4–§6 is the level research that found the gaps.
- **Research**: `docs/reference/native-entrance.md` (the wither's charge-up; a body summoned and removed in one tick), `docs/reference/warden-behaviour.md` §6 (the emerge and its window). Every rule below is marked **[cited]** with the record or constitution line it rests on, or **[authored]** where this spec decides.
- **Numbers**: spec `0132`; `dsl_version` `0.71.0`; `DW1081`, `DW1082`, `DW1083`, all three used (§4).
- **Non-goals**: a kind-specific action at any other moment or trigger — a roar, a dig, a sonic boom on a beat — is the general form of §8 and a later spec; a second entrance for one kind; a player-chosen entrance; an entrance for a stage-2 NPC (an NPC is never given AI); the maze level itself; a two-player proof; any judgement of whether a player could survive the body (CLAUDE.local.md: *Whether a body can dodge a hazard is the designer's call*).

## 1. The object: a body at its arrival **[authored, from the `unleash-actor` row of `compiler.md`]**

The twin `unleash_<id>` summons is the one free-AI body the compiler summons (`emit/actor.rs`, `actor_twin_summon`); every other summon is `NoAI`. An entrance is how a body arrives in the world with its will, so it is a property of that arrival and of nothing else: it is declared on the actor, because it belongs to the body (CLAUDE.md: *a capability belongs to the object class it acts on*), and it plays on the twin, because only the free body can play it — a `NoAI` body summoned with an entrance keeps the data and never consumes it (`native-entrance.md` §1, the `NoAI` arm; `warden-behaviour.md` §6, the puppet).

## 2. The surface and the registry

### 2.1 `actors[].entrance` **[authored]**

`entrance: "native"`, optional. Absent, the actor is byte-identical to today (ADR-0006). `"native"` says: this body enters the way its kind does. The one value is a declaration of intent, not a knob: the engine looks the kind up and plays what it finds, and an author who wants a different arrival for a kind that has one writes a different kind.

### 2.2 The registry `dsl::entrance` **[cited: `native-entrance.md` §1, §3; `warden-behaviour.md` §6]**

One module, `crates/dsl/src/entrance.rs`, holds a table of **kind actions**: records keyed by `(kind, action)`, where `kind` is a vanilla entity id and `action` names what the body does. This spec ships the action `enter` for two kinds and no other row. A record states, for the emitter and the author alike: what the action is in a player's eyes; the vanilla mechanism as the NBT the twin's summon carries; its length in ticks; what the body can and cannot do while it runs; what it ends with; and the record's read site in `docs/reference/`.

| kind | what it is | mechanism | ticks | while it runs | ends with | read site |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `minecraft:warden` | digs out of the ground (the EMERGING pose) | `Brain.memories."minecraft:is_emerging":{value:{},ttl:133L}`, in the same `Brain.memories` compound as the `dig_cooldown` the twin already carries | 133 | does not move, hears nothing, cannot be hurt; a seeded anger is kept | stands and sniffs; a kept anger opens the hunt | `warden-behaviour.md` §6 |
| `minecraft:wither` | the spawn charge-up, the boss bar filling | `Invul:220`, and `Health` at a third of the body's `max_health` (vanilla's own start) | 220 | does not move, fires no skull, cannot be hurt; heals 10 every 10 ticks to full | a blast of power 7: breaks no block under the delve's `mob_griefing false`; about 51 damage to an unarmoured body at 3 blocks and 40 at 6 | `native-entrance.md` §1 |

The table is data, not a rule about kinds: a kind absent from it has no native entrance as far as the engine knows, and §4's refusal says so by name. The `while it runs` column is the **entrance window** (§5.6) and what a cutscene author reads to size a shot (§6). The wither's `Health` is `max_health / 3` as a float with one decimal (`100.0f` for the default 300; the declared `attributes.max_health` when one is stated), rendered by the one attribute renderer the twin already uses.

### 2.3 Emission **[authored]**

`unleash_<id>`'s twin summon carries the kind's `enter` NBT. For the warden it is merged into the `Brain:{memories:{…}}` compound `spawn_finalize_nbt` already writes — one `Brain` key, two memories — because SNBT keeps the last of two keys with one name, and a second `Brain` key would silently drop the `dig_cooldown` and bury the body (`compiler.md`, the `unleash-actor` row). For the wither the two top-level fields are appended. `actor_restand_<id>`, the bonfire's undefeated re-seat, does **not** carry the entrance: a re-seat restores a body that already arrived; it is not an arrival the story wrote. The health bar (`health_bar`) is unchanged: it reads `Health`, so a charging wither's bar fills from a third as vanilla's own does, and an emerging warden's stands full.

## 3. The arrival without a puppet **[cited: `native-entrance.md` §2; authored]**

Today `unleash_<id>` summons the twin `execute at @e[tag=dw_pup_<id>,limit=1]`, so an `unleash-actor` fired while no puppet stands does nothing, and nothing refuses it. A body that enters from nothing cannot be a puppet first: the client is told of a puppet that stands for no tick, at the cell it stood in (`native-entrance.md` §2), and the engine does not rely on a frame not drawing it.

So **`unleash-actor` becomes the body's arrival, with or without a puppet**, for every actor: when a puppet stands, the twin stands where it knelt and the puppet leaves unseen, as today; when none does, the twin is summoned at the actor's own mark (`Plan::body_point`, the cell `actor_restand_<id>` already summons at), wearing the actor's `facing` as the puppet would (`Rotation`), guarded `unless entity @e[tag=dw_actor_<id>]` so an unleash of a body already standing is a no-op, as `spawn-actor` is. The answer-box kill, the removal lines, the striker aggro lock and the health-bar capture keep their places; the lock is written whether or not a puppet stood. The story-state model (`compiler::presence`, both walks that read `SpawnActor`) and the cutscene presence check (`framing::shot`, `Presence::NeverPlaced`, `DW1034`) count an `unleash-actor` as placing the body when it is not already placed, and `DW1034`'s remedy names `spawn-actor` or `unleash-actor`. An `unleash-actor` the story-state replay cannot date (a trigger, a trap, a reaction bundle) is counted as bringing the body in at any state only for an actor no `spawn-actor` stages anywhere: where one does, the unleash may as well stand up the puppet that spawn placed, and the spawn is what is counted — so a staged elite unleashed by its own strike trigger reads as it always has.

An actor with an `entrance` has **no puppet life** (§4, `DW1083`), so its arrival is always at its mark, and it is never an inert body: `BodyRef::inert` is false for it, so `DW1055` (spec-0119) never owes it the `answers` `DW1083` refuses on it — the two rules guard one body and must not prescribe what the other refuses; the `ambushes[]` sugar (`dsl::ambush`) desugars such an actor to its `unleash-actor` alone, with no `spawn-actor` before it, and the row in `compiler.md` says so.

### 3.1 An entering body starts searching **[cited: `warden-behaviour.md` §6, T10; authored]**

An entering body arrives the way its kind does in vanilla, and a warden that digs out of the ground is angry at nobody: it roams until its own hearing or sniff finds a player, and a lure turns it while it roams (`warden-behaviour.md` §6, T10: `anger.suspects` `[]` at every reading over 25 s, the body walking 8.7 blocks off its cell, a sourceless snowball 11.6 blocks away drawing it to within 0.5). That searching state is what a pursuit level is built on — the walker's lures work only on a body that is not yet hunting — so the engine seeds no target on an entering body, whatever fires its `unleash-actor`.

The lock a click places on a provoked body (the warden's `anger.suspects` seeded at 150 from the striker the click's bundle holds, the `unleash-actor` row of `compiler.md`) is therefore not in `unleash_<id>`. It is in `unleash_struck_<id>` — the plain arrival, then the lock — emitted for an actor a click trigger unleashes at its bundle's top level, whose kind has a target primitive and which does **not** enter, and called from that click's bundle alone, while the striker is held. Every other `unleash-actor` — a story beat, an approach trigger, a timeline step, an `on_arrive` — calls `unleash_<id>`, which holds no lock line at all; so a striker left in storage by an earlier click, or by a bundle that stopped before its release line, can never seed a body nobody struck. A click that unleashes an entering actor calls its plain arrival: the click is the beat that wakes the body, not a provocation the body remembers, and a hunt opened by a seeded anger would skip the searching state the entrance exists to play.

## 4. The refusals **[authored]**

All three are declared in `dsl::entrance` (their catalog rows on `docs/reference/dsl/entrance.md`), raised by `dsl::validate` through `actor_checks`, validation-tier (exit 1), subject the campaign — except `DW1082`'s second arm, raised at build (exit 3) where the path's pursuers are derived.

| Code | Refuses | Names | Remedy it prescribes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DW1081` | `entrance: "native"` on an actor whose kind — the twin's `entity` as written, since a `skin` dresses only the puppet — has no `enter` row in the registry | the actor, the kind, the action (`enter`), and every kind that has one | drop the field, or give the body a kind that enters |
| `DW1082` | a declaration about the **free** body on an actor the free body never comes to: an `entrance` or an `outrun` (§5.1) on an actor no `unleash-actor` names anywhere (`dsl::unleashed_actors`); and an `outrun` whose actor is never loose while any step of the exported critical path is walked (§5.3 derives the set; empty everywhere is this arm). The second arm needs the exported path, which only the compiler derives, so it is raised at build by the plan (exit 3), not by `dsl::validate` | the actor, the field, and which arm | unleash the actor on the path, or drop the field |
| `DW1083` | a puppet surface on an actor that enters: a `spawn-actor` or `move-actor` naming it anywhere, an `ambushes[]` entry that would spawn it (the sugar omits the spawn instead, so this arm fires only on a hand-written `spawn-actor`), or any of `vulnerable`, `watch`, `answers` on it — each a property of a body that stands caged, which an entering body never does | the actor, the surface, and where it is entered | remove the surface; the body's first appearance is its `unleash-actor` |

`DW1082`'s message shape is the one `DW0909` and `DW0913` already use for a bar and a kill bundle on a body nothing unleashes; those two codes are not changed by this spec, and the next field of this shape is a third arm of `DW1082`, never a fourth code. `DW1081`'s message names the action, so the general round (§8) raises it unchanged for a kind that lacks a later action.

## 5. The bot arrives alive **[cited: `playtest-methodology.md` *What the ladder asserts at a combat encounter*; `harness/src/executor/staging.ts`; authored]**

### 5.1 The declaration: `actors[].outrun`

The ladder does not fight, and it removes every body that lands a hit so the route can be read (`staging.ts`, `stageAway`; `walk.ts`, `gotoStaged`; `sustain.ts`, `holdFullHealth`). That is right for a fight the player wins and wrong for a body the player escapes, and the engine cannot tell the two apart from an `unleash-actor` alone: a souls elite and a maze hunter are both unleashed hostiles. So the campaign says which it is. **`outrun: true`**, optional on an actor, default `false`, declares that the party survives this body by moving and never by fighting, and that the proof is a walk beside it with nothing removed and nothing healed. It is a declaration of intent the ladder confirms (CLAUDE.md: *A check confirms intent; it never restricts*), never an inference from the kind. Absent, every actor is judged as today.

### 5.2 What the leg proves

For every exported critical-path step walked while at least one `outrun` body is loose (§5.3), the bot performs the step exactly as it performs every other — the same movement, sprint allowed unless the leg is `sneak`, crouched on a `sneak` leg but for the moves it makes standing (below), the same completion oracle (the objective's anchored marker, `markers.ts`), eating from its kit as a player would — and the step passes only when all of the following hold:

1. the step's own completion arrives;
2. no body was staged away and no refund or restore was given during the step: `stageAway`, the refund of a removed body's blows and `holdFullHealth` are **off** for the whole step, and the run artifact asserts zero of each over it;
3. every pursuer read alive at every reading (§5.4), the last reading after the completion;
4. at least one reading was taken.

**A death on a pursued step is a measurement** (amended after the warden maze's branch runs; this is a **loosening** of what `died` asserted, which was that the bot completed the step with no death since it began). Whether a body survives a pursuer with its vanilla will is the designer's call (the non-goal above; CLAUDE.local.md: *Whether a body can dodge a hazard is the designer's call*), and a solo player passes such a level by dying and trying again. So a death on a pursued step is recorded — per attempt, the step and the attacker `attributeBotDamage` names, and the run's count — and is no red. The bot then respawns the way a player does (the death screen's button, `onDeath`) and walks the step again from where the game put it, through the same recovery every retry after a death takes (`sequencer.ts` `respawnInPlace`: wait out the respawn, re-arm the kit where the bot stands, never move it): the leg cursor goes back to where the step began and the step's first leg resumes from its proven waypoint nearest the bot. **A death loop is red by name.** The bound guards termination, never survival: a step may take at most as many deaths as the proven legs it walks have waypoints, and the death past that fails the step, naming the death, the bound and the legs. Whether one life got further than another is not asked — that is the pursuer's vanilla will, the designer's call. The bound is the route's own length, never a number picked from a level.

**Moves made standing.** On a step the exported path marks `sneak`, the moves a crouched body cannot make — a climb down, a step from a route cell to a lower one outside any climb, a declared jump — are the leg's `stand_ups` (`compiler::waypoints::stand_ups`, exported on the leg and read by the bot, the one answer the sculk model also hears), and the bot makes each of them standing and every other hop crouched.

The runtime does not demand that a pursuer's feet leave its arrival cell. Whether a roaming warden walks off its cell inside one walked step is vanilla's wandering, hearing and sniffing, established once against the pinned jar (`warden-behaviour.md` §6) and never a recurring check (CLAUDE.md: *A check guards only what our toolchain owns*); a displacement requirement would red a body that did nothing wrong. What the toolchain owns is that the body it unleashes is a free body — no `NoAI`, not `Invulnerable`, no `movement_speed` of zero written by the emitter — and that is the compiler's output, so it is proven by the compiler's tests (§9, criterion 10) on the emitted lines of every `unleash_<id>` and `actor_restand_<id>`, which go red when the emitter writes any of the three. The runtime demands that each pursuer is read standing at every reading, so a body the build never produced or one that was removed still reds `pursuer_lost` or `unread`. The displacement is a reported measurement (§5.6).

### 5.3 What the engine hands the harness

`critical-path.json` gains, on every walked step, `pursuers: [{actor, kind, census, entrance_ticks}]` — the `outrun` actors loose while the step is walked, derived by the compiler and never by the bot: a step is pursued when an `unleash-actor` of the actor has fired at an **earlier** step of the exported path — the body arrived before the walk began — and no `despawn-actor` of it has fired since; the step whose own completion (or performance, for a `trigger` step) fires the unleash is not pursued, because the body arrives only at its completion and every reading before it would find no body; and the step whose completion certainly fires a `despawn-actor` of it is not pursued, because the reading after its completion would find no body. A trigger performed in front of an objective is its own step, so the objective walked after it is pursued. Lines are dated by their root at the path's own step maps (`PathFiring`: an objective's bundle at its step, a quest's `on_complete` at the step of its last objective on the path, a trigger's or a loop's bundle at the step that performs it), nested lists at their root, a reaction bundle (`on_respawn`, `on_rest`, `on_caught`) never. An unleash the path cannot date makes the actor a pursuer of every step from the path's first, stated as `undated: true` on the row; a despawn it cannot date removes nothing. A step that stands nowhere (`select-class`, `mock`, `assert-complete`) is never pursued, and a `rest` or `witness-strike` the layout lays between two steps is pursued by the bodies loose on both sides of it. `entrance_ticks` is the registry's length for the kind (0 for a body without an `entrance`). `census` names the generated function `actor_census_<id>`, emitted for every `outrun` actor, which writes one anchored line `[dw:actor <campaign> <actor> <seq> <alive> <x> <y> <z>]` for the body wearing `dw_actor_<id>` and not `dw_pup_<id>` (`alive` 0 and no coordinates when none stands) — the harness calls what the plan names, as it does a wave's census. The contract's `format_version` moves to 6 and the key is required in both directions: a step without `pursuers` is refused by the harness, because a path that cannot say whether a step is pursued cannot be walked under this rule, and a harness that guessed would be the compiler's knowledge written where it cannot be right (the reasoning of versions 3, 4 and 5 in `critical-path.ts`).

### 5.4 The readings

During a pursued step the harness calls each pursuer's `census` once a second on the server's clock (`game-time.ts`) and records `alive`, the body's feet, and its distance to the bot's feet; the first reading is at the step's start; an `undated` pursuer read absent before it has ever been read standing has not yet arrived, and is not lost. Readings continue until the completion marker lands, then one more.

### 5.5 What it refuses to count as a pass

Each is a red row of a new ladder stage **`pursuit`** in `report.ts` `STAGES`, judged by `redStages` like every other, with the step index, the pursuer and the reading that decided it: `staged` or `restored` (zero asserted, so this row can only mean the ladder's own rule broke — it is kept because a check that cannot red is not a check); `pursuer_lost` (absent or dead at a reading); `unread` (no reading). There is no inert row: the body being free to act is proven by the compiler's tests (§5.2), and its displacement is a measurement (§5.6). A death on a pursued step is not one of them (§5.2, amended): its attempt's row records the attacker, and the step is walked again; a death loop fails the step, and so the critical path, by name.

### 5.6 The entrance window and the measurement

`entrance_ticks` is the registry's length for the kind and sizes a cutscene shot; the runtime judges nothing by it. Over every pursued step the artifact states, as measurement and never as verdict: the closest approach (the least bot–pursuer distance read), the pursuer's total displacement, the bot's lowest health, the number of readings, and whether the bot died and to whom (§5.2). The run's closing line is `[pursuit] <n> pursued step(s) of <N>, <m> pursuer(s), <k> reading(s); closest approach <d>; <x> death(s)`, `<N>` the exported steps walked, `<n>` one per attempt, `<x>` the deaths on pursued steps; a campaign declaring an `outrun` actor with zero pursued steps is refused at build by `DW1082`'s second arm, so the line's zero is never a pass on such a campaign.

### 5.7 The pair: the sculk ear **[cited: spec-0100 §4.7; `harness/src/sculk.ts`]**

`SculkEar.onSpawn` counts every warden spawn and `runFailure` fails the run on one, because a shrieker with `can_summon=false` summons nothing. A warden the campaign itself summons — an actor's puppet or twin — is counted today and fails the run, so a campaign with a warden actor cannot run the ladder green at all, with or without this spec: the two gates guard one artifact and one refuses what the other requires (CLAUDE.md: *Pairs*). The rule becomes: at the run's end the harness asks the server how many wardens stand that wear no `dw_actor` tag (`execute if entity @e[type=minecraft:warden,tag=!dw_actor]`, through the anchored channel), and that count is the failure; wardens the campaign declares are not a shrieker's. The darkness half of the same ear is the same pair: a warden pulses darkness onto every player within 20 blocks of it every 120 ticks, so a declared warden near the walk would fail every run on darkness. The client cannot tell a warden's darkness from a shrieker's by the effect alone, so the excuse demands what a shrieker cannot supply: **every shriek** (`world_event` 3007) heard at a cell no leg of the proven route predicts fails the run on its own, whatever stands near it; and a darkness is set apart as a declared warden's only when the client saw a warden within 20 blocks of the bot and **no** shriek, predicted or not, was heard within a second of it. Every other darkness fails the run as before. The `[sculk]` line states darkness counted and set apart, unpredicted shrieks with their cells, spawns heard and undeclared standing.

## 6. The cutscene **[cited: the `cutscene` row of `compiler.md`; `framing/shot.rs`]**

The existing camera surface reaches the entrance with no new field: a `cutscene` whose shot's `subject` is `{actor}` (spec-0110), placed in the same effect list after the `unleash-actor`, or in a `sequence` step at tick 0 beside it, frames the arriving body — the reference point is the feet cell's centre one block up and the sight box the feet and head cells, which the emerging warden rises through and the charging wither stands in. `DW1034` accepts the `unleash-actor` as the placing beat (§3). The shot's `seconds` against the registry's ticks is the author's call and is not checked: a shot longer than the window shows the body stand up, which may be the shot wanted. Players in a cutscene are spectators, so a wither's blast cannot reach them while the camera plays; outside a cutscene the blast's reach is the registry's stated fact (§2.2) and a design matter, not a refusal (CLAUDE.local.md: the designer's call on a dodge; the charge is visible and audible for 11 s with its own boss bar, so it is not an invisible hazard).

## 7. The gallery and the demo **[cited: spec-0039; CLAUDE.md *A campaign is never the engine's test surface*]**

The gallery gains one actor of kind `minecraft:warden` with `entrance: "native"` and `outrun: true`, never `spawn-actor`ed, unleashed by a beat on the critical walk, followed by one walked step while it stands and then a `despawn-actor` `vanish`; its hall is sized so the warden's roaming ground and the step's lane are the designer's to place, and the README names the element and what each check fires on it. Units bound: `actors[].entrance` (and its value `native`), `actors[].outrun`. Probes, each the primary plus one declared edit, refused with the named code: `a-zombie-that-digs-out` (`DW1081`), `an-entrance-nothing-unleashes` (`DW1082`, first arm), `a-puppet-for-a-body-that-digs-out` (`DW1083`, a `spawn-actor` added for the entering actor). The wither's entrance is not in the gallery — its blast in a shared hall is a hazard to every other element — and is proven by the emission tests of §9 against the record of `native-entrance.md`; the README says so in one line. The capability is confirmed on the maze level, the map in progress that needs it (CLAUDE.local.md: *Work toward a whole map is confirmed on that map*); if no such map is in progress when the implementation merges, `docs/demo-levels.md` gains a row instead.

## 8. How the general form extends this **[authored]**

The later round's object is a kind-specific action at any moment: a warden's roar or dig, a wither's skull volley, on a beat or a trigger. Everything here is built as that round's first row rather than a special case it replaces: the registry is already keyed `(kind, action)`, with `enter` as its first action; a record already carries the mechanism, the length, the window facts and the read site that every later row needs; `DW1081` already names the action it was asked for. The general round adds an effect that binds a registry action to a beat — one verb, taking an actor and an action name, on the actor class — and adds rows, each measured on the pinned server before it is written; `entrance: "native"` remains the declaration of the one action whose moment is fixed by the engine (the body's arrival) rather than by a beat, and nothing about the field, the module, the record shape or the codes moves.
