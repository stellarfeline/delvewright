- **What it is for**: a killing volume that is not there until the story puts it there — a pit's floor that is a floor until the ceiling comes down into it, a shaft that is a way through until the furnace is lit, a chamber whose bottom kills only once the thing in it has woken. Today a `lethal_volumes[]` entry is live from world-load to the end (coverage map row M04, rank 6), so a creator who wants a hazard that arrives has to choose between a volume that kills before the story says so and no volume at all. This spec gives the volume the one gate every other gated object carries, holds it in the navigation world per quest configuration exactly as a gate seal is held, and asks the danger-is-visible question in every configuration a body can meet the volume in — including the one in which it switches on.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record of what the tree does and of what the rig measured. Each statement is marked **cited** (a line of the tree at `2316b1c6`, a reading in the rig's record, a published source already in `docs/reference/`, or a constitution rule) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0088`. Two diagnostics, named here as placeholders until the planner allocates them: `DW_LETHAL_STAGE_GATE` (a gate that cannot stage a volume, §9) and `DW_LETHAL_STAGE_UNMET` (a staged volume the forced route never meets live, §9). `DW0891` gains a fourth shape under its own code (§5), `DW0510` and `DW0921` widen their messages and take no new code. `when` on `lethal_volumes[]` is a stage-5 surface change inside the unpublished `dsl_version`, which unreleased format changes share; this spec states no version literal.
- **Non-goals**: a volume whose liveness differs per player (§3.2 refuses it); a volume whose region moves (a region is a declaration; a moving hazard is spec-0038's open gap and the tide rule of the operating practice, not a gate); rising or falling fluid as the hazard (spec-0038); any change to what a live volume does to a body (the sweep, the wording, the death edge are spec-0031's and unchanged); a hazard that reads as danger only by being lit or by sound (a signal is a block under or in a caught cell, spec-0062 §3, and nothing here widens that); a `collapse` or any trap's own lethality (spec-0011 and `DW0342` own what a trap does to the body on its trigger cell; this spec owns what the pit it opens does afterwards).

## 1. The thing, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (`stages.rs`, `LethalVolume`): a lethal volume is *a declared box that kills whatever enters it*, five fields, no gate. **Cited** (`gate.rs`, module doc): a gate is *the campaign's answer to "may this happen yet?"*, one value of three fields, carried by seven object classes in a closed set (`GateConsumer::ALL`), with `crates/dsl/tests/gate_consumers.rs` failing when any schema object declares `requires_flags` without `requires_state`. **Cited** (`stages.rs`, `Guard`): the three fields as one object, declared once and carried by `QuestEffect::when`, *so every verb is gatable on exactly the same terms and a fourth axis is one field here*.

**Authored — the object.** A volume's liveness is a gate. It is not an effect — nothing fires, nobody is addressed, the world's blocks do not change; the volume has a property every tick for whatever is in it, and the property is now conditional on campaign state. So the capability belongs to the volume, as a field, and the field is the `Guard` that already exists: `lethal_volumes[].when`. The volume becomes the eighth gate consumer, written into `GateConsumer` and `for_each_gate` beside the other seven, so every proof written over "every gate" sees it and the binding ledger counts it (**cited**: CLAUDE.md, *a capability belongs to the object class it acts on, not to the verb that first needed it*; `gate.rs`, *the list of gate consumers is one fact*).

Two placements were considered and rejected. **An effect pair** (`arm-volume` / `disarm-volume` verbs in a bundle): two verbs for one property, each needing the region model to learn a new write, and a volume armed by one branch and disarmed by another becomes a question about effect ordering the DAG cannot always answer. **A second list** (`staged_volumes[]`): a private twin of `LethalVolume` with a gate on it — the shape `tools/ci/check-capability-ownership.py` check C refuses, and a creator would have to know in advance which list a hazard belongs in.

## 2. What the tree does today, and what the rig measured

### 2.1 The volume and its proofs

**Cited**, `compiler.md` `lethal_volumes[]` row and `lethal.rs`: one tick line `function <ns>:lethal_<id>` per volume; a two-line body sweeping players (`execute as @a[<box>,tag=!dw_cutscene] run function <ns>:lethal_<id>_kill`) and every other body less the engine's own machinery types; the kill is an ordinary `/damage`, so the death edge, the checkpoint re-seat and `keep_inventory` see the death they already handle. The volume's cells are **impassable** in the shared navigation `World` (`World::lethal`, with `lethal_regions` carried so a refusal can name the volume), widened by the walker's own half-width (`metrics::keep_out_box`, `World::meets_lethal_fp`; `World::standable_fp` refuses a body whose hitbox the selector would reach), so every route proof inherits the volume for free — `DW0510` is the counterfactual over `World::without_exclusions`. `DW0511` refuses a posted place (the entry spawn, a checkpoint or bonfire seat, an NPC post, a `cast` placement, an actor post, a wave's seated cell) whose body meets a volume. `DW0891` (spec-0062) refuses a volume whose keep-out catches a cell of the walked population that no block under or in it shows, over the population `World::reachable_walkable` from every cell the party is put at (`population_roots`: the entry, every checkpoint seat, every transit-teleport destination), lethality and furniture lifted; its zero binding is a warning. `DW0922`/`DW0923` refuse a seated wave whose members can walk, drop or sink into a volume. The ledger is `validation/lethal-gate.json`; a campaign that declares no volume emits none of it.

**Cited**, `Premises::of_plan`: the volumes enter the world as one premise among the ambient, the built extent, the furniture, the world-load seals and the teleport sources — one value, filled by one function, *so a caller cannot state a subset and cannot forget one*. The record of why that type exists is in its doc: the world-edits replay arm once applied the seals and not the volumes, and the gallery's exported path walked six waypoints through two declared volumes.

### 2.2 The one gate, and how a line's firing is read

**Cited**, `gate.rs` and `plan.rs`: `Plan::gate_terms` reduces a gate to scoreboard terms — a required flag to `#party dw.f_<flag> matches 1`, a forbidden flag to the same under `unless`, a numeric comparison to a range on the datum's holder — and `GateTerm::clause` renders each; *the one reduction, so the `execute` condition the emitter writes and the terms `validation/death-plan.json` hands the bot tier are the same reading of the same declaration*. `firing_of` is *the one reading* of when a firing happens and whether the party can avoid causing it: an objective's or quest's bundle fires at its step on a path that plays it and is forced there *only for a line the path's replay fires with its whole gate decided open* (`PathFiring::fires`); a trigger's lines at the `trigger` step the path performs it in; a trap payload, a death bundle, an offer, a shortcut's far side and a line whose gate the replay cannot date are unforced at step 0. `respawn::flag_bounds` is the reading of *the earliest step each required flag can be set, resolved recursively over its own producers' gates, with 0 on any cycle or unresolvable producer*. **Cited**, `stages.rs`: flags are set by `Verb::SetFlag` and its dialogue twin, and no verb clears one — a flag, once set, is set for the rest of the delve.

### 2.3 The per-configuration model

**Cited**, `nav.rs`: `RegionState` is *what every runtime-written region has become, as of one point in the quest DAG* — four cell sets (`solid`, `cleared`, `flooded`, `unforced`) with the boxes behind two of them for blame; `World::region_state_at(events, arrival, ancestor)` derives it from the plan's `RegionEvent`s and the world-load seals, latest write winning, crediting a write only when `ancestor(fire_step, arrival)` holds (`Plan::gate_fired_before`: a strict DAG ancestor, or step 0, which precedes every leg); `World::with_region_state` is *the one place that knows what a runtime region write does to the geometry a proof reasons over*. **Cited**, `collect_region_events`: *an unguaranteed firing may seal a region and may never open one* — an unforced fill lands in `unforced`, pointwise the worse of both futures (impassable and not floor), and an unforced clear is dropped. **Cited**, the consumers: the critical path routes each leg under `leg_region_state`; `DW0921` is *judged once per quest configuration the critical path passes through (`region_state_at` of each leg's arrival), with that configuration's gates as they stand and its own route cells as the place to get back to*; `reachable_under_every_quest_state` floods a respawn seat under every distinct configuration from the seat's step on; `world_while_next` hands `DW0924` the configuration while a fight is the beat the story waits on; `check_branch_path` routes each branch under its own step space. So a region written at runtime is already a per-configuration fact in every proof, and the lethal premise is the one region-shaped fact that is not.

### 2.4 The bot

**Cited**, `executor.ts` and `death-loop.ts`: `useDeathPlan` hands the navigator *the declared regions* of every volume as impassable boxes, widened once by the harness's own mirror of the server's rule (`volumeReachesCell`), for the whole run; the death-loop stage walks a real client into every declared volume and asserts what the campaign promised; a `drop-stake`'s conditional promise reaches the bot as gate terms (`drops_stake[].gates[].terms[]`), and `gateVerdict` puts each term to the server as its own `execute if|unless score … matches …` clause and reads which way it answered — *a term answered neither way establishes nothing and reds the trial*. `death-plan.json` carries `format_version` (`DEATH_PLAN_FORMAT_VERSION`, 3) and the parser refuses a version it does not understand.

### 2.5 What the rig measured: a body standing in the volume when the gate flips

**Instrument.** `tools/spike-staged-lethal/run.sh` boots the pinned server image (`itzg/minecraft-server@sha256:3e7db256…`, `versions.toml [images.base]`, vanilla 1.21.11, flat world with the stone top at y 0) and installs `spikepack/`: the emitted shape with one party score standing in for `dw.f_<flag>` — a tick line `execute if score #party dwsl.f matches 1 run function dwsl:lethal`, two volume boxes (A at foot level, cells x 100..102 y 1..2 z 100..102; B the same box moved ten cells in x and one course up), a volume function that stamps `time query gametime` the first tick it matches a body and `damage @s 1000 minecraft:generic`s it, and an `arm` function that stamps the tick and flips the score in one act, so the two land in the same server tick. `measure.mjs` drives one mineflayer body (harness pin 4.37.1) standing still at five points, three trials each, plus a control; every rcon reply is read through `tools/lib/rcon.mjs`. Readings are in `observations.json`.

| Body, standing still | Hitbox meets the box | Trials | Killed | Server ticks from the flip to the kill | Client saw its death after the arm command |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| feet in the box, at its centre (volume A) | yes | 3 | 3 of 3 | 0, 0, 0 | 104, 114, 126 ms |
| feet one cell outside the box, body 0.25 from its face (A) | yes | 3 | 3 of 3 | 0, 0, 0 | 91, 130, 124 ms |
| the same cell, body 0.75 from the face (A) | no | 3 | 0 of 3 | — | — |
| two cells out (A) | no | 3 | 0 of 3 | — | — |

Three facts for this spec. **A body standing where a volume comes live is killed in the same server tick the gate flips** — there is no tick in which it could step out. **Whether it is killed is decided by its hitbox, not by the cell its feet are in**: the same cell beside the face kills at 0.25 from the face and spares at 0.75, which is the keep-out rule (`metrics::keep_out_box`, spec-0062) measured at the switch-on rather than at the walk-in, and the course under a box is caught exactly as its ring is. **The gate withholds the volume completely**: a body stood inside the box for 61 ticks with the gate shut and the kill counter did not move. Each is a reading of the server's own tick counter and the body's own `Health`; the client's death event is reported beside it and decides nothing.

## 3. The surface

### 3.1 `when`

**Authored.** `lethal_volumes[]` gains one optional field:

```
{ id: lethal/<kebab>, region: { anchor, extent }, message, damage_type?, shown_by?,
  when?: { requires_flags?, forbids_flags?, requires_state? } }
```

`when` is the `Guard` type `QuestEffect::when` carries, verbatim — one object, the three axes, `deny_unknown_fields`. Absent, the volume is live from world-load to the end and every proof, every emitted byte and every ledger row is what it is today. Present, the volume is **live while its gate holds**: `requires_flags: [flag/the-ceiling-fell]` is a pit that kills from that beat on; `forbids_flags: [flag/the-furnace-cold]` is a shaft that kills until that beat; `requires_state: [{state: state/water-level, op: at-least, value: 3}]` is a chamber that kills while a party datum stands at or above three. Nothing is inverted and nothing is new: each term means what it means on every other consumer (**cited**: `Guard`'s field docs; `EnvTrigger::forbids_flags`'s doc, *suppressed while any listed flag is set*).

`LethalVolume` answers `gate()` in `gate.rs` beside the other seven, `GateConsumer` gains `LethalVolume`, `for_each_gate` visits every volume after every shop offer in declaration order, and `GateSite.path` for it names the `when` object as it does for an effect. `crates/dsl/tests/gate_consumers.rs` then holds the volume to the numeric axis without a new assertion.

### 3.2 What a gate on a volume may not say

**Authored.** Two shapes are refused at the document, `DW_LETHAL_STAGE_GATE` (validation tier, exit 1, `dsl::validate`), where they are entered:

- **`when` present and empty.** An always-live volume is spelled by leaving `when` out; `when: {}` is the same volume with words that say nothing, and a gate with no term is not a stage. (The same shape spec-0086 §3.2 refuses on a loop, for the same reason.)
- **A `requires_state` term naming a `player`-scoped datum.** A volume's liveness is a fact about the place, so the gate is a fact about the party: a term one player satisfies and another does not is a pit that kills one body and spares the one beside it, and the sweep's entity half (`@e[<box>,type=!minecraft:player…]`) has no player to read a per-player score from. Flags are campaign state and need no such rule (**cited**: `EnvTrigger::forbids_flags`'s doc, *by any player — flags are campaign state*).

Everything else a gate may say today it may say here: a flag nothing sets, a datum nobody declares, a contradiction between a required and a forbidden flag are each already refused by the gate's own existing diagnostics at every consumer, and this spec adds no second copy of them.

## 4. Liveness, and where the proofs read it

### 4.1 Two readings of one gate

**Authored**, over §2.2's cited readings. A proof asks one of two questions of a staged volume at a quest configuration — the configuration of a leg's arrival, as `region_state_at` keys it — and the two questions have the two readings every other region fact already has.

- **May the volume be live here?** (`may_live`) The reading a route proof takes, in the direction that makes the volume impassable whenever the campaign cannot prove it is not: every required flag has a setter the configuration credits — a forced firing the arrival's ancestry contains (`firing_of`, `Plan::gate_fired_before`), or **any** unforced setter (a trap payload, an offer, a death bundle, a line whose gate the replay cannot date), credited at step 0 as `collect_region_events` credits an unforced fill; no forbidden flag has a **forced** setter the ancestry contains (an unforced setter of a forbidden flag is never credited: it would make the volume passable, and *an unguaranteed firing may never open*); every numeric term may hold — the replay's forced value satisfies it, or some unforced root writes the datum at all. Flags are never cleared (§2.2), so a required flag once credited stays credited and a forbidden flag once credited stays credited, which is what keeps the first reading monotone along any path and `may_live` derivable from the region model's own events.
- **Is the volume live here?** (`is_live`) The reading the ladder's exercise takes, in the direction that counts only what the forced route guarantees: every required flag set by a forced firing the ancestry contains; no forbidden flag with **any** setter credited at or before here (forced ancestor, or unforced at 0); every numeric term decided true by the replay (`PathFiring::fires`'s own standard: *its whole gate decided open*).

`is_live` implies `may_live`. An unstaged volume answers both with yes at every configuration. Neither reading is a new model: the first is the unforced-fill rule applied to a term, the second is the effect-firing rule applied to a term, and `flag_bounds` is the existing function that answers *from which step a flag may be set*.

### 4.2 The navigation world holds the volume per configuration

**Authored**, on the cited model of §2.3. `RegionState` gains a fifth set and its regions: the cells, and the `(id, box)` list, of every staged volume that `may_live` at this configuration. `World::with_region_state` applies them with the rule `World::from_plan` applies the premise today — impassable, widened by the walker's body, never floor, carried by id so a refusal can name the volume — in the order it already orders writes (a lethal cell is impassable like a flood and additionally never floor, so it joins the flood at the strict end). `Premises::of_plan` keeps carrying the **unstaged** volumes, so a campaign that stages none builds a `World` and a `RegionState` byte-identical to today's; a staged volume reaches the base world through no other door. The one function that answers *which volumes may be live at this arrival* lives beside `region_state_at`, reads `Plan::gate_terms`'s reduction of the gate and the plan's flag events (every `set-flag` line, with `firing_of`'s step and forcedness, collected by the same walk `collect_region_events` takes), and is the only place a gate is turned into a lethal set.

So every proof that already asks `region_state_at` inherits the staged volume without a line of its own, and each is named here with what changes for it:

| Consumer | How it sees a staged volume | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| `check_critical_path` (`DW0311`/`DW0510`), via `leg_region_state` | the leg's world has the volume impassable iff `may_live` at the leg's arrival | `DW0510`'s message names the volume **and the configuration** (the arrival step and the terms that hold there); a leg walked before the flip crosses the volume's cells as floor |
| `check_branch_path` (per-branch `DW0311`) | the same, under the branch's own step space | none beyond the above |
| `critical_path_routes` / `branch_path_routes` → waypoint artifacts | a leg before the flip is exported through the cells; a leg after, around them | none; the artifacts follow the proof |
| `check_checkpoints` (`DW0315`/`DW0316`) and `reachable_under_every_quest_state` | a seat's way forward is judged with the volume live in every later configuration it may be live in | **new coverage, conservative**: a checkpoint before the flip whose only way onward crosses the volume is refused — the respawn-into-a-stranding this spec would otherwise create |
| `check_bodies_can_leave` (`DW0921`) | per configuration, a live volume's cells are not reached by `body_moves` and not a way out; a dead volume's cells are ordinary cells | the message names the volume's state in the configuration it names |
| `world_while_next` → `DW0924` | the party's cells exclude a volume live while the fight waits | none |
| `edit::check_batch_invariants` (the world-edits replay) | the same `Premises` and the same `RegionState` | none: a gate bound at one of two doors is bound at neither (**cited**: `DW0891`'s row) |
| `strand`, `reach`, `render_plan`, the editor re-proof | read the routes and the worlds the rows above produce | none |

**Residual, named.** The configuration model assumes the party moves through the DAG together: a body left behind a seal is outside every per-configuration proof, for a staged volume exactly as for a `close-gate` today. This spec does not widen that gap and does not close it.

## 5. Danger is visible in every configuration a body can meet the volume in

**Cited**, the standing rule and spec-0062: a killing volume may not reach a cell that reads to a player as safe floor; the repair for an invisible hazard is to move the hazard, never to mark walkable-looking ground unwalkable; `DW0891` is *one code, three shapes, one rule — a killing volume and what shows it agree*, judged *once per volume over the final assembled world*.

**Authored — when "once" becomes "per configuration".** A staged volume has no one assembled world to be judged over: the floor around it may be plain stone before the flip and settled debris after, reachable after a `clear-region` and roofed before. So `DW0891` is judged per configuration, the way `DW0921` is, and the configurations it is judged in are chosen by the one question the rule asks — *can a body be on this floor while this volume can reach it?* — which has two answers:

1. **Every configuration the critical path passes in which the volume `may_live`.** A body walking there walks beside a hazard that may be armed.
2. **The configuration of the leg that arrives at each step where the volume first `may_live`** — the last configuration before each switch-on. §2.5 is why: a body standing in the keep-out when the gate flips is dead in the same tick, so the floor it was standing on must have read as danger **before** the flip, in that configuration's own bytes. Where the volume `may_live` from step 0 (a required flag some unforced root can set, a gate with no required flag), there is no "before", the volume is judged in every configuration from the first, and the first configuration's floor must already show it. This subsumes the narrower reading — the cells the forced route stands on when the gate flips, the firing step's own footing and the arrival leg's route cells — because those cells are in the configuration's walked population by construction.

In each judged configuration `C`, the terms are the ones spec-0062 already defines, each taken over `C`'s world (`with_region_state(C)` with exclusions lifted, as `DW0921` takes its world) and `C`'s bytes: the population is `reachable_walkable` from the put-at roots (`population_roots`, or spec-0083 §3.8's put-at roots once that lands); the caught cells are the population inside `metrics::keep_out_box`; a caught cell is shown when the block under or in it **in `C`'s bytes** is one of `shown_by`. `C`'s bytes are the assembled `BlockMap` with every write `C` credits laid as the block its emitted command writes (a fill's block, air for a clear or an unseal, the fluid for a flood, a collapse's settled `falling_block` and its `then_floor` where declared) — so a `RegionEvent` carries the block it writes beside the class of write it is, and one function derives a configuration's block map for whoever asks (spec-0086 §4.4 asks the same function, whichever lands first). The two existing shapes keep their wording and gain the configuration (the arrival step, and the terms that hold there); the **fourth shape**, raised in the configuration before a switch-on, says so in its own words — *`lethal/<id>` goes live at step N and a body may be standing on these cells when it does; nothing in the world before that beat says they kill* — and prescribes, in the geometry's own terms and in this order: roof or wall the volume's cells off until the beat that opens them (the beat that arms the volume is usually the beat that should open the way to it); lower the volume so its keep-out's top course lies under the floor a body stands on before the flip; author one of the blocks vanilla hurts a body with under those cells, visible before the flip, and declare it in `shown_by`. Never mark the floor unwalkable, and never fire the flag later to pass — a hazard that arrives silently under a body is the finding, wherever on the path it arrives.

**The zero binding is per configuration too.** `reached_by` is asked in each judged configuration over that configuration's world — a volume no body can get into before the flip (roofed) and every body can fall into after (opened) is **bound** by the live configuration, and the ledger says in which. A staged volume no modelled body reaches in any configuration in which it may be live is the existing `DW0891` warning, worded with the configurations examined.

**The population is still the lethality-free one, in every configuration.** `the_population_is_the_lethality_free_one` (spec-0062 §10.4) is extended to a staged fixture: over the lethal-applied world of the live configuration the caught count is asserted zero, over the counterfactual non-zero, and the check is bound to the second.

## 6. Posted places and seated waves are judged as if every volume were live

**Authored.** `DW0511` (`posted_places`, `check_respawn_seats`) and `DW0922`/`DW0923` (`wave_reach`) do not read the gate: a post, a seat or a wave's reach that meets a staged volume is refused whether or not the volume is live at the moment the body is put there. The quantifier is deliberate and is stated in each message (*this volume is live from a story stage; the body is judged against it as live*): an NPC posted in a pit that wakes at act two is deleted in silence at act two, a checkpoint seat in it is a death loop from that beat on, and a wave whose members can walk into it thins itself after the flip — the same defects, arriving later. A creator who wants a body somewhere a volume will later be moves the body before the beat (a `cast` placement, a `move-actor`) and the proof sees the move where it already sees moves; nothing here needs a new surface for it.

## 7. Emission and PackTest

**Authored**, on the cited emission of §2.1. For a staged volume the tick line becomes `function <ns>:lethal_<id>_tick`, a one-line function `execute <terms> run function <ns>:lethal_<id>` whose `<terms>` are `Plan::gate_terms` over the volume's gate, each rendered by `GateTerm::clause(false)` and joined — the formatter every effect guard and every trigger gate is written by, so the volume cannot disagree with them about what a term means. `lethal_<id>` and `lethal_<id>_kill` are unchanged: what a live volume does is spec-0031's. For an unstaged volume the tick line is `function <ns>:lethal_<id>` as today, so every campaign that stages nothing is byte-identical.

**PackTest**, one more template per staged volume beside today's `lethal_<id>`: `lethal_<id>_shut` summons the dummy in the box as the existing template does, sets the gate **shut** by exactly one term (the first required flag's score cleared, else the first forbidden flag's score set, else the first numeric datum set to a value outside the set its term admits — the complement of what `DatumSet::pick` chooses), drives `lethal_<id>_tick`, and asserts the dummy's `Health` is still 20; the existing `lethal_<id>` for a staged volume sets the gate **open** (every required flag 1, every forbidden flag reset, every numeric datum to the value `DatumSet::pick` chooses from the set its term admits) and drives `lethal_<id>_tick` rather than `lethal_<id>`, so a stripped guard reds the shut template and a stripped sweep reds the open one. Both bind the dummy by tag and assert it stood in the box first, as the existing template does.

## 8. The bot

**Authored**, on §2.4. `death-plan.json`'s volume rows gain `gate: {terms: [...]}` — the same `GateTerm` rows a `drop-stake`'s gates carry, empty for an unstaged volume — and `DEATH_PLAN_FORMAT_VERSION` moves; the parser accepts the new version and refuses a staged row with no `gate` key. Two consumers read it:

- **The navigator.** `useDeathPlan` hands the pathfinder an unstaged volume's region as today. A staged volume's region is excluded **per walk leg**: before each leg the executor puts every term of the volume's gate to the server through the one term-asking the stake trial already uses (`askTerm`, `gateVerdict`'s reading) and excludes the region when the gate reads open; a term the server answers neither way excludes the region (the conservative direction, and a `[lethal]` line says so). A leg walked before the flip therefore crosses the cells the proof crossed, and a leg after goes round them; a die-retry walk back from a seat crosses them only if the volume still reads shut, which is exactly what §4.2's checkpoint row makes the compiler refuse otherwise.
- **The death loop.** A staged volume's trial opens by asking its gate. A volume that reads **open** is entered as any volume is and judged by every existing assertion. One that reads **shut** is not entered: the trial records `not_live_at_trial` with the term that shut it, the stage's binding reports `staged_volumes` and `staged_live_at_trial` beside `volumes`, and a build whose staged volumes were all shut at trial has exercised none of them — a stated fact in the report, never a pass. A `forbids_flags` volume is the common case of that fact (the forced route has set the flag by the time the path completes), and the report's row for it says so.

## 9. Refusals, the binding line and the ledger

**Authored.** Two codes, each raised where the fault is entered.

| Code | Tier | Fault | Message names | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DW_LETHAL_STAGE_GATE` | validation (exit 1), `dsl::validate` | `when` present and empty; a `requires_state` term on a `player`-scoped datum (§3.2) | the volume, the term | leave `when` out, or name a flag or a `party` datum |
| `DW_LETHAL_STAGE_UNMET` | advisory (warning), `compiler::lethal` | the forced route passes no configuration in which the volume `is_live` (§4.1): the ladder cannot exercise it, the death loop will find it shut, and only `may_live` configurations were judged for visibility | the volume, the step its gate was first read, and the term that never held | none required; a hazard the party need never arm is a design, and the bot's report will say it was not exercised |

`DW0891` gains the fourth shape (§5) under its own code. `DW0510` and `DW0921` widen their messages with the configuration and the volume's state in it (§4.2) and take no new code.

**Binding**, printed on every build that declares a volume, refusals included — one line, extended: `danger-visibility binding: N volume(s), S staged; judged over C configuration(s) as K (volume, configuration) pair(s) against walked populations of M..M′ cell(s); X caught, Y shown, Z read as safe floor; D declaration(s) of E borne out by the bytes; R of N volume(s) reached by a body the engine models (in P of K pairs)`. `S`, `C` and `K` are computed from the volumes and the path's configurations with `N` as the denominator; a staged volume judged in zero configurations is a defect of the enumeration and is a red, not a line. `validation/lethal-gate.json` gains `staged` beside `volumes`, per volume `gate_terms` and `configurations: {judged, may_live, is_live, of}`, and `danger_visibility.volumes[]` becomes one row per (volume, configuration) with the configuration's arrival step on it.

## 10. spec-0083 and spec-0086, whichever lands first

**Authored**, from reading both at the revisions the ground names. Neither is depended on; this section says how the three compose in either order.

- **The roots.** spec-0083 §3.8 splits `lethal::population_roots` into put-at and stands-at roots; spec-0086 §5.3 adds a loop's landing cells to the same list. This spec reads the put-at roots through whatever one function the tree holds when it lands and adds nothing to the list: the population of §5 is rooted exactly where `DW0891`'s is. Three edits to one function merge as one function.
- **A gated region, built once.** spec-0086 §5.1 reads a loop's slab as *a region the route proof treats as solid while the loop holds and clear while it stands down, read per leg from the gate's state at that leg*. That is this spec's §4.1 and §4.2 with `solid` in place of `lethal`: one evaluation of a gate at a configuration, in two readings, feeding `RegionState`. Whichever of the two lands first builds that evaluation beside `region_state_at` and names it in `compiler.md`'s region-solidity record; the second adds its set to `RegionState` and its row to the consumer table, and neither carries a private copy of how a term is read at a leg.
- **A link onto a live volume.** spec-0083 §3.6 judges a link's `to` standable *in the world as the link's own root leaves it at the teleport's tick*; with lethality in `RegionState`, that world holds every volume that `may_live` there, `World::is_standable` refuses a cell a volume can reach, and a link onto a pit that has woken is `DW0932` by spec-0083's own rule. A gather's `to` stays unjudged, as spec-0083 leaves it; this spec notes and does not widen that.
- **A loop's span.** spec-0086 §4.6 requires every declared volume intersecting the span to equal its own image under the offset in every configuration; with liveness part of the configuration, a volume live in one bay whose image in the next is dead is a `DW0946` mismatch in that configuration — spec-0086's rule, reading this spec's set, with no new code.
- **Nothing here reads a carry.** A link's or a loop's `transport` step moves the party; the configuration at the step after it is `region_state_at`'s business as for any step, and a staged volume is read there like any region.

## 11. The gallery's obligation, and the demo level

**Authored**, under spec-0039 and against `gallery/` at `2316b1c6`, where `lethal/west-pit` and `lethal/east-pit` are both unstaged and the hall's `collapse` drops the east vault's ceiling onto cobblestone.

1. **Bound.** The gallery declares one **staged** volume beside its two unstaged ones, so the ledger's `staged` column is non-zero and the `volumes` column still counts both kinds: a pit under a floor the party crosses, roofed by that floor, opened by a beat a forced objective fires (a `collapse` or a `clear-region` over the floor in the same bundle as the `set-flag` the volume's `when.requires_flags` names), the volume at the pit's bottom at least three courses under the rim so no rim cell is in its keep-out. Before the flip the pit is unreachable and the volume dead; after it the hole is a hole and the volume is live and bound by a fall. ## 12. Decisions

None. The one judgement a reader might expect to be put to someone — whether a hazard that arrives under a standing body is refused or merely warned (§5) — is decided by the standing rule the operating practice already holds: danger is visible, or the engine refuses it, and §2.5 measures that the body has no tick in which to see it. For the planner: allocate the two codes; read §4.2's checkpoint row as the one place this spec can turn an existing campaign's proof red, in the direction that can only stop something shipping.
