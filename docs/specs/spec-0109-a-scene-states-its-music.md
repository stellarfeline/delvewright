# spec-0109: A scene states its music — every biome a camera can stand in declares a track or silence, a story stage moves it by repainting the place, a cutscene hears the place it looks at, and the build proves what each scene plays

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Ground**: written against engine `a845fa114` (`origin/main`), read only — `Atmosphere`, `WorldContent::atmospheres`, `Horizon`, `HorizonBase`, `HorizonSpec`, `atmosphere_dangling_refs` in `crates/dsl/src/world.rs`; `Verb::{SetAtmosphere, Cutscene, PlaySound}`, `Verb::addresses_players` in `crates/dsl/src/quest/verb.rs`; `CameraShot` in `crates/dsl/src/cutscene.rs`; `horizon::{ground_biome, void_biome_definition}` in `crates/delvec/src/compiler/horizon.rs`; the atmosphere attribute reader (`compiler/atmosphere.rs`, `music`, `background_music`); the `playsound` writers in `compiler/emit/effect.rs`; the `atmospheres[]`, `horizon`, `set-atmosphere`, `play-sound`, `pulses[]`, *A cutscene is pure observation*, *The ground biome*, *Atmosphere biomes* and *The biome map* rows of `docs/reference/compiler.md`; the pinned command tree `crates/delvec/data/commands-1.21.11.json` (`stopsound`, `playsound`); the vendored sound-event registry `crates/delvec/data/sounds-1.21.11.json` (1838 ids) and the environment-attribute registry `environment-attributes-1.21.11.json` (`audio/background_music`, `audio/music_volume`, the `background_music` and `music` shapes). The pinned 1.21.11 game, read from the client jar (sha256 `1473c948…6cd3bd`) and the dedicated server's bundled `META-INF/versions/1.21.11/server-1.21.11.jar` (server jar sha256 `f83b8e09…dd1726`, `versions.toml`), disassembled with `javap -c -p` and named through Mojang's official 1.21.11 mappings (`client.txt` sha1 `031a68be…bf232a`, `server.txt` sha1 `5621e925…525955`); the pinned client's `assets/minecraft/sounds.json` (asset index 29, sha1 `123370fb…391dfb`); the server jar's `data/minecraft/dimension_type/overworld.json` and the biomes `pale_garden`, `warped_forest`, `ocean`, `cherry_grove`, `windswept_forest`, `the_void`. `StopSoundCommand` and `ClientboundStopSoundPacket` are byte-identical in the two jars (class sha256 `b2f9d563cce9b370…` and `769ace87e1f33e7e…`), so every server-side fact below is the dedicated server's; every client-side fact is the client's.
- **What it is for**: a scene whose music contradicts it. In the first full playtest of a campaign, vanilla's own calm overworld music played over an ending in which the town is destroyed, and the campaign had no way to silence it or to choose a track (the findings ledger's capability row for music). The pinned game decides music from exactly one thing — the biome under the camera — and the engine already owns that biome (spec-0080). What is missing is the obligation to state it, the ground's statement, the hard cut, and the proof that a scene plays what it declares.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's research record of the pinned game; the craft record is `docs/reference/game-audio.md`, written with this spec. Each rule is marked **cited** (a reading of the pinned bytes or data, a reading of the tree, a constitution rule, a named source) or **authored** (this spec chooses). Nothing below was run on a server; what the bot tier must measure is named in §5.3.
- **Numbers**: no ADR. **Two DW codes**, allocated by the planner — **`DW1031`** (a scene that does not state its music, or states one no delve body can hear, §6.1) and **`DW1032`** (a cutscene that hears what it did not declare, §6.2). A sound id takes `DW0326`, an attribute value outside its pinned range takes `DW0928`, a dangling atmosphere reference takes `DW0112`, a `region`/`place` neither-or-both takes `DW0929`, an enum word the schema lacks takes `DW0100`: no second code for a rule that exists. **`dsl_version` moves to `0.47.0`**; it numbers a surface and promises nothing.
- **Non-goals**: a track of the campaign's own (a `sounds.json` entry and `.ogg` in the resource pack the delve already serves — a spec of its own; under ADR-0013 such a file is admissible only as original work or under CC0, CC BY, MIT, Apache-2.0 or a GPL-3.0-compatible licence, never CC BY-NC, CC BY-ND or an unstated licence, with provenance recorded; until it lands the vocabulary is vanilla's registered sound events, §2.6); a fade whose length the creator chooses (the client's fade curve is fixed, §2.1); layered or tempo-adaptive music (the client has one music channel and plays one file); music keyed to time (a story stage moves it, never a clock); a per-player track in one place (music is a fact about where the camera stands); the jukebox and the `record` category; the client's own music options (frequency, the now-playing toast, the music slider — the player's, §2.1); occlusion; the valley surround's bands, which keep the generator's biomes and their vanilla music (spec-0080 non-goal; §3.2 names the leak).

## 1. The defect, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (the tree at `a845fa114`): music reaches a delve by one channel, an atmosphere's `audio/background_music` and `audio/music_volume` attributes (spec-0080), carried by a place from the first tick or painted by `set-atmosphere`. Both are optional. The ground biome — `<ns>:void`, or vanilla's `minecraft:ocean` under an ocean horizon — declares neither (`void_biome_definition` sets `visual/sky_color` alone). There is no verb that stops a sound. `play-sound` writes every `playsound` in the `master` category, so a `music.*` event it plays is neither governed by the player's music slider nor known to the client's music manager, which starts its own track over it.

**Cited** (`data/minecraft/dimension_type/overworld.json`, pinned server jar): the overworld dimension type carries `audio/background_music: {default: music.game 12000..24000, creative: music.creative 12000..24000}`. The attribute's own default is `BackgroundMusic.EMPTY` (`EnvironmentAttributes.<clinit>`), so **a biome that declares no music inherits the dimension's**, and the dimension's is vanilla's calm overworld music. That is the track that played over the destroyed town: nobody chose it, and nothing could refuse it.

**The object music belongs to is the biome under the camera** (§2.1: `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic` reads `audio/background_music` from the camera's attribute probe and nothing else in a loaded world). The engine's name for that biome is the **atmosphere** (spec-0080), and a scene — a place, a quest stage, a cutscene — is already expressed in it: a place carries one; a story stage repaints a volume with another; a cutscene's camera stands in one. So this spec adds no music object. It makes the statement **obligatory** at every biome a camera can stand in (every atmosphere and the ground), gives the cut its verb, lets a cutscene declare what it hears so the build can confirm it, and proves the declarations.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 How the pinned client chooses, starts, replaces, fades and stops music

**Cited — `MusicManager.tick / startPlaying / stopPlaying / fadePlaying / canReplace`, `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic / getMusicVolume`, `MusicManager$MusicFrequency.getNextSongDelay`, `BackgroundMusic.select`, `Musics.<clinit>`, `Mth.nextInt`, bytecode.**

| Fact | Reading |
|---|---|
| Where music is read | `getSituationalMusic`: a screen with its own music (only `WinScreen` — the credits) wins; otherwise, with a player and a camera, the END dimension's boss overlay yields `END_BOSS`; otherwise **`gameRenderer.getMainCamera().attributeProbe().getValue(BACKGROUND_MUSIC, 1.0)`** — the camera's 4-cell, not the body's — then `BackgroundMusic.select(creative, underwater)`. `creative` is `abilities.instabuild && abilities.mayfly`: creative mode only, never adventure or spectator. `underwater` is `player.isUnderWater()`. `select` returns `underwater` if present and submerged, else `creative` if present and creative, else `default`; an empty `default` yields **null**. With no player: `MENU`. |
| What null does | `tick`: music null → `nextSongDelay = max(nextSongDelay, 100)`, return. **Nothing starts; a track already playing is not stopped** — it plays to its end. |
| Replacement | with a current track and a new `Music`: `canReplace` = `music.replaceCurrentMusic() && music.sound ≠ current.location` → `soundManager.stop(current)`, `nextSongDelay = nextInt(0, music.minDelay / 2)`. Then, once the current instance is no longer active, `currentMusic = null` and `nextSongDelay = min(nextSongDelay, frequency.getNextSongDelay(music))`; the next track starts when the countdown reaches 0. With `replace_current_music: false` the current track **plays to its end** and the new one follows after the delay. |
| The delay between tracks | `getNextSongDelay(music)`: `CONSTANT` → 100 ticks; else `nextInt(min(minDelay, cap), min(maxDelay, cap))` with `cap` = 20 min (`DEFAULT`, 24000 ticks) or 10 min (`FREQUENT`, 12000). `Mth.nextInt(r, a, b)` = `a` when `a ≥ b`. So `min_delay = max_delay = 0` **loops the track back to back** under `DEFAULT` and `FREQUENT`, and with a 5 s gap under `CONSTANT`; the player's option can only shorten a declared gap, never lengthen it. |
| Volume and fade | `getMusicVolume`: the camera probe's `MUSIC_VOLUME` (float, default 1.0, range `[0, 1]`, not spatially interpolated — the camera's own 4-cell, lerped only across one tick). `tick` runs `fadePlaying(target)` first whenever a track plays and the gain differs: **down**, `gain ← 0.03·target + 0.97·gain` per tick, so a cut to 0 reaches the `1e-4` floor in 303 ticks (≈ 15 s) and then `stopPlaying()`; **up**, `gain += clamp(gain, 5e-4, 0.005)` per tick, 0 → 1 in ≈ 203 ticks (≈ 10 s). The gain is applied through `SoundEngine.updateCategoryVolume(MUSIC, gain)`, a multiplier on **every** instance in the `music` category, so it also governs a `playsound … music`. The fade runs beside the rest of the tick: a replacement can cut a fading track, and only the tick on which the fade stops the track returns early. |
| The toast | `startPlaying` shows the client's now-playing toast when the play result says so; the toast names vanilla's track and artist, under the player's own *Show Music Toast* option. A creator who names a vanilla track names the one the toast will show. |

**Vanilla's own constants** (`Musics.<clinit>`): `MENU` (20, 600, replace), `CREATIVE` (12000, 24000, no replace), `CREDITS` (0, 0, replace), `END_BOSS` (0, 0, replace), `END` (6000, 24000, replace), `GAME` and `UNDER_WATER` (12000, 24000, no replace). A scene vanilla wants to own its music — the boss, the credits — declares `replace_current_music: true` with no delay; ambient game music waits ten to twenty minutes and never interrupts.

### 2.2 What a biome declares

**Cited** — the vendored attribute registry and `EnvironmentAttributes.<clinit>`: `audio/background_music` has shape `{default?, creative?, underwater?}`, each a `music` `{sound, min_delay ≥ 0, max_delay ≥ 0, replace_current_music?}` (`replace_current_music` defaults false in the codec); `audio/music_volume` is a float in `[0, 1]`. Neither is `spatiallyInterpolated` — spec-0080 §2.2's reading stands: the `audio/` attributes are read from the camera's own 4-cell with no blend. **`{}` silences**: `pale_garden.json` declares `background_music: {}` and `music_volume: 0.0`, so a track crossing into it fades out and nothing starts. **An event with no files silences too**: `warped_forest` names `music.nether.warped_forest`, whose `sounds.json` row has zero files; `{}` is the explicit form and the one this spec uses.

### 2.3 `stopsound`

**Cited — the pinned command tree, `StopSoundCommand.register / stopSound`, `ClientboundStopSoundPacket`, `ClientPacketListener.handleStopSoundEvent`, `SoundEngine.stop(Identifier, SoundSource)`.** `stopsound <targets> [<source> [<sound>]]`, `<source>` one of the ten category literals (`master`, `music`, `record`, `weather`, `block`, `hostile`, `neutral`, `player`, `ambient`, `voice`, `ui`) or `*` for any category with a named sound. The server sends one packet per target carrying the optional name and category and reports success per form; the client calls `SoundEngine.stop(name, source)`: with a category, every playing instance in that category (or only those with that name); with a name alone, every instance of that name in any category; with neither, `stopAll`. **It stops instances; it does not touch the music manager's state.** On the next tick the manager finds its track inactive and, where the camera's biome declares a track, re-arms the countdown by §2.1: a looping track restarts within a few ticks, a spaced one after its declared delay. **Where the camera's biome declares silence, nothing restarts.** So the command is the hard cut of a play-out — and of a stinger — and nothing else.

### 2.4 A positioned sound in the `music` category

**Cited** — spec-0102 §2.2–2.3 (`PlaySoundCommand`, `SoundEngine.play`, `Channel.linearAttenuation`) and `SoundEngine.calculateVolume(volume, source) = clamp(volume) · clamp(options[source]) · categoryGain[source]`. A `playsound <music.*> music …` is a positioned instance with linear attenuation to `16 · max(V, 1)` blocks, governed by the player's music slider and by the music fade, stopped by `stopsound … music`, and **never** the manager's `currentMusic` — the manager will start its own track over it unless the camera's biome declares silence. It is a stinger's shape, not a scene's music.

### 2.5 Which biome the camera is in, and when the client learns it

**Cited** — spec-0080 §2.1–2.2 and `compiler.md` *The biome map*: the server holds the biome per 4-cell; `fillbiome` reaches a connected client through `chunk_biomes` with no chunk reload (`harness/src/repaint.ts` measures it); a block whose coordinates are all 2 mod 4 always reads its own cell. A cutscene puts the watcher in spectator at the camera (`compiler.md`, *A cutscene is pure observation*), so **the camera's path, not the body's cell, decides a cutscene's music**.

### 2.6 The track vocabulary

**Cited** — the vendored registry (1838 ids, read by `DW0326`) and the pinned client's `sounds.json` (1837 rows): 31 `music.*` events and 21 `music_disc.*` events. **An event names a set and the client picks one file at random each start**: `music.game` 27 files, `music.overworld.forest` 21, `music.overworld.frozen_peaks` 9, `music.overworld.deep_dark` 2; the single-file events are `music.credits`, `music.dragon`, `music.end` and all 21 `music_disc.*`. A creator who wants one specific piece names a single-file event; one who wants a mood names a set and accepts the draw. Any registered event is legal as music — a drone loop under a biome is a design — and the registry is data, not surface (spec-0039). `minecraft:intentionally_empty` is registered and has no `sounds.json` row; silence is `{}` (§2.2), not an empty event.

### 2.7 Established practice

**Cited**, in full in `docs/reference/game-audio.md`: music that answers the state of the world rather than the player's input is the *adaptive* class (Collins, *Game Sound*); the standard structure is a set of **music states**, one per area or situation, joined by composed **transitions** and punctuated by **stingers** (Sweet, *Writing Interactive Music for Video Games*, ch. 8 and 10; the Wwise interactive-music model of switch containers, states, transitions and stingers); **silence is a state** chosen for weight — the *Dark Souls* line scores bosses, hubs and endings and leaves exploration to its ambience, and treats a hidden room's music as a reward (Canela; Freitas). Vanilla's own constants (§2.1) are the same practice: owned scenes replace with no delay, ambient scenes wait and never interrupt, and `pale_garden` is a declared silence.

## 3. The surface

### 3.1 Every atmosphere states its music

**Authored.** `world.atmospheres[].attributes` **must carry `audio/background_music`** — `{}` for silence, or `{default: {sound, min_delay, max_delay, replace_current_music?}, underwater?: {…}}` for a track (`underwater` is heard only where a camera can be submerged; a flooded place is a design). An atmosphere without it is `DW1031`: absent, the dimension's music would play, and a scene whose music nobody chose is the defect (§1). A `creative` entry is `DW1031`: no delve body is in creative mode, so it is a declaration nothing can hear (CLAUDE.md, *vacuity: unemitted* — **cited**). `audio/music_volume` stays optional in `[0, 1]`; its range is `DW0928`'s. There is no default and no engine-invented value: silence is spelled.

What the declaration means for a track already playing when the camera enters the atmosphere (§2.1), as the ledger states it per atmosphere (§5.1):

| Declared | A playing track… | Then |
|---|---|---|
| track, `replace_current_music: true` | is cut within `min_delay / 2` ticks (a few ticks at `min_delay: 0`) | the declared track starts; with `min_delay = max_delay = 0` it loops back to back |
| track, `replace_current_music: false` | plays to its end | the declared track starts after `nextInt(min_delay, max_delay)` ticks, shortened by the client's frequency option |
| `{}`, `music_volume: 0.0` | fades out over ≈ 15 s, then stops | nothing starts |
| `{}`, `music_volume` above 0 or absent | plays to its end at that gain | nothing starts |

The hard cut into silence is §3.4's verb; the engine never infers one.

### 3.2 The ground states its music

**Authored.** The ground biome is the one biome a camera can stand in that no atmosphere declares, so `world.horizon` gains **`attributes`**, the same attribute map an atmosphere carries (one type, one reader), **admitting the `audio/` ids only** — the ground's look stays the horizon's (spec-0080 §2.5, `compiler.md` *The ground biome*); a `visual/` or `gameplay/` id here is `DW1031`. `audio/background_music` is required within it, so **`horizon` becomes a required field in its object form** and the string shorthand (`"void"`, `"ocean"`) is removed: a base with a required statement to make has something to say. The engine writes the declared attributes into the ground biome it already emits — `<ns>:void` as today for `void` and `valley`; for `ocean`, the pinned `minecraft:ocean` definition field for field under the delve's own id `<ns>:ocean`, named in the generator settings, so no vanilla registry entry is overridden (the shape of `void_biome_definition`: *the pinned jar field for field, except* — **cited**). An audio id the horizon does not name keeps the pinned definition's value, which for both bases is none.

**The valley's bands are a named leak.** A valley's surround keeps the generator's biomes (`cherry_grove`, `windswept_forest`; spec-0080 non-goal): `cherry_grove` declares `music.overworld.cherry_grove` and `windswept_forest` inherits the dimension's `music.game`. A body that crosses the boundary onto the gap floor hears them until the boundary returns it. The ledger lists the bands with their vanilla music (§5.1), and the binding line counts them as biomes a camera can stand in that the campaign does not declare.

### 3.3 A story stage moves the music

**Authored, on what exists.** `set-atmosphere` (spec-0080 §3.3) is the only mover: a stage that changes a place's music repaints the place (or a region) with an atmosphere that declares the new state, in the same effect list as the beat that earns it. Restoring is the same verb naming the place's own atmosphere. No `set-music` verb is added: a second mover of the same attribute would be two authorities for one fact (CLAUDE.md, *a general mechanism privately re-implemented inside one verb* — **cited**), and the biome map, the fog proofs and the bot already read the one. A stage that wants music the place's atmosphere does not have declares a second atmosphere; two atmospheres may differ in music alone.

### 3.4 `stop-sound`, and a sound's category

**Authored.** Stage 5 gains one effect verb, the counterpart of `play-sound`:

```json
{ "type": "stop-sound", "category": "music" }
{ "type": "stop-sound", "category": "ambient", "sound": "minecraft:ambient.cave" }
{ "type": "stop-sound", "sound": "minecraft:entity.warden.heartbeat" }
```

- **`category`** — optional, one of the ten vanilla categories as the command tree spells them (`master`, `music`, `record`, `weather`, `block`, `hostile`, `neutral`, `player`, `ambient`, `voice`, `ui`), one `SoundCategory` enum; a word outside it is `DW0100`.
- **`sound`** — optional, a registered sound event through the one rule `DW0326` reads.
- Neither: every sound the audience hears stops (`stopsound <who>`), vanilla's own meaning.
- It addresses players (`Verb::addresses_players` is true) and takes the `audience` envelope like `play-sound`; a `dw_cutscene` watcher **is** addressed — a cut is not harm, and the watcher is exactly who hears the scene.

**`category` on every played sound.** `play-sound` and `pulses[]` gain `category`, the same `SoundCategory`, default `master` — vanilla's own default for `playsound`, so a document that never writes it emits what it did. A stinger declared in `music` is governed by the player's music slider and the scene's fade and is cut by `stop-sound music`; one in `master` is none of those. The capability belongs to a played sound, so it lands on both sites at once (CLAUDE.md, *a second bespoke field is the defect* — **cited**).

What `stop-sound music` does, stated honestly from §2.3: it cuts the play-out of a track in a scene that declares silence, and it cuts a `music`-category stinger; in a scene that declares a track it is a hiccup of a few ticks. The ledger says per `stop-sound` which it is (§5.1). A cut on *walking in*, not at a beat, is an environment trigger carrying this verb — the trigger is the edge detector the engine already has.

### 3.5 A cutscene hears the place it looks at

**Authored.** A cutscene has no music of its own: its camera stands in a biome, and the biome's declaration plays (§2.1, §2.5). The surface added is a confirmation, never a mover: `cutscene` gains **`hears`**, optional — an `atmosphere/<id>`, or `null` for the ground — the one atmosphere every 4-cell the camera passes through wears in the configuration the forced route reaches the cutscene in (the biome map after each `set-atmosphere` repaint in route order — the reading the fog proof already takes, `compiler.md` *Seamlessness*, **Fog**; a cutscene the forced route never reaches is judged against the first tick's map, and the ledger says so). A camera cell wearing another biome is `DW1032`, naming the shot, the cell and both atmospheres. A `hears` naming an undeclared atmosphere is `DW0112`. Absent, nothing is judged and the ledger still reports what the camera hears (§5.1). The cells judged are the path's waypoints and every 4-cell the straight segments between them cross, for `path` shots and for the dolly a `shot_style` expands to, read from the same resolved camera the rehearsal reads.

A cutscene whose camera flies from one atmosphere into another hears vanilla's transition at the boundary (§3.1's table); a creator who wants one music under a flyover declares it on every place the camera crosses, or repaints the crossed volume for the cutscene's length with the verbs of §3.3 — the engine refuses nothing here and reports everything.

## 4. Emission

**Authored.** Nothing runs per tick. The declared attributes are written into each atmosphere's biome file and the ground's as every attribute is today (`compiler.md`, *Atmosphere biomes*: canonical form, `minecraft:` id). `stop-sound` emits one line under the effect's audience, `stopsound <who> [<category> [<sound>]]`, with `*` written for the category when a `sound` is given without one — the tree's only spelling of that form. `play-sound` and a pulse write their `category` token where `master` stands today. Every line is walked against the pinned command tree as every emitted line is. **Determinism** (ADR-0006 — **cited**): every emission is a function of the document; two builds are byte-identical, asserted by the existing double-build gate. A campaign that declares no `stop-sound` and no `category` emits the same lines for its sounds as before; every campaign's ground biome file gains the declared audio attributes, because every campaign now declares them.

## 5. Proving a scene plays what it declares

### 5.1 The binding line and the ledger

**Authored.** Every build prints `music binding: B biome(s) a camera can stand in — A atmosphere(s) and the ground declared (T with a track, S silent: F fading, O playing out), U band biome(s) undeclared; C cutscene(s), H declare what they hear, G of H confirmed; K stop-sound effect(s), M of K cut a play-out, N a stinger, Q a hiccup; P sound(s) in a category other than master` — every figure computed from the declarations with its denominator, zeroes included. `validation/music.json` carries: per biome, `id`, `kind` (`atmosphere` | `ground` | `band`), `music` (`silence` or the `default`/`underwater` tracks with their delays and replace flag), `music_volume`, `entering` (one word of §3.1's table: `replaces`, `plays-out`, `fades`, `plays-out-at-gain`), and for a `band` the vanilla declaration it carries; per cutscene, its effect path, `hears`, the atmospheres its camera cells wear in the judged configuration with the configuration's repaint list, and the verdict; per `stop-sound`, its path, `category`, `sound`, audience, the biome the forced route stands in when it fires and the word (`cut`, `stinger`, `hiccup`); per `play-sound` and pulse, its `category`. The ledger carries the canonical hash of the stage documents it was computed from (CLAUDE.md, *a record that claims to be a measurement* — **cited**).

### 5.2 PackTest

**Authored.** Music is client-side; the server-side fact is **which biome a camera cell wears**, and spec-0080's generated templates already assert it for carried places and repaints (`atmosphere_places`, `atmosphere_repaint_<n>`). Per cutscene with `hears`, one generated template `music_hears_<n>` re-establishes the judged configuration the way `atmosphere_repaint_<n>` does, then asserts `execute if biome <x> <y> <z> <biome>` at each judged camera cell's 2-mod-4 sample — the cell the biome map says, which the client reads (§2.5). `stopsound` is a client-bound packet no PackTest observes; the command tree walk proves the line, and the bot hears it. What a PackTest cannot observe is said in the template's comment.

### 5.3 The bot

**Authored, on mineflayer 4.37.1.** `harness/src/music.ts` reads `validation/music.json`. For each `stop-sound` the forced route performs, it asserts the clientbound stop-sound packet with the ledger's category and sound arrives within 10 s of the bundle's completion-marker token, through the harness's existing packet reading (the packet's protocol name is read from the pinned protocol data at implementation, never typed from memory). For each listening station of the route (the first route cell inside each declared biome, the shape `pulses.json`'s stations take), it asserts the biome id under its eye is the ledger's, read from the chunk data the server sent and the biome registry the client received at login — a second instrument sharing no configuration with the compiler's biome map. A cutscene's camera cells are not stood in by the bot (it does not watch cutscenes); their proof is §5.2's. What the bot cannot hear is said: no instrument outside a client can observe the music manager, so the fade, the loop and the toast are confirmed on the demo level (§7.4), never asserted.

## 6. Refusals

**Authored**, each raised at the document it is entered in, with the remedy named and a `remedy_reachability.rs` row each.

### 6.1 `DW1031` — a scene that does not state its music (validation tier, `dsl::validate`, at the world document)

- an atmosphere whose `attributes` lack `audio/background_music`;
- `horizon` absent, in string form, or without `attributes.audio/background_music`;
- a `creative` entry in any `audio/background_music`;
- a `horizon.attributes` id outside `audio/`.

The remedy names the field and the two spellings: `{}` for silence, `{default: {sound, min_delay, max_delay, replace_current_music}}` for a track. A sound id is `DW0326`; a value outside its pinned range is `DW0928`; an unknown category or an unknown enum word is `DW0100`.

### 6.2 `DW1032` — a cutscene that hears what it did not declare (build tier, `compiler::music`)

- a cutscene with `hears` whose camera passes a 4-cell wearing another biome in the judged configuration. The message names the shot, the cell, the biome it wears and the one declared; the remedies are the path, the place's atmosphere, or a repaint before the beat.

No advisory is added: a cutscene without `hears` has declared nothing, and the ledger reports what it hears without judging it (CLAUDE.md, *a check never infers an undeclared intent* — **cited**).

## 7. The gallery, and the demo level

## 8. Decisions for the owner

Only what is heard.

1. **Music is a property of the place, and the story moves it by repainting the place.** What is heard: the music of a hall changes on the beat that changes the hall, for a walker and for a cutscene camera alike, and nothing else can change it. The alternative — a `set-music` verb — hears the same and makes two authorities for one fact.
2. **Every place and the ground say their music, silence included.** What is heard: vanilla's calm overworld music never plays in a delve unless a scene names it. The cost: every campaign's `world.json` writes `horizon` in its object form with a music statement, and every atmosphere writes one; the string `"void"` is gone.
3. **Silence fades; a cut is a verb.** What is heard: a track crossing into a silent place fades over about fifteen seconds (vanilla's curve, not the engine's); a sudden hush is a `stop-sound music` at the beat or at a trigger on the threshold. The alternative — an engine-invented edge detector per silent place — is a per-tick cost for a cut the trigger already expresses.
4. **A cutscene hears the place it looks at, and can say so.** What is heard: a cutscene over the ruined town plays the ruin's music because the town is ruined, not because the cutscene asked; `hears` makes the build refuse a camera that wanders into another music. The alternative — music on the cutscene — would need a positioned sound that attenuates as the camera moves (§2.4).
5. **Vanilla's tracks only, for now; a named event may be a set.** What is heard: `music.overworld.forest` is one of twenty-one pieces each time; a `music_disc.*` event is one piece. A track of the campaign's own is a later spec under the licence rules.
