## 1. The defect, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (the tree at `a845fa114`): music reaches a delve by one channel, an atmosphere's `audio/background_music` and `audio/music_volume` attributes (spec-0080), carried by a place from the first tick or painted by `set-atmosphere`. Both are optional. The ground biome — `<ns>:void`, or vanilla's `minecraft:ocean` under an ocean horizon — declares neither (`void_biome_definition` sets `visual/sky_color` alone). There is no verb that stops a sound. `play-sound` writes every `playsound` in the `master` category, so a `music.*` event it plays is neither governed by the player's music slider nor known to the client's music manager, which starts its own track over it.

**Cited** (`data/minecraft/dimension_type/overworld.json`, pinned server jar): the overworld dimension type carries `audio/background_music: {default: music.game 12000..24000, creative: music.creative 12000..24000}`. The attribute's own default is `BackgroundMusic.EMPTY` (`EnvironmentAttributes.<clinit>`), so **a biome that declares no music inherits the dimension's**, and the dimension's is vanilla's calm overworld music. That is the track that played over the destroyed town: nobody chose it, and nothing could refuse it.

**The object music belongs to is the biome under the camera** (§2.1: `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic` reads `audio/background_music` from the camera's attribute probe and nothing else in a loaded world). The engine's name for that biome is the **atmosphere** (spec-0080), and a scene — a place, a quest stage, a cutscene — is already expressed in it: a place carries one; a story stage repaints a volume with another; a cutscene's camera stands in one. So this spec adds no music object. It makes the statement **obligatory** at every biome a camera can stand in (every atmosphere and the ground), gives the cut its verb, lets a cutscene declare what it hears so the build can confirm it, lets a cutscene score itself by the same primitive the story uses (a repaint, §3.6), and proves the declarations.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 How the pinned client chooses, starts, replaces, fades and stops music

**Cited — `MusicManager.tick / startPlaying / stopPlaying / fadePlaying / canReplace`, `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic / getMusicVolume`, `MusicManager$MusicFrequency.getNextSongDelay`, `BackgroundMusic.select`, `Musics.<clinit>`, `Mth.nextInt`, bytecode.**

| Fact | Reading |
|---|---|
| Where music is read | `getSituationalMusic`: a screen with its own music (only `WinScreen` — the credits) wins; otherwise, with a player and a camera, the END dimension's boss overlay yields `END_BOSS`; otherwise **`gameRenderer.getMainCamera().attributeProbe().getValue(BACKGROUND_MUSIC, 1.0)`** — the camera's 4-cell, not the body's — then `BackgroundMusic.select(creative, underwater)`. `creative` is `abilities.instabuild && abilities.mayfly`: creative mode only, never adventure or spectator. `underwater` is `player.isUnderWater()`. `select` returns `underwater` if present and submerged, else `creative` if present and creative, else `default`; an empty `default` yields **null**. With no player: `MENU`. |
| What null does | `tick`: music null → `nextSongDelay = max(nextSongDelay, 100)`, return. **Nothing starts; a track already playing is not stopped** — it plays to its end. |
| Replacement | with a current track and a new `Music`: `canReplace` = `music.replaceCurrentMusic() && music.sound ≠ current.location` → `soundManager.stop(current)`, `nextSongDelay = nextInt(0, music.minDelay / 2)`. Then, once the current instance is no longer active, `currentMusic = null` and `nextSongDelay = min(nextSongDelay, frequency.getNextSongDelay(music))`; the next track starts when the countdown reaches 0. With `replace_current_music: false` the current track **plays to its end** and the new one follows after the delay. |
| The delay between tracks | `getNextSongDelay(music)`: `CONSTANT` → 100 ticks; else `nextInt(min(minDelay, cap), min(maxDelay, cap))` with `cap` = 20 min (`DEFAULT`, 24000 ticks) or 10 min (`FREQUENT`, 12000). `Mth.nextInt(r, a, b)` = `a` when `a ≥ b`. So `min_delay = max_delay = 0` **loops the track back to back** under `DEFAULT` and `FREQUENT`, and with a 5 s gap under `CONSTANT`; the player's option can only shorten a declared gap, never lengthen it. |
| Volume and fade | `getMusicVolume`: the camera probe's `MUSIC_VOLUME` (float, default 1.0, range `[0, 1]`, not spatially interpolated — the camera's own 4-cell, lerped only across one tick). `tick` runs `fadePlaying(target)` first whenever a track plays and the gain differs: **down**, `gain ← 0.03·target + 0.97·gain` per tick, so a cut to 0 reaches the `1e-4` floor in 303 ticks (≈ 15 s) and then `stopPlaying()`; **up**, `gain += clamp(gain, 5e-4, 0.005)` per tick, 0 → 1 in ≈ 203 ticks (≈ 10 s). The gain is applied through `SoundEngine.updateCategoryVolume(MUSIC, gain)`, a multiplier on **every** instance in the `music` category, so it also governs a `playsound … music`. The fade runs beside the rest of the tick: a replacement can cut a fading track, and only the tick on which the fade stops the track returns early. |
| The toast | `startPlaying` shows the client's now-playing toast when the play result says so; the toast names vanilla's track and artist, under the player's own *Show Music Toast* option. A creator who names a vanilla track names the one the toast will show. |

**Vanilla's own constants** (`Musics.<clinit>`): `MENU` (20, 600, replace), `CREATIVE` (12000, 24000, no replace), `CREDITS` (0, 0, replace), `END_BOSS` (0, 0, replace), `END` (6000, 24000, replace), `GAME` and `UNDER_WATER` (12000, 24000, no replace). A scene vanilla wants to own its music — the boss, the credits — declares `replace_current_music: true` with no delay; ambient game music waits ten to twenty minutes and never interrupts.

### 2.2 What a biome declares

**Cited** — the vendored attribute registry and `EnvironmentAttributes.<clinit>`: `audio/background_music` has shape `{default?, creative?, underwater?}`, each a `music` `{sound, min_delay ≥ 0, max_delay ≥ 0, replace_current_music?}` (`replace_current_music` defaults false in the codec); `audio/music_volume` is a float in `[0, 1]`. Neither is `spatiallyInterpolated` — spec-0080 §2.2's reading stands: the `audio/` attributes are read from the camera's own 4-cell with no blend (§2.9 names the exact cell). **`{}` silences**: `pale_garden.json` declares `background_music: {}` and `music_volume: 0.0`, so a track crossing into it fades out and nothing starts. **An event with no files silences too**: `warped_forest` names `music.nether.warped_forest`, whose `sounds.json` row has zero files; `{}` is the explicit form and the one this spec uses.

### 2.3 `stopsound`

**Cited — the pinned command tree, `StopSoundCommand.register / stopSound`, `ClientboundStopSoundPacket`, `ClientPacketListener.handleStopSoundEvent`, `SoundEngine.stop(Identifier, SoundSource)`.** `stopsound <targets> [<source> [<sound>]]`, `<source>` one of the eleven category literals (`master`, `music`, `record`, `weather`, `block`, `hostile`, `neutral`, `player`, `ambient`, `voice`, `ui`) or `*` for any category with a named sound. The server sends one packet per target carrying the optional name and category and reports success per form; the client calls `SoundEngine.stop(name, source)`: with a category, every playing instance in that category (or only those with that name); with a name alone, every instance of that name in any category; with neither, `stopAll`. **It stops instances; it does not touch the music manager's state.** On the next tick the manager finds its track inactive and, where the camera's biome declares a track, re-arms the countdown by §2.1: a looping track restarts within a few ticks, a spaced one after its declared delay. **Where the camera's biome declares silence, nothing restarts.** So the command is the hard cut of a play-out — and of a stinger — and nothing else.

### 2.4 A positioned sound in the `music` category

**Cited** — spec-0102 §2.2–2.3 (`PlaySoundCommand`, `SoundEngine.play`, `Channel.linearAttenuation`) and `SoundEngine.calculateVolume(volume, source) = clamp(volume) · clamp(options[source]) · categoryGain[source]`. A `playsound <music.*> music …` is a positioned instance with linear attenuation to `16 · max(V, 1)` blocks, governed by the player's music slider and by the music fade, stopped by `stopsound … music`, and **never** the manager's `currentMusic` — the manager will start its own track over it unless the camera's biome declares silence. It is a stinger's shape, not a scene's music.

### 2.5 Which biome the camera is in, and when the client learns it

**Cited** — spec-0080 §2.1–2.2, `compiler.md` *The biome map*, and §2.8 below: the server holds the biome per 4-cell; `fillbiome` reaches a connected client through `chunk_biomes` with no chunk reload; a block whose coordinates are all 2 mod 4 always reads its own cell. A cutscene puts the watcher in spectator at the camera (`compiler.md`, *A cutscene is pure observation*), so **the camera's path, not the body's cell, decides a cutscene's music** — and §2.9 shows the body is at the camera anyway.

### 2.6 The track vocabulary

**Cited** — the vendored registry (1838 ids, read by `DW0326`) and the pinned client's `sounds.json` (1837 rows): 31 `music.*` events and 21 `music_disc.*` events. **An event names a set and the client picks one file at random each start**: `music.game` 27 files, `music.overworld.forest` 21, `music.overworld.frozen_peaks` 9, `music.overworld.deep_dark` 2; the single-file events are `music.credits`, `music.dragon`, `music.end` and all 21 `music_disc.*`. A creator who wants one specific piece names a single-file event; one who wants a mood names a set and accepts the draw. Any registered event is legal as music — a drone loop under a biome is a design — and the registry is data, not surface (spec-0039). `minecraft:intentionally_empty` is registered and has no `sounds.json` row; silence is `{}` (§2.2), not an empty event.

### 2.7 Established practice

**Cited**, in full in `docs/reference/game-audio.md`: music that answers the state of the world rather than the player's input is the *adaptive* class (Collins, *Game Sound*); the standard structure is a set of **music states**, one per area or situation, joined by composed **transitions** and punctuated by **stingers** (Sweet, *Writing Interactive Music for Video Games*, ch. 8 and 10; the Wwise interactive-music model of switch containers, states, transitions and stingers); **silence is a state** chosen for weight — the *Dark Souls* line scores bosses, hubs and endings and leaves exploration to its ambience, and treats a hidden room's music as a reward (Canela; Freitas). Vanilla's own constants (§2.1) are the same practice: owned scenes replace with no delay, ambient scenes wait and never interrupt, and `pale_garden` is a declared silence.

### 2.8 What `fillbiome` does, exactly

**Cited — the pinned server jar, `FillBiomeCommand.{register, quantize, makeResolver, fill}` (mapped `apa`), `GameRules.<clinit>` (`eua`), `ChunkMap.resendBiomesForChunks` (`awj`), `QuartPos.{fromBlock, toBlock}`, bytecode.**

| Fact | Reading |
|---|---|
| The grammar | `fillbiome <from> <to> <biome> [replace <filter>]`; `<biome>` is a `resource` of `minecraft:worldgen/biome`, `<filter>` a `resource_or_tag` of the same registry (a biome id or a `#tag`), as the pinned command tree spells it. |
| The box | both corners are quantized to their 4-cell (`QuartPos.toBlock(QuartPos.fromBlock(c))`, a floor to a multiple of 4) and the `BoundingBox` is taken from the quantized corners; **the volume judged is `xSpan · ySpan · zSpan` of that box in blocks** (so a box from 0 to 7 has span 5, not 8 — `atmosphere::command_volume` computes the same). A volume above the gamerule **`max_block_modifications`** (default **32768**, `GameRules.<clinit>`) is `commands.fillbiome.toobig` and nothing is written. |
| Loaded chunks only | every chunk the box touches is fetched with `getChunk(x, z, FULL, false)` **before any cell is written**; one unloaded chunk fails the whole command (`argument.pos.unloaded`) and writes nothing — a line is all-or-nothing. |
| The cell rule | per 4-cell of each touched chunk (`makeResolver`): the cell's current biome is read; **if the cell's 2-mod-4 block lies in the box and the filter accepts the current biome, the cell takes the new biome and the count rises; otherwise it keeps what it had.** Without `replace` the filter accepts everything. So `fillbiome … X replace Y` moves exactly the cells wearing `Y` to `X` and touches nothing else, and running it where no cell wears `Y` is a successful command that changed nothing (the result is the count, the success is 1 — `execute store success` reads 1, `store result` reads 0). |
| The client | after the fill, `chunkMap.resendBiomesForChunks(touched)` sends every tracking player one `ClientboundChunksBiomesPacket` (`chunk_biomes`) naming the touched chunks; no chunk is resent (spec-0080 §2.1's spike measured the same on a running server: 2 packets naming all 6 of 6 chunks, 0 `map_chunk`, 0 `unload_chunk`). The client's `handleChunksBiomes` replaces each chunk's biomes and marks the surrounding sections dirty. |

### 2.9 Where the spectating camera is, and what persists across a restart

**Cited — the pinned client jar: `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic`, `Camera.{setup, tick, attributeProbe}` (`ger`), `EnvironmentAttributeProbe.tick` (`ced`), `EnvironmentAttributeSystem.getValue` and its biome layer (`cef`), `EnvironmentAttribute.isSpatiallyInterpolated` (`cea`), `BiomeManager.getNoiseBiomeAtPosition(double,double,double)` (`dxq`); the pinned server jar: `ServerPlayer.tick` (`axg`), `PrimaryLevelData` (`fnm`) and `TimerQueue` (`fsz`); bytecode.**

- **The listener is the camera's own 4-cell, unfiddled.** `Camera.setup` and `Camera.tick` both call `attributeProbe.tick(level, position)` with the camera's `position` field. For an attribute that is not spatially interpolated — both `audio/` attributes (§2.2) — the biome layer of `EnvironmentAttributeSystem` reads `BiomeManager.getNoiseBiomeAtPosition(x, y, z)`, which is `getNoiseBiomeAtQuart(QuartPos.fromBlock(Mth.floor(x)), …)`: the cell containing the camera position, with none of the seeded fiddle `BiomeManager.getBiome(BlockPos)` applies. The 2-mod-4 rule of §2.5 is therefore a convenience for a reader of the fiddled path; the music reads the cell the camera is in.
- **The body stands at the camera.** `ServerPlayer.tick`: when `getCamera() ≠ this` and the camera entity is alive, the player is `absSnapTo` the camera's position and rotation and the chunk source is told it moved; when the camera is gone, the camera resets to the player. So while a cutscene plays, every watcher's body is in the camera's cell: the chunks the camera passes are the chunks the watchers load, and `player.isUnderWater()` (§2.1's `underwater` choice) is read where the camera is.
- **A scheduled function survives a stop.** `PrimaryLevelData` writes its `TimerQueue` under `ScheduledEvents` (`Name`, `TriggerTime`, `Callback`) and reads it back, so a `schedule function … 1t` pending at a clean stop fires when the world next ticks — a cutscene's `cs_tick_<bare>` chain resumes and its `cs_end_<bare>` runs. A crash restores whatever the last save holds: `level.dat` (the queue) and `scoreboard.dat` are written by the same save pass, so the queue and the bracket's scores agree with each other; a chunk is also written when it unloads, so a chunk's biomes may be newer than both.

### 2.10 Scoring a cutscene

**Cited.** A cutscene is a fixed-length picture, and music written to a fixed picture is the *linear* class — the distinction between linear and dynamic game music is Collins, *Game Sound*, ch. 1; the transition into and out of an owned scene is composed, not left to chance (Sweet, *Writing Interactive Music for Video Games*, ch. 10, transitions and stingers). Vanilla's own owned scenes are scored exactly so: `CREDITS` and `END_BOSS` replace the current track with no delay (`Musics.<clinit>`, §2.1). **Authored**, on those: a cutscene that declares music declares an owned scene, so its default shape is `replace_current_music: true` with `min_delay` and `max_delay` 0 (the track starts within a tick or two and loops under the whole shot list); the way back out is the place's own declaration (§3.1's table), because the cutscene ends by restoring the place, and a creator who wants the cutscene's track cut dead at the end writes `stop-sound {category: music}` after it in a `sequence` — the engine adds no second mover.

## 3. The surface

### 3.1 Every atmosphere states its music

**Authored.** `world.atmospheres[].attributes` **must carry `audio/background_music`** — `{}` for silence, or `{default: {sound, min_delay, max_delay, replace_current_music?}, underwater?: {…}}` for a track (`underwater` is heard only where a camera can be submerged; a flooded place is a design). An atmosphere without it is `DW1031`: absent, the dimension's music would play, and a scene whose music nobody chose is the defect (§1). A `creative` entry is `DW1031`: no delve body is in creative mode, so it is a declaration nothing can hear (CLAUDE.md, *vacuity: unemitted* — **cited**). `audio/music_volume` stays optional in `[0, 1]`; its range is `DW0928`'s. There is no default and no engine-invented value: silence is spelled.

What the declaration means for a track already playing when the camera enters the atmosphere (§2.1), as the ledger states it per atmosphere (§5.1):

| Declared | A playing track… | Then |
|---|---|---|
| track, `replace_current_music: true` | is cut within `min_delay / 2` ticks (a few ticks at `min_delay: 0`) | the declared track starts; with `min_delay = max_delay = 0` it loops back to back |
| track, `replace_current_music: false` | plays to its end | the declared track starts after `nextInt(min_delay, max_delay)` ticks, shortened by the client's frequency option |
| `{}`, `music_volume: 0.0` | fades out over ≈ 15 s, then stops | nothing starts |
| `{}`, `music_volume` above 0 or absent | plays to its end at that gain | nothing starts |

The hard cut into silence is §3.4's verb; the engine never infers one.

### 3.2 The ground states its music

**Authored.** The ground biome is the one biome a camera can stand in that no atmosphere declares, so `world.horizon` gains **`attributes`**, the same attribute map an atmosphere carries (one type, one reader), **admitting the `audio/` ids only** — the ground's look stays the horizon's (spec-0080 §2.5, `compiler.md` *The ground biome*); a `visual/` or `gameplay/` id here is `DW1031`. `audio/background_music` is required within it, so **`horizon` becomes a required field in its object form** and the string shorthand (`"void"`, `"ocean"`) is removed: a base with a required statement to make has something to say. The engine writes the declared attributes into the ground biome it already emits — `<ns>:void` as today for `void` and `valley`; for `ocean`, the pinned `minecraft:ocean` definition field for field under the delve's own id `<ns>:ocean`, named in the generator settings, so no vanilla registry entry is overridden (the shape of `void_biome_definition`: *the pinned jar field for field, except* — **cited**). An audio id the horizon does not name keeps the pinned definition's value, which for both bases is none.

**The valley's bands are a named leak.** A valley's surround keeps the generator's biomes (spec-0080 non-goal): the flora `horizon` reaches lays `windswept_forest`, which inherits the dimension's `music.game`; the generator's second flora, `cherry_grove` (declaring `music.overworld.cherry_grove`), is not reachable from `horizon` at this tree. A body that crosses the boundary onto the gap floor hears them until the boundary returns it. The ledger lists the bands with their vanilla music (§5.1), and the binding line counts them as biomes a camera can stand in that the campaign does not declare.

### 3.3 A story stage moves the music

**Authored, on what exists.** `set-atmosphere` (spec-0080 §3.3) is the only mover: a stage that changes a place's music repaints the place (or a region) with an atmosphere that declares the new state, in the same effect list as the beat that earns it. Restoring is the same verb naming the place's own atmosphere. No `set-music` verb is added: a second mover of the same attribute would be two authorities for one fact (CLAUDE.md, *a general mechanism privately re-implemented inside one verb* — **cited**), and the biome map, the fog proofs and the ledger already read the one. A stage that wants music the place's atmosphere does not have declares a second atmosphere; two atmospheres may differ in music alone.

### 3.4 `stop-sound`, and a sound's category

**Authored.** Stage 5 gains one effect verb, the counterpart of `play-sound`:

```json
{ "type": "stop-sound", "category": "music" }
{ "type": "stop-sound", "category": "ambient", "sound": "minecraft:ambient.cave" }
{ "type": "stop-sound", "sound": "minecraft:entity.warden.heartbeat" }
```

- **`category`** — optional, one of the eleven vanilla categories as the command tree spells them (`master`, `music`, `record`, `weather`, `block`, `hostile`, `neutral`, `player`, `ambient`, `voice`, `ui`), one `SoundCategory` enum; a word outside it is `DW0100`.
- **`sound`** — optional, a registered sound event through the one rule `DW0326` reads.
- Neither: every sound the audience hears stops (`stopsound <who>`), vanilla's own meaning.
- It addresses players (`Verb::addresses_players` is true) and takes the `audience` envelope like `play-sound`; a `dw_cutscene` watcher **is** addressed — a cut is not harm, and the watcher is exactly who hears the scene.

**`category` on every played sound.** `play-sound` and `pulses[]` gain `category`, the same `SoundCategory`, default `master` — vanilla's own default for `playsound`, so a document that never writes it emits what it did. A stinger declared in `music` is governed by the player's music slider and the scene's fade and is cut by `stop-sound music`; one in `master` is none of those. The capability belongs to a played sound, so it lands on both sites at once (CLAUDE.md, *a second bespoke field is the defect* — **cited**).

What `stop-sound music` does, stated honestly from §2.3: it cuts the play-out of a track in a scene that declares silence, and it cuts a `music`-category stinger; in a scene that declares a track it is a hiccup of a few ticks. The ledger says per `stop-sound` which it is (§5.1). A cut on *walking in*, not at a beat, is an environment trigger carrying this verb — the trigger is the edge detector the engine already has.

### 3.5 A cutscene hears the place it looks at

**Authored.** A cutscene without a declaration of its own has no music of its own: its camera stands in a biome, and the biome's declaration plays (§2.1, §2.5). The surface added here is a confirmation, never a mover: `cutscene` gains **`hears`**, optional — an `atmosphere/<id>`, or `null` for the ground — the one atmosphere every 4-cell the camera passes through wears in the configuration the forced route reaches the cutscene in (the biome map after each `set-atmosphere` repaint in route order — the reading the fog proof already takes, `compiler.md` *Seamlessness*, **Fog**; a cutscene the forced route never reaches is judged against the first tick's map, and the ledger says so). A camera cell wearing another biome is `DW1032`, naming the shot, the cell and both atmospheres. A `hears` naming an undeclared atmosphere is `DW0112`. Absent, nothing is judged and the ledger still reports what the camera hears (§5.1). The cells judged are the path's waypoints and every 4-cell the straight segments between them cross, for `path` shots and for the dolly a `shot_style` expands to, read from the same resolved camera the rehearsal reads. **`hears` is judged over the places themselves**: for a cutscene that also declares `music` (§3.6), the biomes judged are the ones the camera cells wear before the cutscene's own paint — what the camera would hear without its score, and what it hears again when the score is restored.

A cutscene whose camera flies from one atmosphere into another, and declares no music, hears vanilla's transition at the boundary (§3.1's table); a creator who wants one music under the whole flight declares it on the cutscene (§3.6).

### 3.6 A cutscene declares its own music

**Authored.** `cutscene` gains **`music`**, optional: the music statement every atmosphere makes, as an attribute map admitting exactly the two music ids — `audio/background_music` (required inside it; `{}` is a scored silence) and `audio/music_volume` (optional) — read by the one attribute reader `horizon.attributes` and `atmospheres[].attributes` are read by (CLAUDE.md, *one shared parse rule and no private copy per gate* — **cited**). A `music` without `audio/background_music`, with a `creative` entry, or with any id outside the two is `DW1031` (§6.1): a cutscene that declares music is a scene, and the rule for a scene's statement is the one that exists.

```json
{ "type": "cutscene", "shots": [ … ],
  "music": { "audio/background_music": { "default": { "sound": "minecraft:music_disc.otherside", "min_delay": 0, "max_delay": 0, "replace_current_music": true } },
             "audio/music_volume": 1.0 } }
```

**What it means.** While the cutscene plays, the camera hears the declared statement wherever it goes; when the cutscene ends, every place hears what it heard before. The engine does this with the one mover the story has (§3.3): it **temporarily repaints the cells the camera passes through** with derived biomes, and restores them.

- **The cells painted** are every 4-cell within the dolly's chord tolerance (0.25 blocks, the tolerance the cadence is chosen by — `compiler.md`, `cutscene`) of the resolved camera path of every shot — the path's waypoints, the cells the straight segments between them cross, and the neighbouring cell wherever the path runs within the tolerance of a cell face — read from the same resolved camera `hears` and the rehearsal read. The camera reads exactly the cell it is in (§2.9), the client tweens within the tolerance of the path (`compiler.md`, `cutscene`), so these are the cells the camera can be in and no other. A painted cell outside the map's declared extent, or a box whose cells can wear a biome the delve does not own (a valley band, which lies outside the map by construction), is `DW0929` (a paint that reaches cells it may not — the rule spec-0080 §6.2 states for every paint).
- **The variants.** For each base biome a painted cell can wear in any configuration — its first-tick biome and the atmosphere of every `set-atmosphere` whose volume covers it, in any order (every configuration the story can produce, since `set-atmosphere` is the only other mover) — the build emits one **variant**: the base's emitted definition, field for field, with its two music attributes replaced by the declaration's (`audio/background_music` set; `audio/music_volume` set when declared, removed to the pinned default of 1.0 when not — the music statement moves as one object, because a base that declares `music_volume: 0.0` would mute the score). Its id is **`<ns>:music/<digest>/<base path>`**, where `<digest>` is the first eight hex digits of the sha256 of the declaration's canonical JSON and `<base path>` is the base's path under the namespace (`atmosphere/<id>`, `void`, `ocean`): derived, never typed, so two cutscenes with one declaration share their variants and a declaration that changes moves every id. A variant joins the void biome's vanilla tags exactly when its base does, so it differs from its base in music alone. The cutscene's own functions carry the same digest as a last `_m<digest>` suffix on `<bare>`, so two cutscenes with one shot list and different scores never share a bracket; a cutscene without `music` keeps its name byte for byte.
- **The paint** is the `replace` form of §2.8, so a cell takes the variant of exactly the base it wears and a cell wearing anything else is untouched: per merged box and per candidate base, `fillbiome <box> <ns>:music/<digest>/<base path> replace <base id>`. The boxes are the painted cells merged along the camera's order into 4-aligned boxes, each split by the existing `fillbiome_lines` so that no line's quantized volume exceeds `FILLBIOME_CAP` (32768, the default `max_block_modifications`, §2.8 — a cutscene fires mid-play, where raising a gamerule would be a second world-wide write). The lines are one function, `cs_paint_<bare>`, called by the cutscene's start **before** `gamemode spectator @a`, so the first frame already hears the score.
- **The restore** is the same lines reversed, `fillbiome <box> <base id> replace <ns>:music/<digest>/<base path>`, in `cs_unpaint_<bare>`, which `cs_end_<bare>` calls. Because both directions are `replace`, a cell another declaring cutscene painted first is left to that cutscene (its base is not wearing a base), two concurrent scores never fight, and running a restore twice is harmless.
- **Interrupt and restart.** The paint is world state and must not outlive its cutscene whatever ends it. The bracket latches **`#cs_paint_<bare> dw.sys`** to 1 as the first line of `cs_paint_<bare>`; `cs_unpaint_<bare>` sets it 0 and then runs every restore line under `execute store success score #cs_ok dw.sys run …`, setting the latch back to 1 whenever a line reports failure — the one failure a restore line has is an unloaded chunk (§2.8), and a restore that could not reach its cells is not done. The campaign `tick` gains one line, `function <ns>:cs_unpaint_tick`, and `cs_unpaint_tick` carries one clause per declaring cutscene, `execute if score #cs_paint_<bare> dw.sys matches 1 unless score #run_<bare> dw.sys matches 1 run function <ns>:cs_unpaint_<bare>` (the shape of `cs_repair`: keyed on the stuck state itself, painted while not running, and silent otherwise), so a restore that found a chunk unloaded is retried each tick until a body comes back and loads it. The `load` function — run on every world load (`#minecraft:load`) — sets `#cs_paint_<bare>` to 1 for every declaring cutscene whose `#run_<bare>` is not 1, so a world whose chunks were saved painted while its scores were saved from before the cutscene (§2.9's crash case) is restored the first tick its chunks are loaded; a cutscene whose schedule survived a clean stop keeps its paint, resumes, and restores itself at its own end. The restore is state-free apart from the latch: it reads the world (`replace`), never a stored list of what was painted.
- **Who else hears it.** Music is a fact about a cell (§2.1). Every body online when the cutscene starts is a watcher, and every watcher is at the camera (§2.9). A body that joins during the cutscene stands where it joins; if that cell is painted it hears the score until the restore, like any other place. The ledger states, per declaring cutscene, the places whose cells the paint reaches (§5.1); the engine refuses none of it.
- **The way in and out.** In: the declaration's own `replace_current_music` and delays (§3.1's table, read at the first painted cell); the recommended shape is §2.10's, true with no delay. Out: the restore returns each cell to its base, and what the score does then is the base's declaration by §3.1's table — a replacing base cuts it, a silent base at `music_volume: 0.0` fades it over ≈ 15 s, any other base lets it play out. A creator who wants it cut dead writes `stop-sound {category: music}` after the cutscene in a `sequence`; the engine emits no `stopsound` of its own at the end.
- **A cutscene with `hears` and `music`** is judged by §3.5 over the bases; the two surfaces answer different questions (where the camera is; what plays there) and each is reported in the ledger.

## 4. Emission

**Authored.** Nothing runs per tick for a campaign that declares no cutscene music; one tick clause per declaring cutscene for one that does (§3.6). The declared attributes are written into each atmosphere's biome file and the ground's as every attribute is today (`compiler.md`, *Atmosphere biomes*: canonical form, `minecraft:` id). `stop-sound` emits one line under the effect's audience, `stopsound <who> [<category> [<sound>]]`, with `*` written for the category when a `sound` is given without one — the tree's only spelling of that form. `play-sound` and a pulse write their `category` token where `master` stands today. A declaring cutscene emits its variant biome files under `datapack/data/<ns>/worldgen/biome/music/<digest>/<base path>.json`, its `cs_paint_<bare>` and `cs_unpaint_<bare>` functions, the call to the first in `cs_<bare>` before `gamemode spectator @a`, the call to the second in `cs_end_<bare>` after the players are returned, its `tick` clause, and its `load` mark line. Every line is walked against the pinned command tree as every emitted line is. **Determinism** (ADR-0006 — **cited**): every emission is a function of the document; two builds are byte-identical, asserted by the existing double-build gate; the digest is of canonical JSON, so `0` and `0.0` are one declaration. A campaign that declares no `stop-sound`, no `category` and no cutscene `music` emits the same lines for its sounds and cutscenes as before; every campaign's ground biome file gains the declared audio attributes, because every campaign now declares them.

## 5. Proving a scene plays what it declares

### 5.1 The binding line and the ledger

**Authored.** Every build prints `music binding: B biome(s) a camera can stand in — A atmosphere(s) and the ground declared (T with a track, S silent: F fading, O playing out), U band biome(s) undeclared; C cutscene(s), H declare what they hear, G of H confirmed, D declare their music over E painted 4-cell(s) in L paint line(s) and V variant biome(s); K stop-sound effect(s), M of K cut a play-out, N a stinger, Q a hiccup; P sound(s) in a category other than master` — every figure computed from the declarations with its denominator, zeroes included. `validation/music.json` carries: per biome, `id`, `kind` (`atmosphere` | `ground` | `band` | `variant`), `music` (`silence` or the `default`/`underwater` tracks with their delays and replace flag), `music_volume`, `entering` (one word of §3.1's table: `replaces`, `plays-out`, `fades`, `plays-out-at-gain`), for a `band` the vanilla declaration it carries, and for a `variant` its base and the cutscene that derives it; per cutscene, its effect path, `hears`, the atmospheres its camera cells wear in the judged configuration with the configuration's repaint list, the verdict, and — when it declares `music` — the declaration, the painted cells, the paint boxes with their line count, the candidate bases per box, the places the paint reaches, and the word for the way out at each base (§3.6); per `stop-sound`, its path, `category`, `sound`, audience, the biome the forced route stands in when it fires and the word (`cut`, `stinger`, `hiccup`); per `play-sound` and pulse, its `category`. The ledger carries the canonical hash of the stage documents it was computed from (CLAUDE.md, *a record that claims to be a measurement* — **cited**). It is the build's statement of what each scene plays, for the creator and for the tests of §5.2; nothing at runtime reads it (§5.4).

### 5.2 Rust tests — the engine's compile-time behaviour

**Authored**, under the ruling carried (§5.4): everything the compiler decides is asserted where it is decided, by a test in `crates/delvec/`:

- the music statement reader (one reader for `atmospheres[].attributes`, `horizon.attributes` and `cutscene.music`), its refusals, and the `entering` word per shape of §3.1's table;
- the ground's emitted definition for each base (criterion 3);
- `stop-sound`'s three emitted shapes and the `category` token (criterion 4);
- the cutscene's judged cells (`polyline_quarts`, the tolerance widening) and `DW1032` over the configuration the route reaches it in (criterion 5);
- the variants' derivation (field for field, the two music ids swapped, the id by digest, sharing between cutscenes with one declaration);
- the paint and restore lines: the `replace` form, the base set per box, every line's quantized volume at most `FILLBIOME_CAP`, the restore being the paint reversed; the bracket's call sites; the tick clause; the `load` mark; byte-identity for a campaign that declares no cutscene music;
- `BiomeMap::at` over a place's painted box, a repaint's box and the ground — the one model every judgement above reads;
- the binding line and the ledger's rows and hash (criterion 8).

### 5.3 PackTest — what the content decides, on a running server

**Authored.** Music is client-side; the server-side fact is **which biome a cell wears**, and spec-0080's templates assert it for every carried place and every repaint (`atmosphere_places`, `atmosphere_repaint_<n>`). This spec adds one family, **`cutscene_music_<n>`**, one per cutscene that declares `music` (`<n>` its index among the build's cutscenes), because a temporary paint's lifetime is runtime behaviour of the emitted functions and the cells and bases are the content's: the template runs `setup`, re-establishes the first tick the way the atmosphere templates do (the ground id under every paint box, then the bootstrap), runs the judged configuration's repaints, and then, at every painted cell's 2-mod-4 sample, asserts the base the configuration says; runs the real `cs_paint_<bare>` and asserts the variant of that base at every cell; runs the real `cs_unpaint_<bare>` and asserts the base again and `#cs_paint_<bare>` at 0 — **every painted cell holds its original biome after the end**. Then the interrupt path: `cs_paint_<bare>` again, `#run_<bare>` cleared, the one function the tick's clauses delegate to (`cs_unpaint_tick`) run once, the bases and the latch asserted. Then the restart path: `cs_paint_<bare>` again with its latch set to 0 (chunks saved painted, scores saved from before the cutscene), `#run_<bare>` cleared, the real `load` run, the latch asserted at 1, `cs_unpaint_tick` run, the bases and the latch at 0 asserted. The suite owes one per declaring cutscene, a template-kind `DW0811` claim (`cutscene-music`). What a PackTest cannot observe — the client's music — is said in the template's comment. The `music_hears_<n>` family is withdrawn (§5.4).

## 6. Refusals

**Authored**, each raised at the document it is entered in, with the remedy named and a `remedy_reachability.rs` row each.

### 6.1 `DW1031` — a scene that does not state its music (validation tier, `dsl::validate`, at the world document; at the quests document for a cutscene)

- an atmosphere whose `attributes` lack `audio/background_music`;
- `horizon` absent, in string form, or without `attributes.audio/background_music`;
- a `creative` entry in any `audio/background_music`, a cutscene's included;
- a `horizon.attributes` id outside `audio/`;
- a `cutscene.music` without `audio/background_music`, or with an id outside `audio/background_music` and `audio/music_volume`.

The remedy names the field and the two spellings: `{}` for silence, `{default: {sound, min_delay, max_delay, replace_current_music}}` for a track. A sound id is `DW0326`; a value outside its pinned range is `DW0928`; an unknown category or an unknown enum word is `DW0100`; a painted cutscene cell outside the map's extent is `DW0929`.

### 6.2 `DW1032` — a cutscene that hears what it did not declare (build tier, `compiler::music`)

- a cutscene with `hears` whose camera passes a 4-cell wearing another biome in the judged configuration, before the cutscene's own paint when it declares `music`. The message names the shot, the cell, the biome it wears and the one declared; the remedies are the path, the place's atmosphere, or a repaint before the beat.

No advisory is added: a cutscene without `hears` has declared nothing, and the ledger reports what it hears without judging it (CLAUDE.md, *a check never infers an undeclared intent* — **cited**).

The yard plays a soft spaced track; the chapel door is a threshold into declared silence, so the yard's track fades as the party steps in; the bell at the altar is a story stage that repaints the yard with a dread track that replaces, and the party hears the cut when they walk back out; the ending cutscene looks over the yard, declares it hears the dread, **and declares its own music — a lament (a single-file disc) that replaces the dread for the length of the shot — so the party, returned to the yard when the shot ends, hears the dread take the yard back**; a trigger at the door carries `stop-sound music` so the dread never plays out into the chapel. The level is the first listen to the fade's length, to the loop's seam, to the replace cut, to the score's way in and out, and to what the toast shows.

## 8. Decisions for the owner

Only what is heard.

1. **Music is a property of the place, and the story moves it by repainting the place.** What is heard: the music of a hall changes on the beat that changes the hall, for a walker and for a cutscene camera alike, and nothing else can change it. The alternative — a `set-music` verb — hears the same and makes two authorities for one fact.
2. **Every place and the ground say their music, silence included.** What is heard: vanilla's calm overworld music never plays in a delve unless a scene names it. The cost: every campaign's `world.json` writes `horizon` in its object form with a music statement, and every atmosphere writes one; the string `"void"` is gone.
3. **Silence fades; a cut is a verb.** What is heard: a track crossing into a silent place fades over about fifteen seconds (vanilla's curve, not the engine's); a sudden hush is a `stop-sound music` at the beat or at a trigger on the threshold. The alternative — an engine-invented edge detector per silent place — is a per-tick cost for a cut the trigger already expresses.
4. **A cutscene hears the place it looks at, and can say so.** What is heard: a cutscene over the ruined town plays the ruin's music because the town is ruined, not because the cutscene asked; `hears` makes the build refuse a camera that wanders into another music.
5. **Vanilla's tracks only, for now; a named event may be a set.** What is heard: `music.overworld.forest` is one of twenty-one pieces each time; a `music_disc.*` event is one piece. A track of the campaign's own is a later spec under the licence rules.
6. **A cutscene may declare its own music, played by a repaint of where its camera goes** (a ruling carried, §3.6). What is heard: the score starts within a tick or two of the first frame, holds under the whole flight whatever places the camera crosses, and when the shot ends each place sounds as it did — the way out is the place's own (a cut, a fade, or a play-out by §3.1's table). The cost: one biome file per (declaration, base) pair and a handful of `fillbiome` lines per cutscene; a body that joins mid-cutscene and stands in a painted cell hears the score until the restore.
7. **A check guards only what our toolchain owns** (a ruling carried, §5.4). What is heard: nothing — the removed checks observed vanilla on a live client. What changes: the bot no longer watches repaint packets, station biomes or stop-sound packets, and no PackTest re-proves `fillbiome`'s cell rule; each removal is a stated loosening, and the facts they re-proved are recorded once from the jar.
