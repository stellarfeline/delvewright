- **What it is for**: a quest item or a piece of loot is not confined to a chest. The owner's direction is that an item sits where the scene would hold it — a sword on a stand, a map in a frame, a potion in a brewing stand — and that every holder is takeable by a player in adventure mode, with no block breaking. spec-0105 built the holder class as the container family. This spec adds the two entity families the pinned game lets an adventure-mode player take from without a hack: the **item frame** (and its glow variant), whose item pops out on a hit while the frame stays, and the **armour stand**, whose slot swaps into the player's hand on a click while the stand itself cannot be broken. After this spec a piece carries the frame or stand as furniture, `loot[]` and `collect` fill it as they fill a container, the census counts it, the build proves a body can stand where it can be taken from, and the bot takes it.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's research record. Each rule is marked **cited** (a reading of the pinned bytes, a reading of the tree, a constitution rule, a named source) or **authored** (this spec chooses). Nothing below was run on a server; what the live tiers must observe is named in §5.
- **Numbers**: no ADR. **One spec** for spec-0105's F1 and F2, this one — §1 says why. **Four DW codes**, handed for this spec: **`DW1035`** (a fill names a slot the holder class does not have, or a stack a slot cannot wear, §6.1, validation tier), **`DW1036`** (a piece's holder carries what the engine owns, §6.2, admission), **`DW1037`** (a holder the party cannot take from, §6.3, build tier), **`DW1038`** (a runtime edit reaches a holder, §6.4, build tier). A fill whose anchor cell holds no holder, or more stacks than the holder's slots, stays `DW0431` / `DW0438`; an entity kind a piece may not carry stays `DW0730`; two fills of one holder stay `DW0435`. The DSL surface changes (§3.3, §3.4), so the implementation takes the **`dsl_version` minor 0.49** (verified free on every remote ref: the `crates/dsl/Cargo.toml` versions held across `refs/remotes/origin` run 0.0–0.48 with no 0.49; the patch number lives only in `crates/dsl/Cargo.toml`). The harness contract moves to **`critical-path.json` format 5** (§5.4). The grammar document version a `mark`'s `holder` is fenced at (§3.2) is **not handed here**: the planner allocates it across every remote ref when the implementation lands, and `version.rs` names it `HOLDER_SINCE`.
- **Non-goals**: the chiseled bookshelf, the 1.21.11 shelf and the jukebox (spec-0105 F3) and the decorated pot (F4) — each keeps its own spec; a holder placed at runtime by a quest verb (furniture is the piece's, as spec-0021 ruled for containers, and §6.4 refuses an edit that reaches one); a holder as a device — a `use` or `strike` trigger or an `interact` objective on a frame or stand (the frame's rotate-on-right-click and the stand's swap-on-click are what those verbs would collide with; the crosshair contest `DW0489` already refuses a hitbox over a body, and a device family for holders is a spec of its own); an actor whose body is an armour stand (a scripted body with `equipment`, drop chance zero, outside this class); a frame whose item is not takeable (the owner's direction has no such thing; an empty frame is scenery, §4.4); drawing holders in a render (the pinned core draws no entity the engine summons, spec-0089 §2.2; §5.1 counts what a frame omits); a hover prompt or highlight on a holder (the pinned client draws none; `docs/reference/game-writing.md` §8 L1).

## 1. The defect, and the object it belongs to

**Cited** (the tree at `adc9a8d14`): `loot[]` fills a container by anchor, positionally into `container.<n>`, and `collect` adopts one through `container` or has the compiler place a bare chest at `anchor` (`emit/objective.rs`, `setblock … minecraft:chest`); `LootItem` has `item`, `count`, `name`, `enchantments` and no slot. No DSL surface places an item frame, a glow item frame or an armour stand. A structure template's `entities` list is decoded by `nbtread::Root` and read by nothing (`#[allow(dead_code)]`); every generator writes `entities: Vec::new()`, the gallery generator's `struct Entity {}` saying why — *every body is summoned by the datapack*; the admission audit judges the palette and the block-entity NBT and never the entities; `as_placed` carries a template's entities through to `/place template` unaudited. The admission allowlist is a block list; the engine has no entity allowlist. An `actor` may be an `armor_stand` with `equipment`, every undeclared slot at drop chance `0.0f`, as a scripted body. The harness takes a `collect`'s item by `openContainer` + `withdraw`, or off the ground when `dropped_by` is set; no step attacks or clicks an entity to take from it. The view writes a world of blocks and draws no entity.

**The object is the item holder, as a class** (spec-0105 §10, **cited**). A container, a frame and a stand are three families of one thing: something with named slots the engine fills at build time and a player empties by one act. `loot[]` and `collect` already speak to the class through an anchor whose cell holds the holder; the census of §4.4 already ranges over it; the only questions the entity families add are *who places the body*, *what the engine must set on it so the take is the only act that works*, and *how the build proves a body can reach it*. Those answers are the same for a frame and a stand — one piece surface, one owned-NBT emission, one summon-and-fill, one tag scheme, one stance proof, one harness contract — and the two families differ only in the kind row of one table (§4.1). Two specs would be two authorities for that machinery, so F1 and F2 are this one spec, and the kind table is where a third entity family would enter.

## 2. Research record

### 2.1 What the pinned game does with a frame and a stand

**Cited — bytecode**, the jar, mappings and `javap` named in **Ground**. Every statement in this section was read at the method it names; what spec-0105 §10.1 claimed is marked *holds* or corrected.

**The attack reaches a block-attached entity through the packet handler, and nothing in it asks the game mode.** `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.handleInteract` refuses a target whose block position is outside the world border, then refuses one the packet's `isWithinRange(player, box, 3.0)` places beyond the player's entity interaction range plus a 3.0 buffer, then builds the anonymous handler whose `onAttack` refuses only an `ItemEntity`, an `ExperienceOrb`, the player themself and an `AbstractArrow` that is not `isAttackable()` (*holds*), and otherwise calls `ServerPlayer.attack(target)`. `Player.attack` asks `target.skipAttackInteraction(player)` before any damage is computed; `BlockAttachedEntity.skipAttackInteraction` answers, for a player, `true` when `Level.mayInteract(player, pos)` is false and otherwise **`hurtOrSimulate(damageSources().playerAttack(player), 0.0f)`** — the hit on a frame is resolved inside that call. `ServerLevel.mayInteract(entity, pos)` is, for a player, *not under spawn protection and inside the world border* (*holds*); the engine writes `spawn-protection=0` (`emit/server.rs`), so inside the border every frame can be hit.

**The frame's hit, slot by slot of `ItemFrame.hurtServer(level, source, amount)`**:

1. `fixed` → the frame reacts only to `canHurtWhenFixed(source)`: a source in `#minecraft:bypasses_invulnerability` or a creative player. **A `Fixed` frame ignores an adventure-mode hit entirely**: no pop, no break.
2. `isInvulnerableToBase(source)` → `false`, nothing happens. **An `Invulnerable` frame ignores the hit.**
3. `shouldDamageDropItem(source)` — `!source.is(#minecraft:is_explosion) && !getItem().isEmpty()` — → `dropItem(level, source.getEntity(), false)`, a `BLOCK_CHANGE` game event, `getRemoveItemSound()`, return `true`. **The item pops and the frame stays** (*holds*).
4. otherwise `BlockAttachedEntity.hurtServer`: `isInvulnerableToBase` → `false`; `!mob_griefing && source entity is a Mob` → `false` (the engine writes `gamerule mob_griefing false`, `emit/world.rs`); else `kill`, `markHurt`, `dropItem(level, entity)`. **An empty frame breaks on a hit and drops its frame item, in adventure mode too** — spec-0105 did not say this, and it is the fact §4.3 answers.

`ItemFrame.dropItem(level, entity, dropSelf)`: `fixed` → return; `setItem(EMPTY)`; **`!entity_drops` → the stack is deleted, not dropped** (the engine does not write that rule today; §4.1 does); `entity is a Player && hasInfiniteMaterials()` → no drop; `dropSelf` → `spawnAtLocation(getFrameItemStack())`; the held stack is copied and dropped **when `random() < dropChance`** — `ItemDropChance`, default `1.0f`, read by `readAdditionalSaveData`, is the one random the engine must pin. `HangingEntity.spawnAtLocation` places the drop in the pop box in front of the frame.

`ItemFrame.interact(player, hand)`: `fixed` → `PASS`; the frame holds an item → `setRotation(getRotation() + 1)` with `getRotateItemSound()` — **a right-click rotates the item and takes nothing**; the frame is empty and the hand is not → `setItem(stack)` and the stack is consumed by one — **a right-click fills an empty frame**, which is the second fact §4.3 answers.

`ItemFrame.survives()`: `fixed` → `true`; else `false` when the pop box collides with the level, or when the block behind the frame (`pos.relative(direction.getOpposite())`) is neither `isSolid()` nor a diode, or when `canCoexist` fails. `BlockAttachedEntity.tick` runs it every 100 ticks and, on `false`, `discard`s the frame and drops its item: **a frame on a non-solid wall falls off within five seconds**, which is why §5.2 proves the support at build time.

`readAdditionalSaveData` reads **`Item`, `ItemRotation`, `ItemDropChance`, `Facing`, `Invisible`, `Fixed`** (`ItemFrame`) and **`block_pos`** (`BlockAttachedEntity`); `BlockAttachedEntity.setPos` sets `block_pos` from the position, so a summoned frame needs no `block_pos`. `ItemFrame.getSlot(0)` serves the item, which is the **`contents`** slot of `SlotRanges` (the string is in the class). `GlowItemFrame` overrides only the five sounds and `getFrameItemStack()`: **everything above holds for the glow frame**. `EntityType` registers both at **0.5 × 0.5**.

**The stand's click, `ArmorStand.interactAt(player, vec, hand)`**: `isMarker()` or a name tag in hand → `PASS`; a spectator → `SUCCESS`; with an **empty hand**, `getClickedSlot(vec)` picks the slot by the click's height on the stand, `isDisabled(clicked)` falls back to the empty hand's slot (`MAINHAND`), and `hasItemInSlot(slot) && swapItem(player, slot, EMPTY, hand)` → `SUCCESS_SERVER`; with an **item in hand**, `getEquipmentSlotForItem(stack)` picks the slot, `isDisabled(slot)` → `FAIL`, a hand slot on a stand without `ShowArms` → `FAIL`, `swapItem` → `SUCCESS_SERVER`. **No ability or game-mode check** (*holds*). `getClickedSlot` (y relative to the feet, divided by the stand's scale; a `Small` stand widens the bands): `FEET` at `0.1 ≤ y < 0.55`, `CHEST` at `0.9 ≤ y < 1.6`, `LEGS` at `0.4 ≤ y < 1.2`, `HEAD` at `y ≥ 1.6`, each **only when that slot holds an item**, tested in that order; otherwise `OFFHAND` when the main hand is empty and the off hand is not, else `MAINHAND`. So a stand holding one helmet gives it up only to a click on the head, and a stand holding one sword gives it up to a click anywhere else — the band is a fact the engine hands the bot (§5.4) and the skill tells the author (§8).

`ArmorStand.swapItem(player, slot, stack, hand)`: the slot holds an item and bit `8 + filterBit(slot)` of `DisabledSlots` is set → `false` (**taking disabled**); the slot is empty and bit `16 + filterBit(slot)` is set → `false` (**putting disabled**); a player with infinite materials fills an empty slot from a copy; a stack of more than one goes in one at a time only into an empty slot; else `setItemSlot(slot, stack)` and **`player.setItemInHand(hand, current)`** — the swap. `isDisabled(slot)` is bit `filterBit(slot)` set, or a hand slot on a stand without `ShowArms`. The filter bits, from `EquipmentSlot`'s static initialiser: **`mainhand` 0, `offhand` 5, `feet` 1, `legs` 2, `chest` 3, `head` 4**.

`ArmorStand.hurtServer`: removed → `false`; `!mob_griefing && Mob` → `false`; `#bypasses_invulnerability` → `kill`; **`isInvulnerableTo(source) || invisible || isMarker()` → `false`**; `#is_explosion` → `brokenByAnything`; `#ignites_armor_stands` / `#burns_armor_stands` → fire damage; neither `#can_break_armor_stand` nor `#always_kills_armor_stands` → `false`; then, **for a player source, `!Abilities.mayBuild` → `false`** (*holds*: an adventure-mode player cannot break, wobble or move a stand); a creative player breaks it; anyone else wobbles it and breaks it on a second hit within five ticks. So an **`Invulnerable` stand still gives up its items** (`interactAt` never reads the flag) and is proof against the explosion, fire and projectile arms above; §4.1 sets it.

`LivingEntity.createEquipmentSlotAccess(entity, slot)`: **`HEAD`, `MAINHAND` and `OFFHAND` accept any stack**; `FEET`, `LEGS` and `CHEST` accept a stack only when `getEquipmentSlotForItem(stack) == slot`, which is the stack's `equippable` component's slot when `canUseSlot` allows it and `MAINHAND` otherwise. `ArmorStand.canUseSlot` refuses `BODY` and `SADDLE` and any disabled slot. So `item replace entity <stand> armor.chest with <a sword>` is refused by the game, and §6.1 refuses it at the document. `ArmorStand.readAdditionalSaveData` reads **`Invisible`, `Small`, `ShowArms`, `DisabledSlots`, `NoBasePlate`, `Marker`, `Pose`**, and `LivingEntity`'s equipment. `EntityType` registers the stand at **0.5 × 1.975**.

**What a taken frame is worth.** `HangingEntityItem.useOn` asks `Player.mayUseItemAt(pos, side, stack)`, which is `Abilities.mayBuild || stack.canPlaceOnBlockInAdventureMode(…)`: **a frame item in an adventure-mode inventory cannot be placed**; it is junk, not a tool.

### 2.2 What the tree holds today, measured

**Cited.** The `tick` function exists and already polls per tick for `collect` (`dw.hold`) and for click triggers; the fixture tag `dw_fixture` marks compiler-owned hardware, and `DW0545` refuses a box-selector `tp` that would carry a fixture away; `DW0489` refuses two bodies contesting one crosshair; `press_cells_outside` finds a standable, walk-reachable cell from whose eye `sees_body` reaches a 1 × 2 interaction body within reach, in tenth-block steps, *a press through a wall is no press* — the one stance-with-sight proof in the tree, bound to interaction bodies only. `completes_from` judges a `collect` by feet-to-cell distance under `BLOCK_INTERACTION_REACH` (4.5) with no sight term; `INTERACTION_REACH` (3.0) is the entity figure. `nav/world/body.rs` already carries the `armor_stand` hitbox. The grammar's `mark` declares an anchor with a cell, a facing and a role, and `export.rs` writes it to the piece's metadata; the grammar writes no entity. `check-structure-emitters.py` requires every site that serialises a `.nbt` to judge its blocks. Across the content repository at `main` no piece carries an entity, so §3.1's refusals bind nothing that exists (a measured zero, stated).

### 2.3 Established practice

The owner's direction — an item sits where the scene would hold it, and every holder is takeable — is the ruling this spec executes, and is not re-asked. What remained a craft question was *how a player learns that a framed or worn item is takeable*, and the record answers it from the pinned game rather than from a studio source: the item frame and the armour stand are vanilla furniture whose take acts (§2.1) every Minecraft player has met in survival, so the affordance is the object's own, and no prompt is owed (**cited**: the pinned client draws none, and `game-writing.md` §8 L1 forbids a glowing marker). What this spec does **not** cite: a studio statement of the weapon-rack or display-case convention from the games where items sit in scenery — the convention is familiar but no source was read for it, so it is named as unsupported and relied on for nothing.

## 3. The surface

### 3.1 The piece carries the holder

**Authored**, on spec-0021's ruling that furniture is the piece's (**cited**). A structure template's `entities` list may carry **`minecraft:item_frame`, `minecraft:glow_item_frame` and `minecraft:armor_stand`, and nothing else**: the admission allowlist gains an entity list beside its block list, judged under `DW0730` as the block list is, and the default list is those three ids. The compiler reads the list (`nbtread::Root.entities` stops being dead), and each entity is a **holder** at the cell it occupies — a frame at `block_pos`, a stand at the cell its feet stand in — rotated and translated with the piece exactly as an anchor is.

A piece may say what the holder **looks like**, and nothing else:

| Kind | Scene properties a piece may carry | Everything else |
|---|---|---|
| `item_frame`, `glow_item_frame` | `Facing`, `ItemRotation`, `Invisible` | refused, `DW1036` |
| `armor_stand` | `Rotation`, `Pose`, `Small`, `ShowArms`, `NoBasePlate`, `Invisible` | refused, `DW1036` |

`Item`, `equipment`, `Fixed`, `Invulnerable`, `ItemDropChance`, `DisabledSlots`, `Marker`, `Tags`, `UUID`, `Pos`, `block_pos` and any other key are the engine's (§4.1): a piece that carries one is refused at admission by name, because a piece cannot smuggle a stocked holder in any more than a stocked chest (spec-0105 §2.4), and a flag the engine must own is not the piece's to set. The position comes from the entity's place in the template, not from a key.

### 3.2 The grammar places one

**Authored.** A `mark` gains an optional **`holder`**: `{"kind": "item-frame" | "glow-item-frame" | "armor-stand", …}` with the kind's scene properties of §3.1 under their DSL spellings (`rotation`, `pose`, `small`, `show_arms`, `no_base_plate`, `invisible`), and `delvec detail` writes the entity into the piece's `entities` list at the mark's cell with the mark's facing — a frame on the face the facing names, a stand turned to it. The anchor the mark declares is the holder's anchor, so a program that marks `anchor/sword-stand` with a holder has placed the stand a `loot` entry fills. The field is optional on a type whose `at` is flattened, so it enters the ledger of `docs/reference/grammar.md` §2e with its version and a fence in `ir.rs` that reads the field (`HOLDER_SINCE`), as `Mark.role` did. `export.rs` becomes a site that writes entities, and `check-structure-emitters.py`'s obligation widens from *judges its blocks* to *judges its blocks and its entities*. The gallery generator writes the same entity shape by hand, as it writes blocks by hand.

### 3.3 A fill names the slot

**Authored.** `LootItem` gains an optional **`slot`**, and the holder class gains a **slot vocabulary**, one per family, read from the pinned game (§2.1):

| Family | Slots | Omitted `slot` means |
|---|---|---|
| container (the twelve kinds) | `container.<n>`, `n` below the kind's slot count | positional: the n-th stack to `container.<n>` (unchanged) |
| frame | `contents` | `contents`; a second stack is `DW0431` / `DW0438`, as a 28th stack is |
| stand | `armor.head`, `armor.chest`, `armor.legs`, `armor.feet`, `weapon.mainhand`, `weapon.offhand` | refused, `DW1035`: a stand's slots have no order a reader would guess, and which hand or head a thing sits in is the author's judgement |

`slot` is the vocabulary's spelling, which is also vanilla's `item replace entity` spelling, so the emitted command and the authored word are one word. A fill mixing named and unnamed stacks, naming a slot twice, naming a slot outside the vocabulary, or putting into `armor.chest`, `armor.legs` or `armor.feet` a stack the game would refuse there (§2.1: a stack whose `equippable` slot is not that slot, read from the vendored item data) is `DW1035`. Whether the named slot belongs to the holder actually standing at the anchor is build tier, as container-ness is: a `contents` fill on a chest, or an `armor.head` fill on a frame, is `DW0431` / `DW0438` naming the holder's family and its vocabulary.

### 3.4 `collect` adopts a holder

**Authored.** `collect.container` is renamed **`collect.holder`**: it adopts any holder at the anchor — a chest, a frame, a stand — and the compiler fills it at activation as it fills an adopted container today (`item` into the holder's first slot, `fill_count` padding into the following `container.<n>` slots on a container and refused with `DW1035` on a frame or a stand, which have no padding slots). A `collect` with neither `holder` nor `dropped_by` keeps the compiler's own chest at `anchor`. **Nothing else is typed**: a holder's kind, flags and tag are derivations the engine makes from the piece; an author never writes them in stage 5.

## 4. Emission

### 4.1 The engine owns the body: strip, summon, set

**Authored.** The compiler **removes every holder from the template it ships** — the shipped `.nbt` carries an empty `entities` list, as every piece does today, so `/place template` places no body — and **summons each holder in `setup_finish`**, before the fills, with NBT the engine writes in full:

| Key | Frame | Stand | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| kind, position, scene properties | from the piece (§3.1), rotated with it | from the piece, rotated with it | the look is the piece's |
| `Tags` | `["dw_fixture","dw_holder","dw_holder_<id>"]` | the same | `<id>` is the holder's anchor stem; the fixture tag puts it under `DW0545`; one body per tag, which the affordance self-check asserts |
| `Fixed` | `0b` while it holds an item; `1b` on an empty holder (§4.4) | — | §2.1 slot 1: a fixed frame cannot be taken from |
| `Invulnerable` | `0b` while it holds an item; `1b` on an empty holder | `1b` always | §2.1: an invulnerable frame cannot be taken from; an invulnerable stand still swaps and is proof against fire, explosion and projectile |
| `ItemDropChance` | `1.0f` | — | the one random on the take path, pinned (ADR-0006) |
| `Marker` | — | `0b` | a marker stand ignores the click |
| `ShowArms` | — | the piece's, forced `1b` when a hand slot is filled | a hand slot on an armless stand is disabled |
| `DisabledSlots` | — | computed: bit `16 + f` set for all six slots; bit `8 + f` set for every slot no fill claims; `f` the filter bit of §2.1 | nothing can be put on a stand, and only what the fill placed can be taken |
| the item | `item replace entity @e[tag=dw_holder_<id>,limit=1] contents with <item>[components] <count>` | `item replace entity @e[tag=dw_holder_<id>,limit=1] <slot> with <item>[components] <count>` | the fill, one command shape across the class, `container.<n>` on a container as today; the stack components are the ones `container_stack_components` already writes |

The setup also writes **`gamerule entity_drops true`** beside the rules `emit/world.rs` already writes, because a frame under `entity_drops false` deletes the item it was asked to give (§2.1). A `collect` adopting a holder fills it at activation by the same `item replace entity` line, as an adopted container is filled at activation today. The summon and the fill are emitted lines, so the command-tree walk judges every one.

**Why summon rather than ship the entity in the template.** `/place template` places a template's entities with fresh identities and the bytes the piece wrote; the engine's claim is that it set `Fixed`, `Invulnerable`, `DisabledSlots` and the tag, and a claim about bytes the engine did not write is not a proof. One emission path, through the command tree, for every body the delve has, is also what the gallery generator's comment already asserts of pieces.

### 4.2 Taking, and what the engine reads

**Authored** on §2.1. The take is the vanilla act and the engine adds nothing to it: a hit pops a frame's item into the pop box, where the player picks it up as any drop; a click swaps a stand's slot into the hand. Completion of a `collect` is read exactly as today — the `inventory_changed` advancement and the `dw.hold` poll over `container.*` — because both act on the item reaching the inventory, and neither knows where it came from. A `loot` holder has no completion to read.

### 4.3 An emptied frame is frozen

**Authored** on §2.1 slot 4 and `interact`. An empty frame breaks on the next hit and refills on a right-click with an item in hand; a stand's emptied slot has putting disabled and cannot be broken. So the `tick` function gains one line per campaign that has a frame holder: `execute as @e[type=#<ns>:frames,tag=dw_holder] unless items entity @s contents * run data merge entity @s {Fixed:1b,Invulnerable:1b}` — the tag `#<ns>:frames` is the two frame ids, written by the compiler — which turns a frame the player has emptied into the frozen scenery an empty frame is from the start. **The cost, stated**: the freeze lands on the tick after the pop, and a second hit landing in that same tick breaks the frame and drops a frame item the player cannot place (§2.1); a right-click with an item in that tick fills the frame, and the filled frame is then a holder the player can empty again. Both windows are one tick wide and both leave the party nothing it can use; neither moves the critical path.

### 4.4 The census counts the class

**Authored.** `compiler::containers` becomes **`compiler::holders`** and its census ranges over the class: every container cell of the twelve kinds and every holder entity of the piece, each a row with its **`kind`** (`container`, `item-frame`, `glow-item-frame`, `armor-stand`), its **slots** (the vocabulary of §3.3, filled slots marked) and its claims (`loot`, `collect`, `device` as today). The ledger is **`validation/holders.json`** — the rows and header `validation/containers.json` carries today plus `kind`, `slots` and the counts below — and `DW1018` and `DW1024` move to the module's page with it. An **empty holder** — a frame or stand no fill claims — is a row with claim `none` and the mark **`frozen`**: it is **not** a decoration cell for `DW1018` or §9 of spec-0105, because it costs the player nothing to learn (a hit on a frozen frame does nothing and a frozen stand gives nothing, where an empty chest opens and shows an empty grid), and the census prints its count on its own (`E empty holder(s), frozen`). A holder is never a `device` (non-goals).

**Amendment (owner ruling, spec-0105 §9.5).** Every frame and stand is a holder whose contents show from outside, filled or empty, and so is exempt from the empty and scenery rules — no decoration cell, no place in the cap, free to stand among holders that matter, owed no notice — as is a brewing stand whose question is its bottle slots. The census line states the exempt count beside `E`: `…, E empty (frozen), X exempt (contents show from outside); …`.

## 5. Proving it reaches the party

### 5.1 The binding line

**Authored.** The census line becomes `holder census: N holder(s) in the assembled world (<kind>: n, …); L loot, C collect, V device, D decoration, E empty (frozen); notice <declared|none>; by place: …; arrangement: …` — every figure computed, `N` the denominator, a campaign with no holder printing `0 holder(s)`. Beside it, the `container binding` line becomes `holder binding: F fill(s) (… by kind), J judged against the assembled world, …`. Every per-camera binding line of the view adds `H holder(s) not drawn`, computed from the holders inside the frame, so a reviewer reading a render of a wall knows what hangs on it (spec-0089 §2.2).

### 5.2 The stance

**Authored** on §2.2's cited `press_cells_outside`. `press_cells_outside` and `sees_body` take the body's box as a parameter instead of assuming the 1 × 2 interaction body, and the holder pass asks of **every holder that carries a fill**: is there a standable, walk-reachable cell from whose eye the holder's box — a frame's 12/16 × 12/16 × 1/16 plate on its face, a stand's 0.5 × 1.975 column — is within **`INTERACTION_REACH`** (3.0, the entity figure, because a holder is an entity) along an unobstructed segment. None → `DW1037` (**unreachable** arm). The same pass asks of every holder whether the game would keep it: a frame's support block (`survives`, §2.1) is solid in the edited model, and a stand's foot cell is standable with the two cells above it clear of the model. Not → `DW1037` (**unsupported** arm). A `collect` adopting a holder completes under `completes_from` from the stance cells this pass found, with `INTERACTION_REACH`, which also fixes the leg's goal for `DW0311`.

### 5.3 PackTest

**Authored.** A generated template **`holder_<id>`** per holder that carries a fill, claimed under `DW0811` (family `holder_`, declared = every filled holder): run `setup`; assert exactly one entity carries `dw_holder_<id>`; assert the filled slot holds the declared item (`if items entity @e[tag=dw_holder_<id>,limit=1] <slot> <item>`); assert the owned flags (`if entity @e[tag=…,nbt={Fixed:0b,Invulnerable:0b}]` on a frame, `{Marker:0b,Invulnerable:1b}` and the computed `DisabledSlots` on a stand). For a frame, the template then empties it (`item replace entity … contents with air`), runs `tick`, and asserts `{Fixed:1b,Invulnerable:1b}` — §4.3 run, not described. A PackTest dummy performs no real attack or click, which `economy.rs` already records, so the take itself is the bot's.

### 5.4 The bot

**Authored.** `critical-path.json` moves to **format 5**: a `collect` step carries **`holder: {kind, slot, click_height}`** — `kind` the row of §4.4, `slot` the filled slot, `click_height` the centre of the longest run of heights §2.1's bands map to that slot on that stand, given its filled slots (a frame carries none), and `stance` the walk-reachable cell nearest the holder the stance pass proved in reach and in sight — because a bot handed a path without it would have to guess which act takes the item, which is the compiler's knowledge written where it cannot be right (the reason formats 3 and 4 moved). The harness: on `container`, as today; on a frame, walk to a stance cell, `bot.attack(entity)` the body tagged `dw_holder_<id>`, then pick up the drop as `pickUpDrop` does for a `dropped_by` collect; on a stand, walk to a stance cell and click the body at `click_height` with the interact-at form (mineflayer's `activateEntityAt`, from memory of its API — the implementation finds the real call and asserts the server received an `interact_at`), with an empty hand. The step passes on the objective's marker, as every collect does. The bot also asserts, after a frame take, that the frame still exists and no `item_frame` entered its inventory — the §4.3 freeze observed once from outside.

## 6. Refusals

**Authored**, each at the document it is entered in, with the remedy named and a `remedy_reachability.rs` row each.

### 6.1 `DW1035` — a fill names a slot the holder class does not have, or a stack a slot cannot wear (validation tier, `dsl::loot`)

A `slot` outside the vocabulary of §3.3; a slot named twice in one fill; a fill mixing named and unnamed stacks; a stack in `armor.chest`, `armor.legs` or `armor.feet` whose `equippable` slot is not that slot; a `collect` with `fill_count > 0` adopting a holder whose family has no `container.<n>` slots (judged at build tier under this code, where the family is known). The message prints the vocabulary of every family, names the slot and the stack, and says which slots take any stack.

### 6.2 `DW1036` — a piece's holder carries what the engine owns (admission, `admit::audit`)

A template entity of a holder kind carrying any key outside §3.1's scene properties for its kind. The message names the entity, the key and whether the engine sets it (and to what) or derives it; the remedy is to remove the key, and to declare the item through a fill. An entity of any other kind is `DW0730`, as a block outside the allowlist is.

### 6.3 `DW1037` — a holder the party cannot take from (build tier, `compiler::holders`)

Two arms (§5.2): **unsupported** — a frame whose backing block is not solid, a stand whose foot cell is not standable or whose column is not clear — naming the cell, the block found and what the game would do (the frame falls within five seconds; the stand drops); **unreachable** — no stance cell within `INTERACTION_REACH` with sight — naming the holder, the nearest standable cell and what blocks the segment. Both arms list the remedies at the piece: move the holder, open the sightline, or stand it on the thing it needs. A campaign whose holders all pass prints `0 unsupported, 0 unreachable` with its denominator.

### 6.4 `DW1038` — a runtime edit reaches a holder (build tier, `compiler::holders`)

A `set-block`, `fill-region` or `clear-region` effect whose cells include a holder's cell or its support cell (a frame's backing block, a stand's foot block): the edit would pop the frame or drop the stand mid-game, silently. The message names the effect, the holder and the cell they share, and the remedy: move the holder or shrink the region. A `morph`, `carve` or `fill` of stage 7 is in the edited model already and is judged by §5.2, not here.

## 8. What the player meets, and what was chosen

1. **An item hangs or stands where the scene puts it, and one act takes it.** A hit on a frame drops the item at the player's feet and leaves the frame; a click on a stand's helmet puts the helmet in the hand. The alternative — a chest beside the stand — is what the owner's direction refuses.
2. **The frame and the stand stay.** An adventure-mode hit cannot break a stand (the game's own rule), and a frame the player has emptied freezes on the next tick, so a room is not emptied of its furniture by a party looking for loot. The cost is a one-tick window (§4.3), stated, in which a frame can be broken for a frame item that cannot be placed.
3. **Nothing goes onto a stand.** Putting is disabled on every slot, so a party's spare gear never ends on the furniture and a stand reads the same before and after the take, minus what was taken. A player who clicks a holding stand with a matching item in hand swaps it in — the game's own swap, and the stand then holds the player's item, which the same click takes back.
4. **A stand gives up a helmet to a click on its head.** The click band is the game's (§2.1); the skill tells the author which band each slot is, so a sword is put in the hand (taken from anywhere) and a helmet on the head.
5. **A right-click on a frame turns the item.** It takes nothing; the hit does.
6. **An empty frame or stand is scenery and does nothing**, and is not counted against the arrival notice of spec-0105.

### Recorded debts

Checked against the implementing tree; each is a debt, never a pass.

- **Criterion 12, the skill.** `tools/ci/check-skill-page.py` refuses a page naming a code or a surface its pinned release lacks (rule 17); the holder surface enters the skill with the release that carries it, and until then the skill carries only the craft rule (an item where the scene would hold it) at the scope the pinned engine reaches.
- **Criterion 11, the baseline.** Regenerated on the feature branch against its merge base; the regeneration after the merge commit belongs to the integration that lands it.
- **§4.3, the one-tick window.** Not closed by this spec: the pinned game resolves the hit inside `skipAttackInteraction` and offers no synchronous hook on a frame emptied by a player, so the freeze is the tick's. The window's effect is stated in §8.2 and the bot observes the freeze once; a playtest finding of a broken frame names this row.
- **§2.1, a frame under fire.** A frame holding an item is `Invulnerable:0b` by necessity, so a trap's projectile or an explosion pops its item early; a stand is proof against both. No proof that a trap's volley line or blast reaches no frame is written here; a campaign placing a frame in a trap's room owns the question until a pass over `compiler::trap_trigger`'s volley lines takes it.
- **§5.4, `activateEntityAt`.** Verified at implementation: mineflayer 4.37.1 (the harness pin) has `bot.activateEntityAt(entity, position)`, which writes `use_entity` with `mouse: 2` — the packet the server reads as `interact_at`. The harness cannot observe the server receiving it except by its effect; the swap that puts the stack in the hand happens only on that packet, so the objective completing is the proof.
- **§5.2, `completes_from` and the leg's goal.** Not done at implementation: `Step::Collect::completes_from` still judges a holder collect by `BLOCK_INTERACTION_REACH` from the step's cell, and `DW0311`'s leg ends at the standable cell nearest the holder's cell, not at a stance cell. The plan is built before the holder entities are read (they live in the template bytes the build is handed), so carrying the holder into `completes_from` is a change to where the plan learns of them; until then the sculk prediction's may-stop cells around a holder collect are judged at 4.5 rather than 3.0.
- **§3.2, the grammar version.** Allocated at implementation: `HOLDER_SINCE` is `1.12.0`, with `1.11.0` reserved for spec-0106's `PREFAB_SINCE`.
