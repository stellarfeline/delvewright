- **What it is for**: a player who takes a branch and is not cheated by it. In a playtest, a long climb ended at a way the story keeps shut; the player learned so at the top and walked back down. The general form the ledger row carries: *a way the story keeps shut is closed where its branch leaves the open route, not at the far end of a long dead end.* The engine today builds every gated connection shut (spec-0107), proves the bar separates its sides (`DW1011`) and knows which shut bars a body walks up to (`DW1011`'s *with the party at a face*); it holds no word from the author about **how the player is to learn the way is shut**, and so it cannot confirm one. This spec makes the author say it, in one of three ways the owner has ruled are the legitimate ones, and makes the engine confirm the one said.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record. Each statement below is **cited** (a reading of the tree at the ground revision, a constitution rule, a research page, a named outside source, or an owner ruling — marked *owner-confirmed*) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0118`. Two diagnostics: **`DW1053`** (a shut way the party meets, learned by no declared means, §6.1) and **`DW1054`** (a declaration the world does not confirm, §6.2). Both handed numbers are used. The surface change is stage 2 and takes the unreleased `dsl_version` minor other unreleased surface changes share; this spec states no version literal.
- **Non-goals**: a threshold on a branch's length or rise — no source gives one (§2.3), so none is judged; both are **measured and written** (§5.4), never refused. Whether a player can be expected to spot a bar once it is in their view (render distance, fog, light) — the demo level is where that is looked at. Where the author shuts a corridor — craft advice, §9, never a check. `DW1011`'s own population and rule, unchanged. A `laid` way, a `timed_gates[]` clock, a prefab's own gate station: not a gated connection, not judged here.

## 1. What the tree does today, re-verified

- **Cited** (`layout.rs`, `Edge::shuts`): a connection is built shut exactly when it crosses a seam and its `gating` names a term — *the one predicate every reader of "is this way shut" asks*. `EdgeGating` is `{flags[], quest?, opens_from}` and nothing else. `opens_from` lives inside `gating` because *a side has no home outside `gating`, so it can only be named for a way that shuts* (`compiler.md`, `edges[].gating`); a `gating` naming a side and no term is `DW0818`.
- **Cited** (`nav/separate.rs`, `check_gates_separate`): the proof already computes, per quest configuration the critical path passes through (the configurations `DW0921` judges), the configuration's **route cells** (`critical_route_cells`), the party's **reach** (`closure`: the body-move closure of the route cells), each shut gate's **two faces** (`faces_of`, on the axis the seam hands), and whether the reach holds a face (`reached`, printed as *with the party at a face*). A gated connection declared `shortcut` is **counted and not judged** there, because for separation a declared way round is the author's statement; the east door of the gallery is one. Nothing reads *what the player learns* at a reached face.
- **Cited** (`nav/separate.rs`): the binding line is `DW1011 binding: N gate(s) resolved (N of N gated connection(s) among them); …; N with the party at a face, N got round …`; the ledger is `validation/separation-proof.json`, written from `emit/routes.rs`.
- **Cited** (`shortcut.rs`): `shortcuts[]` is `{id, gate, unlock, on_unlock[]}` and carries **no hint field**; the wrong-side answer is a `use` trigger the author anchors on the `gate`, riding the bodies `compiler::wrongside` stands on the sealed side (`compiler.md`, `shortcuts[]`). A sealed body nothing answers is `DW0429`, discharged by `QuestsContent::answers_press_at(anchor)` — *any `use` trigger at the anchor* — or a `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint`. The gallery's `trigger/east-door-wrong-side` is a `use` trigger at `anchor/seam-east-door`, audience `presser`, one `narrate`.
- **Cited** (`pressable.rs`, `Body::Region`, `shell_cells`; `promise.md`, `DW0963`): a `use` trigger anchored on **any gate region** — a gated connection's `anchor/seam-<edge>` included — arms the region's clickable shell, and *a one-block-deep doorway is all shell*: the bar cells themselves are the pressable, met from either face.
- **Cited** (`body.rs`): `has_line_of_sight(a, b)` walks `walk_cells` between two eyes 1.5 above their cells — the vanilla **mob** eye height, by its own comment — and holds when no cell on the segment `blocks_camera`; `blocks_camera` is `solid ∪ tall ∪ use_gates`; `walk_cells` tests its starting cell first and returns the **first** hit. The player's eye is `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` (1.62). The loop proof's *Sight* (`compiler.md`, *Seamlessness*) is the same walk from the player's eye.
- **Cited** (`blockshape.rs`, `the_conservative_default_still_refuses`): `minecraft:iron_bars` is `Collision::FullCube` to the model — a bar cell is in `solid`, so it **blocks the camera**. A closed iron trapdoor holds a body (`nav/world/tests.rs`). So a bar is a thing the sight walk **hits**, never a thing it sees through; a check that asks *is the bar in sight* must ask whether the bar is the first cell the walk hits, as the loop proof's *Sight* does of any seen blocking cell. The research recorded this classification as unknown; it is settled here from the tree.
- **Cited** (`lethal.rs`, `check_danger_is_visible`): a staged volume is judged in the critical path's configurations **and** in every reachable branch's own path (`branch::realize`, `plan.branch_critical_path`, `plan.branch_gate_model`), and a volume no modelled body reaches is a **warning**, not a refusal, *because refusing would reject a legitimate design for an engine limitation*. `DW1011` reads the critical path's configurations only.
- **Cited** (`plan/anchors.rs`; `objective.rs`; `loot.rs`; `wave.rs`; `ambush.rs`): every anchor resolves to a `Point {pos}` or a `Gate {from, to}`; a `collect` and an `interact` carry an `anchor`, a `kill` names a `wave` whose `anchor` is where its mobs spawn, a `loot[]` entry names the `anchor` whose cell holds its holder, an `ambush` names the anchor `at` which it fires. These are the pickups, interactions and fights a campaign binds to cells.
- **Cited** (the finding row, mechanism only): the row for the long climb to a shut way is triaged `capability` with carrier `null`, and its binding is `layout-graph.json` edges of `class: barred` — a class the tree no longer has (`DW0100`); its note says the check is to be built and the row reads *no general form* until it lands.

## 2. What practice does

**Cited** — a dead end is acceptable when the player **can see it before deciding to go in**, and dead ends are not to be thrown in the player's way without warning (Totten, *Dead Man's Trail*, Game Developer; a practitioner's account of internal playtests, no method stated); a locked fire door with a pane the exit is seen through, so the player explores other avenues knowing the goal (Laidlaw, in Saltzman's *Secrets of the Sages*); long trips to dead-end rooms are bad, even for a power-up (Jaquays, same collection); branches and dead ends were simplified after watching playtesters get lost (Valve, *Half-Life 2: Episode Two* developer commentary); a room with one exit is avoided so the player is never stuck (Jensen, IOI, via GMTK).

**Cited** — a dead end that is entered must **lead to something**: dead ends without meaning are not acceptable (Glans, *On Flow*, Game Developer); locked doors kept off the critical path, with the locked room likely to hold extra treasure (Short, Kitfox); exploration raises the expectation that the effort will be acknowledged by a reward or by interactive content, and disappointment follows when it is not (Gómez-Maureira et al., CHI PLAY 2021, N = 254 — the exploration figures are measured; the disappointment is participants' comments).

**Cited** — the one-way door: a door the player finds locked and later opens permanently from the other side, so the start is not overwhelmed with branches (Brown, *Boss Keys*, on Dark Souls — a critic's analysis; no first-party statement of the design was found). **Owner-confirmed**: the press at the wrong side of a one-way door that says *this does not open from this side* is the valuable part — the wrong-side press teaches the player the loop.

**Cited** — at a fork, about three quarters of participants took the longest branch first, and at one fork 73–80 % chose the longest line of sight (Marples, Huddersfield doctoral thesis, 74 screen / 58 VR participants, 8 forks). **Authored** from it: a long, open, rising branch is the shape most likely to be walked first, so the shape of the finding is the shape this preference amplifies.

### 2.3 What practice does not give

**Cited** (the research record): no source states a length, a step count or a rise at which a branch to a shut way becomes too long; the one dedicated essay (Gee, *Dead-End Theory in Level Design*, SMU Guildhall) could not be read. The constitution refuses an invented number (*a craft question the record does not answer is researched, never invented*). So the rule here is **not a threshold**: it is the two conditions the sources agree on — *known before entering* or *paid for entering* — plus the one-way door the owner ruled legitimate, each made a declaration the engine confirms.

### 2.4 The ruling

**Owner-confirmed.** Every story-shut way a body can reach declares how the player learns it is shut, one of: **visible from the junction** where its branch leaves the open route; **told at the near face** by a pressable hint (the souls one-way door: the press says *this does not open from this side*); or **the branch ends in a reward**. The engine confirms each declaration — a sight line from the junction; a pressable with an answer at the near face; a bound pickup, interaction or fight on the branch. **An undeclared one is refused.**

**Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *A check confirms intent*; the brief's reading): the declaration is the intent, and requiring the declaration is the pattern `DW1051` adopts for a press — the engine supplies the body, the state and the check; the author supplies the word. The check never infers which of the three the author meant.

## 3. The decision

**Three sentences, authored.**

1. **`gating` gains `learned`**, one of `seen | told | rewarded`: how the player learns this way is shut. It lives inside `gating` for the reason `opens_from` does — only a way that shuts can be learned shut — and `DW0818` widens by one more word to refuse a `learned` with no term.
2. **A shut way the party meets owes the word.** For every gated connection, in every configuration `DW1011` judges, at every face the party's reach holds while the connection is shut — a declared `shortcut` **included**, because the wrong side of a one-way door is exactly the face this rule is about — a connection with no `learned` is refused (`DW1053`).
3. **The engine confirms the word it is given** (`DW1054`): `seen` — from the junction where the branch to that face leaves the route cells, the bar is the first thing the player's eye meets; `told` — a `use` trigger answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>` (`answers_press_at`, the predicate `DW0429` reads); `rewarded` — a bound pickup, interaction or fight stands on the branch's ground.

**Why a declaration and not a rule.** The research record weighed five rules the engine could compute without asking (junction visibility for every way; a step cap; a rise cap; zero-length branches; report-only). Each of the first four refuses a legitimate design somebody has shipped — the one-way door round a corner, the tower-top lock seen from below and returned to, the short walk past a threshold that makes a door memorable — and each infers an intent the author never declared, which the constitution forbids. The fifth catches nothing. The declaration is the only shape that both refuses the finding's case (a long climb to a way that is neither seen from its foot, nor answers a press, nor pays) and admits every one of those designs, because each of them is one of the three words.

**Rejected, authored.**

- *Confirm all three and pass on any.* That is the inferring rule again, with three heads; a design whose author meant *told* and whose branch happens to hold a chest would pass on the wrong reason, and the message could name no remedy.
- *A fourth word, `none`.* It would be the opt-out secured by a property the defect can supply (CLAUDE.md, *Vacuity*): the finding's connection would carry `none` and go green.
- *Judge at the document.* Whether a face is reached, where the branch leaves the route and what the eye meets are world facts; `told` alone is a document fact and is still judged in the one place with the other two, so that one module answers *how is this way learned* (CLAUDE.md, *One question, one implementation*).

```json
      "gating": { "flags": ["flag/muster-cleared"], "learned": "rewarded" } }

```

**`learned`** is an optional field of `EdgeGating`, a closed set: `seen`, `told`, `rewarded`. Optional in the schema because a gated connection no body meets shut owes no word (§6.3); owed by `DW1053` where a body does. A `gating` that names `learned` and no term is `DW0818`, in the words that rule already uses for `opens_from`. A `learned` on a `carry`'s gating is `DW0100` (nothing on a carry shuts, so nothing is learned). No other document moves: the site plan's seams, the quest documents and the detail plan are read, not written.

**The three words, defined over the world** (§5), each in the player's terms: `seen` — *I can see the bar from where I would turn off*; `told` — *I walked up and pressed, and it told me*; `rewarded` — *the walk was worth it anyway*.

## 5. The analysis — `compiler::nav::learned`

Build tier, beside `DW1011`, from the same call site in `emit/routes.rs`, over the same configurations, faces and reach. **One authority**: the configurations are `critical_route_cells` grouped by `region_state_at` as `check_gates_separate` groups them; the faces are `faces_of`; the reach is `closure`; a cell a body can be in is `can_be`. The implementation **extracts** these from `check_gates_separate` into functions both proofs call, and lands an agreement test that drives both over generated worlds and asserts the same (gate, configuration, face) set is *reached* by each. Nothing here is a second copy of the separation proof's model.

### 5.1 The population

Every gated connection (`Edge::shuts()`), keyed by `anchor/seam-<edge>`, with the axis its seam hands — the population `DW1011` resolves. For each configuration `C`, for each connection `G` shut in `C` (every region cell solid in `C`'s state), for each face `f` of `G` that the reach of `C` holds: the triple `(G, C, f)` is a **shut way met**. Unlike `DW1011`, a connection declared `shortcut` is **judged**, and a one-sided connection is judged at **whichever face the reach holds**, not only its openable one: the wrong side of a one-way door is the face the player presses.

### 5.2 The junction and the branch

**Authored.** From the route cells of `C`, a multi-source shortest path over the body moves `closure` floods (`World::body_moves`: walking, falling, jumping, swimming, climbing) to the nearest cell of `f`, with the route cells as sources; the path's **last route cell** is the **junction** `J`, the cells after it are the **branch path** `P`. The **branch ground** `B` is the body-move closure from `f` that enters no route cell — the pocket the branch opens into, the route itself excluded. Where `f` is itself a route cell, `J = f`, `P` is empty and `B` is `f`'s pocket off the route: the shut way stands on the open route and the player is already at it.

Two measurements are taken from `P`, **written and never judged** (§5.4): its **length** in moves, and its **rise**, the highest `y` on `P` minus `J`'s `y`.

### 5.3 The three confirmations

- **`seen`.** From the player's eye at `J` — `cell_center(J)` raised by `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT`, the player's and not the mob's 1.5 — `nav::walk_cells` is walked to the centre of each cell of `G`'s region; the declaration **holds** when, for at least one region cell, the first cell the walk hits (`blocks_camera`) is a cell of `G`'s region. A walk that hits another cell first is blocked there; the message names the blocking cell per region cell, as `DW0821` names every blocking cell. A hatch is seen from above, a cover from below on the ladder, a bar in a wall from along the branch: one definition, the loop proof's *Sight*, with the bar as the seen blocking cell.
- **`told`.** `QuestsContent::answers_press_at(anchor/seam-<edge>)` holds — the predicate `DW0429` reads, so a `use` trigger that satisfies the one satisfies the other. The body is the region's shell (`Body::Region`), met from either face, or the wrong-side bodies where the connection is a `shortcuts[]` gate. What the line says is the author's (`game-writing.md` F2); that it is said in a state the presser can be in is spec-0117's question, not this one.
- **`rewarded`.** At least one **reward anchor** resolves to a `Point` whose cell is in `B` or is a face-neighbour of a cell in `B`. The reward anchors are: a `collect` objective's `anchor`; an `interact` objective's `anchor`; a `loot[]` entry's `anchor`; the `anchor` of a wave a `kill` objective names; an `ambush`'s `at`. A `reach-anchor` is not a reward — arriving is the thing the branch already made the player do. The message names the anchors examined and the nearest to `B`.

### 5.4 The ledger and the binding line

`validation/shut-ways.json` carries one row per shut way met: the connection, its class and gating, the configuration (as `DW1011` words it, *while `obj/…` is next*), the face and whether it is the openable one, `J`, the branch length and rise, the declaration, and the confirmation's finding (seen from `J` / blocked at `[x,y,z]`; answered by `trigger/…` / nothing answers; rewarded by `anchor/…` / no reward within `B` of `N` cell(s)). The row is written for green and red alike; a campaign with no gated connection writes an empty ledger with its zero.

The binding line, on every build: `shut ways (DW1053/DW1054): G gated connection(s); over C configuration(s), M shut way(s) met at a face — D declared (S seen, T told, R rewarded), U undeclared, F declaration(s) not confirmed; I declared and never met shut`. Every count is a number; `0` is written as `0`.

## 6. Diagnostics

### 6.1 `DW1053` — a shut way the party meets, learned by no declared means

Error, build tier (exit 3), `compiler::nav::learned`, raised once per gated connection (not per configuration) at the first configuration and face it is met shut without a `learned`. Quantifier: every shut way met (§5.1). The message names the connection with its class and gating, the configuration, the face (near hall side / far hall side, by node), `J`, the branch's length and rise, and the three words with what each would need here: *`seen` — the bar is [seen from `J` / hidden from `J` behind `[x,y,z]`]; `told` — [`trigger/…` answers a press / nothing answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>`]; `rewarded` — [`anchor/…` stands on the branch / no pickup, interaction or fight within the branch's `N` cells]*. Remedy: write the word that is true, or make one true and write it. The remedy row in `remedy_reachability.rs` writes `seen` on a connection whose bar the junction sees and asserts green.

### 6.2 `DW1054` — a declaration the world does not confirm

Error, build tier (exit 3), same module. Quantifier: every shut way met whose connection declares `learned`. One code, three shapes, by the word: *declared `seen`, but from `J` the eye meets `[x,y,z]` before the bar*; *declared `told`, but nothing answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>`* (the message carries the `use` trigger JSON `DW0429` prescribes, so the two rules name one remedy); *declared `rewarded`, but no pickup, interaction or fight stands within the branch's `N` cell(s)* — naming the nearest reward anchor and its distance. Remedy, always the world's or the word's: move or open the geometry so the bar is seen, write the trigger, place the reward, or change the word to the one that is true. Three remedy rows.

### 6.3 Inert declarations

A `learned` on a gated connection **never met shut** in any judged configuration is **counted** (*I declared and never met shut*) and raised as a **warning** under `DW1054`, naming the connection — not refused. **Cited** reason: the judged configurations are the critical path's (`DW1011`), and the closure is branch-blind (`compiler.md`, *Reachability*), so a way met shut only on a branch's own path is invisible to this population; refusing there would reject a legitimate design for an engine limitation, the rule `DW0891` states for a volume no modelled body reaches. Widening the population to branch paths is §11's first item.

### 6.4 Not new

`DW0818` widens by one word (§4); `DW0100` on a carry (§4); `DW0429` is unchanged and `told` reads its predicate; `DW1011` is unchanged in population and verdict.

## 8. Demo level

The row for `docs/demo-levels.md`: **Three Ways Shut** — a small yard with three shut ways off it, one per word. A barred arch across the yard, seen from the path the moment the player enters (`seen`); a one-way door at the end of a short passage, met first from its wrong side, whose press says it does not open from this side, and which the player later opens from the far side as the loop home (`told`, a `shortcuts[]` gate); a side stair up to a ledge with a shut hatch and a chest beside it (`rewarded`). Beside them the motivating shape, built right: a long corridor between two areas that the story opens later, **shut at its mouth** on the yard side and hidden by a structure that belongs to the setting, with a short close-up shot of the opened mouth when the beat fires. What the owner judges and no machine decides: whether the arch's bar reads as a bar at the distance the yard gives it; whether the wrong-side line lands as a promise and not a refusal; whether the chest pays for the stair; whether the corridor's hidden mouth reads as wall before the beat and as a way after the shot.

## 9. Craft guidance — advisory, for the skill

Lands in the skill page that teaches the layout graph and gating (`references/placement.md`, the `layout-graph.json` item), as advice with its motivating case, no plot: **shut a corridor at the end the player reaches first, not at the end they reach last** — a long way joining two areas that the story opens later is shut at its mouth by the earlier area, so the player who finds it shut has spent two steps, not the corridor; **hide the shut way with structure that belongs to the setting** — a collapsed arch, a portcullis in its housing, a stacked barricade — rather than a bare block in a doorway; **when it opens, show the player the opened place** — a short close-up shot of the changed mouth says the way is open better than a line does. **Owner-confirmed** as craft, never a check: the engine confirms the declaration of §4 and nothing about where the bar stands.

## 11. What stays open

- **Branch-path configurations.** `DW1011` and this check judge the critical path's configurations; `DW0891` already enumerates every reachable branch's own path. The one configuration enumeration both should read is the general form; this spec takes the existing population and counts the inert word (§6.3) rather than widening two proofs at once.
- **The junction is one cell.** `J` is where the shortest branch leaves the route; a player may leave it a cell to either side. The ledger carries `J`; the demo level is where it is seen whether one cell is enough, and a wider junction (the route cells within one move of `J`) is the recorded next shape if it is not.
- **A `told` line's side.** The region shell is pressable from both faces, so a line true on the wrong side (*this does not open from this side*) is said on the opening side too for a gated connection that is not a `shortcuts[]` gate. `compiler::wrongside` solves it for shortcuts; a one-sided gated connection reached from both faces in different configurations is a gap, recorded here, that spec-0117's state-gated answers may close.
- **Length and rise.** Written, never judged; the thresholds the sources do not give are measured on the demo level and on playtests, and a rule is proposed only once a number has a source.

## Acceptance criteria

Each is a test or a gate run, named; a criterion the tree cannot yet satisfy is a recorded debt, never a pass. `delvec` is the implementing tree's binary, force-rebuilt before any comparison.

1. **Surface.** `delvec schema --stage layout-graph` exports `gating.learned` on the four traversal classes as the closed set `seen | told | rewarded`; `gating.learned` on a `carry` is refused as `DW0100`; `gating: {learned: "seen"}` with no term is refused as `DW0818` with the message naming `learned` beside `opens_from`; `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py` reports `gating.learned` and each value bound.
2. **One model.** `nav::separate` and `nav::learned` call the same extracted functions for configurations, faces, reach and `can_be`; an agreement test (`crates/delvec/tests/` or `#[cfg(test)]` in `nav/`) drives both over generated worlds and asserts the identical set of (gate, configuration, face) triples reached, red on any difference.
3. **The population.** On a fixture with a one-way door declared `shortcut` and met from its wrong side, `DW1011` counts the door (*declared a way round*) and this check judges it (*met at a face*); a test asserts both lines over one build.
4. **`seen`.** A fixture with a bar at the end of a straight corridor off a route cell: `seen` holds and the ledger row names `J` and the bar cell; the same fixture with one solid cell placed on the line of sight is refused `DW1054` naming that cell; the eye height used is `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` (asserted by a fixture whose line clears at 1.62 and not at 1.5). A hatch (thin on `y`) is confirmed from the route cell beside it; a cover is confirmed from a body on the rung below it.
