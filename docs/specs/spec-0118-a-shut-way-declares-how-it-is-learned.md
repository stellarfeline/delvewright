- **What it is for**: a player who takes a branch and is not cheated by it. In a playtest, a long climb ended at a way the story keeps shut; the player learned so at the top and walked back down. The general form the ledger row carries: *a way the story keeps shut is closed where its branch leaves the open route, not at the far end of a long dead end.* The engine before this spec built every gated connection shut (spec-0107), proved the bar separates its sides (`DW1011`) and knew which shut bars a body walks up to (`DW1011`'s *with the party at a face*); it held no word from the author about **how the player is to learn the way is shut**, and so it could not confirm one. This spec makes the author say it, in one of the ways ruled legitimate, and makes the engine confirm the one said. In the playtest of a second demo level (§0) a way meant to be learned by nothing but the shot that opens it was seen through its own shutter and told by the text before the shot; the spec now has the fourth word for that way, **`hidden`**, and the engine confirms it — the eye, the shot and the text.
- **Research**: §2 is this spec's record; `docs/reference/sight-through-blocks.md` is the record of what the eye looks through. Each statement below is **cited** (a reading of the tree at a named revision, a constitution rule, a research page, a named outside source, or a confirmation — marked *owner-confirmed*) or **authored** (this spec chooses).
- **Numbers**: spec `0118`. Diagnostics: **`DW1053`** (a shut way the party meets, learned by no declared means, §6.1), **`DW1054`** (a declaration the world does not confirm, §6.2), and **`DW1060`** (a secret told by text before the shot that shows it, §6.4). The surface change of the original text is stage 2 at `dsl_version` 0.59.0; the amendment adds the word `hidden` and the field `gating.secret[]` and takes `0.62.0` (spec-0120's amendment shares it).
- **Non-goals**: a threshold on a branch's length or rise — no source gives one (§2.3), so none is judged; both are **measured and written** (§5.4), never refused. Whether a player can be expected to spot a bar once it is in their view (render distance, fog, light) — the demo level is where that is looked at. Where the author shuts a corridor — craft advice, §9, never a check. `DW1011`'s own population and rule, unchanged. A `laid` way, a `timed_gates[]` clock, a prefab's own gate station: not a gated connection, not judged here. Whether the text that *does* reach the player before a shot reads well — `game-writing.md` is the record; this spec judges only that the declared secret is not in it.

## 0. Amendment — a hidden way is seen, and the engine did not look

### 0.1 What the engine passed, and why

**Cited** (the tree at `57f0e7026e9d5c4f2df8529a0b4b49b041424eb1`; *The Hearth Door* at `efa28499d54aed83a32365d28e794a411f0150a3`, built by that engine; `validation/shut-ways.json`, `validation/gate-seal.json` and the `DW1011` and shut-ways binding lines read):

- The level's one gated connection is `edge/hearth-door` (`walk`, `node/hall` → `node/passage`, `gating: {flags: [flag/hearth-out]}`, **no `learned`**). Its seam gate `anchor/seam-hearth-door` is nine cells of `minecraft:iron_bars[east=true,west=true]` at `[10..12, 64..66, 19]`; one cell in front of it, in the hall, a second gate `anchor/hearth-fire` is three cells of `minecraft:campfire` at `[10..12, 64, 21]`, cleared by the same beat. The passage's reveal is a `cutscene` in that beat's effect list, `look_at: anchor/seam-hearth-door`.
- **Nothing owed a word.** `verify_with_eye` (`learned.rs`, line 541 at that revision) judges a connection only at a face the party's reach holds: `meet` (`shutmodel.rs`, line 212) keeps a face when `reach.contains` one of its cells a body can be in, and `Meeting::met` (line 191) yields the faces so held. The hall-side face of the seam gate is the row at `z 20` under the mantel; the reach is `party_reach`'s closure over a world in which every gate region is *made solid* in its shut state (`Configurations::judged`, line 325: *every region cell made solid*), so the campfire row at `z 21` is a wall and `z 20` is never reached. The binding line said it exactly: `shut ways (DW1053/DW1054): 1 gated connection(s) …; over 2 configuration(s), 0 shut way(s) met at a face — 0 declared …, 0 undeclared …`; `DW1011` beside it: `2 (gate, configuration) pair(s) shut, … 1 with the party at a face` — the fire's. So §5.1's population, *a face the party's reach holds*, held nothing, and `DW1053` had nothing to refuse. The player stood two cells from a grille and looked through it into the passage.
- **Had it looked, it would have looked with the wrong eye.** `sight` (`learned.rs`, lines 458–481) walks `nav::walk_cells` from the player's eye to each region cell and stops at `World::blocks_camera` (`body.rs`, line 515: `solid ∪ tall ∪ use_gates`, documented as *block geometry a cutscene camera must not fly through*). `solid` is the collision class `FullCube`, and `minecraft:iron_bars` is `FullCube` by the conservative default (`blockshape.rs`, `the_conservative_default_still_refuses`, line 1525, whose stated direction is *over-block a body*). §1 of the original text recorded that classification as settling *a bar is a thing the sight walk hits, never a thing it sees through*. That is the collision answer, and the game's renderer gives the other one: iron bars are built `noOcclusion()` and cull nothing behind them (`sight-through-blocks.md` §1). A check that asked *is this way hidden* with that predicate would have called the grille a wall.
- **No word covered the way.** `seen`, `told` and `rewarded` each say how the player learns the way is shut; none says *the player does not learn of it until the shot shows it*. `told` is confirmed by `answered_by.is_some()` (line 647) and `rewarded` by a reward on the branch (line 651); neither reads the eye at all, so a way carrying either word is as visible as its shutter lets it be, and nothing judges that.
- **The text told it first.** The hall's arrival trigger (`trigger/the-burning-hearth`, a `narrate`) says the records are kept behind the hearth and that the sluice floods its flue; the quest plan's goal says the sluice puts out the hearth. Every one of those strings is placed by the play-order walk (`Flow::journal`, the walk `DW0982` dates strings with) **before** the step whose effect list carries the shot. No rule read them against the shot.

**The general form.** Three, each wider than the hearth. (i) Any shut way whose approach cell is taken — by a second gate region, a prop, a holder, a relight fixture, a hazard, water — is *never met* and owes no word, however plainly it stands in view: the population was a body's, and the finding is an eye's. (ii) Every sight question in the tree is answered with the collision model: `nav::learned::sight`, the loop proof's *Sight* (`compiler::loop`), the hazard watch cell (`nav::hazard`, whose own comment claims glass and grates are transparent to it, which the tree does not hold) and the declared sightline `DW0821` (`compiler::blockout`) each stop at `blocks_camera`; for the 547 colliding block ids that are not light-opaque (`sight-through-blocks.md` §3) — every stair, slab, wall, fence, door, trapdoor, pane, bar, chain and leaf block — the model calls the eye stopped where the player sees through. (iii) A reveal the author stages with a shot can be pre-empted by any player-facing string the walk places before it — a narrate, a hint, a title, a goal, a line, a tooltip, in any language — and no check reads the two together, although the walk that dates every string and the inventory that holds them both exist (`compiler::telling`).

### 0.2 The tightening, in those words

**Owner-confirmed**, from the playtest: only the close-up shot tells the player what is behind the fireplace — not the grille, not the text. **This amendment is a tightening** in each of its three parts, and admits nothing the original refused:

1. **A way the eye sees is met.** §5.1's population gains every (connection, configuration, face) at which **an eye in the party's reach sees the gate** — a sight line from the eye at any reached cell to a cell of the gate region that meets no hiding cell before it (`sight-through-blocks.md` §2) — beside every face the reach holds. A way met only by the eye owes the word as a way met at a face does (`DW1053`). More is met; nothing met before is released.
2. **The eye looks through what the game looks through.** `seen` and the new confirmations read `World::hides` — occlusion in the pinned jar — and never `blocks_camera`. For `seen` this admits a bar seen through a grille that the collision walk refused (a loosening of that one confirmation, declared here as one; criterion 4 asserts what it asserted plus the grille case); for the population and for `hidden` it is the tightening, because it is the only predicate under which a grille is not a wall.
3. **A fourth word, `hidden`, with three confirmations and no opt-out.** A way the author means the player not to know of until it opens declares `learned: hidden` and the **secret** it keeps (`gating.secret[]`, the words that would give it away). The engine confirms (a) **the eye**: in every configuration the way is shut in, no eye in the party's reach sees a cell of its gate region or a cell a body can be in on its far side without a hiding cell before it; (b) **the shot**: the effect list that opens the way carries a `cutscene` that looks at its seam; (c) **the text**: no player-facing string the walk places before the shot's step, in English or any sidecar, mentions a word of the secret, and every word of the secret is mentioned by some string at or after the shot (`DW1060`, §6.4). The word is never inert: a `hidden` way is judged in every configuration it is shut in, met or not. §3's rejected fourth word `none` is not this: `none` excused the finding's connection; `hidden` holds it to more than any other word does.

## 1. What the tree did at the ground revision, re-verified

- **Cited** (`layout.rs`, `Edge::shuts`): a connection is built shut exactly when it crosses a seam and its `gating` names a term — *the one predicate every reader of "is this way shut" asks*. `EdgeGating` is `{flags[], quest?, opens_from}` and nothing else. `opens_from` lives inside `gating` because *a side has no home outside `gating`, so it can only be named for a way that shuts* (`compiler.md`, `edges[].gating`); a `gating` naming a side and no term is `DW0818`.
- **Cited** (`nav/separate.rs`, `check_gates_separate`): the proof already computes, per quest configuration the critical path passes through (the configurations `DW0921` judges), the configuration's **route cells** (`critical_route_cells`), the party's **reach** (`closure`: the body-move closure of the route cells), each shut gate's **two faces** (`faces_of`, on the axis the seam hands), and whether the reach holds a face (`reached`, printed as *with the party at a face*). A gated connection declared `shortcut` is **counted and not judged** there, because for separation a declared way round is the author's statement; the east door of the gallery is one. Nothing reads *what the player learns* at a reached face.
- **Cited** (`nav/separate.rs`): the binding line is `DW1011 binding: N gate(s) resolved (N of N gated connection(s) among them); …; N with the party at a face, N got round …`; the ledger is `validation/separation-proof.json`, written from `emit/routes.rs`.
- **Cited** (`shortcut.rs`): `shortcuts[]` is `{id, gate, unlock, on_unlock[]}` and carries **no hint field**; the wrong-side answer is a `use` trigger the author anchors on the `gate`, riding the bodies `compiler::wrongside` stands on the sealed side (`compiler.md`, `shortcuts[]`). A sealed body nothing answers is `DW0429`, discharged by `QuestsContent::answers_press_at(anchor)` — *any `use` trigger at the anchor* — or a `close-gate`'s `sealed_hint`. The gallery's `trigger/east-door-wrong-side` is a `use` trigger at `anchor/seam-east-door`, audience `presser`, one `narrate`.
- **Cited** (`pressable.rs`, `Body::Region`, `shell_cells`; `promise.md`, `DW0963`): a `use` trigger anchored on **any gate region** — a gated connection's `anchor/seam-<edge>` included — arms the region's clickable shell, and *a one-block-deep doorway is all shell*: the bar cells themselves are the pressable, met from either face.
- **Cited** (`body.rs`): `has_line_of_sight(a, b)` walks `walk_cells` between two eyes 1.5 above their cells — the vanilla **mob** eye height, by its own comment — and holds when no cell on the segment `blocks_camera`; `blocks_camera` is `solid ∪ tall ∪ use_gates`; `walk_cells` tests its starting cell first and returns the **first** hit. The player's eye is `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` (1.62). The loop proof's *Sight* (`compiler.md`, *Seamlessness*) is the same walk from the player's eye.
- **Cited** (`blockshape.rs`, `the_conservative_default_still_refuses`): `minecraft:iron_bars` is `Collision::FullCube` to the **collision** model — a bar cell is in `solid`, so `blocks_camera` holds for it. **Corrected by the amendment** (§0.1): that is what a body meets, not what the eye meets; the renderer looks through a bar, and the sight walk that reads `blocks_camera` reads the wrong model. The sight predicate is `World::hides` (`sight-through-blocks.md` §2), and a bar is a thing the eye **sees through to the thing behind** — which is why a shut way barred by iron bars can be `seen` (the eye reaches the bar) and can never be `hidden`.
- **Cited** (`lethal.rs`, `check_danger_is_visible`): a staged volume is judged in the critical path's configurations **and** in every reachable branch's own path (`branch::realize`, `plan.branch_critical_path`, `plan.branch_gate_model`), and a volume no modelled body reaches is a **warning**, not a refusal, *because refusing would reject a legitimate design for an engine limitation*. `DW1011` reads the critical path's configurations only.
- **Cited** (`plan/anchors.rs`; `objective.rs`; `loot.rs`; `wave.rs`; `ambush.rs`): every anchor resolves to a `Point {pos}` or a `Gate {from, to}`; a `collect` and an `interact` carry an `anchor`, a `kill` names a `wave` whose `anchor` is where its mobs spawn, a `loot[]` entry names the `anchor` whose cell holds its holder, an `ambush` names the anchor `at` which it fires. These are the pickups, interactions and fights a campaign binds to cells.
- **Cited** (the finding row, mechanism only): the row for the long climb to a shut way is triaged `capability` with carrier `null`, and its binding is `layout-graph.json` edges of `class: barred` — a class the tree no longer has (`DW0100`); its note says the check is to be built and the row reads *no general form* until it lands.
- **Cited** (`telling.rs`, the amendment's ground): every player-facing string of a campaign is in the l10n inventory (`TextKind`: title, description, objective hint, refusal, dialogue, bark, option label, button tooltip, item name, item tooltip, prompt, narration; the quest `goal` is inventoried in its own right), and `DW0982` places each at *the first state of the compiler's play order at which the player can read it* — the walk `Flow::journal` takes along the exported critical path and every realised branch — with an undated string placed at the start. The `happening.text` is *never player-visible* and is not inventoried.

## 2. What practice does

**Cited** — a dead end is acceptable when the player **can see it before deciding to go in**, and dead ends are not to be thrown in the player's way without warning (Totten, *Dead Man's Trail*, Game Developer; a practitioner's account of internal playtests, no method stated); a locked fire door with a pane the exit is seen through, so the player explores other avenues knowing the goal (Laidlaw, in Saltzman's *Secrets of the Sages*); long trips to dead-end rooms are bad, even for a power-up (Jaquays, same collection); branches and dead ends were simplified after watching playtesters get lost (Valve, *Half-Life 2: Episode Two* developer commentary); a room with one exit is avoided so the player is never stuck (Jensen, IOI, via GMTK).

**Cited** — a dead end that is entered must **lead to something**: dead ends without meaning are not acceptable (Glans, *On Flow*, Game Developer); locked doors kept off the critical path, with the locked room likely to hold extra treasure (Short, Kitfox); exploration raises the expectation that the effort will be acknowledged by a reward or by interactive content, and disappointment follows when it is not (Gómez-Maureira et al., CHI PLAY 2021, N = 254 — the exploration figures are measured; the disappointment is participants' comments).

**Cited** — the one-way door: a door the player finds locked and later opens permanently from the other side, so the start is not overwhelmed with branches (Brown, *Boss Keys*, on Dark Souls — a critic's analysis; no first-party statement of the design was found). **Owner-confirmed**: the press at the wrong side of a one-way door that says *this does not open from this side* is the valuable part — the wrong-side press teaches the player the loop.

**Cited** — at a fork, about three quarters of participants took the longest branch first, and at one fork 73–80 % chose the longest line of sight (Marples, Huddersfield doctoral thesis, 74 screen / 58 VR participants, 8 forks). **Authored** from it: a long, open, rising branch is the shape most likely to be walked first, so the shape of the finding is the shape this preference amplifies.

**Cited** — the secret door: a way the player is not meant to know of until it opens is a staple of the form (the bookcase door, the fireplace passage), and its effect depends on the player not having been told (`game-writing.md` L1: *the level carries the information; the text only restates it*, and a journal *never names a place, person or thing the player has not been told*). **Owner-confirmed**: a passage a shot reveals is told to the player by that shot and by nothing before it. **Authored**: the secret door is the fourth way a shut way is learned — *not at all, until it opens* — and it is a declaration because the engine cannot tell a hidden way from a forgotten one.

### 2.3 What practice does not give

**Cited** (the research record): no source states a length, a step count or a rise at which a branch to a shut way becomes too long; the one dedicated essay (Gee, *Dead-End Theory in Level Design*, SMU Guildhall) could not be read. The constitution refuses an invented number (*a craft question the record does not answer is researched, never invented*). So the rule here is **not a threshold**: it is the two conditions the sources agree on — *known before entering* or *paid for entering* — plus the one-way door and the secret door, each made a declaration the engine confirms.

### 2.4 The confirmation

**Owner-confirmed.** Every story-shut way a body can reach or an eye can see declares how the player learns it is shut, one of: **visible from the junction** where its branch leaves the open route; **told at the near face** by a pressable hint (the souls one-way door: the press says *this does not open from this side*); **the branch ends in a reward**; or **hidden until the shot that opens it shows it**. The engine confirms each declaration — a sight line from the junction; a pressable with an answer at the near face; a bound pickup, interaction or fight on the branch; no sight line from anywhere reached, a shot on the opening beat, and no text before the shot that tells the secret. **An undeclared one is refused.**

**Cited** (CLAUDE.md, *A check confirms intent*; the brief's reading): the declaration is the intent, and requiring the declaration is the pattern `DW1051` adopts for a press — the engine supplies the body, the state and the check; the author supplies the word. The check never infers which of the four the author meant.

## 3. The decision

**Three sentences, authored.**

1. **`gating` gains `learned`**, one of `seen | told | rewarded | hidden`: how the player learns this way is shut — or, for `hidden`, that they do not, until it opens. It lives inside `gating` for the reason `opens_from` does — only a way that shuts can be learned shut — and `DW0818` widens to refuse a `learned` with no term, a `hidden` with no `secret`, and a `secret` on any other word.
2. **A shut way the party meets owes the word.** For every gated connection, in every configuration `DW1011` judges, at every face the party's reach holds while the connection is shut — a declared `shortcut` **included**, because the wrong side of a one-way door is exactly the face this rule is about — **and at every face an eye in the reach sees** (§5.1), a connection with no `learned` is refused (`DW1053`).
3. **The engine confirms the word it is given** (`DW1054`, `DW1060`): `seen` — from the junction where the branch to that face leaves the route cells, the eye reaches the bar through nothing that hides; `told` — a `use` trigger answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>` (`answers_press_at`, the predicate `DW0429` reads); `rewarded` — a bound pickup, interaction or fight stands on the branch's ground; `hidden` — no eye in the reach sees the way or its far side while it is shut, the beat that opens it shows it, and the text before that beat keeps its secret.

**Why a declaration and not a rule.** The research record weighed five rules the engine could compute without asking (junction visibility for every way; a step cap; a rise cap; zero-length branches; report-only). Each of the first four refuses a legitimate design somebody has shipped — the one-way door round a corner, the tower-top lock seen from below and returned to, the short walk past a threshold that makes a door memorable — and each infers an intent the author never declared, which the constitution forbids. The fifth catches nothing. The declaration is the only shape that both refuses the finding's case (a long climb to a way that is neither seen from its foot, nor answers a press, nor pays) and admits every one of those designs, because each of them is one of the words. The secret door is the same argument once more: a way nobody sees and nobody is told of is either hidden on purpose or forgotten, and only the author can say which — so the author says it, and the engine holds the saying to the eye, the shot and the text.

**Rejected, authored.**

- *Confirm every word and pass on any.* That is the inferring rule again, with four heads; a design whose author meant *told* and whose branch happens to hold a chest would pass on the wrong reason, and the message could name no remedy.
- *A fifth word, `none`.* It would be the opt-out secured by a property the defect can supply (CLAUDE.md, *Vacuity*): the finding's connection would carry `none` and go green. `hidden` is not `none`: it is the word with the most to confirm.
- *Judge at the document.* Whether a face is reached, whether an eye sees, where the branch leaves the route and what the eye meets are world facts; `told` alone is a document fact and is still judged in the one place with the others, so that one module answers *how is this way learned* (CLAUDE.md, *One question, one implementation*). The text confirmation of `hidden` is the exception that proves the rule: it is a question of the play order and the inventory, which `compiler::telling` already answers for `DW0982`, so it lives there and `nav::learned` reads its verdict — one module per question, never two copies of the walk.
- *Reading the secret from the far place's name or `intent`.* The words that give a secret away are the author's to know (`behind the hearth` gives away a passage named nothing of the kind); an inferred list would be the inferred intent the constitution forbids, and an empty inferred list would be the opt-out.

**`learned`** is an optional field of `EdgeGating`, a closed set: `seen`, `told`, `rewarded`, `hidden`. Optional in the schema because a gated connection no body meets and no eye sees shut owes no word (§6.3); owed by `DW1053` where one does. **`secret`** is a non-empty list of words or phrases, each non-empty, present exactly when `learned` is `hidden` (`DW0818` widens by the two shapes: `hidden` without `secret`, `secret` beside another word or no word). A `gating` that names `learned` and no term is `DW0818`, in the words that rule already uses for `opens_from`. A `learned` or a `secret` on a `carry`'s gating is `DW0100` (nothing on a carry shuts, so nothing is learned). No other document moves: the site plan's seams, the quest documents and the detail plan are read, not written.

**The four words, defined over the world** (§5), each in the player's terms: `seen` — *I can see the bar from where I would turn off*; `told` — *I walked up and pressed, and it told me*; `rewarded` — *the walk was worth it anyway*; `hidden` — *I did not know it was there until it opened in front of me*.

## 5. The analysis — `compiler::nav::learned`

Build tier, beside `DW1011`, from the same call site in `emit/routes.rs`, over the same configurations, faces and reach. **One authority**: the configurations are `critical_route_cells` grouped by `region_state_at` as `check_gates_separate` groups them; the faces are `faces_of`; the reach is `closure`; a cell a body can be in is `can_be`; **a cell the eye looks through is `!World::hides`** (`sight-through-blocks.md` §2, the one sight predicate, read from the pinned jar's occlusion table — never `blocks_camera`, which answers what a camera flies into). The implementation **extracts** these from `check_gates_separate` into functions both proofs call, and lands an agreement test that drives both over generated worlds and asserts the same (gate, configuration, face) set is *reached* by each. Nothing here is a second copy of the separation proof's model.

### 5.1 The population

Every gated connection (`Edge::shuts()`), keyed by `anchor/seam-<edge>`, with the axis its seam hands — the population `DW1011` resolves. For each configuration `C`, for each connection `G` shut in `C` (every region cell solid in `C`'s state), for each face `f` of `G`: the triple `(G, C, f)` is a **shut way met** when the reach of `C` holds a cell of `f`, **or when an eye in the reach of `C` sees `G` from `f`'s side** — a cell `e` of the reach, on `f`'s side of the gate's axis, whose eye (`cell_center(e)` raised by `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT`) has a sight line to the centre of some cell of `G`'s region that meets no hiding cell before it. Unlike `DW1011`, a connection declared `shortcut` is **judged**, and a one-sided connection is judged at **whichever face the reach holds or sees**, not only its openable one: the wrong side of a one-way door is the face the player presses. The eye is tried from every reach cell on that side; the ledger names the nearest seeing cell by moves from the route. A face the reach neither holds nor sees is not met.

### 5.2 The junction and the branch

**Authored.** From the route cells of `C`, a multi-source shortest path over the body moves `closure` floods (`World::body_moves`: walking, falling, jumping, swimming, climbing) to the nearest cell of `f` — or, for a face met by the eye alone, to the nearest cell from which the eye sees `G` — with the route cells as sources; the path's **last route cell** is the **junction** `J`, the cells after it are the **branch path** `P`. The **branch ground** `B` is the ground only the branch leads to: the cells a body reaches from `f` that it cannot reach from the open route without passing a **cut** — the body-move closure from `f`, less the body-move closure of the route cells with the cut taken out (the open side), less every cell from which one body move enters the open side, less the route cells themselves. It is a cut reading over the body graph the shut model already floods: the cut is what separates the pocket from the open route, and where it separates nothing — an open floor, round which the open side flows — `B` is empty, and nothing on that floor pays for a branch, because an open floor is not a branch. Where `f` is itself a route cell, `J = f`, `P` is empty and `B` is whatever only `f` leads to, usually nothing: the shut way stands on the open route and the player is already at it. For a face met by the eye alone, `B` is empty: a way the player cannot walk up to pays for no walk. **The earlier reading tightened §5.2 and did not loosen it**: the reading before it, the closure from `f` that enters no route cell, flowed round a route one cell wide and took most of an open room as the branch (the gallery's site-plan point measured 454 cells at every face met before the muster), so a reward anywhere in the room confirmed `rewarded`.

**The cut is the narrowest cross-section the branch path passes that separates `f` from the open side**, tried in this order, the first whose ground is not empty taken:

1. **The path's own cells**, `J ∪ P` — one body wide, the narrowest a path can be. A dead end entered through an opening one body wide is cut here, exactly as the reading before this amendment cut it. *Cited*: that reading (above), kept first so every ground it admitted is still admitted.
2. **The full declared width of a declared way the path walks**: `J ∪ P` with every cell of one **station** of a declared way that a cell of `J ∪ P` lies in, across the station's whole width. The stations are tried narrowest first; among stations of equal width, the one the path walks nearer `f` first; then by the way and the station in words (ADR-0006). *Cited* (spec-0120 §1.2, §1.3, §3, §5): the ways and their stations are spec-0120's, taken from its one population (`compiler::nav::width::population`) — every portal (its plane included, which `DW1057` defers to `DW0836` but a body still passes), stair run, contact, way place and piece way, each station's cells the cells a body passing any of its columns can be in, its width its column count, the denominator `DW1057` judges its clear width against. *Authored*: narrowest first, because the choke of a branch is where it is narrowest; nearer `f` first among equals, because the cut nearer the shut way claims the least ground for the branch, so a way the open floor continues into — a road whose every station spans it — never hands that floor to the branch through a station far from the face; only a cut that leaves ground counts, because a cross-section the open side flows round cuts nothing.
3. **Otherwise** `B` is empty, and the cut is the path's.

**A branch that walks no declared way** is cut by its own cells alone, step 1, and nothing else. *Cited* (spec-0120 §1.2; §4, *Reading the width from the reach graph*, rejected): the engine never measures the bytes to learn how wide a way is — the author said — and a width read from where a body can be measures the very thing a narrowing produces. So no cross-section is measured where none is declared: a pocket entered through an undeclared opening wider than one body is open ground, and the author who means it as a branch declares the way the branch walks (a seam's `opening`, a box's `way`) or writes the word that is true.

**This loosens §5.2, declared as a loosening**: the ground a branch is judged by only grows — step 1 is the old cut and is kept wherever it leaves ground, and a wider cut takes more cells out of the open side, never fewer — so a `rewarded` the reading before this amendment refused can now be confirmed: a dead end up a declared stair three wide (the demo level's keeper's hatch, §8) or through a declared way wider than one body. No acceptance criterion asserts less: criteria 1–15 assert what they asserted, and criterion 16 is added. The ledger row names the cut (§5.4).

Two measurements are taken from `P`, **written and never judged** (§5.4): its **length** in moves, and its **rise**, the highest `y` on `P` minus `J`'s `y`.

### 5.3 The four confirmations

- **`seen`.** From the player's eye at `J` — `cell_center(J)` raised by `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT`, the player's and not the mob's 1.5 — `nav::walk_cells` is walked to the centre of each cell of `G`'s region; the declaration **holds** when, for at least one region cell, **no cell before it hides** (`World::hides`). A walk that meets a hiding cell first is blocked there; the message names the blocking cell per region cell, as `DW0821` names every blocking cell. A hatch is seen from above, a cover from below on the ladder, a bar in a wall from along the branch, a grille through a fence: one definition, the loop proof's *Sight* once it reads `hides`, with the bar as the seen cell. **Declared as a loosening of this one confirmation** (§0.2): a bar behind a grille, a fence or a pane, which the collision walk refused, is seen.
- **`told`.** `QuestsContent::answers_press_at(anchor/seam-<edge>)` holds — the predicate `DW0429` reads, so a `use` trigger that satisfies the one satisfies the other. The body is the region's shell (`Body::Region`), met from either face, or the wrong-side bodies where the connection is a `shortcuts[]` gate. What the line says is the author's (`game-writing.md` F2); that it is said in a state the presser can be in is spec-0117's question, not this one.
- **`rewarded`.** At least one **reward anchor** resolves to a `Point` whose cell is in `B` or is a face-neighbour of a cell in `B`. The reward anchors are: a `collect` objective's `anchor`; an `interact` objective's `anchor`; a `loot[]` entry's `anchor`; the `anchor` of a wave a `kill` objective names; an `ambush`'s `at`. A `reach-anchor` is not a reward — arriving is the thing the branch already made the player do. The message names the anchors examined and the nearest to `B`.
- **`hidden`.** Judged in **every** configuration `G` is shut in, whether or not any face is met, and over the whole reach of each. (a) **The eye.** For every reach cell `e` and every target cell `t` — the cells of `G`'s region that do not themselves hide, and every cell a body can be in (`can_be`) inside the box of the node on the far side of `G` from `e` — the sight line from `e`'s eye to `t`'s centre meets a hiding cell before `t`. A region cell that hides is a wall the player sees and is not a target; a region cell that does not hide (a grille, a pane, a door, a trapdoor) is a target, because looking at it is looking through it. The first failing `(e, t)` by moves from the route, then by cell (ADR-0006), is the message's: *seen from `[x, y, z]`: the eye reaches `[x', y', z']` (`minecraft:iron_bars`) through nothing that hides*. (b) **The shot.** The effect list that opens `G` — the `on_objective_complete`, `on_complete`, trigger or option effects carrying the `open-gate` of `anchor/seam-<edge>` (or of a gate region cleared by the same list) — carries a `cutscene` whose `look_at` anchor, or one of whose `path` anchors, is `anchor/seam-<edge>`. The shot's **step** is the play-order step of that effect list. (c) **The text.** `compiler::telling`'s verdict for `G` (§6.4): no player-facing string placed before the shot's step mentions a word of `secret[]`, and every word of `secret[]` is mentioned by a string at or after it. A `hidden` way that is never shut in any judged configuration is counted inert like any other word (§6.3).

### 5.4 The ledger and the binding line

`validation/shut-ways.json` carries one row per shut way met: the connection, its class and gating, the configuration (as `DW1011` words it, *while `obj/…` is next*), the face and whether it is the openable one, **how it is met** (*at a face* / *by the eye from `[x, y, z]`*), `J`, the branch length and rise, the cut (§5.2: the path's own cells, or the way and station and its width), the declaration, and the confirmation's finding (seen from `J` / blocked at `[x,y,z]`; answered by `trigger/…` / nothing answers; rewarded by `anchor/…` / no reward within `B` of `N` cell(s)). A `hidden` way adds one row per configuration it is shut in: the reach's size, the targets examined, the eye verdict (hidden / seen from `[x, y, z]` at `[x', y', z']`), the shot (the effect list and its step / none), and the text verdict (the strings read before the shot, the first mention if any, the words told after). The row is written for green and red alike; a campaign with no gated connection writes an empty ledger with its zero.

The binding line, on every build: `shut ways (DW1053/DW1054): G gated connection(s); over C configuration(s), M shut way(s) met — A at a face, E by the eye alone — D declared (S seen, T told, R rewarded, H hidden), U undeclared, F declaration(s) not confirmed; I declared and never met shut; K hidden way(s) judged over V configuration(s), X eye line(s) examined`. Every count is a number; `0` is written as `0`.

## 6. Diagnostics

### 6.1 `DW1053` — a shut way the party meets, learned by no declared means

Error, build tier (exit 3), `compiler::nav::learned`, raised once per gated connection (not per configuration) at the first configuration and face it is met shut without a `learned`. Quantifier: every shut way met (§5.1), at a face or by the eye. The message names the connection with its class and gating, the configuration, the face (near hall side / far hall side, by node), how it is met (*the party stands at its face* / *the party sees it from `[x, y, z]`*), `J`, the branch's length and rise, and the four words with what each would need here: *`seen` — the bar is [seen from `J` / hidden from `J` behind `[x,y,z]`]; `told` — [`trigger/…` answers a press / nothing answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>`]; `rewarded` — [`anchor/…` stands on the branch / no pickup, interaction or fight within the branch's `N` cells]; `hidden` — [the way is seen from `[x, y, z]` through `[x', y', z']` (`block`), so it cannot be hidden as built / nothing in the reach sees it; the opening beat [carries / does not carry] a shot of its seam]*. Remedy: write the word that is true, or make one true and write it. The remedy row in `remedy_reachability.rs` writes `seen` on a connection whose bar the junction sees and asserts green; a second row writes `hidden` with a `secret` on a connection behind an opaque shutter with a shot and asserts green.

### 6.2 `DW1054` — a declaration the world does not confirm

Error, build tier (exit 3), same module. Quantifier: every shut way met whose connection declares `learned`, and every `hidden` connection in every configuration it is shut in. One code, five shapes, by the word: *declared `seen`, but from `J` the eye meets `[x,y,z]` (`block`) before the bar*; *declared `told`, but nothing answers a press at `anchor/seam-<edge>`* (the message carries the `use` trigger JSON `DW0429` prescribes, so the two rules name one remedy); *declared `rewarded`, but no pickup, interaction or fight stands within the branch's `N` cell(s)* — naming the nearest reward anchor and its distance; *declared `hidden`, but the way is seen from `[x, y, z]`: the eye reaches `[x', y', z']` (`block`) through nothing that hides* — naming the eye cell, the target and, where the target is the shutter itself, that the shutter's block does not hide (*`minecraft:iron_bars` is looked through; a shutter that hides is a block the pinned game draws as a full opaque cube*); *declared `hidden`, but the beat that opens it carries no shot of `anchor/seam-<edge>`* — naming the effect list. Remedy, always the world's or the word's: move or open the geometry so the bar is seen; write the trigger; place the reward; make the shutter hide, wall the line of sight or move the eye's ground; add the shot to the opening beat; or change the word to the one that is true. Five remedy rows.

### 6.3 Inert declarations

A `learned` on a gated connection **never met shut** in any judged configuration — not at a face, not by the eye — is **counted** (*I declared and never met shut*) and raised as a **warning** under `DW1054`, naming the connection — not refused. A `hidden` on a connection never shut in any judged configuration is the same warning. **Cited** reason: the judged configurations are the critical path's (`DW1011`), and the closure is branch-blind (`compiler.md`, *Reachability*), so a way met shut only on a branch's own path is invisible to this population; refusing there would reject a legitimate design for an engine limitation, the rule `DW0891` states for a volume no modelled body reaches. Widening the population to branch paths is §11's first item.

### 6.4 `DW1060` — a secret told by text before the shot that shows it

Error, build tier (exit 3), `compiler::telling`, beside `DW0982` and over the same walk. Quantifier: every gated connection declaring `learned: hidden`, and every word of its `secret[]`. **The shot's step** is handed by `nav::learned` (§5.3 b): the play-order step of the effect list that opens the way and carries the shot; where no shot exists, `DW1054` has already refused and this check reports *no shot to read against* and judges nothing. **A mention** is an occurrence of a secret word or phrase, matched as `DW0982` matches a name (`core_of`, `occurrences`: the word's own spelling, case-insensitive, and its definite reference), in any inventoried player-facing string (`TextKind`, every kind, the quest `goal` included; never the `happening.text`), in English and in every sidecar language. **Two shapes under one code**, opposite in remedy: (i) *told early* — a string the walk places **before** the shot's step mentions a word; an undated string (an environment trigger, a refusal, a reaction bundle, loot — the strings `DW0982` places at the start) counts as before, because the dating errs toward silence for a name and must err toward refusal for a secret; the message names the string's key, its language, the word and the step at which the player reads it against the shot's step; (ii) *never told* — a word no string at or after the shot's step mentions: a secret nobody ever tells is a dummy word, the property a defect could supply, and is refused naming the word. Remedy for (i): cut the word from the string or move the string after the shot; for (ii): tell it after the shot, or remove the word. The binding line: `secrets (DW1060): K hidden way(s), W secret word(s); N string(s) read before the shot(s), M mention(s) refused; W' word(s) told after`.

**Why a diagnostic, and why also a page** (the brief's question). The finding is machine-recognisable once the author has said what the secret is: the walk that dates every string and the inventory that holds them in every language already exist for `DW0982`, and the shot's step is a fact of the plan. What no machine reads is whether a string that names none of the words still gives the game away (*the sluice that floods its flue* tells the player the flue matters); that is craft, and it lives on the pages §9 names. The two are one rule stated at two strengths: the diagnostic holds the declared words; the page holds the author to the rest.

## 9. Craft guidance — advisory, for the skill

Lands in the skill page that teaches the layout graph and gating (`references/placement.md`, the `layout-graph.json` item), as advice with its motivating case, no plot: **shut a corridor at the end the player reaches first, not at the end they reach last** — a long way joining two areas that the story opens later is shut at its mouth by the earlier area, so the player who finds it shut has spent two steps, not the corridor; **hide the shut way with structure that belongs to the setting** — a collapsed arch, a portcullis in its housing, a stacked barricade — rather than a bare block in a doorway; **when it opens, show the player the opened place** — a short close-up shot of the changed mouth says the way is open better than a line does. **Owner-confirmed** as craft, never a check: the engine confirms the declaration of §4 and nothing about where the bar stands.

**Added by the amendment, two lines, the first on `placement.md` beside the above, the second on the page that teaches the content documents (`references/content.md`, step 5) with a pointer from `placement.md`:**

> **A way the player is not meant to know of hides behind something the eye cannot see through.** A grille, a pane, a fence, a door, a trapdoor, a stair or a slab is looked through or past in the game; a hearth's back, a bookcase, a plug of the wall's own stone is not. The shutter of a hidden way is a block the game draws as a full opaque cube, and nothing the player can stand on has a line past it into the place behind. The beat that opens it carries the close-up shot of the opened mouth: the shot is what tells the player.

> **A secret the level shows is not told by the text first.** Where a shot reveals a way, a room or a thing, no line the player reads before that shot — a narrate on arrival, an objective's title or hint, a quest's goal, a line of dialogue, a tooltip, in any language — names it or says where it is. The text before the shot says what the player can see and what they are to do (*the hearth burns; the sluice is at the lane's end*), never what the shot will show (*the records are kept behind it*). The reveal is the shot's; the text after it may say anything.

The record's form of the second line, for `docs/reference/game-writing.md` §8 as **L4**, beside L1 (*the level carries the information; the text only restates it*):

> **L4 — A secret the level shows is not told by text before it is shown.** A reveal the design stages — a passage behind a fireplace opened by a beat and shown by a close-up shot — is told to the player by that shot and by nothing before it: no arrival line, hint, title, goal or tooltip names the hidden thing or says where it is. Text before the reveal describes what is in view and what is to be done; text after it may name what was shown. The engine holds the declared words of the secret to the play order (`DW1060`); the rest of the sentence is the author's. **Authored**, from a playtest, on L1's pattern.

## 11. What stays open

- **Branch-path configurations.** `DW1011` and this check judge the critical path's configurations; `DW0891` already enumerates every reachable branch's own path. The one configuration enumeration both should read is the general form; this spec takes the existing population and counts the inert word (§6.3) rather than widening two proofs at once.
- **The junction is one cell.** `J` is where the shortest branch leaves the route; a player may leave it a cell to either side. The ledger carries `J`; the demo level is where it is seen whether one cell is enough, and a wider junction (the route cells within one move of `J`) is the recorded next shape if it is not.
- **A `told` line's side.** The region shell is pressable from both faces, so a line true on the wrong side (*this does not open from this side*) is said on the opening side too for a gated connection that is not a `shortcuts[]` gate. `compiler::wrongside` solves it for shortcuts; a one-sided gated connection reached from both faces in different configurations is a gap, recorded here, that spec-0117's state-gated answers may close.
- **Two chokes in series.** The cut is one cross-section, the narrowest that separates (§5.2), so where a branch climbs a stair three wide to a ledge and enters a closet through a door one wide, the closet is the branch ground and the ledge between the two is not; a reward on the ledge does not confirm `rewarded` for a way in the closet. Reading the ground behind every separating cross-section the path walks, not only the narrowest, is the recorded next shape, decided on a demo level that has the shape.
- **An undeclared wide mouth.** A branch through an opening wider than one body that no declared way spans is cut by its own cells (§5.2), so a dead end behind it reads as open ground. The remedy is a declaration, never a measured width; whether creators meet this where no seam or way place is natural is a playtest question.
- **Length and rise.** Written, never judged; the thresholds the sources do not give are measured on the demo level and on playtests, and a rule is proposed only once a number has a source.
- **The eye is a cell's centre at 1.62.** The hidden confirmation walks one line per (reach cell, target), eye at the cell's centre; a player leaning to a cell's edge sees a little more round a corner. A widened eye (the four corners of the reach cell) is the recorded next shape, decided on *The Hearth Door* if a corner line finds what the centre line does not.
- **A told way that is also seen.** `told` and `rewarded` are confirmed without reading the eye, so a way the junction sees and the press answers passes on either word; which word is true when two are is the author's, and the ledger writes the eye verdict for every met way so the author can read it. Whether the engine should prefer `seen` when it holds is not decided here.
- **Partial occlusion.** A stair, a slab or a trapdoor hides the part of its cell it fills; the rule reads them as looked through (`sight-through-blocks.md` §2, the stricter direction for `hidden`). A shutter built of stairs is therefore never hidden; whether that refuses a design somebody wants is a playtest question.

## Acceptance criteria

Each is a test or a gate run, named; a criterion the tree cannot yet satisfy is a recorded debt, never a pass. `delvec` is the implementing tree's binary, force-rebuilt before any comparison.

1. **Surface.** `delvec schema --stage layout-graph` exports `gating.learned` on the four traversal classes as the closed set `seen | told | rewarded | hidden` and `gating.secret` as a non-empty list of non-empty strings; `gating.learned` or `gating.secret` on a `carry` is refused as `DW0100`; `gating: {learned: "seen"}` with no term is refused as `DW0818` with the message naming `learned` beside `opens_from`; `learned: hidden` without `secret`, and `secret` beside any word but `hidden`, are each refused as `DW0818` (asserted); `tools/ci/check-gallery-coverage.py` reports `gating.learned`, each of its four values and `gating.secret` bound.
2. **One model.** `nav::separate` and `nav::learned` call the same extracted functions for configurations, faces, reach and `can_be`; an agreement test (`crates/delvec/tests/` or `#[cfg(test)]` in `nav/`) drives both over generated worlds and asserts the identical set of (gate, configuration, face) triples *reached at a face*, red on any difference. **One eye**: every sight walk in `nav::learned` is stopped by `World::hides` and by nothing else (asserted by a test that plants a grille on the line and a wall on the line and checks the two verdicts), and `World::hides` reads the committed occlusion table and no list in a module.
3. **The population.** On a fixture with a one-way door declared `shortcut` and met from its wrong side, `DW1011` counts the door (*declared a way round*) and this check judges it (*met at a face*); a test asserts both lines over one build. **Met by the eye**: a fixture whose shut way's approach cell holds a second gate region (a campfire row) and whose shutter is `minecraft:iron_bars` is met (*by the eye from `[x, y, z]`*) and, with no `learned`, refused `DW1053` with `E` at 1 and `A` at 0; the same fixture with the shutter a full opaque cube and no `learned` is not met and passes with `M` at 0 (asserted).
4. **`seen`.** A fixture with a bar at the end of a straight corridor off a route cell: `seen` holds and the ledger row names `J` and the bar cell; the same fixture with one hiding cell placed on the line of sight is refused `DW1054` naming that cell; **the same fixture with a fence post, then a glass pane, on the line is confirmed `seen`** (the declared loosening, asserted); the eye height used is `metrics::PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT` (asserted by a fixture whose line clears at 1.62 and not at 1.5). A hatch (thin on `y`) is confirmed from the route cell beside it; a cover is confirmed from a body on the rung below it.
5. **`told`.** A fixture with `learned: told` and a `use` trigger at `anchor/seam-<edge>` passes; the trigger removed is refused `DW1054` carrying the trigger JSON `DW0429` prescribes, and a test parses that prescription and asserts it clears both codes.
6. **`rewarded`.** A fixture with a chest (`loot[]`) on the branch ground passes; the chest moved onto a route cell is refused `DW1054` naming it as the nearest reward and its distance; a `reach-anchor` on the branch does not satisfy it (asserted).

### Recorded debts

- **Criterion 11's count.** The count of gated connections assumes the overlay's; a round that changes the overlay re-derives the number and states it in the test, never by hand here.
- **Criterion 12's page.** The codes are declared in `nav/learned.rs` and `telling.rs`, and the catalog gate (`tools/ci/check-dw-codes.py`) puts a code's row on the page of its declaring module, so the `DW1053` and `DW1054` rows are on `docs/reference/delvec/compiler/nav/learned.md` and `DW1060`'s on `docs/reference/delvec/compiler/telling.md`; `nav.md` carries a pointer beside `DW1011`. The criterion's page name is superseded by the gate, not loosened.
- **Criterion 9's red ledger.** A refused build writes no tree, so `validation/shut-ways.json` exists only for a build that passes; on a red the rows' findings reach the reader in the binding line and the refusal's message. Recorded debt: the ledger is not written on red.
- **Criterion 10's sight line and reward.** The remedy rows in `remedy_reachability.rs` cover writing `seen`, writing the prescribed trigger (clearing `DW1054` and `DW0429`) and changing the word, over the blockout fixture's gated door, and assert that a reward placed on that door's open hall floor is still refused. *Clear the sight line* and *place the reward on the branch* are asserted red→green over synthetic worlds in `nav::learned`'s unit tests, not in `remedy_reachability.rs`: the fixture's door has no branch (its face is on the hall's open floor), and it has no campaign-level surface that stands one blocking cell on the junction's line without a second geometry authority. Recorded debt. The `hidden` rows need a fixture with an opaque shutter and a shot; whether the blockout fixture can carry one without a second geometry authority is settled by the implementing round, and recorded here if not.
- **Criterion 11's `rewarded`.** No gated connection of the gallery has every face it is met at on a dead-end branch: each is met somewhere on open ground the route crosses, so under §5.2 no gallery way is `rewarded`. `Learned::rewarded` is refusal-proven by the probe `a-way-said-rewarded-on-an-open-floor` rather than bound; a confirmed `rewarded` is the demo level's keeper's hatch. Recorded debt: the gallery owes a dead-end branch to a shut way.
- **Criterion 11's words.** The built overlay measured the west and centre doors, the annex chute and the undercroft shaft hidden from their junctions, unanswered and unpaid, so each gained a `use` trigger at its seam and is `told`; the loft stair is `seen` from both of its faces, and the undercroft crawl `seen` (on the solid overlay, `told`, behind the hall wall from the undercroft side). `gallery/baseline/` therefore moves by the five triggers and their zh-cn lines as well as the ledger and the binding line — a wider move than the criterion names, declared here. **The amendment re-measures every word with the eye of §5.3**: a word measured *hidden from its junction* under the collision walk may be seen under `hides` (a bar behind a grille), and the met set may grow (criterion 3's eye); the round that lands it states the re-measured words and the baseline's move.
- **Criterion 14's re-run.** The row `str-19` is carried by `DW1053`; its campaign's own redo is the re-run that closes it, and the count of shut ways met there is the one measured on the redo's build (see the implementing commit body for what was measurable on this tree).
- **Criterion 17's table.** Until the occlusion table is committed, no check may call a cell hidden (`sight-through-blocks.md` §4); the `hidden` word and the met-by-the-eye population are blocked behind it, structurally — `World::hides` has no fallback and no hand list.
- **The eye.** `has_line_of_sight`'s 1.5 is the mob's eye and is read by the fight and hazard checks that want a mob's view; this check does not change it. Two eye heights in the model are two authorities for one question only if they answer the same question; a note on `has_line_of_sight` saying whose eye it is, is owed by the implementation. **The other sight consumers** (`compiler::loop::sight`, `nav::hazard`, `DW0821`) still read `blocks_camera` at the ground revision; `sight-through-blocks.md` §2 names each and its direction, and moving them is a change to those checks' own specs, recorded there, not here.
