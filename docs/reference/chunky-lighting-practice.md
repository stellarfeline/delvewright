# How Chunky users light a dusk scene with lanterns

How people who render Minecraft with Chunky set the sky, the emitters, the exposure, the tone curve and the sample budget for a dusk or night scene lit by torches and lanterns, set beside what the engine writes (`compiler.md` §4). Agent-facing; read before changing a scene's light.

Every source is listed with where it is, the Chunky version it speaks for, and what its licence allows (ADR-0013). Sources under CC BY-NC-SA, under no stated licence, or posted on a forum are **ideas only**: nothing below quotes them at length or reuses their text or images. A source read only through a search-result summary is marked *(summary only)*.

## 2. Practice beside the engine, setting by setting

| Setting | What users do | What the engine writes | Departure, and its cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky and sun at dusk | Simulated sky with the sun placed by hand: Nishita for twilight (S6), or a skymap or HDRI (S11), sun on for a key light | The game's own sky at the tick as an equirectangular map, sun light 0, the disc drawn (`compiler.md` §4) | Derived from the game rather than chosen for the look. It costs the warm directional key light and the sun's shadows a dusk render usually has, and leaves a flat evening, which is what the game draws. |
| Emitter intensity | Raised above the default 13 until torches read right, tuned by eye per scene (S7; 33 in S11) | 13 × `LANTERN_LIGHT_SCALE`, one value for every scene, fitted to the game model (`compiler.md` §4) | Same lever (S7), value derived instead of eyeballed. It costs per-view fit: resampling the twelve fitted frames puts the scale between 9.8 and 27.5. One value is right on average and wrong per view. |
| Per-material emittance | Raised per block type to taste (S3, S11) | The core's defaults (lantern, torch, campfire 1.0); only the night-vision emulation overrides materials | The engine keeps one global control. It loses the per-type balance users tune, e.g. lanterns against torches. |
| Emitter sampling | ONE or ALL to cut noise (S1, S2) | NONE | Measured on doune: ONE and ONE_BLOCK bought no noise per second of render (`showcase-shots.md` §8). No cost found. |
| Exposure | Set per scene by eye (S4) | 1.0; the tone curve carries the game's mid-tone | The no-hand-exposure rule. It costs the per-frame fix users make to a frame that reads dark or bright. |
| Tone curve | Whatever suits the look; ACES in S11 (S4) | Hable with its mid-grey held at the game's (`compiler.md` §4) | Chosen by measurement over the core's five curves, then anchored. No cost found against practice, which names no single choice. |
| Sample budget | 4096–16384 spp with emitters in daylight, 16384+ at night (S5); 2000–15,000 in S11 | 512 for the place frames, 1024 for the whole camp, 2048 for the climb and the night | A tenth to a thirtieth of what the manual asks for an emitter-lit scene. It costs fireflies and grain near emitters, and raising the emitter scale makes them worse. |
| The falloff gap | Not closed. Raise intensity until it looks right and denoise (S7, S11). The maintainers refuse a radius cap as unphysical (S8). Very high intensity makes the lit area outshine the lamp (S9). | One global scale, fitted so the mean display luma of lantern-lit pixels equals the game model's | Same kind of fix with a stated objective. It leaves the shape: near a lantern the render is brighter than the game and far away dimmer, with a per-pixel mean error of 18.5 luma on the fit set. |

## 3. What this decides

- **The frame is right in kind.** Users close the torch-light gap with the same single lever the engine uses: raise emitter intensity (S7, S11). Nothing found closes the falloff shape inside the core, and its maintainers say it cannot be closed without leaving path tracing (S8). **[cited]**
- **The value is derived where users eyeball it.** The scale is fitted to the game model by a stated objective (`compiler.md` §4), not tuned by eye per scene. **[authored]**
- **The sample budget departs from practice.** The manual's budget for an emitter-lit scene is 16384 spp or more (S5), and the engine's showcase frames take 512–2048. A frame lit by scaled emitters is noisier than the same frame at the default intensity, and practice answers that with samples and a denoiser, not a lower intensity (S5, S11). **[cited]**
- **The tone curve, the dusk sky and the exposure stay derived from the game.** Practice gives no single choice for any of them (S4, S6). **[authored]**
