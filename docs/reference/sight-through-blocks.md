# Sight through blocks

What a player's eye sees through, in the pinned game, and how the engine must read it. Agent-facing; the reader is whoever builds or reads a check that asks *can the player see this*. Every statement is **cited** (the pinned jar, a vendored dump named by path, or a reading of the tree at a named revision) or **authored** (this page chooses, and says the direction its error runs).

## 1. The two questions, and why they are not one

A cell holds a block. Two different things can be asked of it:

- **Does a body or a camera pass it?** — the collision question. The engine's one answer is `delvewright_dsl::blockshape::collision_class` (`crates/dsl/src/blockshape.rs`), and the nav model's `World::blocks_camera` (`crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/world/body.rs`) is that answer read for a cutscene camera: *block geometry a cutscene camera must not fly through: a full-cube solid, a 1.5-tall fence/wall, or a fence gate*.
- **Does it hide what is behind it?** — the sight question. **Cited** (the pinned jar, Minecraft Java 1.21.11): the renderer culls a face only when the neighbouring block state **occludes** — `BlockBehaviour.BlockStateBase.canOcclude()` together with an occlusion shape that fills the cell (`isSolidRender()` is the cached conjunction; the Java names here are *from memory* and are resolved from the pin's mappings by the dumper, §4, never written down by hand). A block built with `noOcclusion()` — iron bars, glass, panes, chains, leaves, a door, a trapdoor, a campfire — culls nothing behind it, so what stands beyond it is drawn and the eye sees it; a block that occludes but whose shape is not a full cube — a stair, a slab, a wall, a fence — hides the part of the cell it fills and nothing more, so the eye sees past it.

**Cited** (the tree at `57f0e7026e9d5c4f2df8529a0b4b49b041424eb1`): `blocks_camera` is `solid ∪ tall ∪ use_gates`, and `solid` is built from `collision_class(...) == FullCube`; `minecraft:iron_bars`, `minecraft:chain` and `minecraft:end_rod` are `FullCube` by the conservative default (`blockshape.rs`, `the_conservative_default_still_refuses`), whose stated direction is *over-block a body*. The shut-way sight walk `nav::learned::sight` (`crates/delvec/src/compiler/nav/learned.rs`) stops at `blocks_camera`. So the engine answers the sight question with the collision answer: a grille of iron bars, a fence, a pane of glass, a stair and a slab each stop the eye in the model, and each lets the eye through in the game. The direction of that error is the wrong one for a check that must prove a way **hidden**: the model calls hidden what the player sees.

## 2. The rule the engine reads

**Authored**, from §1. One predicate, `World::hides(cell)`: **a cell hides what is behind it exactly when its block state occludes with a full-cube occlusion shape in the pinned jar.** Everything else — air, water, a thin block, a no-collision fixture, a non-occluding block, an occluding block whose shape is not a full cube — is **looked through**. The predicate is a table read from the jar (§4), never a hand list and never derived from the collision class: the two questions have different answers for 547 of the 922 block ids that collide (§3).

**A sight walk** (`nav::walk_cells`, Amanatides–Woo over the segment from the eye to the target cell's centre; both endpoint cells included, the first hit returned) **is stopped by the first hiding cell strictly before its target.** A target reached with no hiding cell before it is **seen**, whatever the target itself is made of: a stone wall seen is a wall the player sees; a grille seen is a grille the player sees, and what stands behind the grille is seen too, because the grille does not hide. So:

- a shut way is **seen from an eye** when some cell of its gate region is seen;
- a place is **seen from an eye** when some cell a body can be in there (`nav::can_be`) is seen;
- a thing is **hidden from an eye** when no such line exists.

**Direction.** Where the table says a block does not hide, the player may still fail to see through it in practice (leaves at fancy graphics draw an opaque texture with holes; a trapdoor's slab fills a third of the cell). Reading those as looked-through can only make a *hidden* proof stricter and a *seen* proof more generous, which is the direction a check about what the player must not see is allowed to err in. Where the table says a block hides, the player sees nothing past it in the game, with no setting that changes it.

**What stays with `blocks_camera`, and what moves.** A cutscene camera flying into a fence post is the collision question (`nav::cutscene`), and `blocks_camera` keeps it. Every other reader of `blocks_camera` at the ground revision asks the sight question and is held to `hides` when the table lands, each move declared in its own direction where it lands: the shut-way sight walk (`nav::learned::sight`); the declared sightline between two places (`compiler::blockout`, `sightlines[]`, `DW0821` — a sightline through a grille then passes, a loosening); the loop proof's *Sight* (`compiler::loop::sight`, spec-0086 §4.3); and the hazard watch cell's line of sight (`nav::hazard`, whose own comment says *glass and grates are transparent to an eye exactly as they are to a camera* — a claim the tree does not hold: `collision_top_16` has no arm for glass or bars, `is_tall_barrier` is fences and walls, so both fall to `FullCube`, into `solid`, and stop the walk). A projectile's line (`nav::trap::blocks_projectile`) is a third question, collision plus water, and stays its own.

## 3. Measurements

**Instrument**: the vendored block table `harness/node_modules/minecraft-data/minecraft-data/data/pc/1.21.9/blocks.json` (the newest dump the harness carries; the pin is 1.21.11, so this is a cross-check of the jar read, never its replacement), read with `python3 -I`. `boundingBox: block` is the dump's collision flag; `filterLight` is the block's light opacity (15 = a full solid-render cube in vanilla's light model); `transparent` is the dump's rendering flag.

- 1166 block ids in the table; 922 collide; of those **547 are not light-opaque** (`filterLight < 15`) and 375 are. The 547 are the class the collision answer gets wrong for sight: 62 slabs, 58 stairs, 26 walls, 21 doors, 21 trapdoors, 16 glass, 16 glass panes, 16 beds, 16 shulker boxes, 16 candles, 13 fences, 12 signs, 11 leaves, 11 heads, 9 bars (iron and copper), 4 skulls, and 219 others (chests, shelves, chains, ladders, ice, cactus, cauldrons, the enchanting table, the brewing stand among them).
- **Light opacity is not the sight rule.** `tinted_glass` is `filterLight 15` and `transparent: true`: light stops, the eye does not. One block in the table disagrees, in the direction that would call a glass pane a wall. So the engine's `hides` reads the occlusion property (§4), not `filterLight`, and the light model's `passes_light` (`crates/delvec/src/compiler/light.rs`) stays what it is: a light question.
- **The finding's blocks.** `iron_bars`: `filterLight 0`, `transparent: true` — looked through. `campfire`: `filterLight 0`, `transparent: true` — looked through. `stone_bricks`, `polished_deepslate`, `stripped_dark_oak_log`: `filterLight 15`, `transparent: false` — hide. On the demo level *The Hearth Door* at content `efa28499d54aed83a32365d28e794a411f0150a3`, built by the engine at `57f0e7026e9d5c4f2df8529a0b4b49b041424eb1`, the shut way `edge/hearth-door` is barred by nine cells of `minecraft:iron_bars[east=true,west=true]` (`validation/gate-seal.json`: `anchor/seam-hearth-door`, cells `[10..12, 64..66, 19]`) behind a gate of three `minecraft:campfire` cells (`anchor/hearth-fire`, `[10..12, 64, 21]`): from the hall, nothing between the eye and the passage hides, and the model held both gates camera-blocking.

## 4. The read site

**Authored.** The table is **measured from the pinned server jar**, the way the light-emission fixture already is: `tools/maintenance/dump-block-light.py` with `tools/maintenance/blocklight/BlockLightDump.java` resolves `BlockState.getLightEmission()` through the pin's mappings and writes `crates/delvec/tests/fixtures/light/emission-1.21.11.tsv`, refusing a jar whose sha256 is not the pin's. The sight table is the same instrument asked a second question — per block state, whether it occludes and whether its occlusion shape is the full cube — written beside it as `occlusion-1.21.11.tsv` and read by `World::hides` through one loader. CI reads the committed table and never runs the dumper; a pin bump regenerates it. The dump is a measurement, so the cross-check of §3 is re-run against it when it lands and the one known divergence (`tinted_glass`) is asserted by a test, as `passes_light_matches_vanilla_filter_light` asserts the light table's agreement.

Until the table lands, no check may call a cell hidden: a check that needs the predicate is a recorded debt against this page, never a hand list in a module.
