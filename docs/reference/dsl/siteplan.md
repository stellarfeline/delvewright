# `delvewright_dsl::siteplan`

The reference page for `crates/dsl/src/siteplan/mod.rs`: the diagnostics-catalog row of every DW code
this module declares. The catalog's shared rules are in [`compiler.md` §5](../compiler.md#5-diagnostics-catalog).

## Diagnostics

### DW0824–DW0835 — the site plan (`dsl::siteplan`; error + one advisory)

Stage 4 of the map pipeline: the geometric embedding of the layout graph, judged
**upstream of any geometry**. No block exists when these run and none of them
reads one; what is being judged is whether the plan is a plan — whether the boxes
fit in the region and not in each other, whether two places that claim to connect
really touch, and whether the numbers the brief fixed still hold once the boxes
are drawn.

**One tier, and it is validation (exit 1)**, as it is for the layout graph beside
it. Every rule below is a property of the document in front of it, so a plan that
is wrong is wrong before anything is analyzed.

**What invokes them**: `dsl::siteplan::check`, from
`dsl::validate::validate_campaign_with`, whenever the campaign directory holds a
`site-plan.json` — the same event-bound shape stages 2, 3 and 7 use. That
function is what every `delvec` subcommand's validation stage calls, so there is
no path from a campaign directory to a verdict, a world or a datapack that goes
round it, no flag to pass and no step to remember.

**No opt-out exists.** Not one rule here has an acknowledgement, an override or
an exemption field, which is the cheapest available answer to the question
`CLAUDE.md` asks of every escape hatch — *could the defect this hatch exists to
catch supply the hatch's own proof obligation?* A hatch that does not exist
cannot be supplied.

**What is deliberately absent, and where it is.** Three obligations are only
decidable over blocks: whether a built seam is the opening the plan allocated,
whether every node's floor is reached, whether a crossing was *discovered*
outside a seam; `DW0833`'s second call site over assembled bytes; and whether a
declared sightline is unobstructed. A version of them written here would be the
derivation's arithmetic replayed against itself — the opposite of an independent
observer — so they are judged over the derived blockout (`DW0836`–`DW0838`,
`DW0821` and `DW0833`'s second call site, below), and this stage states the
plan-side half they are checked against.

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0824` | **The graph and the plan do not agree exactly.** Six correspondences and three references under one claim: everything the graph declares is embedded exactly once, and everything the plan embeds is something the graph declared. A place with no box or with two; a box naming no place; a traversal connection with no seam or with two; a seam naming no connection, or naming a `vision` one (which carries a sightline — a vista's two ends are routinely not adjacent, so the seam construct cannot state what it asserts); a `vision` connection with no sightline; a sightline naming a traversal connection, or whose end does not lie in the place its connection names (the stage-5 proof walks exactly that segment, so ends elsewhere prove a different claim); `stair_in` on a non-stair or naming a third place; an identity naming a fact the brief does not state or a place the plan does not embed. **And the ordering tooth**: a site plan present with no layout graph, or with no geometry brief, is refused naming the missing document — one refusal, not one per dangling name inside the plan, and with no graph not one per anchor name outside it either: the message says that the campaign's anchor references are left unjudged rather than refused against a vocabulary the missing document creates. The type carries the other half, which no check could: a box's `node` is required, so a plan that describes a space without naming the place it is the space of does not parse. This is also the **two-artifact question's instrument** (spec-0049 §10): how often it fires *alone* — a graph edit with no plan edit, or the reverse — is the CI-visible number that decides whether graph and plan stay two documents. |
| `DW0826` | **Something the plan places leaves the region.** A box cell or a whole-owned volume cell outside `region`, named with the offending span against the region's on each axis. The prescription is deliberate and is in the message: **the region is the brief's number flowing down, and a box is never grounds to grow it** — move the box, shrink it, or change the brief's fact and re-derive so the change is visible in the document that owns it. Volumes answer to it too, because a `massif` outside the region is the whole owning mass beyond its own declared extent, which is extent-flows-up arriving through the back door. **One region is one finding**: where more than one box leaves it, the boxes are one line at `/content/boxes` stating the count, the region and every offender with its own overrun, and the volumes are one line at `/content/volumes`; a single offender still gets its own line at its own index. **Its quantifier is everything the plan places** (spec-0098): a place's **claim** — its play space, the one-cell ring its walls may stand in, the ground under it down to the claim's bottom and any declared roof zone — leaving the region is a line of its own, named with the claim's span; and so is the **terrain** of an `open` fill: a heightmap the plan names that nothing read (a campaign handed to the checks without its directory), that cannot be read or decoded, that is not exactly the region's `x × z` pixels (named with both sizes), or whose surface runs outside the region's `y` span (named with the surface's span, `base_y` and `range`). |
| `DW0827` | **Two owners for one block, and no rule to pick by.** Two boxes' play spaces overlap — named with both places and the intersection on all three axes. **And, widened to claims** (spec-0098 §2 rule 3d): cells several places' claims share that none of the ownership rules awards — places exactly one cell apart, side by side or corner to corner, with no connection across that plane, or with connections between one pair that disagree about which is `a`. Named with every contesting place, the contested cells (the first six and a count) and the three remedies: give them a seam (its `a` draws the wall), stand them two or more cells apart (the gap between is the site's fill — on an `open` site the commons, walkable ground both may open onto), or make them one place. The engine neither arbitrates nor writes. At a corner column three places share, where the connections meeting there are led by different places, the cell is awarded to the `a` of the first of them in the plan's seam order (spec-0098 departures). Validation tier (exit 1). **Binding: box pairs compared, and shared claim cells awarded, in the site-plan binding line.** |
| `DW0828` | **A seam is not on a shared face.** The declared face is not shared by the edge's two boxes — their spans miss each other on an in-plane axis, a horizontal seam names a sky-open place with no ceiling or floor plane to sit in, or, through a floor or ceiling, the two are not one cell apart (the message states the gap it measured, and an overlap is stated as one) — or the crossing's corner lies off the shared area. Three more shapes of relational placement (spec-0059): an `at` or `meets` that lies off its own box's face; an offset of the wrong shape for the face (one number through a floor, two along a wall); and, on a seam whose two boxes both already stand, a crossing that is not the same cells seen from `a` and from `b` — **the loop that does not close**, refused at the seam that closes it with both corners and how each was obtained. **Seams are allocated on faces both boxes already have**: the two-places-cannot-mate failure class is resolved here, where both boxes are still free to move, and never later between two finished buildings. The refusal prints both boxes' cell ranges and floors. |
| `DW0829` | **A seam's opening does not fit, or its sill cannot be reached.** Two halves of one claim that the opening is usable. Geometric: the opening — a named standard's `width × height`, or the size the seam declares itself (`{"width": w, "height": h}`) — anchored where `at`/`meets` put it runs past the shared face, named with both rectangles; the opening is the author's declaration, so it is never quietly cropped to fit. Physical: on a vertical face, a `walk` or `barred` seam's sill is more blocks over the floor of a side the connection is entered from than a body reaches by jumping, so the connection the graph declares is not one; the sill is the higher of the two floors (spec-0059), so the prescription is to bring the floors within a step of each other or declare a `stair` and let the treads carry the climb. In a floor (an `up`/`down` face) there is no sill, and a `walk` or `barred` seam between two floors further apart than a jump is refused the same way, naming `climb`, `stair` and `drop` as the connections that carry a body between them. (An opening name the table does not define is `DW0812`.) |
| `DW0830` | **A stair seam cannot be built.** Three refusals and one stand-in finding. Refused: the seam declares no `stair_in`, so massing that has to stand somewhere stands nowhere; the two floors are the same plane, so the stair climbs nothing — a walk that has been called a stair, catchable precisely *because* the rise is derived rather than authored; or the treads are hosted in the HIGHER place, which no walk plane can rise off. **A finding, never a refusal (warning, exit 0)**: no standard pitch fits the run the host affords, so the **stand-in** cannot lay its treads. The stand-in never ships: a piece detailed into the host carries its own stair, judged over its bytes (`DW0836`, `DW0837`), and a host left to the stand-in lays no treads and leaves the place above it unreached (`DW0837`). Climb and run are both read from `siteplan::stair_run`, the one function the derivation lays treads by. The climb is what the OPENING asks for — the seam's own sill across a vertical face, the pierced plane through a floor or ceiling. The run is the host's whole extent on the seam's normal across a vertical face, and beside a pierced floor it is the room on the roomier side of the hole plus the hole's own width. The finding names the rise, the climb, the tightest standard's required run and the run available. |
| `DW0992` | **A climb that climbs nothing** (spec-0098 §2c). A `climb` connection — a way a body climbs on a ladder or a vine the lower place hangs (spec-0099) — joins two places whose floors are one plane, so a ladder between them rises nothing: a doorway called a climb. A climb asks no pitch, no run and no sill; its rise is the two floors' difference, and the proofs that move a body count the climb over the built bytes (`DW0837`, `DW0986`). Move one floor, or declare the connection a `walk`. Validation tier (exit 1). **Binding: climb seams compared.** |
| `DW0831` | **A drop seam falls the wrong way, further than a body survives, or past the plan's declared cap.** The derived fall along the edge's declared direction is zero or negative — a drop that rises is a mislabelled stair, and a drop is one-way only because a body cannot climb back up the way it came; it is deeper than the unarmoured survivable fall (`fall.unarmoured-survivable`, a fact of the game: a deeper drop is a death, not a connection), whether or not a cap is declared; or it is deeper than the plan's own `max_drop`, when the plan declares one. Absent `max_drop`, no policy cap applies: how deep a designed fall goes is the author's decision. |
| `DW0876` | **A seam does not declare a connection this engine builds** (spec-0053). **Three shapes of one claim**, because a seam exhibiting any of them has no crossing for any rule below to judge and the author's next action is the same in every case: it declares neither an `opening` nor a `contact`, or both; its contact's span leaves the face `DW0828` established; or it is a contact on a `stair`, `barred` or `vision` connection. A contact's width is the author's — a front one cell wide (a rope bridge's end) is as legal as one fifty-five wide. The class exclusion is the standing falsifier re-armed rather than an oversight: a stair needs a run and a pitch, a barred door needs a gate region that seals and clears, and a sightline is not a crossing at all. (A `vision` connection carrying a seam at all is `DW0824`, which reaches it first.) Validation tier (exit 1). |
| `DW0833` | **A brief identity does not hold.** An `identities[]` comparison is false. The refusal names **both numbers** and quotes the brief's own sentence, because the author's next action is deciding which of the two to move — and the prescription says where: change the fact *in the brief*, where the design is written down, so the change is a decision somebody took rather than a plan that drifted. Raised here over the plan; **its second call site is the built world**, where the same rule recomputes the same measures from assembled bytes so a derivation defect that moved a datum cannot hide behind a plan-time green. That site's own prescription is different and is stated in §5.3: the mass is what disagrees, and the plan's own massing is as likely to be the cause as the derivation. |
| `DW0834` | **The identity gate binds nothing.** Zero `facts[]` in the brief, or zero `identities[]` in the plan: the binding that holds the whole to its written design is empty, which is the vacuity the whole stage exists to prevent — with either side empty the plan may say anything at all and every rule above still passes, because none of them has an opinion about how big the map was meant to be. **Warning (exit 0)**, naming the empty side, so a deliberately minimal plan stays compilable; printed every run, so the emptiness is never quietly a pass. |
| `DW0835` | **A whole-owned volume enters a box.** A `volumes[]` region intersecting a box's play space, named with both and the intersection. The whole's mass may stand beside a place, under it and over it; inside it, the volume and the place are two authorities writing one cell, which the derivation must never be asked to arbitrate. |
| `DW0883` | **A box is not placed exactly once** (spec-0059). Two shapes of one claim. A connected component of the seam graph in which no box pins its `min`: nothing places it — a box stands only where a pin puts it or where a seam hangs it off a box that already stands — named with every box in the component and the prescription to pin one of them (the entry's, when it is there). Or a pinned box a seam also reaches, at a different corner: the pin and the seam disagree, named with both corners and how each was obtained, addressed at the seam. A pin is a claim the packing verifies, never a second authority; a box the packing does not reach has no cells, so no geometric rule fires over it and the one refusal is the whole story. Validation tier (exit 1). Binding: the pinned / derived / component counts in the site-plan binding line. |

### DW0988 — a roof the plan has no room for (`dsl::siteplan`; spec-0098)

A box may declare `roof: {courses, eaves}` — the roof zone the whole reserves
over a roofed place: the shell footprint grown by `eaves` on every side, from
the ceiling course up `courses` courses. It is massed solid at stage 5 over a
place no piece fills yet, and drawn by the place's own piece once detailed.

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0988` | **A roof the plan has no room for.** Two shapes of one claim, both read off the plan before any geometry: `roof` on a box whose `ceiling` is `open` — a sky-open place claims its ground and its headroom and nothing above it, so it has no lid to put a roof on; and a course of the roof proper (over the shell footprint, above the lid) lying in another place's play space or floor course — the stacked case, named with both places and the colliding courses. Eaves are not this refusal: an eave stops where a neighbour's shell begins, and the handout prints where it stopped. Remedy: fewer courses, a taller neighbour, or one place whose piece carries both. Validation tier (exit 1). **Binding: roofed boxes, in the site-plan binding line.** |

### DW0821/DW0836–DW0839 — the derived blockout (`compiler::blockout`; error + two advisories)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::compiler::blockout` page](../delvec/compiler/blockout.md#dw0821dw0836dw0839--the-derived-blockout-compilerblockout-error--two-advisories).

| Code | Rule |
|---|---|
| `DW0839` | **Two placement authorities in one campaign.** A stage-1 world declaring `areas[]` in a campaign that also carries a site plan. `areas[]` seats prefab pieces on the compiler's fixed stride and the site plan seats the derived blockout in its own declared region, so a world with both has two answers to every question about where something is and nothing says which. One or the other, per campaign; both surfaces are legal. Validation tier (exit 1). |
