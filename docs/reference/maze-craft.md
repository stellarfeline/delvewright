# A maze a hunter roams — how it is generated, textured and made legible

Reader: the agent designing a maze level where a hunting body roams the lanes, one player walks them and another watches from above, and the engine work that gives that level its pieces. This is the research record behind that design: which spanning-tree algorithm gives which corridor texture, what a pursuit asks of the texture, how a maze is kept legible and fair from the ground and from a platform, and where in this engine a maze is generated. Every rule is marked **[cited]** with its source, or **[authored]** — reasoned here from a cited rule or from the engine, with no source of its own. Sources are read for their rules; nothing of their text beyond the quoted sentences is taken (ADR-0013: an unlicensed or non-commercial source is ideas only). What the hunter itself does is `warden-behaviour.md`; where a volume may kill is `reach-and-hazard-volumes.md`; what a player's eye sees through is `sight-through-blocks.md`.

## 2. The vocabulary

- **A perfect maze is a spanning tree.** [cited] Wikipedia: *"maze generation is often approached as generating a random spanning tree"*; Pullen: *"A 'perfect' Maze means one without any loops or closed circuits, and without any inaccessible areas."* Between any two cells there is exactly one route.
- **A braid maze has no dead ends; loops are what it has instead.** [cited] Pullen: *"A 'braid' Maze means one without any dead ends"*, and *"A well-designed braid Maze can be much harder than a perfect Maze of the same size."* Wikipedia: *"Loops, which can confound naive maze solvers, may be introduced by adding random edges to the result during the course of the algorithm."*
- **River is how far a passage flows before the next junction; run is how long a straight goes before a forced turn; elitism is how long the solution is against the maze's size.** [cited, ideas only] Pullen's terms. He states that a maze with less river *"will tend to have many short dead ends"* and one with more river has fewer, longer ones.
- **A dead-end ratio is the share of cells with one open side.** [cited, ideas only] Pullen's table measures it on 2-D orthogonal mazes and the figures below are his.

## 3. The algorithms and their texture

Figures are Pullen's dead-end percentages (ideas only; a measurement this engine repeats on its own generator before it is relied on, §6). The one-line textures are Wikipedia's where quoted and Buck's where paraphrased.

| Algorithm | Dead ends | Texture | Bias / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recursive backtracker (randomized depth-first) | 10 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"Mazes generated with a depth-first search have a low branching factor and contain many long corridors."* Buck: the fewest dead ends, long and winding passages. | The highest river; the solution is long and twisty (Pullen). |
| Hunt-and-kill | 11 % | [cited, ideas only] Buck: long, winding passages, like the backtracker. | River slightly below the backtracker's; needs no stack (Pullen). |
| Growing tree | 10–49 % | [cited, ideas only] Buck: works identically to the backtracker or very like Prim's, by how the next cell is picked. | The texture is a parameter, not a property. |
| Eller's | 28 % | [cited, ideas only] Buck: a balance between long-and-winding and many cul-de-sacs. | Row-by-row; unbounded height (Wikipedia: *"perfect mazes of unlimited height"*). Not uniform. |
| Kruskal's (randomized) | 30 % | [cited] Wikipedia: Kruskal's and Prim's are *"biased toward many short dead ends"*; Kruskal's *"tends to produce regular patterns which are fairly easy to solve."* | Low river. |
| Prim's (randomized, true / simplified / modified) | 30 / 32 / 36 % | [cited] Wikipedia on the simplified form: *"relatively easy to find the way to the starting cell, but hard to find the way anywhere else"*. | Radial from the start cell; very low river. |
| Wilson's | 29 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"generates an unbiased sample from the uniform distribution over all mazes"*. | Uniform; no texture of its own. |
| Aldous–Broder | 29 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"also produces uniform spanning trees"* but *"one of the least efficient maze algorithms"*. | Uniform; slow. |
| Recursive division | 23 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"long straight walls crossing their space, making it easier to see which areas to avoid"*. | Wall-adding; fractal; the straight walls read from above. |
| Binary tree | 25 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"has no dead ends in the directions of bias"*. | Strongly diagonal; two whole edges are one corridor. |
| Sidewinder | 27 % | [cited] Wikipedia: *"trivial to solve from the bottom up because it has no upward dead ends"*. | One edge is one corridor. |
| Cellular automata (Maze, Mazectric) | — | [cited] Wikipedia: Mazectric *"has a tendency to generate longer and straighter corridors"*, and the mazes *"tend to be relatively predictable"*. | Not a spanning tree; no guarantee of connectivity. |

- **The dead-end ratio and the river are one dial.** [authored, from the table] Every algorithm that carves depth-first (backtracker, hunt-and-kill, growing-tree-newest) sits near 10 % dead ends with long river; every one that picks frontier cells or edges at random (Prim's, Kruskal's, the uniform samplers) sits near 30 % with short river. Nothing in the perfect-maze family offers long river *and* loops: loops are a second pass.
- **One comparative measurement exists and it agrees.** [cited, ideas only] Bellot et al. count, on one 10 × 10 recursive-backtracker maze, 36 dead ends, 12 junctions and a longest path of 79 cells, and recommend the backtracker for games for its low bias and speed. One maze is one sample; it is a check on the table, not a second table.

## 4. What a pursuit asks of the texture

- **A pursuit maze is braided, never perfect.** [authored, from §2 and §5] In a spanning tree every lane off the solution is a dead end, and a walker who meets the hunter in one has no second way out: the hunter's approach is a death. A loop is the only shape that gives the walker a way round the hunter, so the loops are the design, and a perfect maze is only the raw material they are cut into.
- **The one pursuit maze every player knows has no dead ends and two escape tunnels.** [cited] Wikipedia on Pac-Man: the two side passages are *"warp tunnels"* that *"allow Pac-Man and the ghosts to travel to the opposite side of the screen"*, and *"Ghosts become slower when entering and exiting these tunnels"* — the escape route has a cost for the hunter and none for the hunted. The chasers differ: *"Blinky gives direct chase to Pac-Man"*; *"Pinky and Inky try to position themselves in front of Pac-Man, usually by cornering him."*
- **The hunter decides the loop's minimum size.** [authored, from `warden-behaviour.md`] A loop is an escape only when the walker can get out of the hunter's reach by running it: the hunter's hearing (its listener radius) and its sniff reach fix how far a block of walls must separate the two for the hunter to lose the trail. A loop whose far side is still inside that reach is a corridor with a decoration in the middle. The numbers are in `warden-behaviour.md` §2–§4 and the loop is sized from them, not from a feel.
- **Long river for the walk, braids at the junctions.** [authored, from §3] The walker's experience of a lane is its river: a long corridor is tension (no exit until the end) and a short one is choice. A depth-first texture gives the long lanes; the braiding pass then opens a loop at every dead end that would otherwise trap the walker next to the hunter's roaming ground. Dead ends are kept only where they hold something worth the risk, and never within the hunter's reach of its entrance.
- **Corridor width is a mechanic, not a texture.** [cited, `warden-behaviour.md` §3] The pinned hunter's pursuit down a one-wide lane and down a two-wide lane are measured there; the maze's lane width is chosen from that measurement, and a width the measurement did not cover is not shipped.
- **The guide's steering has a reach, and it is shorter than the maze.** [cited, `warden-behaviour.md` §2, §5] A projectile or a bell turns the hunter only from within its hearing. A platform the guide throws from must stand within that reach of the lanes the walker needs cleared, or the guide is a spectator; the platform's height against the hunter's ranged attack is the same page's §2.

## 5. Legibility and fairness

- **A landmark is a point reference with one aspect unique in its context.** [cited] Wikipedia on Lynch: landmarks are *"Points of reference person cannot enter into"* and *"At least one aspect of them is unique or memorable in the context they exist"*; imageability is *"a measure of how easily a physical object, word or environment will evoke a clear mental image"*. A maze's landmark is seen over the walls — a tower, a tree, a lit post — and tells the walker which quarter they are in.
- **Lynch's five elements apply directly to a maze.** [cited] Wikipedia names them — paths (*"channels in which people travel"*), edges (*"Boundaries"*), districts (*"Medium to large areas that are two-dimensional"* with *"common identifying characteristics"*), nodes (the *"foci"*) and landmarks. [authored] In a maze the paths are the lanes, the edges the outer wall and any inner wall that reads as a boundary, the districts quarters told apart by material or planting, the nodes the junctions worth remembering, and the landmarks what rises above the wall line.
- **Wayfinding is a hike, not an expedition.** [cited, ideas only] The Level Design Book's *Wayfinding* page defines landmarks as *"easily identifiable entities which are used for point-referencing"*, names breadcrumbs (collectibles visible in the distance) and denial (a *"thick solid opaque unbreakable wall"* says do not pass), and describes a fragmented, unreadable layout as one where players feel they are *"actively creating their own route and path"*; its summary is *"To pass through a well-designed level is a hike, not an expedition."*
- **A maze is the one level whose point is the expedition, so its legibility is built from above, not from the lanes.** [authored, from §5's cited rules and the design] The walker is meant to be lost; the guide is not. The maze is legible from the platform (every lane visible, the exit marked, the hunter visible) and opaque from the lanes (walls above eye height, no sightline down a lane past its first turn). Everything that tells the walker where they are comes from the guide's words or from a landmark that rises above the walls.
- **Walls above the eye, floors that differ.** [authored, from `sight-through-blocks.md`] A lane wall hides what is behind it only when its cells occlude (full-cube occluding states); a wall two blocks high is looked over by a jumping player and three is not, so a maze wall is at least three blocks high. The hunter is 2.9 blocks tall: a three-high wall hides it from the walker but not its sound, which is the intended asymmetry. Lane floors or wall bands that differ by quarter are what a guide names ("the red quarter, second right").
- **What the guide can see is bounded by the server, not by the eye.** [cited, `entity-tracking.md`] A player entity is sent to a client only within `min(tracking range, view distance × 16)` blocks horizontally; the engine's `view_distance` floor is 10 chunks (`compiler.md`, stage 1). [authored] The whole maze, and the walker in it, must therefore fit inside that distance from the platform; the client's own render limit for a player body at the platform's height and distance is not recorded here and is a gap to measure before the maze's size is fixed.
- **The size a player can read from a platform is a measurement, not a rule.** [authored — an absence in the sources] No source read states the ground size a player reads as one picture from a given height. The reading is taken on the built level, from the platform, with a frame (`delvec frames`) and the owner's eye; the maze is sized so that its far wall is still read.
- **Fairness is the hunter's visibility and the walker's second way.** [authored, from CLAUDE.local.md *Danger is visible, or the engine refuses it* and §4] The hunter is a danger: it is visible or audible before it is lethal, and every lane the walker is sent down has a loop out of it within the hunter's reach. A dead end the hunter can reach while the walker is in it is the same defect as a killing volume under safe-looking floor, and the repair is the same: move the danger (braid the lane), never mark the floor.

## 6. Where a maze is generated in this engine

- **A maze is a campaign generator's job, and it is seeded.** [authored, from the content repository at `c1e29e0c` and ADR-0004, ADR-0006] A campaign's pieces come from a generator under its own `design/generator/` that writes a **grammar program**, which `delvec grammar expand --region <size> --seed <n>` turns into the piece, with the region and seed stated once in `design/programs/zones.json` (`campaigns/vesperhold/design/generator/build_castle.py` lines 1–12 and 156–179; `finish.py` lines 1–40). The grammar expansion is byte-identical for one program, region and seed (`grammar.md`, *Determinism*); so the maze algorithm runs inside the generator, from the zone's seed through its own named PRNG, and emits a partition of lanes and walls as the program's `split`/`fill`/`void` tree with a `mark` per anchor (spawn at the centre, the exit, the platform stand, the hunter's start, every bell). Nothing in the engine needs to know it is a maze.
- **No engine surface is missing for the generation.** [authored, CLAUDE.md *This is a general engine*] The grammar already carries everything a maze needs: boxes, a palette by role, marks, and a spatial contract (`grammar.md` §contract) whose `contract-reachability` gate proves every lane cell reachable from the entry. A `maze` op in the grammar would encode a design decision (what a lane is for) into a mechanism that already expresses it.
- **The braid is proven, not hoped.** [authored, from §4] The generator asserts its own texture before it writes the program: dead-end count, loop count, lane widths, and for every dead end it keeps, its distance from the hunter's start and from the nearest loop. These are the generator's invariants in the sense `gallery/prefab-generator/src/prefab_invariants` gives the word, and the numbers are printed on every run so the maze page can quote them.
