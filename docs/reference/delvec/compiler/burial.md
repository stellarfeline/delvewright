## Diagnostics

### DW046x — the NPC scene ledger (`compiler::cast`; spec-0020)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::compiler::cast` page](cast.md#dw046x--the-npc-scene-ledger-compilercast-spec-0020).

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0885` | **A placed piece's outward solid boundary standing in open air, with nothing answering for it.** Build tier (exit 3), `compiler::burial::check`, bound in `emit::build_with_warnings` — the one function that turns a plan into a datapack, and the first point at which the three things the question needs are all in hand: the assembled blocks, the placed boxes, and the walk region the party is proved to stand in. `Plan::build` cannot ask it, because it runs before a single `.nbt` byte is read and so knows where the boxes are and nothing about which of their boundary cells are solid. **The gap it closes.** A prefab is authored for a context and nothing in the pair of files it ships as said which: `cave-cavern` is 69 % solid box boundary and every one of those cells is the cut edge of a hill it expects to stand inside, `hero-galleon-oak` is 6 % and every one of those is hull a player walks up to. So an `ocean` world can lay a cave piece on a shore, bury none of it, and stand a stone-and-grass slab in open air over the beach with the party walking underneath — while `DW0780` finds the sockets mated, `DW0782` the envelope closed and `DW0344` the waterline on the sea. Each of those judges a piece against itself or against its neighbour; none of them asks whether the world the piece was placed in is the world it was built for. **The rule**: every outward-facing solid boundary cell of a placed piece is buried — by another piece's box, by a block, or by what the horizon puts there — or the piece declares that side in `shown_faces`. **Burial is read off the world the build writes**, never off what a horizon is supposed to provide: a `valley`'s ground is real blocks in real templates and arrives in the assembled block map like any other piece's, an `ocean`'s sea is analytic and arrives as `nav::Ambient`, a neighbour is a box, and all three answer one question about one cell — so a horizon that grows a surround, or a layout that moves a piece, moves this verdict with no change here. **Where it binds.** Over the air the party can be in, flooded from the reachable walk region through the same blocks and the same ambient every other proof reads, bounded two cells outside the content. A sealed one-room delve is solid and exposed on all six sides and is NOT judged, and that is the rule rather than a softening: there is nowhere out there for a body to be, because `DW0322` refuses a reachable walkable cell over a bottomless column and the sea is not standing room. A piece the party can get outside of answers for its **whole** outside — air wraps, and no sightline is computed anywhere here, deliberately: a sightline would make a building's obligation a fact about one walk through the delve, so moving a waypoint would move what the piece has to be. **Two arms.** *The placement arm* names the side, how many of its cells stand in that air, the first of them, what the document declares, and the three moves. *The declaration arm* refuses a `shown_faces` naming a face of pure air — a side is finished exterior surface or it is nothing — read off the piece's OWN template bytes rather than off the assembled world, so a campaign that carves a doorway through a wall cannot invalidate a claim about the asset. A `shown_faces` entry that is not one of the six sides (`east` `west` `up` `down` `north` `south`, the face contract's own vocabulary) is refused where it is written, because nothing can be judged against a side that does not exist. **The three moves, and which ones a horizon leaves open**: BURY it with terrain — only `valley` builds any (spec-0026), so under `ocean` the sea buries what stands below y=62 and nothing above it, and under `void`, the horizon that declares there is nothing outside the placed geometry, the move does not exist; PLACE something against it, available everywhere, and the pair then answers to `DW0780`; DECLARE the side shown, available everywhere and the only move for a piece meant to be looked at — a claim about the PIECE, made in the prefab document, so it changes what that piece is in every world, which is the cost that keeps it from being the cheap answer. **For a piece a grammar program produced, the program is where that move is taken**: `delvec grammar expand` rewrites the metadata every run, so a value typed into the document survives until the next expansion and no longer, and a value a tool erases is not a declaration. Program version `1.9.0` carries `shown_faces` and the export writes it through on every expansion (`grammar.md` §2f); measuring it off the material instead is what `DW0888` rules out by name, because the hull-against-a-hillside distinction this code exists for is not in the blocks. Declaring a side the piece was built to have covered is refused in the message's own words as the fiction it is. **Every offender, not the first.** The check walks every placed piece and collects; `emit::build_with_warnings` raises the first as the refusal and prints the rest, because one placement defect is routinely several pieces and a report naming one sends a creator round the loop as many times as the world has walls. **Binding**: printed on every build as `piece-exposure binding:` — before the refusal, so the run that finds something owes its reader the same counts as the run that finds nothing — and shipped as `validation/piece-exposure.json`; the residual, exposure the two-cell skin left unasked, is `exposed_cells` beside `judged_cells` rather than a sentence.
