## Diagnostics

### DW047x — combat winnability (`compiler::combat`; spec-0023)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::compiler::combat` page](combat.md#dw047x--combat-winnability-compilercombat-spec-0023).

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0850` | **A `reach` the party can arrive at without completing.** Build tier (exit 3), `compiler::reach::check_reach_completion`, run at the same site and over the same final assembled world as `DW0314`. Two assertions per reach objective, one code, because the remedy is the same. **Occupiable:** some cell of the completion volume is standable — a volume no body can be in is an objective nothing completes, which is the reported instance (a point sphere too tight for a human standing on the altar cell) stated as a property instead of as one altar. **Delivered into:** where the critical path walks to the anchor, the cell the leg actually ends on is inside the volume. That half is the one no existing proof could see, and it is live arithmetic rather than a hypothetical: `nav::SNAP_RADIUS` is **3** and the completion cube's half-extent is `max(1, radius)`, so a reach whose only footing lies further out than its own volume reaches is routable (`DW0311`), standable-on-export (`DW0314`) and walkable — and the party, standing exactly where the campaign routed them, is outside the volume that fires the objective. **The volume has one authority** (`compiler::reach::reach_completion`): the emitter formats its `tick` selector from the value this proof judges, and `critical-path.json` carries that same value to the bot as `completion`, so the string and the check cannot come to disagree about a rule that is invisible in the DSL and shows up only as a beat that never happens. Membership is read conservatively — vanilla tests hitbox intersection, so a body one cell out may complete on a face-touching tie, and demanding the certain case is what makes a green here mean *the party completes this* rather than *might*. **Binding: `reach` objectives whose anchor the plan resolves**, quantified over the quests rather than over the exported path, because a reach on an optional quest is a reach a player can be standing at. **Prescription names a VERIFIED radius** (spec-0062 §7.2): `r_min`, the smallest radius from 1 to `max(authored, SNAP_RADIUS)` whose judgement — taken again there, over the same world — is green under both this rule and `DW0881`. The message says `set radius: r_min`, or, when no radius in that range answers, that none does and the remedy is the anchor's placement. The message does not say *never widen the volume*: widening it BLIND leaves the other number live, and a number this rule has re-judged is not blind. Move the anchor onto the footing, or give the anchor cell floor, remain the other two moves; never nudge the waypoint. Note the arithmetic the authored radius sets: an endpoint snaps inside a box of half-extent `SNAP_RADIUS`, so at `radius >= 3` every arrival the route can deliver is inside the volume and the **delivered into** half has nothing left to catch, while **occupiable** binds at every radius. That is the defect being absent rather than the check being blind, and `tests/reach_completion.rs` pins it so a later narrowing of the volume cannot re-open the gap silently. |
| `DW0881` | **A raised `reach` anchor that completes from the floor below it.** Build tier (exit 3), `compiler::reach::check_reach_footprint`, run at the same site and over the same final assembled world as `DW0850`, immediately after it. The completion volume is centred on the anchor cell in all three axes and vanilla adjudicates the selector against a body's whole AABB rather than against the cell its feet are in — a standing body rises `metrics::PLAYER_HEIGHT`, so a cell one course under the volume's bottom layer already reaches into it. Put the two together and any raised anchor whose radius reaches a lower floor completes from that floor: the party never climbs, the beat fires during whatever they were doing down there, and every other proof stays green (`DW0311` routes to the anchor, `DW0314` finds the waypoints standable, `DW0850` finds footing in the volume because the footing is the anchor's own). **The rule is over the FOOTPRINT, never over the radius**, because a number is the wrong thing to bound: a radius generous over a flat plaza and one reaching through a mezzanine floor are the same number. The footprint is every standable cell whose body box meets the volume (`ReachCompletion::possibly_completes_from` — the GENEROUS reading, deliberately the opposite of the conservative one `DW0850` takes, because a rule that refuses a completion must ask what could complete rather than what certainly does), and the demand is that each cell can walk to the anchor's own footing **without leaving the footprint**, under `World::neighbors`, the engine's one step rule. **The footing is a SET** (spec-0062 §7.1): the anchor's own cell when it is standable — one cell, and nothing changes — otherwise every footprint cell at the minimum distance² from the anchor, and the demand is a walk to *some* cell of it. That is a **loosening**, declared in those words, and it is bounded exactly: floor farther from the anchor than its footing is refused, because the set is taken at the minimum. What it stops doing is resting the verdict on a tie-break. `World::snap` is not consulted for the reference: for an anchor no body can stand on — a one-cell killing volume, a lever in a wall, a solid altar — the lips around it are equidistant and mutually unreachable inside the volume, so which one `(distance², cell)` picked decided the verdict, and a resolve-by-name over a scope where names are not unique yields a candidate, not a match. The snap's own defect — preferring a lip the party cannot reach over a doorway it can — is recorded against the snap and not repaired here. A ramp or stair inside the volume satisfies it and is meant to: a body on it is arriving. **Directional**: the walk runs backwards from the footing, so that a body able to fall off a loft into the hall does not excuse the hall floor — the question is whether a body standing there can get to the anchor, not whether the anchor can get to it. **An offender has to be somewhere the party can already stand, and which cell that walk is rooted at is the whole difference between a rule and a false alarm.** The population is the walk from where the party CERTAINLY stands — the campaign's own start, every checkpoint seat, every crossing's entry point and every link's `to` (`lethal::stands_at_roots`, spec-0083 §3.8) — over the same assembled world, so an anchor in an area only a carry reaches is judged too, so a roof, a stone canopy or the top of a stamped shard that a generous radius happens to cover is not reported, and neither is floor beyond a doorway whose planks a beat lays at runtime — before that beat nobody is over there, and the beat that puts them there is the same one that joins the two inside the volume. Rooting it at the ANCHOR's footing instead looks equally reasonable and is wrong, measured rather than argued: the gallery's mezzanine ships with its flight broken and laid by an `open-way`, so a population walked from the loft drops the hall floor the party actually completes from — zero offenders where there are nine. **Residual, named rather than implied:** a cell the party reaches only after a runtime write is outside the population and so outside this rule; that is the conservative direction, and it is bounded by the same write, since whatever joins the party to such a cell also changes the geometry the volume is measured against and no static world holds two versions of it at once. **Binding: reach objectives examined, the cells their volumes cover, the cells the party can stand on, the footprint drawn from those two, and how many of the footprint stand off the anchor's floor** — printed on every build whether anything was found or not. A code of its own rather than a `DW0850` variant for the reason `DW0510` is one: `DW0850` says the objective cannot be completed and sends the author looking for missing footing; this says the objective completes too easily, from a place that is not the place, and more floor is the last thing that would help. **Prescription names a VERIFIED radius**, and never one above the authored value — this rule's whole finding is that the volume reaches too far, so a wider one cannot be its remedy. The message says `set radius: r_min`, the smallest radius from 1 to the authored one whose judgement is green when taken again there (spec-0062 §7.2); where none answers it says so and sends the author to the anchor's placement, which is the move `DW0850` also names and which `remedy_reachability.rs` takes under that code. The other two moves stand: move the anchor so its own footing is the only floor inside the volume, or build a way UP inside it. **One judgement, two callers**: `compiler::reach::ReachJudgement` derives the standable set, the footprint, the footing and the walk once, and `check_reach_completion` and `check_reach_footprint` read it — neither derives geometry of its own, which is what makes the pair unable to prescribe each other's refusal. **Provenance, so a later reader knows which half to distrust:** the PROBLEM is cited — an oversized trigger volume activating from the wrong side of geometry is a known level-design failure, and "floor plane" is the established unit a place is chunked into (*The Level Design Book*, `book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/flow/verticality`: verticality is chunked into floor planes and most areas hold at most three). The RULE — walk-connectivity to the anchor's footing inside the volume, over the stands-at population — is AUTHORED: no published source was found stating a connectivity criterion for trigger-volume extent, and searches for one turned up only engine documentation on how trigger volumes are configured. That is the weak spot, recorded rather than smoothed over, and it is a gap against the level-design research line rather than a permanent hole. |
