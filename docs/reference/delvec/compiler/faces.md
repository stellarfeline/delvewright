## Diagnostics

### DW07xx — asset, render and authoring tooling (spec-0007; `delvec` subcommands)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::admit::diag` page](../admit/diag.md#dw07xx--asset-render-and-authoring-tooling-spec-0007-delvec-subcommands).

| Code | Tool | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| `DW0780` | `compiler::faces` | **Two placed pieces that do not agree about the face they share.** Build-tier (exit 3), raised from `compiler::plan` after placement, in three shapes. (1) *A way out the neighbour does not answer* — one piece declares a way in or out on the face they share, and the neighbour declares nothing there, declares an opening elsewhere along the same wall, or declares a different class (a sightline where the first declares a walk). (2) *A pair that touches and says nothing* — the two boxes abut and NEITHER declares anything across the plane they meet on, so the one thing this check exists for is the one thing it cannot judge; the message names both pieces, the shared plane's world extent, and both documents a side can be declared in. (3) *A mated socket with nothing beyond it* — the solver recorded this jigsaw socket as mated and `solver::seal_layout` therefore cleared its doorway to air, and no placed piece stands at the plane it opens onto: the world ships an open hole with nothing behind it. This is the shape a placement can reach with no author writing anything (a mated piece moved off its seam), and the message says not to seal the socket to hide it. In every shape both pieces pass every prefab gate individually — the pair is what is wrong, which is why no single-piece check can see it. **What is compared**: the two DECLARATIONS a prefab document makes about its own sides — `spatial_contract.faces` (spec-0036) and the jigsaw `connectors` socket (ADR-0004) — never re-derived from the assembled voxels. One authority per piece: the contract where a piece declares one, its sockets where it does not; nothing is merged. A socket's world opening is resolved through `solver::socket_world` and `solver::opening_region`, the same two functions that mated and sealed it. A socket declares no class, so a seam with a socket at either end is compared on its geometry and not on a class claim neither document makes. A face that opens onto no placed piece is not a finding unless the layout said it was mated: a box garden has an outside. A world whose ways are ALLOCATED — the one area a site plan has — is exempt from all three shapes; see `DW0781`. |
| `DW0781` | `compiler::faces` | Advisory: **no two placed pieces touch**, so the piece-mating check had no pair to examine and `DW0780` proved nothing about this world. The binding is a fraction of the PLACEMENT and never of the declarations: two boxes either share a plane or they do not, and no metadata is consulted to find out. That is what makes the zero readable — it says the world was built with nothing adjacent to anything, which is a fact about the world rather than about what its documents happen to declare. A non-zero pair count that nothing declares a face across is not this advisory: it is a `DW0780` refusal. The message states, beside the zero, what the run did read: pieces with a `spatial_contract`, pieces judged by jigsaw socket, pieces with a face on either, and faces declared in all. **The message is one line — the counts and the object class — and the reading is here.** A site-plan world allocates its ways at stage 4, on faces two boxes already share, and proves them over the built bytes by `DW0836`, so a zero there is the honest count of a question that world does not ask rather than a proof that went missing. **Binding line**: `FaceBinding::line`, printed by `Plan::build` on every plan whether or not anything was found — `piece-mating binding: N of M placed piece(s) touch in P pair(s), J of which a declared face crosses; D face(s) declared (C piece(s) by spatial contract, S by jigsaw socket), B of them abutting another piece.` Subject: the campaign, so the advisory prints among the campaign advisories. |
