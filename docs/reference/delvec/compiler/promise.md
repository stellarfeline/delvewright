# `delvec::compiler::promise`

The reference page for `crates/delvec/src/compiler/promise.rs`: the diagnostics-catalog row of every DW code
this module declares. The catalog's shared rules are in [`compiler.md` §5](../../compiler.md#5-diagnostics-catalog).

## Diagnostics

### DW0860–DW0863, DW0961, DW0964, DW1016, DW1017 — an objective keeps the promise its prompt makes (`compiler::promise`; error; exit 1, `DW0863` at the build, exit 3)

An objective carries two player-facing strings — `title` and `hint` — and two
visibilities, `announcement` and `compass`, each defaulted by the campaign's
`guidance` block (spec-0093, spec-0104), and may declare a `highlight`. These rules are the whole of what the compiler
asserts about them. They are one defect class: what the game tells the party and
what the machine requires are not the same thing.

One emitter fact is load-bearing under most of them and is not visible in the
schema: the activation announcement is emitted **only for an announced
objective** — a `title` whose resolved `announcement` is `shown`
(`Objective::announced`) — and the hint's line is nested inside that guard. An
objective that is not announced therefore prints nothing at all — no chat line,
no cue sound — and an objective with a `hint` that is not announced shows
*neither*.

`DW0862`, `DW0961`, `DW1016` and `DW1017` judge what the document says — a `hint`
that asks to be shown where nothing will show it, an announcement asked for with
no title to print, a highlight on an act with nothing to light, a compass asked
for on an objective nobody is told about — each a declaration that binds to
nothing, refused rather than ignored. `DW0860`, `DW0861` and `DW0863`
require the campaign to have something: they are the forcing function `DW0481`
applies to the story, turned to face the player.

Every run prints a binding line naming what it examined, zeroes included.

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0860` | **A failure clock nothing explained.** A `begin-stealth` with a non-empty `on_caught` is a clock: a player outside every zone for `grace_ticks` is punished by that bundle. It is refused when no `narrate` fires at or before the arming in the same effect bundle, or when the interval between the last such line and the clock's first bite is shorter than that line takes to read. The interval is `(arming offset − prompt offset) + grace_ticks` on the arming's own timeline, so a `sequence` step's `at_ticks` counts toward it; the reading floor is `20 + 2 × characters` ticks — one second to appear, then ten characters a second, which at five characters a word is 120 wpm. **The prompt the clock races is the LAST one before it**, not the sum of the bundle and not the longest: three mutually exclusive branch retellings of one beat are ordinary authoring and must not accumulate into a refusal, and the line still being read when the clock starts is the instruction. Prose in `on_caught` does not satisfy it — that line is read after the punishment. **Binding: `begin-stealth` effects with a non-empty `on_caught`**, over every effect root (the population is enumerated from `dsl::for_each_effect_root`, which asserts it reached all of them, never from a remembered list of places effects live). A `timed-gate` is deliberately out of scope: it arms at world load, not at a beat, so no prompt can precede it — what the party is owed there is the chance to watch it (`DW0388`) and a window worth reading (`DW0378`). Prescription: put a `narrate` before the arming saying what is now being asked, and give the clock time for it. Do NOT shorten the line to fit the clock. |
| `DW0861` | **An adopted container nothing distinguishes.** A `collect` that sets `container` adopts a chest or barrel the prefab already placed, and is refused without both a `title` and an `item_name`. Adoption is what creates the ambiguity: the compiler's own chest at `anchor` is a new object that appears the tick the objective activates, whereas an adopted container is scenery the party has been walking past since the beat began, identical to every other barrel the piece put down, and the compiler adds nothing to it. The announcement and the name on the stack inside are then the only two things that can tell one box from its neighbours. **Binding: `collect` objectives with `container` set** — a `collect` that conjures its own chest is not this rule's subject. Prescription: give the objective a `title` and an `item_name`. Do NOT reach for `fill_count`: padding makes the right box read full, it does not say which box is right. |
| `DW0862` | **A prompt the emitter will never show.** An objective authors a `hint` and is **not announced** — no `title`, or its resolved `announcement` is `hidden` (its own field, or the campaign's `guidance.announcements`; the message says which). Because the announcement is emitted only for an announced objective and the hint is nested inside it, the party is shown neither — while the hint is still inventoried for translation and rendered into every language sidecar, so nothing anywhere reports that a translated, shipped line is never on a screen. **Binding: every objective** (the binding line counts the announced ones). An objective with no `hint` is not refused: this is a rule about a prompt that was written and is not shown, not about one nobody wrote; a quiet objective with a title and no hint is legal. Prescription: give the objective a `title` and let it be announced; the hint is an announcement's second line, not an announcement. Do NOT delete the hint to clear it — that silences the beat rather than fixing it. |
| `DW0863` | **An act the party cannot find** (spec-0104 §3, widening spec-0093 §5; build tier, `compiler::promise::check_acts_found` from `emit::build`, because it needs places and blocks). An **act** is every objective step and every `use`, `strike`, `strike-npc` or `strike-assembly` trigger a walked path performs (`compiler::acts`); the walks are the exported critical path and every realised branch's own path. An act is **found** when (1) it is an objective **announced with a `hint`** and **armed while the party walks to it** — the replay's guaranteed armed set in front of the act's step (`plan::PathFiring::armed`, through `RegionEvents::armed`, from `flow::Walk::armed_objectives_unless_shut`: its quest running, every `after` done, its flag gate holding and no numeric term shown shut — a term on a datum no ordered walk dates, such as one a trigger writes, reads open — the triggers due at the step performed), because an announcement is shown when its objective arms, so one whose gate opens only at or after its own place says nothing on the way there and is judged by sight instead (a trigger has no lines and cannot take this arm), or (2) it is **in sight** of the cell the party stands on before it — the preceding positioned step's cell, or where that step's completion carries the party (an inter-area crossing, a link), or the campaign's entry cell for the first act: the one "in sight" rule, `framing::in_sight` (spec-0110 §5.5), asked from the eye (`cell_center + 1.62`) of the target's box — a body, a wave or a place stood in is two cells, feet and head; a block is its cell — at least one of its nine lines (the centre, at chest height `+1.0` for a body and the block's centre for a block, and the eight corners inset) crossing no `World::blocks_camera` cell, within `SIGHT_BLOCKS` (64) of the centre, in the configuration that walk holds when the act is next (`nav::worlds_at`: the doors its earlier steps opened are open). The eye cell, the vantage cell and the target's cells never block. `every_consumer_of_in_sight_answers_alike` (`crates/delvec/src/compiler/framing/shot/tests.rs`) holds `DW0863`, a shot's sight and a review frame's manifest to one answer over generated cases. The target is the step's own cell: an interact's or reach's anchor, a talk-to's NPC station on that path, a collect's container (or the compiler's chest, or the dropping wave's anchor), a kill's wave anchor, a trigger's `at` cell or the struck NPC's or assembly's cell. **A `kill` is judged from every act that fires its wave** — each must be a placed act, see the wave's anchor, and stand within the wave's reach (`nav::wave_aggro_radius`: the lane's `aggro_radius`, else the largest `follow_range`, else 16) — so a fight found by reach alone, the bodies coming unseen round a wall, is **not** found; a `kill` on no walk is judged from those acts too. A placed act, per effect root: an objective's completion fires where the objective completes; a quest's `on_complete` where each of its last objectives completes; a trigger at its `at` anchor or the struck NPC's or assembly's cell; a trap payload at the trap's cell; a shop offer at the shop's anchor; a fight's `on_kill` at that fight's anchor; an assembly's `on_land` at its mark; a checkpoint's `on_respawn`, a shortcut's `on_unlock`, `on_death` and a loop's `on_cross` place the party nowhere. **A place walked through is judged too.** Every positioned step of a walk that is no act — an `approach` or `step` trigger the path walks through (`acts::Pass`) — ends a leg the party walks, and one with an act after it on the walk is **guided** when an objective announced with a `hint`, performed later on that walk, is armed on the way to it, or when the next act is in sight (the same rule) from where the leg starts; otherwise it is refused, naming the leg, the next act, why it is unseen, and every announced objective after it that is not yet armed. A place after the walk's last act leads to nothing left to find, and a place the party already stands on (the leg's start is its cell) is no leg; neither is judged. **Binding line**: `DW0863 binding: N act(s) over W walk(s) — A announced with a hint, S in sight (K kill(s) also in reach), T trigger(s) judged by sight alone, U announced but not yet armed on the way and judged by sight; P place(s) walked through — PA with an announced objective armed, PS with the next act in sight; R refused; O kill(s) on no walk judged from the acts that fire their wave`. Every refusal is printed; the first is the verdict. The message names the walk, the act, the vantage and its cell, the target and its distance, the first cell that stops the centre line when all nine are stopped (or the distance past 64, or the reach missed), whether a fight's muster is in sight, and the remedies: a `title` and a `hint` (an objective), or moving the act or its target into sight (any act; a trigger's only remedy, or make it an `interact` with a hint). Never widen `follow_range` to buy the distance. |
| `DW1016` | **A highlight with nothing to light** (spec-0104 §7.5; validation tier, `compiler::promise::check_highlights`). A `highlight` on a `collect` whose items fall from a wave (`dropped_by` — no container stands), or on an `approach`, `step` or `strike-assembly` trigger (a place, a plate's cell, or an assembly lit as no one body), is refused. On an `interact`, a `collect` with a container or the compiler's chest, a `talk-to`, a `kill`, a `use` or `strike` trigger and a `strike-npc` trigger it lights the block or the body. **Binding line**: `highlight binding: H highlight(s) over A act(s) — B on a block, Y on a body; R refused`. Prescription: remove `highlight`, or give the act a thing to light. |
| `DW1017` | **A compass on an objective nobody is told about** (spec-0104 §6.6; validation tier, `compiler::promise::check_compass`). `compass: shown` on an objective that is not announced — no `title`, or a resolved `announcement` of `hidden` (the message says which) — is refused: the compass would point at a thing the player has no name for, and the line naming what is followed would have nothing to say. **Binding line**: `compass binding: T tracked objective(s) of N — F by their own field, D by the campaign default; R refused`. Prescription: announce the objective, or remove the field. |
| `DW0961` | **An announcement asked for with nothing to announce** (spec-0093 §7). An objective states `announcement: shown` and has no `title`. The announcement's first line is the title; without one the emitter prints no line, plays no cue and marks nothing complete on screen, so the declaration binds to nothing — refused rather than ignored, because an inert opt-in is the vacuous shape: the author believes the party is told, and the party is not. `hidden` with no title is quiet on purpose and is not refused. Prescription: give the objective a `title`, or remove the field. |
| `DW0964` | **A prop on a trigger with no cell of its own** (spec-0093 §6.5, §7; validation tier, `compiler::promise`). A prop is the visible object a click acts on, at the `use` or `strike` trigger's own cell; an approach is a body's position, a `strike-npc` a character and a `strike-assembly` an assembly, so the block would be placed and nothing would read it — refused rather than ignored. Prescription: remove `prop`, or make the trigger a `use` or a `strike`. |

**What these deliberately do not claim.** The ledger's general form behind `DW0862`
is *an objective's prompt names the place and the act that actually complete it*,
and that is not what is proven: a machine cannot read prose for whether it names
the right place. What is proven is the necessary condition — that the prompt
reaches a player at all. The stronger reading was built and measured: keying "the
place" to the objective's quest's declared `area` finds three objectives on a live
campaign, and all three are correct — a quest booked in one area whose objective is
*travelling to* the next names the destination on purpose. A quest's `area` is
where the beat is booked, not where each objective completes, so it is not a sound
proxy for the place. Likewise `DW0861` proves that the party is told what to look
for and what they have found, not that the target is visually distinguishable from
its neighbours; and the ledger's second disjunct — *or every copy satisfies it* —
is not expressible at all, because `container` names one anchor.
