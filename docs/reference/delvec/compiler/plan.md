# `delvec::compiler::plan`

The reference page for `crates/delvec/src/compiler/plan/mod.rs`: the diagnostics-catalog row of every DW code
this module declares. The catalog's shared rules are in [`compiler.md` §5](../../compiler.md#5-diagnostics-catalog).

## Diagnostics

### DW03xx — build / solver / nav (`compiler`; error; exit 3, `stage:"build"`)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::compiler::nav` page](nav.md#dw03xx--build--solver--nav-compiler-error-exit-3-stagebuild).

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `DW0300` | Generic build/resolution failure (missing prefab metadata/`.nbt`, unresolved anchor, critical-path dependency cycle). |
| `DW0306` | Gate-aware reachability deadlock (an anchor reachable only through a gate no earlier objective opens). Modelled by **splitting the carrying piece into two halves** along the gate plane, joined only by the gate cut-edge — so an in-piece bypass *around* the gate is invisible to it and a far-side objective always reads as a deadlock. **Shortcut gates are exempt by construction, not by a special case**: the heuristic's gate set comes from `open-gate` effect anchors only (`collect_open_gate_anchors`), and a stage-5 `shortcut` gate has no `open-gate` effect, so it never enters the piece-split graph. Its deadlock obligation is discharged by a strictly stronger proof instead — `Plan::build` seals every shortcut gate at step 0, so the cell-level `DW0311` critical-path proof must find the long route over real geometry (delete the long route and the build fails at `DW0311`, not `DW0306`). This is why a souls loop-back — whose `unlock` sits on the far side by definition — must be declared as a `shortcut` and not as a plain `open-gate` reward on the far-side objective; the latter is genuinely self-deadlocking and is still rejected. Pinned by `souls_shortcut::{a_shortcut_owned_gate_is_not_a_dw0306_deadlock, the_same_gate_as_a_plain_open_gate_is_still_dw0306, a_shortcut_with_no_long_route_is_rejected_by_the_critical_path_proof}` — the same geometry, green as a shortcut and red as a plain gate. |
| `DW0344` | **A piece that stands in the sea answers for where it meets it.** Two arms, one code. *The document arm* (`compiler::seating::walk_plane_over_waterline`, **validation tier, exit 1**, and reported by `delvec prefab seating`): in a `horizon: ocean` world a piece whose metadata declares BOTH `walk_y` and `waterline_y` must stand its walk plane one course above its own waterline. The area's origin is DERIVED from the walk plane (spec-0060 §3.2), so `pos.y + waterline_y == 62` is exactly `waterline_y == walk_y - 1`, and both numbers are in the document: nothing has to be placed to know it, and the message names the walk plane that would seat this waterline on the sea AND the waterline the bytes would have to hold, because either number could be the typed one. A piece that declares no `walk_y` at all meets `DW0886` before this. Measured on the content library at revision `8e09c30`, where the three pieces that declare both stand one course clear: `cave-shore` (`walk_y: 2`, `waterline_y: 1`), `island-beach-camp` and `island-galley` (both `walk_y: 3`, `waterline_y: 2`). **`compiler::plan::check_ocean_waterline` keeps the binding count and refuses nothing**: the document arm's coverage is a superset for every piece seated at its area origin, and the one case it does not cover — a stair child, mated through a socket at a different local y — would be an over-refusal by construction, of the same shape §1.2 diagnoses in the other arm, since a piece four courses up a stair cannot stand its water at sea level and nothing is wrong with it unless a body walks there. *The walk-plane arm* (`compiler::plan::check_ocean_walk_plane`, build tier, called from `emit` where the walk region and the placed boxes are both in hand): **is any walk cell of a placed piece at or below the sea plane?** A walk cell is where a body's feet go, and one under the sea is a party wading its critical path. **What this arm does not ask, and why.** It does not ask whether the placement BOX reaches the sea plane: the box's minimum y IS the area origin, so under one global ocean datum that is true of every piece in every ocean world whatever its bytes hold, and a refusal's remedy — *raise the piece clear of the sea* — could not be performed by any amount of authoring. The quantifier is the cells, and the RAISE move is observable: the count in the refusal is what the author's own edit moves (`crates/delvec/tests/remedy_reachability.rs`). The static half of the same question, which needs nothing placed, is `DW0886`; this arm is the backstop for what the documents could not know — a piece the campaign edits after placement above all. It carries **`DW0344` and not `DW0886`** deliberately: it is one fact, and giving its earlier statement a different number would leave a creator with two codes for one answer. **Binding**: `waterline binding: horizon base …` states pieces placed and pieces held to sea level on every build, ocean or not; `sea walk-plane binding: horizon base …` states walk cells judged out of walk cells examined, and how many stand at or below the sea. |
| `DW0345` | The assembled world resolves **no entry anchor** — no area places a piece whose prefab metadata declares an anchor with `"role": "entry"`; an anchor's NAME is never consulted, so no spelling supplies one. See §4 "Entry point". The compiler then has no cell to call the campaign's start: no `setworldspawn`, no class-apply teleport, no first-join placement, no `dw:cp` seed. Build-tier (exit 3), `compiler::emit`, and raised **before any model is built** — a world with no start does not have a walking problem, and letting it through would report `DW0311` over a leg nothing was meant to walk, which sends a reader looking for a wedged doorway. Unrefused, the delve would fall back to the vanilla spawn search, which a **dedicated** server resolves to the surface (so every rung of the validation ladder stays green) and the **integrated singleplayer** server resolves to the build floor, i.e. inside solid stone. Prescription: give the piece the party arrives in an anchor at that cell and put `"role": "entry"` on it (in a `prefab_pool`, that is the prefab the layout is seeded from), or bind the area to a prefab that already has one. Every producer can write it — `delvec prefab anchor <nbt> --name <anchor> --pos <x,y,z> --role entry` for a hand-built or ingested piece, `"role": "entry"` on the `mark` for a grammar program. |
| `DW0872` | **A crossing into an area with nowhere to arrive.** A leg of the party's forced route changes area, and the destination declares no entry point — no anchor in its prefab carries `"role": "entry"`, or the one that does resolves to a region rather than to a cell. Areas sit `AREA_SPACING` (256) blocks apart across void, so a leg that changes area is never a walk: the party has to be put down inside the destination, and nothing says where. Build tier (exit 3), `compiler::plan`, raised while the critical path is built — so it precedes the walk proof, which is the whole point. Unrefused, the crossing would not be emitted and the leg would fall through to `DW0311`, telling the author *the player cannot walk … a wedged doorway seam, a void gap, an unbroken fence ring* — every word true about a walk, and this leg is not one. `DW0345` is the same rule over the **whole world** (no area at all declares one) and `DW0872` over **the one area a body must be put down in**; different quantifiers, different remedies, two codes. Prescription, in spec-0046's vocabulary: give the piece the party arrives in an anchor at that cell and put `"role": "entry"` on it (in a `prefab_pool`, that is the prefab the layout is seeded from), or bind the area to a prefab that already has one. |
| `DW0873` | **The party's first leg is a crossing, and nothing can carry it.** The campaign spawn and the first objective on the critical path stand in different areas. A crossing rides on the completion of the objective the party leaves from, and at the spawn they have completed nothing — so the first leg can be neither ridden nor walked and the delve cannot be started. Build tier (exit 3), `compiler::plan`. A leg enumeration that pairs *consecutive objectives* misses this member: the spawn is a leg's origin that is not an objective, so the party's opening move is in no pair, and unrefused, such a campaign compiles clean, passes every generated game test, and strands the harness at the spawn with `No path to the goal!` and no code at all. Prescription: put the campaign's first beat where the party starts (the quest's `area` in the quest plan), or start the delve where the beat already is by listing that area first in `world.areas` — the delve starts in the first area declaring an entry point of its own, so that area needs one. There is no third way across, and that is deliberate: a delve whose opening move is a teleport out of the area it starts in has put its spawn in the wrong area, and the refusal says so rather than inventing a start-of-run crossing to carry it. |

### DW0932–DW0934 — links (`compiler::plan` / `compiler::link` / `compiler::nav`; spec-0083)

A **link** is a `teleport` in a repeatable trigger carrying a body within one area (effect `teleport`). These three refusals hold the claim the route proof leans on: *a body in this volume is carried onto a route cell, after the camera returns, along a way the layout graph draws*.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `DW0932` | **A link whose geometry does not hold.** Build tier (exit 3). Five faults under one code, because they are one claim — *a body in this volume is carried onto a route cell*: **no stand cell** (no standable cell inside `from` performs the trigger — the body it presses stands outside its own volume, so whoever presses it is not carried); **`to` inside `from`** (a regroup, not a way onward); **`to` not standable at the teleport's tick** (the world as the link's own root leaves it then: the root's region writes at an earlier tick applied, forced, and its fills at that tick or later not yet laid — a link onto air is a drop); **`from` and `to` in different areas** (a move between areas is the compiler's crossing). The second and fourth are judged in `Plan::build` for every link; the first and third where the route proof leans on the link — a leg it is considered for, or a trigger the path performs that carries — because a stand cell and footing are questions about blocks. The message names the trigger, the teleport, the volume's box, the `to` cell and the fault; and a fifth, **pressed from outside its volume** (spec-0092 §10): a link whose root plays a `cutscene` that ends before the teleport takes everyone or no one, because `cs_end` puts every player on the cell the presser stood on — so every cell the walk region reaches (`World::reachable_walkable_rooted` from the anchor roots) that performs the trigger (`nav::press_cells_outside`, the rule `stand_cells` reads) must lie inside `from`; one outside strands the whole party there. The trigger's own cell may not be in the volume (`DW0542`), so it must be a cell no body stands in. Every build prints `link gathering binding: G link(s) carried after their root's cutscene, P press cell(s) outside their volumes over W walk cell(s)`. The remedies: move the body or widen the volume so a cell inside it reaches the body; move `to` off the volume and onto footing, or lay the floor at an earlier tick of the same root; use a crossing for another area. |
| `DW0933` | **A teleport under its root's cutscene.** Validation tier (exit 1), read off the documents. `cs_end_<bare>` puts every player back on the marker `cs_start_<bare>` saved, so a `teleport` — link or gather — that fires at or before the tick its root's `cutscene` ends is undone for everyone when the camera returns. The tick is the emitter's own: a cutscene starting at tick `t` runs `cs_end` at `t + Σ(shot_ticks(seconds) + 1)` over its shots (`link::cutscene_end_offset`, the sum `emit::cutscene_fns` lays the shots by). A `teleport` and a `cutscene` in one flat bundle, or a `sequence` whose teleport tick is at or before `cs_end`'s, is refused; the message names the root, the teleport, the cutscene, the tick `cs_end` runs at and the first tick that holds. **Exclusive gates never overlap**: a cutscene and a teleport that sit under two effects of the root's own list whose `when` gates exclude each other (`Gate::exclusions` — a flag one requires and the other forbids, or one datum's terms across both meeting in the empty set) never play in one firing, provided the root's own pass writes none of the keys that exclude them. That pass is each effect of the list, a `sequence`'s tick-0 steps, and every other nested bundle counted whole. Gates on distinct flags still overlap, and so does a pair whose excluding flag the pass itself sets (the cutscene's branch setting it at tick 0). A `sequence` step's own `when` is tested when the step runs, so it is never read as exclusive. Remedy: put the teleport in a `sequence` step at that tick or later. |
| `DW0934` | **The layout graph and the quests disagree about carries.** Validation tier (exit 1), for a campaign that carries a layout graph. A link whose `from` station and `to` mark stand in two places (a station a node declares, or a node's own `anchor/node-<slug>`) that no `carry` edge joins in that direction; a link whose anchor belongs to no place; or a `carry` direction no link realises. **A crossing realises a carry**: where the two places' beats belong to quests the quest plan books in different areas, and the graph's `critical_path` steps from the one to the other, the compiler's crossing (`Plan::transport`, one-way by construction) is the carry, and no link is owed; the route proofs judge the crossing itself (`DW0872`, `DW0873`). A link whose two anchors stand in one place is a move inside it and owes the graph nothing. The message names the link or the edge, both places and the direction. Remedy: draw the edge, or host a teleport from a station of the one place to a mark in the other on a repeatable trigger — or make the edge one-way (or remove it) where that direction is not a way. |

### DW0985 — a numeric gate only presses move (`compiler::plan`; error)

The critical path drives a gate whose datum only hand-pressed triggers write ([`compiler.md`](../../compiler.md), `trigger` steps).

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `DW0985` | **No press sequence opens a gate only presses move.** Build tier (exit 3), `plan::path::drive`. An objective's `requires_state` reads a driven datum — one every write of which is a top-level effect of a `use`/`strike` trigger on an anchor — and from the value the path holds walking up to it, no sequence of at most 64 presses (16384 distinct states) of those triggers, each bundle replayed line by line with every `when` read against the value the lines before it produced, satisfies the gate. The message names the objective, the terms, the presses tried, and every value they reach. The usual cause is a bundle whose write is followed by a line gated on the value it just produced (the shape `DW0527` describes). Remedy: move every reading effect ahead of the write, or change the gate. Pinned by `numeric_gate_drive::a_gate_no_press_sequence_can_open_is_dw0985`. |

### DW07xx — asset, render and authoring tooling (spec-0007; `delvec` subcommands)

This module's rows of a section whose prose is on the [`delvec::admit::diag` page](../admit/diag.md#dw07xx--asset-render-and-authoring-tooling-spec-0007-delvec-subcommands).

| Code | Tool | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| `DW0804` | `compiler::plan` | **Two anchors in one area declare the entry role.** An area has one place the party arrives at, and two claims to it is a question the compiler cannot answer: picking first-wins would settle it by piece order, or by the `BTreeMap` order of two anchor names nobody chose for their sort, and a spawn that moved for that reason is a mystery nothing in the build output mentions. Build tier (exit 3), raised while the anchors are being resolved, so it precedes every check that would have been computed from the wrong start. The message names the area and **both** claimants. Only reachable through a declared `role`, which is the only thing that makes an anchor an entry point. Prescription is to take the `role` off the anchor that is not the arrival cell — the anchor itself stays and content still binds it by name — and, in a `prefab_pool`, to leave the role on the piece that seeds the layout. The remedy is reachable where the anchor was written: `delvec prefab anchor <nbt> --name <anchor> --pos <x,y,z> --no-role` for a hand-built or ingested piece, dropping `role` from the `mark` for a grammar program. Renaming an anchor achieves nothing, because a name is never read here. |
